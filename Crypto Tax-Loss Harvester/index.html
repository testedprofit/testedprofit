How the estimate is built

Ordinary tax uses the 2026 standard deduction and the 2026 rate brackets published by the IRS. For single filers the standard deduction is $16,100. For married couples filing jointly it is $32,200. The single brackets run 10% through $12,400, 12% through $50,400, 22% through $105,700, 24% through $201,775, 32% through $256,225, 35% through $640,600, and 37% above that. Joint brackets are twice the 10% and 12% thresholds at the bottom, then $211,400, $403,550, $512,450, and $768,700. Those dollar amounts are the tops of each bracket. The IRS newsroom states them as the incomes the next rate applies above.

Long-term capital gains are stacked on top of taxable ordinary income. Revenue Procedure 2025-32 sets the 2026 maximum zero-rate amount and the maximum 15% amount. For single filers those amounts are $49,450 and $545,500. For married filing jointly they are $98,900 and $613,700. Gain above the 15% amount is taxed at 20% in this checker. Head of household and married filing separately are not options here.

A harvestable loss is the amount by which cost exceeds the current price, times the number of units. Losses reduce long-term gains first. If the loss is larger than the gains, the checker applies up to $3,000 against ordinary income. IRS Tax Topic 409 describes that $3,000 limit, and a $1,500 limit for married filing separately. This checker does not include the separate limit.

The checker does not apply the wash-sale rule, the net investment income tax, state tax, or the crypto-specific reporting forms. Selling a security at a loss and buying that same security again inside 30 days can disallow the loss under IRS wash-sale rules. This page does not recommend buying the asset back.

Worked example

The numbers in the form when the page loads are this example. A single filer has $85,000 of ordinary income and no long-term gains already booked. One holding is 0.5 units bought at $68,000 and now priced at $42,000. The unrealized loss is 0.5 times $26,000, or $13,000.

Taxable ordinary income before any harvest is $85,000 minus the $16,100 standard deduction, or $68,900. Tax on that income is 10% of $12,400 ($1,240), 12% of the next $38,000 ($4,560), and 22% of the remaining $18,500 ($4,070). The bill is $9,870.

The $13,000 loss is larger than the $0 of gains, so $3,000 reduces ordinary income and the rest is unused in this estimate. Taxable income becomes $65,900. The 22% slice shrinks to $15,500, or $3,410, and the bill becomes $9,210. The difference is $660, which is $3,000 times the 22% rate still in force on that slice.

Questions

What tax year does this checker use?

Tax year 2026. Ordinary brackets and the standard deduction follow the IRS newsroom figures. Long-term capital gain thresholds follow Revenue Procedure 2025-32.

How does a loss change the estimate?

The checker subtracts harvestable losses from realized long-term gains first. If losses are larger, it subtracts up to $3,000 from ordinary income, which is the limit in IRS Tax Topic 409 for filers other than married filing separately.

Does the checker apply wash-sale rules?

No. It estimates the tax change from realizing a loss. Selling a security and buying it again within 30 days can disallow the loss. This page does not tell you to buy the asset back.
