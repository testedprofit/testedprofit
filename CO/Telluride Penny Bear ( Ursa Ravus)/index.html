Telluride, Colorado Ave

Ursa Ravus

Ursa Ravus is the Telluride penny bear, a 15-foot grizzly on East Colorado Avenue. People in town call her the Penny Bear. Her fur is 187,000 pennies set in concrete over carved styrofoam and a frame of steel tubing. Judy and Steven Gluckstern commissioned it and gave it to the town. It went in during July 2019, and Telluride Arts still lists it as active public art.

Place
635 East Colorado Avenue, Telluride, Colorado 81435. Pin 37.93586, -107.80490.
Size
15 feet tall. The Daily Planet weighs her at 6,000 pounds and counts the fur as over 187,000 pennies. The artists and Telluride Arts count 187,000.
Makers
Lisa and Robert Ferguson of Hayward, California, with Jane Poynter and Dottie Lester. Tanner Reed formed the nose and claws. Bee Kingdom Glass of Calgary blew the eyes.
Gift
An Independence Day gift from the Glucksterns, installed July 2019. The first sanctioned public art installation in Telluride.

A bear built of pennies

Mr. and Mrs. Ferguson Art built Ursa Ravus for Judy and Steven Gluckstern as the first sanctioned piece of public art for Telluride. Lisa and Robert Ferguson are the artists. The Daily Planet, in Amy M. Peters's 21 July 2019 story Raising Ursa Ravus, calls them self-taught and places their studio in Hayward, California. The work took four months. Assistants Jane Poynter and Dottie Lester worked on it with them. Tanner Reed, a welder at Ferguson Welding Service, formed the nose and claws. The eyes are glass blown by Bee Kingdom Glass of Calgary, Canada.

The fur is the part you notice from the sidewalk. Telluride Arts and the artists describe 187,000 pennies embedded in concrete, over carved styrofoam, on steel tubing. The Daily Planet's count is over 187,000 pennies, and that story is the one that gives the weight, 6,000 pounds, and the street, 635 East Colorado Avenue. She stands 15 feet. The same story calls her a grizzly. The artists title the piece The grizzly bear returns to Colorado.

The Glucksterns gave Ursa Ravus to the town as an Independence Day gift. Installation was July 2019. By 21 July the Daily Planet was already writing about the bear on Colorado Avenue. Telluride Arts files the sculpture under Art in Public Places and marks the page Ursa Ravus (Active).

Telluride's public art, and this valley

Telluride Arts is the organization that publishes the public-art record. Ursa Ravus sits in that record as the piece the Glucksterns commissioned and then gave away. The artists and the Daily Planet both call it the first sanctioned public art installation in town. Later works have a list to join. This one opened the list: a private commission, a gift to the town, and a permanent figure on the main street rather than a gallery piece you have to buy a ticket to see.

The artists describe the setting as an alpine valley with two major film festivals and one of the country's beloved ski resorts. That is the town around the bear. Colorado Avenue is the business street, and 635 sits on the east end of that walk. The sidewalk is the viewing spot. The car needs a plan, because Visit Telluride says parking in town is limited.

Parking, the gondola, and the bus

Visit Telluride says parking in town is limited. Vehicle camping is prohibited. Colorado Avenue through the business district is metered: one meter per block, or a QR code on the street sign. Many side streets offer free two-hour parking from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Read the sign on the block you choose, and watch for winter snow-removal rules.

Two free all-day lots sit on the west end. Carhenge is at 700 W. Pacific Ave., about a 10-minute walk from downtown, and the Galloping Goose bus serves it. Shandoka is at 860 Black Bear Road, at the far west end. Neither lot allows overnight parking. If you need a covered space or an overnight stay with the car, the Silverjack garage at 155 W. Pacific Ave. charges by the hour, and overnight parking there is available for a fee.

The easier arrival is the gondola. The Telluride Mountain Village Owner's Association describes it as free public transportation between Mountain Village and Telluride, opened 20 December 1996, built first for air quality and the ski area. It runs 6:30 a.m. to midnight daily in the winter and summer seasons, and to 2 a.m. on Fridays and Saturdays in the busy season. In the off-season the gondola closes and a bus covers the two towns. The Telluride end begins on Oak Street. From there, walk Colorado Avenue east to number 635. The line climbs to San Sophia, where you can step off for trails or Allred's, then continues to Gondola Plaza in Mountain Village.

If you drive to the mountain side, the Gondola Parking Garage at 433 Mountain Village Blvd. is free in the daytime in summer and paid in winter. Overnight parking there is allowed for a fee. Ride across and walk east. In town, the Galloping Goose loops past the high school, Shandoka, Carhenge, the gondola, Town Park, the courthouse, and the post office. In the regular season it runs about every 15 or 20 minutes. For the town-loop schedule, call (970) 728-2177. Visit Telluride's visitor line on the parking page is 1-888-605-2578.

Other Colorado figures

Ursa Ravus is the Telluride bear. In the San Juans, the Christ of the Mines Shrine in Silverton is the next high-town stop to the east, and Riverfront Park in Durango is the river town down the valley. Farther east, the Tennyson Sculpture Garden in Pagosa is another outdoor collection. At Red Rocks, the Spirit statue is a different Colorado bronze. The full list is the Colorado directory.

Questions

How tall is the Telluride penny bear, and what is the fur?

Ursa Ravus is 15 feet tall. The fur is 187,000 pennies set in concrete over carved styrofoam and a steel-tubing frame. The Daily Planet puts the weight at 6,000 pounds and the penny count at over 187,000.

Where does it stand, and who commissioned it?

It stands at 635 East Colorado Avenue in Telluride. Judy and Steven Gluckstern commissioned Lisa and Robert Ferguson of Hayward, California, and gave the bear to the town. It was installed in July 2019.

Is the penny bear still on display?

Telluride Arts lists Ursa Ravus as active public art. Tanner Reed formed the nose and claws. The eyes are glass blown by Bee Kingdom Glass of Calgary, Canada.

How do I get there without a parking fight?

The free gondola runs from Oak Street in Telluride to Mountain Village, 6:30 a.m. to midnight in winter and summer. In summer, daytime parking at the Gondola Parking Garage, 433 Mountain Village Blvd., is free. In town, Carhenge at 700 W. Pacific Ave. and Shandoka at 860 Black Bear Road are free all day, with no overnight parking. Colorado Avenue in the business district is metered.
