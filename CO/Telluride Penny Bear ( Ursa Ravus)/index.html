Ursa Ravus

The penny bear

Telluride Arts lists Ursa Ravus as active and says people in Telluride also call it the Penny Bear. The page says she is 15 feet tall. Her fur is 187,000 pennies embedded in concrete over a body of carved styrofoam and a frame of steel tubing. The same paragraph says the bear was finished in four months with assistants Jane Poynter and Dottie Lester, that Tanner Reed of Ferguson Welding Service formed the nose and claws, and that the eyes are glass blown by Bee Kingdom Glass of Calgary, Canada. It says Ursa Ravus was an Independence Day gift from the Glucksterns to Telluride, installed in July 2019. The artists' own page repeats that description and says Judy and Steven Gluckstern commissioned it as the first sanctioned piece of public art for the town.

The street, and the weight

The Telluride Daily Planet, in Raising Ursa Ravus, says a 6,000-pound grizzly named Ursa Ravus stands at 635 East Colorado Avenue. It says the Gluckstern family commissioned Mr. and Mrs. Ferguson Art of Hayward, California, to build and install it, and it calls the work the first sanctioned public art installation in Telluride. It says Lisa and Robert Ferguson took four months and that the fur is comprised of over 187,000 pennies. The arts page says 187,000 pennies. The news story says over 187,000 and adds the weight and the street. This guide keeps both penny counts.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

How tall is the Telluride penny bear, and what is the fur?

Telluride Arts says Ursa Ravus is 15 feet tall and that the fur is 187,000 pennies in concrete over carved styrofoam and steel tubing. The Telluride Daily Planet says the bear weighs 6,000 pounds and that the fur is over 187,000 pennies.

Where does it stand, and who commissioned it?

The Daily Planet places it at 635 East Colorado Avenue and says the Gluckstern family commissioned Lisa and Robert Ferguson. Telluride Arts says the Glucksterns gave it to the town and that it was installed in July 2019.
