Westminster City Park

Westminster City Park covers 205 acres at 10455 Sheridan Boulevard, Westminster, Colorado 80031. The city calls it a regional or citywide park. The grounds hold ball fields, a tribute garden, a pond, trails toward Big Dry Creek, and a disc-golf course the city reopened in 2025.

Plan your visit

Address
10455 Sheridan Boulevard, Westminster, CO 80031, on the city facility page. The disc-golf reopening notice uses 10495 Sheridan Boulevard and ZIP 80020 for the lot west of the Parks Operation Center.
Parking
The facility page lists parking among the amenities. For the disc-golf grand reopening, the calendar says there is a parking lot just to the west of the Parks Operation Center, at 10495 Sheridan Boulevard.
Hours
The facility page does not post a general open or close time for the grounds. Parks, Recreation and Libraries administrative hours are Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., at City Hall, 4800 W 92nd Avenue, phone 303-658-2192. Water-feature hours are listed separately.
Accessibility
The city lists restrooms, including a portable restroom and a restroom at Christopher Fields, plus parking and a paved loop walking path with climbing stairs. It does not publish a separate accessibility map on the facility page.
Best time
The water feature is described as open from Memorial Day through Labor Day, and the same page marks those hours closed for drought restrictions. Fountains may also be shut off for weather, climate, maintenance, or operations. Field sports and the paved loop are listed year-round amenities. The page does not name a quieter hour.

What the city lists on the grounds

The facility page classifies City Park as regional or citywide and gives the size as 205 acres. The amenity list names access to Big Dry Creek and North Hylands Creek open space, and access to the Big Dry Creek regional trail. It names the Armed Forces Tribute Garden, Christopher Fields with four softball fields, and four soccer fields. It lists a drinking fountain, a fitness center, parking, and a paved loop walking path with climbing stairs.

Shelters and tables come next. There are three pavilions, and the page says one of them is reservable. Nineteen picnic tables are available. Playground equipment is listed, including a tiny-tot area in the photo captions on the same page. The pond is listed with a plain warning that fishing is not allowed. The recreation center, restrooms, a skateboard park, and trails finish the list. One restroom line says portable. Another says the restroom facility is at Christopher Fields.

The fountain and the tribute garden

Water-feature hours on the facility page carry two lines at once. The heading says closed for drought restrictions. The next line says open from Memorial Day through Labor Day, with operational hours marked not available. A note says the fountains may be shut off at any time in the season because of weather, climate, maintenance, or other operations. Both lines are on the page, so a summer visit can still find the water off.

The city’s Armed Forces Tribute Garden page places that garden just east of U.S. 36 on 104th Avenue, between Christopher Fields and Big Dry Creek. It says the garden is dedicated to people who serve in the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, and Merchant Marines, past, present, and future. The description names a central water feature, seating for rest and contemplation, granite pillars, and walkways with engraved pavers. The fountain season on that page is the same Memorial Day through Labor Day window, with the same warning that the fountain may be shut off. The page says the city holds a ceremony at the garden every year on Armed Forces Day. Under honorees, it refers to more than 1,600 people.

Christopher Fields, named in both the facility list and the tribute-garden location, are the four softball fields. Big Dry Creek is the open-space and trail connection on the facility page and the eastern edge the tribute-garden page uses. The park is the ground that holds those pieces together along Sheridan Boulevard.

Disc golf

The city calendar lists a disc-golf grand reopening on Saturday, 25 October 2025, from 11:45 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. The address on that notice is 10495 Sheridan Boulevard, Westminster, CO 80020, with parking in a lot just west of the Parks Operation Center. The notice says the course had been temporarily removed for a construction project and has been rebuilt and refreshed. That street number is not the same as the park’s 10455 Sheridan Boulevard. Both numbers are on city pages. The park page is the facility address. The calendar is the reopening address.

A UDisc course page says the course was established in 2003, closed in 2023, and redesigned in 2025. It says the original layout had 18 holes that started near the baseball fields, and that the new layout has nine holes as the first of two phases toward 18, with short and long positions on most holes. Play starts at the end of the main parking lot. Those details are on UDisc. The city facility page does not repeat them.

What to look for

OpenStreetMap places Westminster City Park Recreation Center at 10455 Sheridan Boulevard. The city’s photo labels on the facility page point at a playground, a walking track, stairs, the pond, soccer, and a tiny-tot area. The pond is the place marked with no fishing. Christopher Fields are the softball side. The tribute garden is the formal landscape east of U.S. 36 on 104th, between those fields and Big Dry Creek.

Nearby sights

Big Dry Creek and North Hylands Creek open space, and the Big Dry Creek regional trail, are the open-space links the city prints on the park page. Other Westminster and nearby guides on this site cover different parks and statues. They are not inside City Park’s 205 acres.

Questions

How big is Westminster City Park, and what is the address?

The city facility page says 205 acres at 10455 Sheridan Boulevard, Westminster, CO 80031. It lists Christopher Fields with four softball fields, four soccer fields, three pavilions, nineteen picnic tables, and a pond where fishing is not allowed.

Are the fountains running?

The facility page labels water-feature hours as closed for drought restrictions, and it also says the feature is open from Memorial Day through Labor Day. It warns that fountains may be shut off for weather, climate, maintenance, or other operations. The tribute garden page uses the same Memorial Day to Labor Day window for its fountain.

When did the disc-golf course reopen?

The city calendar sets a grand reopening for 25 October 2025, 11:45 a.m. to 12:30 p.m., at 10495 Sheridan Boulevard, after the course was removed for construction. UDisc says the course was established in 2003, closed in 2023, and redesigned in 2025, with nine holes as the first phase toward 18.
