Former Mine Sites of Mount Harris

The town

The Colorado Cattlemen's Agricultural Land Trust announced on 13 September 2021 that it had conserved the site of the former town of Mount Harris. The release says the town was once the most populous community in Routt County, with as many as 1,295 residents along the Yampa River in the canyon between Hayden and Steamboat Springs. It was founded in 1914, when the Colorado-Utah Coal Company acquired the land and George and Byron Harris developed the town. Miners and their families from the United States, Mexico, Italy, Greece, Eastern Europe, and England worked in three mines and lived in Mount Harris. The release says segregation existed, and that school children of every race and ethnicity attended the Mount Harris school together.

The trust calls Mount Harris the largest coal company town in Routt County. Citing the Historical Guide to Routt County, the release says most homes were painted white with gray trim, that barns along the river were provided for families who kept milk cows, chickens, and geese, and that a large sandstone building held the mining office, a general store, a drug store, a pool hall, a barber shop, and the post office. It also lists three churches, three boarding houses, a community center, and a two-story bandstand. On 20 May 1958, the release says, the entire town was dismantled and auctioned off. Houses and mining equipment were sold and moved, and what remained was demolished.

The easement

The conserved property is 88.29 acres: open meadows, sagebrush rangeland, and a forested riparian and wetland corridor along about 1.3 miles of the Yampa River. The release says the land is bordered by and highly visible from U.S. Highway 40. It supports modest summer grazing and beekeeping, and the trust describes big-game habitat for mule deer, elk, and moose, plus bald eagle, golden eagle, sandhill crane, and Columbian sharp-tailed grouse. It says this part of the river holds river otter and rainbow, brown, and cutthroat trout, and that it is a float-fishing corridor. Partners named in the release are landowners Karen and Richard Whitney, the Upper Yampa Habitat Partnership Program, Keep It Colorado, and The Nature Conservancy. The acreage is private land under easement. It is not a public ghost town, and this guide does not send visitors off the highway onto the property.

The Wadge Mine

The Hayden Heritage Center's account of the Wadge Mine explosion is a separate place from the townsite easement. It says 34 miners died in an explosion at about 9:45 p.m. on Tuesday, 27 January 1942, at the Victor American Fuel Company's Wadge Mine, one of two mines then operating near Mount Harris. It says the main tunnel was northwest of town. The center also says the Mount Harris mine closed on 20 May 1958 and that the town, mostly owned by the coal company, was dismantled to avoid taxes and liability, with many buildings moved to Hayden and nearby areas. The trust's release uses the same date for the auction and demolition and does not give the tax wording.

Questions

When was Mount Harris dismantled?

The Colorado Cattlemen's Agricultural Land Trust says the entire town was dismantled and auctioned on 20 May 1958. The Hayden Heritage Center says the Mount Harris mine closed that day and the town was dismantled to avoid taxes and liability.

Can visitors walk the old townsite?

The trust says the 88.29-acre property is highly visible from U.S. Highway 40 and is protected by a conservation easement. It is private land. This guide treats the highway view and the river corridor described by the trust as the public record, not a license to cross the property.
