U.S. 40, milepost 114.5

Former Mine Sites of Mount Harris

Mount Harris was a coal camp in the Yampa canyon between Hayden and Steamboat Springs. The Colorado-Utah Coal Company opened the Harris Mine on 12 June 1914. The town was auctioned away on 20 May 1958. A four-panel marker from 1990 stands in the highway pull-out. The old townsite itself is private land. This is an independent Profitnet guide.

1958

1914

Forty-four years of a company town, then an auction. What you can walk today is the rest-area kiosk, not the meadows.

See the marker, and stay off the land

Pull-out
South side of U.S. 40 at milepost 114.5, at County Highway 52, between Hayden and Milner. Traveling east, the marker is on the right, across from a dirt road. It sits in a large pull-out and rest area. Four panels share a wooden kiosk. Hayden post office 81639. The pin is 40.47965, -107.14077.
Private acres
The 88.29-acre townsite is a conservation easement held by the Colorado Cattlemen's Agricultural Land Trust. It was closed to the public for more than sixty years. Picnics there are for former residents, by the landowners' invitation. Foundations that remain are on that private ground. Stay in the pull-out. Do not walk onto the meadows or park off the pavement.
Highway
U.S. 40 through the canyon is a through road. Use the pull-out. Do not walk the shoulder to look for mine entries. The Hayden Heritage Center and the Colorado Department of Transportation care for the memorial. The center is planning a perimeter path and more panels. Until that work is in place, the kiosk is the public stop.

The Harris brothers and the camp

The Hayden Heritage Center, which cares for the memorial with the highway department, says two brothers from Iowa, George and Byron Harris, developed the town after the Colorado-Utah Coal Company bought the land in 1914. The 1990 marker panel names the directors as George Homer and Bryon Harris, and it dates the Harris Mine opening to 12 June 1914. The camp was first called Harris. The name changed because another Colorado post office already used it.

The Harris Mine was a slope mine just south of the Yampa River. A conveyor crossed the river to a tipple on the D.S. and L. railroad. By 1920 the camp was the largest town in Routt County, with as many as 1,295 residents. Miners and families came from the United States, Mexico, Italy, Greece, Eastern Europe, and England. Segregation existed in the camp. Children of every race and ethnicity went to the Mount Harris school together.

The marker calls it a model mining camp. The town had its own power plant and water. Rent, with electricity and water, ran from $8 to $35. A rock building from the rim rocks held the general store, drug store, pool hall, barber shop, post office, and mine office. Around it were two churches, doctors' offices, a theater, a service station, two hotels, a bunk house, and a baseball field with a grandstand. The Athletic Club was the only licensed club in the area until 1933, and it drew athletes from Denver, Utah, and Wyoming. The heritage center adds wide, tree-lined streets, a cottonwood playground, generous yards, two town baseball teams, and rodeos in the park. Two doctors lived in town, one of them on call.

Colorado-Utah and Victor-American built the elementary school together. The first building was frame, and it burned. A brick school replaced it. The heritage center dates the company's four-room grade school to 1916 and the larger replacement to the 1930s, for grades 1 through 8. Older students went to high school in Hayden. A 2021 land-trust account, drawing on the Historical Guide to Routt County, describes white houses with gray trim, barns along the river for milk cows, chickens, and geese, three churches, three boarding houses, a community center for dances and movies, and a two-story bandstand.

The mines around the town

Three company operations framed the camp. The Harris Mine, Colorado-Utah Coal, ran from 1914 to 1958, with entries on the south side of the river. The marker counts about seven lives lost there in 44 years and names Bill Assmusson Jr., Burton Burns, Larry Phoenix, Gene Plaza, and Van Close among them. It calls the Harris Mine the safest in the area.

James Wadge had homesteaded the ground and mined it on a small scale before the companies arrived. The heritage center dates that homestead work to 1887 and names Sarah Wadge with him. Victor-American Fuel Company, John Osgood's firm, opened the Wadge Mine as a company operation in 1916 on the marker and in the heritage center's explosion account. The center's memorial page dates the company's purchase to 1917. A later seam opened under Gibraltar Rock, just west of the townsite. Worker housing for Wadge stood north of today's highway, on the hillside. The company store, hotel, and bathhouse served that camp. The Mount Harris business street, theater, post office, doctors, and churches served both camps. The two camps sat on adjacent ground and were hard to tell apart. Wadge closed in 1951. Victor-American had applied for reorganization bankruptcy in January 1950, listed the mine for sale in May 1951, and by May 1952 liquidation of the Wadge holdings had begun.

Wolf Creek, also called the P-K or Pinnacle-Kemmerer, started in 1915 with the Zarlango brothers and International Fuel. It sat northeast of town, up the draw from Wadge, and the heritage center carries it to 1942. Other producers inside about seven miles, on the marker, include Bear River (1913 to 1940, reopened as Sun Coal from 1975 to 1984), Coal View, and McGregor west of Milner, whose power plant later ran under Yampa Valley Electric until the Hayden plant opened in 1965. The marker says the exact opening and closing of every camp in that ring is not on the panels, and that the group of mines falls between 1913 and 1965.

27 January 1942

At about 9:45 p.m. on Tuesday, 27 January 1942, gas exploded in the Victor-American Wadge Mine. Thirty-four miners died. The Hayden Heritage Center ranks it the eighth-worst mining disaster in Colorado by the number of deaths. The blast was in the 19th branch tunnel, the one farthest west and north, a little over a mile from town and about 5,500 feet in from the portal. The main tunnel of Wadge No. 2 dropped northwest of town, a quarter mile north of the Yampa on Wolf Creek. The explosion was not heard outside. It cut the telephone lines into the mine.

Four men working on broken equipment near the entry got out: Joe Gall, 40, of Milner; Bill Fickle, 35, of Hayden; Elmer Everson, 23, of Hayden; and Mike Atansoff, 53, of Mount Harris. They heard a dull thud, smelled smoke, and ran for the air shaft. They alerted officials about 10:30 p.m. The shift had started at 3:30 p.m. and was due out at 11. Carbon dioxide filled the shaft. A crew from the Harris Mine, under pit boss Joe Burns, went in with blowers. They reached the miners around 4 a.m. and found no one alive there. The last body came out Friday evening. Many of the men were known by the brass check clipped to a belt with the cap lamp. Twenty-four of the thirty-four were married. Forty-three children lost a father. Ralph, Ross, and Raymond Cable were brothers, and Ralph was a brother-in-law of Elmer Hindman.

The record cause is a gas explosion set off by arc-electric machinery that was being used to clear gas. An inspection two months earlier had called the ventilation good. The Routt County field had been treated as almost free of explosive gas. The shafts themselves were not badly wrecked, and within a few weeks crews were putting power back to the pumps so the mine could reopen. The State of Colorado paid the funerals and $4,375 to each family, in payments of $750 a year for six years. The United Mine Workers paid about $100 to each family, and the local district gave $25 the day after the blast. Of the 34, the marker counts 22 from Wadge, 6 from Craig, 3 from Hayden, and 3 from Steamboat Springs.

Closure, and what is left

On 20 May 1958 the Harris Mine closed and the town was auctioned. The coal company owned most of it. The heritage center says the dismantling was to avoid taxes and liability, and that many buildings were moved to Hayden and nearby towns. The marker says the majority of the houses went to neighboring towns. What was not moved was demolished. The center says some foundations and remnants are still on the ground. A joint survey with Historic Routt County and the University of Colorado Denver is looking for 63 buildings that were auctioned and moved.

On 13 September 2021 the Colorado Cattlemen's Agricultural Land Trust announced an easement on the site, with landowners Karen and Richard Whitney, the Upper Yampa Habitat Partnership Program, Keep It Colorado, and The Nature Conservancy. The 88.29 acres take in meadows, sagebrush, and about 1.3 miles of forested river and wetland. The ground is beside U.S. 40 and easy to see from the road. It supports modest summer grazing and beekeeping. The trust names habitat for mule deer, elk, moose, bald eagle, golden eagle, sandhill crane, and Columbian sharp-tailed grouse, and a river reach with otter and rainbow, brown, and cutthroat trout. Float fishing uses the river. The banks of the easement are private. Landing there is not a public access.

Karen Whitney told Keep It Colorado that the land looks empty and that people were born, worked, loved, and died there. She and her husband bought it after it came on the market, approached the trust in late 2020, and donated the easement. It limits subdivision and the size of houses. Former residents are invited back for picnics and gatherings. That invitation is not a general opening.

Nearby stops

The Hayden Heritage Center is in the old depot at 300 West Pearl Street, Hayden, Colorado 81639. The phone is (970) 276-4380. The Mount Harris exhibit is there, and the museum holds a cleanup at the memorial and a Mt. Harris Day on a two-year rhythm. Call before you go. In the same county, the Routt County firing range is a separate public guide. In Craig, Bulldog Towing is an independent local guide. Woodbury Park is a Westminster field guide on this site, a long way from the Yampa.

Questions

Where is the Mount Harris marker?

In a pull-out and rest area on the south side of U.S. 40 at milepost 114.5, at County Highway 52, between Hayden and Milner. Traveling east, it is on the right. The kiosk went up in 1990.

What happened on 27 January 1942?

An explosion at about 9:45 p.m. in the Victor-American Wadge Mine killed 34 miners. Four men working near the entry escaped. Rescue crews reached the miners around 4 a.m.

Can you walk the old town?

The public stop is the highway pull-out. The townsite acres are private land under a conservation easement. Stay in the pull-out and off the property.
