Christ of the Rockies, the Santa Maria Statue

Christ of the Rockies is the Santa Maria statue in Colorado, a figure of Christ the King on the hill behind Camp Santa Maria near Grant. The figure is 33 feet high on a 22-foot base. The words Santa Maria are spelled out on the hillside below it. The statue and the letters are on private camp property. The view for a photograph is a roadside pull-off.

The camp on the South Platte

Camp Santa Maria sits on the old Cassells resort, a stop on the Denver, South Park and Pacific Railroad along the upper South Platte River. The railroad reached Platte Canyon in the 1870s. A resort opened in 1882, with a hotel, and a later hotel on the site burned in 1905. The place is near Shawnee, in northeastern Park County, the stretch of U.S. 285 that runs from Bailey toward Grant.

John and Mary Dower bought the 160-acre property in 1929. Mary Dower was the daughter of John Kernan Mullen, the Denver miller known as J. K. Mullen, who died on August 9, 1929. Thomas J. Noel dates the purchase to 1930 and names John L. Dower as Mullen's son-in-law. The Dowers also bought four nearby homesteads, called the Phantom Ranch, and brought the holding to almost 1,000 acres. They gave the place to Catholic Charities as a summer camp for children from eight to fifteen. By 1931 the new Camp Santa Maria was running three-week sessions, and the children slept in the old Cassells hotel.

Noel writes that since the 1950s the Mary Mullen Dower Benevolent Corporation has leased the camp to Catholic Community Services, for children's summer camping and also for retreats and autumn camping. A 2012 waymark places the same hill on YMCA Camp Santa Maria, just outside Bailey, and files the landmark under Grant. The statue and the letters are on that private ground either way. A photograph is the visit from the road.

How Christ of the Rockies was built

Christ of the Rockies was built between 1930 and 1934. Noel dates the Dowers' gift of the statue to 1933. Before the figure could rise, a road had to reach the site. In the Depression the Dowers recruited out-of-work men in Denver to build it. The road included a bridge that was later removed. After the statue was finished, the Dowers drove from their house at camp up to the figure in a 1930s Ford touring car. That road has narrowed. It is suited to hikers now, and it stays on camp property.

The figure has a steel frame. The robe is terra cotta tile, fired and glazed in Denver, brought to camp on the train, and carried up to the site. Concrete was poured into the frame and the tiles were attached. The hands and the head are separate pieces, fired to a glossy white so they would catch the sun and electric lights. One arm reaches out. That arm was meant as protection for the children at camp.

The base is 22 feet high. Inside it is a grotto, a cave, with a figure of Christ in death attended by two angels. A heavy wooden door with iron hinges and iron accents seals the grotto. The 33-foot figure on that 22-foot base is the 55-foot landmark Noel describes, and the height the Grant waymark gives for the statue of Christ the King. Noel adds that the statue is supposedly second in height only to the Christ in Rio de Janeiro. That is his comparison. The measurements to carry are the 33 feet of the figure and the 22 feet of the base.

What the statue is for

Christ the King is the title on the figure: a standing Christ with an open arm over a children's camp. Santa Maria, the name in letters on the hill, is the camp. The grotto below is the other scene, Christ in death, with two angels, behind a wooden door. Along the camp road to the statue, the Catholic Stations of the Cross mark the walk. Taken together, the hill is a small pilgrimage laid onto a summer camp: the letters, the stations, the grotto, and the figure above them.

The white hands and head were fired glossy so they would read in sun and in electric light. From the roadside pull-off, those are the parts to look for once you have found the figure against the trees: the pale head and hands, the outstretched arm, and the block of the base. The letters Santa Maria sit lower on the same slope. A clear day shows both. In the afternoon the glossy white picks up more light than the terra cotta robe.

This is not Christ of the Mines in Silverton. That shrine is a different Christ figure, in the San Juan Mountains, hours from Park County. Christ of the Rockies is the Santa Maria statue above the South Platte, near Grant. If a search for a Colorado Christ statue drops you in Silverton, you are in the wrong range.

The roadside view, and the camp hike

On October 6, 2013, the Park County Historical Society brought about 60 people to Camp Santa Maria for a tour of the grounds. After lunch, 20 of them hiked from camp to the statue. The trail they used is 1.5 miles, with a climb of more than 427 feet, from about 8,437 feet at the start to about 8,864 feet at the base. The Stations of the Cross stand along it. The walk took about an hour and a half. That hike began inside the camp, with the camp's people leading it. It is not a public trail you can join from the highway.

The public stop is the pull-off. Its pin is 39.44875, -105.63360. From there the statue and the Santa Maria letters can be photographed without crossing onto the camp. Park fully off the roadway, take the picture, and leave the gate and the camp road alone. U.S. 285 through Bailey and Shawnee toward Grant is the highway approach. The camp is about 8.3 miles west of Bailey. If you only have the time for one look, the pull-off is the look.

Questions

What is the Santa Maria statue in Colorado?

It is Christ of the Rockies, also called a statue of Christ the King, on the hill behind Camp Santa Maria near Grant. The words Santa Maria are spelled on the hillside below the figure. The camp is just outside Bailey, in northeastern Park County near Shawnee.

How tall is Christ of the Rockies?

The figure is 33 feet high and stands on a 22-foot base. Together those heights are the 55-foot landmark named for the statue. The base holds a grotto with a figure of Christ in death and two angels, closed by a heavy wooden door.

Is the Santa Maria statue on public land?

No. The statue and the Santa Maria letters are on private Camp Santa Maria property. The pin at 39.44875, -105.63360 is a roadside pull-off for photographs, not an entrance to the camp.

Can I hike to Christ of the Rockies?

The 1.5-mile walk from camp, a climb of more than 427 feet past the Stations of the Cross, was a hosted hike on October 6, 2013. The old road is narrow and suited to hikers, and it is on camp property. Use the roadside pull-off for a photograph unless the camp has invited you in.
