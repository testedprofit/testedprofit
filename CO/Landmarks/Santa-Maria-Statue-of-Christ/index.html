Christ of the Rockies

Two heights that add up

The Park County Flume, in its account of a Camp Santa Maria tour, says the Christ of the Rockies statue was built between 1930 and 1934. It says the figure is 33 feet high and rests on a 22-foot-high base. The base includes a grotto with a figure of Christ in death attended by two angels, sealed by a heavy wooden door with iron hinges and accents. Waymarking calls the same landmark a 55-foot statue of Christ the King and quotes Thomas J. Noel calling it a fifty-five-foot statue. Thirty-three plus twenty-two is fifty-five. This guide treats the Flume's 33 feet as the figure, the 22 feet as the base, and the 55 feet as the height Waymarking and Noel give for the whole landmark, not as a rival measurement of the figure alone.

How it was built

The Flume says the statue has a steel frame and terra-cotta tiles for the robe. The tiles were fired and glazed in Denver, brought to camp on the train, and carried to the site. Concrete was poured into the frame and the tiles were attached. The hands and head are separate pieces fired to a glossy white. The outstretched arm, the Flume says, signifies protection for the children at camp. Out-of-work men recruited in Denver built the road during the Depression, including a bridge later removed. The Flume says that road has narrowed and is suitable for hikers. On the tour it describes, 20 people hiked 1.5 miles from camp, a gain of more than 427 feet, from a starting elevation of 8,437 to 8,864 at the base of the statue, passing Stations of the Cross. The hike took about 1.5 hours. That is a report of a hosted walk, not a public trail warranty.

The property, and the purchase year

The Flume says John and Mary Dower purchased the 160-acre property in 1929, and that Mary Dower was the daughter of the miller J.K. Mullen. Waymarking says the statue was erected in 1933 as a gift from the Dower family, and the Noel passage it quotes says the place was purchased in 1930 and the statue was donated in 1933. This guide keeps 1929 from the Flume and 1930 from that Noel passage. Waymarking places the statue on YMCA Camp Santa Maria property just outside Bailey, files the waymark under Grant, and says the words Santa Maria are spelled on the hill. It says the statue and the words are on private property and that its coordinates are a pull-off where they can be photographed. Do not treat this guide as permission to enter the camp. Noel is quoted as saying the statue is supposedly second in height only to the statue of Christ in Rio de Janeiro. That is his comparison, not a ranking this guide asserts.

Questions

How tall is Christ of the Rockies?

The Flume says the figure is 33 feet high on a 22-foot base. Waymarking calls the statue 55 feet, and the Thomas J. Noel passage it quotes says fifty-five feet. The 33-foot figure and the 22-foot base add to 55.

Is the statue on public land?

Waymarking says the statue and the Santa Maria lettering are on private YMCA Camp Santa Maria property just outside Bailey, and that its posted coordinates are a roadside pull-off for photographs. The Flume dates the Dower purchase of the 160-acre property to 1929. The Noel passage says 1930.
