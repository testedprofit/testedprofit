Hank the Cowboy

At the Busy Corner

The Gazette, in an article dated 4 May 2026, describes the Hank the Cowboy statue at the corner of East Pikes Peak Avenue and Tejon Street in Colorado Springs. The article says he stands in the median at the intersection once known as downtown's Busy Corner, reading the Colorado Springs Gazette-Telegraph and looking west toward Cascade Avenue and Pikes Peak. The photograph caption in that article places him at the same corner.

The Gazette says Colorado Springs already had its own Hank the Cowboy in 1939, created to honor the Pikes Peak or Bust Rodeo. Spencer Penrose, founder of The Broadmoor, asked Colorado Springs Gazette-Telegraph artist Stanley Reed to create the character. The Hank sculpture by Rusty Phelps was commissioned by the Pikes Peak or Bust Rodeo Association and donated to the city in 1997. The article says support also came from El Pomar Foundation, the Anna Keesling Ackerman Trust, and the Gazette.

What the figure shows

The Gazette says Hank is dressed as a working cowboy: jeans, chaps, a button-down shirt, a denim jacket, a cowboy hat, and boots, with a can of chewing tobacco in a back pocket. This is the Colorado Springs rodeo figure. It is not the Cowboy in Pajamas sculpture in Golden, which has its own guide. The Gazette's date for the gift to the city is 1997.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Who made the Hank statue, and when did the city receive it?

The Gazette says Stanley Reed created the Hank character in 1939 and Rusty Phelps sculpted the figure. The Pikes Peak or Bust Rodeo Association commissioned it, and it was donated to the city in 1997.

Where does Hank stand?

The Gazette places him in the median at East Pikes Peak Avenue and Tejon Street, the intersection it calls the old Busy Corner, looking west toward Cascade Avenue and Pikes Peak.
