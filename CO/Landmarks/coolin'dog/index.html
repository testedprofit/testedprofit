Coolin' the Dogs

Gus and Dusty

Coolin' the Dogs is a life-size bronze by Craig Bergsgaard. On his studio site he describes an early frontiersman taking a break with a foot soak, and a horse named Dusty standing close by. The title uses "dogs" for tired feet. The figure beside Gus is the horse, not a dog. The same page says life-size casts have been placed in Castle Rock, Colorado, and in Fountain Hills, Arizona, and that the piece is meant to sit with Gus's foot in water.

Bergsgaard's account of a Castle Rock design award says the 1,300-pound bronze was installed at Fifth and Gilbert streets in 2009. He writes that the Town of Castle Rock planning body chose it for the Sense of Place and Community Character award at a 27 January meeting, and that the city presented the award on 22 February 2011 at the second annual Site Design Award program. The studio header on that site lists Windsor, Colorado.

Where to look

A Town of Castle Rock note about the town arborist places the Coolin' the Dogs statue along Gilbert Street at Paintbrush Park. That agrees with the Fifth and Gilbert installation in the sculptor's account. Miller Library. The sculptor's own description and the town's Gilbert Street note replace that account.

Questions

Is Dusty a dog?

Craig Bergsgaard describes Dusty as the frontiersman's horse, standing beside Gus while Gus soaks a foot. The sculpture's name refers to that rest, not to a canine figure.

Where is the Castle Rock bronze?

Bergsgaard says the 1,300-pound bronze was installed at Fifth and Gilbert streets in 2009. The Town of Castle Rock places the statue along Gilbert Street at Paintbrush Park.
