General William Jackson Palmer statue

In the intersection

The statue of Gen. William Jackson Palmer stands in the intersection of Nevada and Platte avenues in Colorado Springs. The Gazette, in an article dated 4 May 2026, says it sits in the middle of that busy intersection. A Waymarking entry for the same statue says it is at E. Platte Avenue and N. Nevada Avenue, just east of downtown, and says the intersection is busy enough that crossing it for a closer look is unwise. Look from the sidewalk.

1929, and two forms of the sculptor's name

Waymarking quotes Colorado: A Guide to the Highest State and says the description comes from the 1941 guidebook. The quotation calls the work a bronze equestrian statue of General William Jackson Palmer at the intersection of E. Platte and N. Nevada avenues, the work of Nathan D. Potter of New York City, unveiled in 1929. The Gazette names the artist Nathan Dumont Potter. It says the General William Jackson Palmer Memorial Association installed the statue in 1929, years after Palmer's death, and that the general looks toward Pikes Peak. Both accounts use 1929. This guide keeps both forms of the sculptor's name.

Who Palmer was

The guidebook passage quoted by Waymarking says Palmer was born in Delaware in 1836, was commissioned a colonel and later a brigadier general in the Union Army, came to Colorado in 1869 as manager of construction on the Kansas Pacific Railroad, and in 1870 incorporated the narrow-gauge Denver and Rio Grande with his associates. It says he died in 1909. The Gazette calls him the city's founder, a Civil War officer who brought the railroad to this part of the West, and says he died in 1909 of injuries and paralysis after his horse stumbled during a ride near his Glen Eyrie home at Garden of the Gods. The Gazette also says there has long been disagreement about whether the horse in the statue is his horse Diablo or a representative horse. This guide does not choose.

Questions

Where is the Palmer statue, and when was it unveiled?

The Gazette places it in the middle of Nevada and Platte avenues. Waymarking places the bronze equestrian at E. Platte and N. Nevada avenues. Both accounts say it was unveiled or installed in 1929. The Gazette names the General William Jackson Palmer Memorial Association as the installer.

Who sculpted it?

Waymarking, quoting the 1941 guidebook, names Nathan D. Potter of New York City. The Gazette names Nathan Dumont Potter. This guide reports both forms of the name.
