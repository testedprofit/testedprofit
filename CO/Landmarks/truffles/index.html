South Tejon Street, Colorado Springs

Truffles, the bronze pig

Truffles is a bronze pig by Walt Horton, seated on the west side of South Tejon Street in downtown Colorado Springs, just south of Colorado Avenue. Horton made the sculpture in 2007. It was on this block by 2014, and it was still in the planted bed on 18 August 2025. This is an independent Profitnet guide to that pig.

The pig on the sidewalk

The bronze is a seated pig with its head and snout lifted. A photograph published on 18 August 2025 shows it among purple flowers and tall grasses, with a brick building behind the bed and a small sign on a black post at its shoulder. The pose is the one you walk up to: heavy body, snout in the air, polished bronze.

Roadside Architecture dates the bronze to 2007 and says it was installed at this spot by 2014. A 2021 visitor account on Roadside America places a sports car and a driver in the brick wall next to the pig, and reads the lifted snout as the pig looking at that car. The 18 August 2025 photograph is a close view of the pig in the flowers.

This is not Rachel, the bronze piggy bank Georgia Gerber made for Pike Place Market in Seattle in 1986. Rachel takes coins for the market foundation. Truffles is Horton's pig on South Tejon Street. If a search sends you to Seattle, you are at the wrong animal.

A garden bronze with the same name

Sage Creek Gallery sells a garden-size bronze also called Truffles, by Horton, in an edition of 35. That casting is 49 inches high, 64 inches wide, and 43 inches deep. Those measurements belong to the edition the gallery offers. They are not a published size for the pig in the Tejon Street flower bed. The street sculpture is the outdoor bronze from 2007.

Walt Horton

Walter Horton was born in 1949, the son of Colonel Clarence Horton II and Ann French. He spent a year at the University of Colorado in 1968, where he met Peggy, and they married in 1970. Their sons, Jesse and Benjamin, grew up while Horton worked as a cartoonist. He later said bronze let him leave the cartoon desk and spend more time with his family. Sculpture became his work in his forties. Micah Olson, his business manager, described the animals as three-dimensional caricatures, with a cartoon-like innocence.

In November 2009 Horton and Peggy opened Walt Horton Fine Art in Beaver Creek. The Vail Daily reported that he died in his sleep at the family's Lake Creek home in Edwards early on Sunday, 28 March 2010. He was 61. His best-known large bronze, Repentance, a boy and a grizzly, stands in Beaver Creek Plaza. Truffles, finished three years before his death, stayed on the Colorado Springs sidewalk.

Other downtown figures

South Tejon Street is a walking downtown. Hank the Cowboy is at East Pikes Peak Avenue and Tejon Street. The General William Palmer statue is at Nevada and Platte. Octo-Maid is another Colorado Springs sculpture with its own corner. Starr Kempf's kinetic towers are on a private residential street in Pine Grove, a different kind of visit from this public sidewalk pig.

Questions

Who made Truffles, and when?

Walt Horton made the bronze in 2007. It was on South Tejon Street by 2014, and a photograph from 18 August 2025 still shows the pig there.

Where is the bronze pig?

On the west side of South Tejon Street, just south of Colorado Avenue, in downtown Colorado Springs. The address used for the stop is 102 South Tejon Street, Colorado Springs, Colorado 80903.

Is this the Seattle market pig?

No. Rachel is Georgia Gerber's bronze piggy bank in front of Pike Place Market in Seattle, installed in 1986. Truffles is Horton's pig on South Tejon Street in Colorado Springs.
