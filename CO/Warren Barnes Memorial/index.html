Warren Barnes Memorial

The chair, the tree, and the book

An archived copy of a Colorado Sun article from 22 November 2021 describes the Warren Barnes memorial in a breezeway off Main Street in downtown Grand Junction, between a bookstore and a bridal shop. The article says ironworker Tim Navin installed it on 20 November 2021. It describes an iron-work tree bent over a curved metal chair and a sheet-metal book titled And You Also. That phrase, the article says, is what Barnes answered when someone told him to have a good day. Navin spent 285 hours on the sculpture at Sparks Fly Studio in Fruita. He recreated the chair in metal, added the tree because he thought Barnes would have liked it, and added the pile of books. Monique Lanotti told the paper she used to set Barnes's chair in a sunny spot behind her bridal shop, where he read paperback thrillers and Louis L'Amour westerns.

Who Barnes was

The Sun article calls Barnes a 69-year-old man known downtown as the reading man, and says he was killed in February 2021 as he slept under an elevated roadway near downtown. Friends then raised the memorial. A PBS Colorado Voices clip posted 6 December 2021 says a metal sculpture in downtown Grand Junction honors him and that he was known for finishing novels. Questions

Who made the Warren Barnes memorial, and when was it installed?

The Colorado Sun says ironworker Tim Navin of Sparks Fly Studio in Fruita made it and installed it on 20 November 2021 in a breezeway off Main Street, between a bookstore and a bridal shop. He spent 285 hours on the metal chair, tree, and books.

What does the book say?

The Sun says the sheet-metal book is titled And You Also, the answer Barnes gave when someone told him to have a good day. The paper calls him the reading man and says he was 69 when he was killed in February 2021.
