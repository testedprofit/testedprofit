Christ of the Mines Shrine

On Anvil Mountain

Christ of the Mines stands on the slope of Anvil Mountain north of Silverton. A Deseret News article published 7 May 2005 describes a 12-ton, 16-foot-tall statue of Jesus about 500 feet up that slope. It says the Catholic men's club first discussed a shrine dedicated to miners in January 1958, when mining was at its lowest ebb since the town started in 1874. Father Joseph Halloran and others chose Anvil Mountain. Community donations paid for the project.

The Deseret News article says the figure was sculpted from Italian marble from Carrera, Italy, and it compares that stone with the marble of Michelangelo's David. It says the statue arrived in the summer of 1959, when an alcove of native stone was already in place and a cutout had been standing in for the real figure. The Catholic Travel Guide account says the plan called for a statue 12 to 16 feet tall, made of Carrara marble from Italy. It says the finished figure is 16 feet tall and made of 12 tons of marble, that it arrived late in the summer of 1959, and that Father Halloran recruited the county crew to set it on the pedestal. That account says alcove work began in 1958 with stone from the Old Fisher Brewery and adjoining land, donated by Mr. and Mrs. Ward Barlow.

The two accounts agree on a finished height of 16 feet and a weight of 12 tons. They spell the quarry town Carrera and Carrara.

Plaques and 1978

Deseret News quotes the original plaque: "This shrine erected in honor of Christ of the Mines by the people of Silverton to ask God's blessing of the mining industry of the San Juans." It says a second plaque, added in 1982, commemorates the lives saved when the mines flooded in 1978. The article dates that flood to 4 June 1978, a Sunday, when a lake above the Sunnyside mine broke loose and flooded a lower portal while the mines were closed. It says there were no deaths or injuries. It also says that only a few months after the shrine's dedication, mining saw a resurgence and new tunnels were started. Later, the article says, Father Joseph McGuiness had St. Patrick's Parish plant 1,000 Scotch pine seedlings behind the shrine.

Questions

How tall is the Silverton statue?

Deseret News describes a 12-ton, 16-foot-tall statue. The Catholic Travel Guide says the finished figure is 16 feet tall and 12 tons of marble. The same two accounts spell the Italian marble source Carrera and Carrara.

What happened at the Sunnyside mine in 1978?

Deseret News says that on 4 June 1978, a Sunday, a lake above the Sunnyside mine broke loose and flooded a lower portal while the mines were closed, and that there were no deaths or injuries. It says a plaque added in 1982 commemorates the lives saved.
