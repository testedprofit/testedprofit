Dalton Trumbo Statue

In front of the Avalon

A bronze of Dalton Trumbo stands in front of the Avalon Theatre at Seventh and Main streets in Grand Junction. The Post Independent's walking-tour article says the bronze shows Trumbo sitting in a bathtub writing a movie script and that it was dedicated in 2007. The article says his 1935 novel Eclipse angered the town's social leaders when it reached Grand Junction, and that sales of a December 2005 reprint raised over $65,000 for Mesa County Library. It says that bronze, together with a 2007 bronze of city founder George A. Crawford, led to Legends of the Grand Valley, a series within the Art on the Corner program.

Westword's article of 8 October 2007 set the unveiling for Saturday, October 13, at the Avalon Theatre. It names sculptor Mike Wilson and says the statue duplicates a photograph of Trumbo writing in his bathtub. The article says a champagne toast would be followed by a screening of Roman Holiday. It describes Trumbo as one of the Hollywood Ten, blacklisted for past associations with Communism, and it names his 1939 novel Johnny Got His Gun.

The sculptor

J. Michael Wilson's project biography says that in 2007 he was awarded the commission to sculpt Dalton Trumbo, an American screenwriter blacklisted in the late 1940s, for Legends of the Grand Valley in Grand Junction. Westword calls the sculptor Mike Wilson. The project biography uses J. Michael Wilson.

Questions

When was the Trumbo bronze dedicated?

The Post Independent says the bronze was dedicated in 2007. Westword, on 8 October 2007, set the unveiling for Saturday, October 13, at the Avalon Theatre.

Who sculpted it, and where does it stand?

Westword names sculptor Mike Wilson. J. Michael Wilson's project biography says he received the 2007 Legends of the Grand Valley commission for the Trumbo figure. The Post Independent places it in front of the Avalon Theatre at Seventh and Main streets.
