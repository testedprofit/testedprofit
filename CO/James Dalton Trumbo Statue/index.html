Dalton Trumbo Statue in Grand Junction

The Dalton Trumbo Statue is a 2007 bronze in downtown Grand Junction, Colorado, in front of the Avalon Theatre at Seventh and Main. Sculptor J. Michael Wilson shows Trumbo sitting in a bathtub, writing a movie script. The figure is part of Legends of the Grand Valley, the Art on the Corner series that started with this bronze and one of city founder George A. Crawford.

The bronze in front of the Avalon

Start a downtown walk at Seventh and Main. Dalton Trumbo sits there in the bathtub, in front of the Avalon Theatre. A 2015 walking tour in the Post Independent calls the theatre newly renovated then, and places the bronze across from the figures of Daily Sentinel publishers Walter and Pres Walker. The theatre itself dates to Trumbo's years in town. The statue is the outdoor piece. You do not need a ticket to stand on the sidewalk and look.

Westword, on October 8, 2007, set the unveiling for the following Saturday, October 13, at the Avalon. Sculptor Mike Wilson's bronze duplicates a photograph of Trumbo writing in his bathtub, the picture people already knew. The evening plan was a toast at 7 p.m. and then Roman Holiday inside. That film is part of the story the statue tells: an Academy Award script he could not sign with his own name.

The same year, Grand Junction commissioned a bronze of George A. Crawford, the city's founder, for the town's 125th birthday. Trumbo and Crawford together started Legends of the Grand Valley, a run of larger-than-life bronzes inside the Art on the Corner program, each one meant to tell a local story. The Post Independent's 2015 tour still begins with Trumbo. Other legends on that walk include Sister Mary Balbina Farrell, the Enstroms, William Moyer, the Operation Foresight trio, John Otto on his horse Rowdy, and the Prinster brothers. The Trumbo figure is the one at the theatre door.

J. Michael Wilson's project biography records the 2007 commission in those words: a sculpture of Dalton Trumbo, an American screenwriter blacklisted in the late 1940s, for Legends of the Grand Valley in Grand Junction. Westword calls him Mike Wilson. It is the same sculptor. He later made the John Otto bronze for the Colorado National Monument, another figure on the same downtown series.

Grand Junction, Eclipse, and the return

Trumbo came of age in Grand Junction. In 1935 his first novel, Eclipse, reached his hometown and angered the town's social leaders. Westword describes the book as painting some residents in an unfavorable light, and says that hurt feeling kept Grand Junction from claiming him for a long time. He was already one of the best-known people born in Colorado since 1900, and also one of the most infamous, and the town he grew up in did not want the portrait he had written.

The break in that freeze was a book sale. A citizens group reprinted the long-out-of-print Eclipse. Sales in December 2005 raised more than $65,000 for Mesa County Library. Two years later the bathtub bronze went up at the Avalon. The statue is the town's later answer to the novel: the writer who had been an embarrassment on the shelf became a figure on the main sidewalk, in the pose of the work he did after he left.

The Hollywood blacklist, in plain facts

The House Committee on Un-American Activities, called HUAC, was created in 1938 to investigate alleged disloyalty and Communist ties among private citizens, public employees, and organizations. During the early Cold War that hunt became the Red Scare. People suspected of Communist ties could be blacklisted, treated as someone to avoid, and could lose their jobs. Trumbo's case is the Hollywood version of that blacklist.

Westword's account of the statue places him among the Hollywood Ten, blacklisted for past associations with Communism. While studios treated him as persona non grata, he kept writing. He won Academy Awards under other names for Roman Holiday, the 1953 film with Audrey Hepburn and Gregory Peck, and for The Brave One in 1956. In 1960 Kirk Douglas and Otto Preminger put his own name on the scripts for Spartacus and Exodus. Westword calls that decision the end of a very dark era in the film business, both as a symbol and as a practical fact: the name Dalton Trumbo could appear on a screen again.

The bathtub is the working pose, not a joke about the blacklist. He wrote in the bath, and the photograph of that habit became the image Wilson copied. Standing at Seventh and Main, you are looking at a Grand Junction novelist and screenwriter whom the town rejected over Eclipse, whom Hollywood refused to name, and whom the city later put in bronze on its main street. The plaque-free sidewalk figure does not spell the committee hearings. The films do. Roman Holiday was the picture they screened at the unveiling. Johnny Got His Gun, his 1939 anti-war novel, is the book Westword names first when it introduces him.

A factual reading keeps both halves. The blacklist was a real bar on employment for political associations, enforced by studios as well as by the climate around HUAC. Trumbo's answer, on the record Westword gives, was to keep writing, to take the awards under other names, and to get his name back on Spartacus and Exodus. Grand Junction's answer, decades later, was the library reprint and this statue. You can disagree with his politics and still see what the bronze is doing: it puts the writer back in the town that Eclipse offended, at the theatre that was already here when he was young.

A short downtown walk

The statue is a sidewalk stop, so pair it with the rest of the Legends walk if you have an hour. From Seventh and Main, the Post Independent route continues to the other bronzes named above, west along Main toward Two Rivers Plaza, John Otto, and the Prinster brothers at Spruce and Main. Art on the Corner is the larger downtown program those legends sit inside.

If you want metal you can buy rather than a city bronze, Desert Metal Art is the yard-sculpture shop at 929 Pitkin Avenue, a short hop from downtown. The Warren Barnes memorial is the other downtown Grand Junction figure on this site, the iron reading man in a breezeway off Main. Trumbo is the one in the bathtub at the Avalon. See him in daylight from the sidewalk, then go inside the theatre only if you have a ticket for a show.

Questions

Where is the Dalton Trumbo Statue?

In front of the Avalon Theatre at Seventh and Main streets in Grand Junction, Colorado. The theatre address is 645 Main Street. The bronze is outdoors on the sidewalk.

When was the Trumbo bronze dedicated?

It was dedicated in 2007. The unveiling was set for Saturday, October 13, 2007, at the Avalon Theatre, with a toast at 7 p.m. and a screening of Roman Holiday.

Who sculpted the Dalton Trumbo Statue?

J. Michael Wilson, also called Mike Wilson. In 2007 he received the Legends of the Grand Valley commission for the figure. The bronze copies a photograph of Trumbo writing in a bathtub.

Why is Dalton Trumbo in a bathtub?

The pose copies a well-known photograph of him writing a script in the bath. He was a Grand Junction screenwriter blacklisted with the Hollywood Ten. While studios would not put his name on films, he still wrote Roman Holiday and The Brave One under other names.
