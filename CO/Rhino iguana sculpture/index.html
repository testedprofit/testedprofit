Rhino Iguana

What the artist lists

Susan Ariniello's own work page titles the piece Rhinoceros Iguana and says it is bronze, 9.5 inches high, 12 inches wide, and 24 inches deep, weighing 13 pounds. It says the sculpture was on display in Grand Junction's Art on the Corner exhibit from October 2016 to October 2017, and that one was purchased by the City of Lafayette for display. That is the artist's account of the object and of the sale. It does not give a Lafayette street address.

Two directory listings

The Boulder County Arts Alliance public-art directory has two Rhino Iguana records, both credited to Susan Ariniello and both placed in a City of Lafayette collection. One says bronze, gives the address 201 N. Public Rd., Lafayette, Colorado, and says the placement is indoors. The other says a steel-green iguana, spells that word s-t-e-e-l-e on the page, gives the address as the northwest corner of Simpson Street and Public Road, and says the placement is outdoors. This guide does not pick one address, one material, or one placement. The 9.5-inch bronze on the artist's page is not treated as the measurement of the outdoor listing, because that listing does not give a size.

The city's own Rhino Iguana page did not return a copy on this check, so it is not linked and is not used to settle the directory. The older page on this URL used 103 N. Public Road, round-the-clock access, and a wheelchair-parking claim. None of those lines is in the artist page or the two directory records, so they are left out.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Who made the Rhino Iguana, and what size does the artist give?

Susan Ariniello's work page says the Rhinoceros Iguana is bronze, 9.5 inches high, 12 inches wide, and 24 inches deep, at 13 pounds. It says one was purchased by the City of Lafayette after an Art on the Corner display in Grand Junction from October 2016 to October 2017.

Where do the Lafayette listings place it?

One Boulder County Arts Alliance record says 201 N. Public Road, indoors, in bronze. Another says the northwest corner of Simpson Street and Public Road, outdoors, and describes a steel-green iguana. This guide reports both.
