Pueblo History and Art Walk

Three loops

Pueblo County's February 2023 brochure describes the Pueblo History and Art Walk as a self-guided walk through three of the oldest neighborhoods in Pueblo. It says the walk has historical stops, rotating art, and three loops of different lengths. The Grove loop is south of downtown, in what the brochure calls the newest addition to the Pueblo Creative Corridor. It mentions local art, one of the oldest churches in the city, and the Arkansas River. The Bojon Town loop includes the turnstile gate where steel-mill workers met their families after a shift. The Bessemer loop adds shops along the Northern Avenue business corridor and Bessemer Park. The brochure dates itself February 2023 and color-codes the three loops.

Where the loops start

The BEGIN Coalition page on the walk says the three loops start and end at the Pueblo Riverwalk. It says the project adds wayfinding and gateway signs and three art sculptures, and that the neighborhoods are Bessemer, Bojon Town, and the Grove. The county brochure and that coalition page are the two sources used here.

One stop the brochure actually describes

The brochure's Grove section describes Water Tower Place. It says Emmett Nuckolls and his son G. H. Nuckolls opened the first Pueblo operation of the Nuckolls Packing Company in 1891 near the Union stockyards, about half a mile east of Bessemer Junction Station, and that in 1915 the family hired Hans Peter Henschien to design a packing plant. It says the Grove began as an immigrant neighborhood of Austrians, Italians, and Slovenians, and that the Pueblo Flood of 3 June 1921 hit this low ground. Those are brochure sentences about a stop on the walk.

Questions

What are the three loops?

The February 2023 county brochure names the Grove, Bojon Town, and Bessemer. The Grove loop is south of downtown along the Arkansas River. Bojon Town includes the millworkers' turnstile gate. Bessemer adds Northern Avenue shops and Bessemer Park. The BEGIN Coalition page says all three loops start and end at the Pueblo Riverwalk.

When was the brochure published?

The county brochure used here is dated February 2023. It calls the walk a self-guided route with historical stops and rotating art.
