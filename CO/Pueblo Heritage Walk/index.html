Pueblo History and Art Walk

Pueblo County's February 2023 brochure describes the Pueblo History and Art Walk as a self-guided walk through three of the oldest neighborhoods in Pueblo, with historical stops, rotating art, and three loops of different lengths.

Plan your visit

The brochure places the Grove just south of downtown, Bojon Town around the steel-mill turnstile, and Bessemer along the Northern Avenue business corridor and Bessemer Park. The map pin is the Historic Arkansas Riverwalk.

The February 2023 brochure does not print opening hours. It is a walking route on neighborhood streets.
Accessibility
The brochure says the walk includes access to physical activity and loops of different lengths. It does not print a grade or a restroom list.
Best time
The brochure is dated February 2023 and does not name a season. Daylight makes the numbered stops easier to match to the map.

Three loops

The brochure's welcome says the walk goes through three of the oldest neighborhoods, with historical stops, rotating art pieces, and access to physical activity. The Grove loop is just south of downtown, in what the brochure calls the newest addition to the Pueblo Creative Corridor. It mentions local art, one of the oldest churches in the city, and the Arkansas River. The Bojon Town loop includes part of the Grove, the turnstile gate where steel-mill workers met their families after a shift, and Pueblo restaurants. The Bessemer loop adds shops along the Northern Avenue business corridor, play at Bessemer Park, and what the brochure calls the oldest rivalry west of the Mississippi. Colored and numbered circles on the brochure match stops on its map. The brochure dates itself February 2023.

Water Tower Place and the 1921 flood

The Grove section describes Water Tower Place. In 1891 Emmett Nuckolls and his son G. H. Nuckolls opened the first Pueblo operation of the Nuckolls Packing Company near the Union stockyards, about half a mile east of Bessemer Junction Station. In 1915 the family hired Hans Peter Henschien to design a packing plant on his rational-factory idea, planning the building so product would move through it like a machine. The brochure says Water Tower Place is being redeveloped.

The same Grove narrative says the neighborhood was an immigrant district where you could hear Slovenian songs on one block and see Italian flags on the next. Then the Pueblo Flood of 3 June 1921 hit. The brochure says the Grove was the lowest area, close to the river, and saw the worst of the flooding. It says most homes of industrial working immigrants were washed away, that pre-flood residents who survived often moved up to Bojon Town, and that others helped rebuild.

Ending at the riverwalk

The brochure's note on completing the Grove loop says that once you reach the Pueblo Riverwalk, you can take it back to where you started the tour. Questions

What are the three loops?

The February 2023 county brochure names the Grove, south of downtown; Bojon Town, including the steel-mill turnstile; and Bessemer, along Northern Avenue and Bessemer Park.

What does the brochure say about the 1921 flood?

It says the flood of 3 June 1921 hit the Grove hardest because the neighborhood was low and close to the river, and that many homes were washed away.

Does the photograph show a sculpture on the walk?

No. It shows the Pueblo Riverwalk. The brochure says that finishing the Grove loop brings you to that riverwalk.
