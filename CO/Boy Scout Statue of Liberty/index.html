Boy Scout Statue of Liberty

The monument

The Boy Scout monument in La Junta is an 8-foot-4-inch copper replica of the Statue of Liberty. The National Register nomination for La Junta City Park says local Boy Scouts constructed it in 1951. It stands on a star-shaped stone base and a stone pedestal, and the nomination says the stonework was designed to resemble the park's earlier WPA masonry. The form calls the object the Boy Scout Monument and says it was built during the park's period of significance even though it is not part of the New Deal work.

The bronze plaque quoted in the nomination reads: "With the faith and courage of their forefathers who made possible the freedom of these United States, the Boy Scouts of America dedicate this copy of the Statue of Liberty as a pledge of everlasting fidelity and loyalty. The Crusade to Strengthen Liberty, 1951."

The national replicas

The same nomination ties the La Junta statue to the 40th anniversary of the Boy Scouts of America. It says that between 1949 and 1951 identical replicas of the Statue of Liberty were installed across the United States, each on a unique base designed by the local Scouts. Friedley-Voshardt of Chicago made the statues and sold them for $300 to $350. The nomination estimates that at least 200 were dedicated in 39 states. It names other Colorado communities with statues: Colorado Springs, Estes Park, Fort Collins, Greeley, Johnstown, Kremmling, Longmont, Pueblo, and Trinidad. A bibliography entry uses the title "Restoring the Little Sisters of Liberty."

Colorado Encyclopedia's article on the park says local Boy Scouts placed a miniature replica of the Statue of Liberty in the park in 1951 as part of a nationwide program for the organization's fortieth anniversary. This guide does not repeat a count of how many replicas survive, a Colorado total of 17, or the name Jack Whitaker. Those claims were not in the nomination text checked here.

Where it stands

The nomination lists La Junta City Park, site 5OT.937, as a public local district in Otero County. The National Register information system id is 07000343. The form bounds the park by Colorado and Park Avenues and 10th and 14th Streets in La Junta, and it gives the zip code 81050. A roadside survey of Boy Scout replicas places the La Junta figure at the southwest corner of Colorado Avenue and 10th Street, calls that corner Blaine Park, and records the position as 37 degrees 58 minutes 42.4 seconds north, 103 degrees 32 minutes 35.9 seconds west. The plaque text in that survey matches the nomination. This guide does not treat Blaine Park and La Junta City Park as two names established by one record. The corner the survey names sits on the boundary the nomination describes.

Questions

Where is the La Junta replica?

The National Register nomination places the 1951 Boy Scout monument in La Junta City Park, bounded by Colorado and Park Avenues and 10th and 14th Streets. A roadside survey places a replica at the southwest corner of Colorado Avenue and 10th Street and calls that corner Blaine Park.

What is the statue made of?

The nomination describes an 8-foot-4-inch copper replica of the Statue of Liberty on a stone pedestal and a star-shaped stone base. It says Friedley-Voshardt of Chicago made the statues in the national series.
