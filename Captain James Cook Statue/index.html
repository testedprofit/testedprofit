Captain Cook statue in Waimea

The Captain Cook Society places this statue in Hofgaard Park at Waimea, almost outside the Bank of Hawaiʻi, in a small triangular area between Kaumualiʻi Highway and Waimea Road. It is a replica of the Whitby, England, statue by Sir John Tweed. The society says it was first installed at Waimea Canyon School and later moved to the park. The Hawaiʻi State Foundation on Culture and the Arts purchased the replica from Derek and Patricia Freeborn, Ltd., in 1978, for the 200th anniversary of Cook’s landing. The society transcribes the plaque as Captain James Cook, R.N., 1728 to 1779, a replica of the original statue in Whitby, England, by Sir John Tweed, R.A., with lines for the State Foundation on Culture and the Arts, Art in Public Places, and the State of Hawaii Department of Accounting and General Services. It prints the coordinates 21.954791, -159.666289.

The society page also lists a heritage heading for the National Register of Historic Places and National Historic Landmark, and the website it attaches to that heading is the Wikipedia article on the Cook Landing Site. That is the society’s pointer. It is not a statement that this bronze is itself the landmark. Nearby, the same page describes a separate block of rough stone with two plaques and points that description at a different memorial.

Wikipedia’s article on Hofgaard Park describes a narrow park between Kaumualiʻi Highway and Waimea Road, named for Judge Christopher Blom Hofgaard, with a replica of the Whitby statue. It says the 20 January landfall on southwestern Kauaʻi was Cook’s first arrival on Hawaiian soil, and that on 20 January 1778 two ships anchored at the mouth of the Waimea River. It says the actual landing site was probably Lucy Wright Park, near the river mouth, just south of the town of Waimea. A gallery caption on that article labels a Cook landing commemoration plaque at Hofgaard Park. The statue is in the town park. It does not stand on the landing spot.

The Garden Island, on 6 December 2020, says that on the afternoon of 20 January 1778 the Resolution and Discovery dropped anchors off the mouth of the Waimea River, and that Cook went ashore with 12 armed marines in three boats. It places that first landing at a site in what is today Lucy Wright Park. The same column describes a different object: on 16 August 1928, sesquicentennial celebrations at Waimea Park, renamed Hofgaard Park in 1931 for Judge C.B. Hofgaard, unveiled and dedicated a stone Cook monument. It says the USS Pennsylvania and its convoy anchored off Waimea that morning, along with the inter-island liner Waiʻaleʻale and the warships HMS Cornwall, HMAS Brisbane, and HMS Dunedin. The 1978 bronze replica and the 1928 stone monument are two objects. Cook’s death is marked at Kealakekua Bay, not here.

The death site has its own guide: Cook monument at Kealakekua Bay.

Questions

Is this the monument at the landing, or the one at Kealakekua?

This bronze is in Hofgaard Park in Waimea. The Captain Cook Society says it is a 1978 replica of the Whitby statue. Wikipedia and The Garden Island place Cook’s first landing nearer Lucy Wright Park. The monument at the place where he died is the Kealakekua Bay guide.

What was dedicated in 1928?

The Garden Island says a stone Cook monument was unveiled at Waimea Park on 16 August 1928. That is not the 1978 bronze replica the Captain Cook Society describes.
