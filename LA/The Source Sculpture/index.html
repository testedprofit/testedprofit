The Source

Tulane University, in a July 19, 2016 note, describes The Source as a large concrete sculpture on the neutral ground of Elk Place, across from the Tulane School of Social Work. It says the sculpture was created for the 1984 World's Fair by the French husband-and-wife artists Claude and Francois-Xavier Lalanne. During the fair it was in Centennial Plaza, with water flowing from its eyes into a pool below.

Very Local, writing about relics of that fair, says the exposition's theme was The World of Rivers — Fresh Waters as a Source of Life. It says one of the first works many visitors would have seen was a gigantic head on its side with water leaking from its eyes. Very Local calls that work Crying Head, credits Claude and Francois-Xavier Lalanne, and says it can be found on the Elk Place neutral ground between Tulane Avenue and Canal Street.

A tour of Centennial Plaza at the 1984 fair says The Source, created by Claude and Francois-Xavier Lalanne of France, was one of three designs selected in the International Water Sculpture Competition. Tulane calls the concrete work The Source. Very Local calls the head on its side Crying Head. Both place the Lalanne sculpture on Elk Place after the fair. The fair tour places it in Centennial Plaza during the exposition, as one of the three selected water sculptures.

Questions

Who made The Source, and where was it during the fair?

Tulane University and a 1984 fair tour both credit Claude and Francois-Xavier Lalanne. The fair tour places The Source in Centennial Plaza as one of three works chosen in an international water-sculpture competition. Tulane says it stood in Centennial Plaza with water flowing from its eyes into a pool.

Where is it now?

Tulane says the large concrete sculpture is on the neutral ground of Elk Place, across from the Tulane School of Social Work. Very Local places the same Lalanne head, which it calls Crying Head, on the Elk Place neutral ground between Tulane Avenue and Canal Street.
