G. K. Chesterton in Ponchatoula

A life-size bronze of Gilbert Keith Chesterton stands in front of Chesterton Square, the events hall a local doctor named for him in downtown Ponchatoula. Milwaukee sculptor David Wanner made it for Dr. Robert Benson. The American Chesterton Society reported the statue and the square complete on 26 June 2009. This is an independent Profitnet guide, not the venue.

2009

G.K.C.

Life-size bronze by David Wanner. Cape, hat, stick, and a book, at the Railroad Avenue front of Chesterton Square.

See it

Front of the hall
Chesterton Square, 143 NW Railroad Avenue, Ponchatoula, Louisiana 70454. The 2008 plan put the bronze at the Railroad Avenue entrance, between two parking-lot gates, in a facade meant to match the older buildings along the avenue.
The hall
A private wedding and event venue of more than 10,000 square feet. The hall itself is for private events and private tours. Call the office before you go. The posted office days are limited weekdays, and the venue lists a summer closure.
Office line
Chesterton Square, (985) 467-1227. The office is in the back building. The parking entrance is between LaCaretta's and the City Hall lot.

Who Chesterton was

Gilbert Keith Chesterton was an English writer, critic, and journalist. He was born in London on 29 May 1874 and died in Beaconsfield on 14 June 1936. He converted to Roman Catholicism in 1922. His books include Orthodoxy, The Everlasting Man, and the Father Brown stories.

He studied at St. Paul's School, then art at the Slade and literature at University College, London. The essays, the criticism of Dickens and Browning, and the Father Brown mysteries made him a public figure on both sides of the Atlantic. Britannica remembers the exuberant personality and the rotund figure. The Ponchatoula bronze is a life-size version of that man, not a slim ideal.

Orthodoxy came out in 1909, before the 1922 conversion. The Everlasting Man followed in 1925. The first Father Brown book, The Innocence of Father Brown, was published in 1911. The Man Who Was Thursday, the allegorical novel, is from 1908. Friends included H. G. Wells, George Bernard Shaw, and Hilaire Belloc. With Belloc he argued for distributism, a wide spread of land and small property, set out in What's Wrong with the World in 1910.

Why this town has him

The statue is Dr. Robert Benson's tribute. He is a local doctor. He had been reading Chesterton on marriage and family in the anthology Brave New Family, published by Ignatius Press in 1990. He named the events hall Chesterton Square and commissioned the bronze as its outdoor face.

Benson told The Times he asked Wanner for a Chesterton that looks as if he fell asleep on the train, which he often did, and just got off in Ponchatoula. The town sits about an hour north of New Orleans, on the north shore of Lake Pontchartrain, in Tangipahoa Parish. The 2024 parish visitor guide calls Ponchatoula the Strawberry Capital of the World. The American Chesterton Society, in its June 2009 note, liked the square's motto, A Place to Celebrate Life.

In autumn 2016 the newsletter of the Australian Chesterton Society, The Defendant, called this the only life-size statue of Chesterton in the world, standing prominently in front of the hall. The same issue noted an active Chesterton society in Baton Rouge. History News Network, on 4 August 2008, had already pointed to the plan for the first Chesterton statue in the United States, to be unveiled later that year.

The sculptor and the two measurements

David Wanner of Milwaukee made the cast bronze. On 1 August 2008 he wrote that the full-size model was nearly finished. He had used eighteen photographs and still wanted a small change to the glasses, a ribbon, and a few adjustments to the face and head. He put the bronze casting at about 6 and a half feet tall. The model stood on a base 38 inches high and 42 inches square, because he wanted the figure and the base to read as one mass.

The Times account quoted that May gave different numbers: 7 feet tall, a pedestal of 3 to 4 feet, and a weight of nearly 600 pounds. Those are the two published figures from the months the bronze was being made. A Beaconsfield Historical Society photograph of the finished statue shows a cape, a hat, a stick, and a book.

Wanner was still modeling in August 2008. The Times had said the statue would go up that September, at the Railroad Avenue entrance to what was then called the Chesterton Centre, Benson's arts and medical complex. On 26 June 2009 the American Chesterton Society wrote that the statue and the square were complete.

The hall now

Chesterton Square still occupies 143 NW Railroad Avenue and still books weddings, banquets, and other private events. The venue describes a castle-like room of more than 10,000 square feet. Its page lists office hours for the fall season as Monday, Wednesday, and Thursday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., and it closes the hall for the summer stretch from 1 July through 1 September. Call (985) 467-1227 before a visit. The bronze was planned as the exterior focal point on the avenue side. In 2016 it still stood in front of the hall.

Nearby stops

The historic train depot is the Ponchatoula Country Market, at 10 East Pine Street, Ponchatoula, Louisiana 70454. The depot was built in 1895 and remodeled in 1920. It holds booths of local goods, Old Hardhide the alligator, a 1914 steam locomotive, and the largest flying American flag. Admission is free, and the market is open year round except major holidays. The market line is (985) 386-9580. A restored Illinois Central baggage and mail car beside the depot houses a railway post office display. The Strawberry Festival grounds are at 301 North 6th Street.

In New Orleans, about an hour south, the Anne Frank sculpture at the National WWII Museum is another Louisiana bronze on this site. On the north shore, the Ronald Reagan bronze in Covington is Patrick Miller's work at the Tammany Trace trailhead. This Ponchatoula figure is David Wanner's Chesterton.
