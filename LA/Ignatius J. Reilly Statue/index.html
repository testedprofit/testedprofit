Ignatius J. Reilly statue

Gambit columnist Blake Pontchartrain answered a reader on July 31, 2020, about the statue of Ignatius J. Reilly on Canal Street. Reilly is the protagonist of John Kennedy Toole's novel A Confederacy of Dunces. Gambit says the statue has been on Canal Street since 1997. The opening scene of the Pulitzer Prize-winning novel places Reilly on Canal Street, waiting for his mother under the clock at D.H. Holmes.

Gambit says the D.H. Holmes department store opened at 819 Canal Street in 1849 and closed in 1989. The property was redeveloped as a hotel, the Chateau Sonesta, which opened in 1995. Gambit says it is now the Hyatt Centric French Quarter. The D.H. Holmes clock, a longtime meeting spot, was stolen in 1989 and later returned to its place outside the hotel. Hotel developer Pres Kabacoff proposed the statue. Local sculptor Bill Ludwig was commissioned for the work. Gambit says the statue is modeled on actor and radio and television personality John Spud McConnell, who had played Ignatius in local theater. Ludwig said McConnell was the closest thing to an Ignatius that existed. Gambit says the sculpture was unveiled in October 1997. Ludwig died in 2011. His other local sculptures include the Louisiana Vietnam War Veterans Memorial at the Superdome and the statue of Malcolm Woldenberg and his grandson at Woldenberg Park.

MyNewOrleans, in a walking list of public art, dates Ignatius Reilly to 1996, names the sculptor William Ludwig, and calls the material bronze. It places the statue on Canal Street in front of the Chateau Bourbon Hotel, on the former site of the D.H. Holmes department store. It says the figure shows the novel's opening scene, waiting for his mother under the clock, and that New Orleans actor John Spud McConnell, host of WWL's Talk Gumbo, was the model. Gambit's unveiling month is October 1997. The magazine's date is 1996. Both accounts name Ludwig, Canal Street, the old department store, and McConnell.

Questions

Who sculpted the Ignatius Reilly statue, and when did it go up?

Gambit says local sculptor Bill Ludwig made the statue and that it was unveiled in October 1997. MyNewOrleans dates the bronze to 1996 and names the sculptor William Ludwig.

Why is the statue on Canal Street?

Gambit says the opening of A Confederacy of Dunces places Ignatius under the D.H. Holmes clock on Canal Street. The store opened at 819 Canal Street in 1849. Gambit says the building is now the Hyatt Centric French Quarter.
