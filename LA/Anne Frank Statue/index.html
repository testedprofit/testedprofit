Anne Frank sculpture

The National World War II Museum in New Orleans unveiled a statue of Anne Frank on Thursday, September 12, 2019. NOLA.com reporter John Pope wrote that the bronze stands alongside Andrew Higgins Drive and is 5 feet, 2 inches tall. Ivan Schwartz, whose Brooklyn studio created the sculpture, called that height his estimate of Anne's height when, at 13, she went into hiding in Amsterdam in 1942 with her parents, her sister, and four other Jews.

Pope wrote that the sculpture shows Anne with her face upturned, clutching her diary, and that it was based on photographs of her. A grant from the Oscar J. Tolmas Charitable Trust underwrote the statue. Trustees Vincent J. Giardina and Lisa N. Romano joined Holocaust survivor Anne Levy in lifting the cloth. Schwartz, lead sculptor Jiwoong Cheh, and their colleagues work at StudioEIS, which Schwartz founded and runs. Cheh said the project took about a year. Pope wrote that Cheh called the sculpture special because of what Anne represents and because of this location.

The sculpture stands near a seated bronze likeness of Franklin D. Roosevelt. Next to the Anne Frank figure is a granite slab with this quotation from her diary: "Sometime this terrible war will be over. Surely the time will come when we are people again, and not just Jews." Pope also reported that the Tolmas Trust would fund three Holocaust-related exhibits in the museum's Liberation Pavilion. The newspaper account is the record for the date, the height, the studio, the pose, and the setting on Andrew Higgins Drive.

Questions

When was the Anne Frank sculpture unveiled?

NOLA.com reported an unveiling on Thursday, September 12, 2019, at the National World War II Museum in New Orleans. The bronze stands alongside Andrew Higgins Drive.

Who made the sculpture, and how tall is it?

Ivan Schwartz's studio, StudioEIS, created it, with Jiwoong Cheh as lead sculptor. NOLA.com says the bronze is 5 feet, 2 inches tall, Schwartz's estimate of Anne's height when she went into hiding at 13.
