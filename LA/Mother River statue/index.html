1350 Port of New Orleans Place

Mother River statue

Joseph Cleary's bronze of the Mississippi stands in the brick plaza in front of the Port of New Orleans administration building, with the river behind her. The base is dated 8 March 2001. The port still names the Mother River statue as the focal point of that plaza. This is an independent Profitnet guide, not the port.

2001

River

Power, beauty, and history of the Mississippi. Hands for the source. Robes for the flow. Figures at her feet for the tributaries.

See it

Plaza
River side of the Port Administration Building, 1350 Port of New Orleans Place, New Orleans, Louisiana 70130. Brick, outdoors, between the visitors parking lot and the employee parking lot. The statue is the focal point, with the Mississippi in view, under the Crescent City Connection.
Hours on the port sheet
The plaza is listed from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., seven days a week. It is 37,720 square feet. A reception rental is capped at 500 people. Seated events vary. The posted plaza fee is $5,000, and it covers the outdoor space.
Drive
From the Central Business District, take Convention Center Boulevard to Henderson Street, turn left, continue to Port of New Orleans Place, turn left, and the visitor lot is on the right. Obey the posted parking signs. The port's main line is (800) 776-6652.

What the figure means

The base says the sculpture represents the power, beauty, and history of the Mississippi River. Her outstretched hands symbolize the source of the waterway, which stretches from practically the Canadian border to the Gulf of Mexico. Her robes and drapes express the river's constant flow through the heartland until it converges here and makes the Port of New Orleans. The figures at her feet represent the tributaries that feed the Mississippi. A relief on the base depicts work along the river, including the men, women, and machines of the maritime industry in Louisiana.

The same inscription calls the statue an acknowledgment of what the river has given the nation, and a salute to the mariners and shore workers who keep the ports and waterways safe and productive. It is dated 8 March 2001 and names Governor Murphy J. Foster. It names the Board of Commissioners of the Port of New Orleans, president and chief executive J. Ron Brinson, architect Arthur Q. Davis, and sculptor Joseph Cleary.

The commissioners named on the base are James M. Baldwin, Jr., Walter J. Boasso, Donald T. Bollinger, Bernard L. Charbonnet, Jr., David Guidry, Francis E. Lauricella, and David P. Schulingkamp.

The sculptor

Joseph Sarsfield Cleary was born in Long Beach, California, on 15 March 1926 and died in Orinda, California, on 16 April 2018, at 92. He grew up in Oakland, won a scholarship to the California College of Arts and Crafts, and served in the Merchant Marine during the Second World War. After the war he worked as a commercial illustrator, then took commissions for portraits, landscapes, and large sculpture. His family remembered a specialty for the flowing form of the female body, and they described Mother River as a 30-foot bronze the Port of New Orleans commissioned. The 2018 obituary said the figure still stood at the front of the port headquarters, watching the Mississippi. The port's current venue page still makes that statue the focal point of the plaza.

The plaza around her

The administration building sits at the cruise and convention end of the riverfront. The port rents the brick plaza for receptions and for events that may be seated. Indoor rooms are separate: a lobby, a 207-seat auditorium, and a 25-seat conference room. The visitor lot holds 38 cars, 36 regular spaces and 2 accessible spaces. The employee lot holds 100 cars, 94 regular and 6 accessible. Those lots are not offered as event parking during weekday business hours, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

A 2020 Tulane walking note sent people who were already at Mardi Gras World, the float workshop next door, down to this building. It called the statue a homage to the river and its tributaries, and it mentioned swings beside the figure for watching the traffic on the water. The spot is tucked off Convention Center Boulevard, so the port's own driving directions are the sure way in.

Other stops in the city

Mardi Gras World is the nearby float barn. Farther into the Warehouse District, the Anne Frank sculpture at the National WWII Museum stands outdoors in Founders Plaza. On Elk Place, The Source is another New Orleans figure. The gilded Joan of Arc on Decatur Street is in the French Quarter. Mother River is the one on the port plaza, facing the Mississippi.

Questions

Who made Mother River, and when?

Joseph Sarsfield Cleary made the bronze for the Port of New Orleans. The base is dated 8 March 2001 and names Governor Murphy J. Foster, architect Arthur Q. Davis, and sculptor Joseph Cleary.

What does the statue mean?

The base says the figure stands for the power, beauty, and history of the Mississippi. Her hands stand for the river's source, her robes for its flow to New Orleans, and the figures at her feet for the tributaries.

How do you see it?

It is the focal point of the brick plaza on the river side of the Port Administration Building, 1350 Port of New Orleans Place, New Orleans, Louisiana 70130. The port lists that outdoor space from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. The main port line is (800) 776-6652.
