De La Salle monument

The Plaquemines Parish Tourism Commission describes the De La Salle Monument in southern Plaquemines Parish. The page says the monument pays tribute to the second expedition of the French explorer René Robert Cavelier, Sieur de la Salle. It says he was the first European to travel the length of the Mississippi River, reaching its mouth in modern-day Plaquemines Parish in April 1682, and that there he claimed all of the land drained by the Mississippi for France.

Wikipedia's article on La Salle dates that claim more closely. It says that on April 9, 1682, he claimed the Mississippi River basin for France and gave it the name La Louisiane, in honor of Louis XIV. The same article says Jolliet and Marquette had preceded him on the upper Mississippi in 1673 and 1674, and that La Salle extended exploration, and France's claims, to the river's mouth.

The tourism page prints the monument account beside its listing for the Fort Jackson Museum and Welcome Center at 38039 Highway 23, Buras, telephone 504-393-0124. On that same page, Fort Jackson is described as a masonry fort about 40 miles upriver from the mouth of the Mississippi, built as a coastal defense of New Orleans between 1822 and 1832, and a battle site in the American Civil War. The monument paragraph is the parish's tribute to the 1682 expedition. The fort paragraph is a separate historic site on the same page.

The founder honored on Decatur Street has a separate guide: the Bienville monument.
