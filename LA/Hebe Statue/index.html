Hebe statue

The Center for Women's History and Leadership keeps a digital exhibit on drinking fountains of the Woman's Christian Temperance Union. Its Louisiana page describes a statue of Hebe in Baton Rouge. The page calls Hebe the Greek goddess of youth and the bearer of nectar to the gods. It says the statue was erected in May 1914.

The same page says the statue was torn down in 1984 to make room for a bus stop. Later, in 2000, the Outdoor Sculpture Committee received additional funding to complete the statue portion. The page says the statue was rededicated on February 13, 2006, and that it was hoped a fountain would be added in the future. Those sentences are the exhibit's timeline for the Baton Rouge figure: a 1914 erection, a 1984 removal, funding in 2000 for the statue portion, and a 2006 rededication.

The exhibit's introduction sets that local page in a longer campaign. It says that at the first meeting of the Woman's Christian Temperance Union, in Cleveland, Ohio, on November 18 through 20, 1874, the First Plan of Work urged unions to erect fountains of water. It says the women of the WCTU continued that campaign well into the 20th century, to provide free drinking water as an alternative to the drinks offered in saloons, and often water for horses and dogs as well. Many fountains, the introduction says, had spouts for people and troughs at two lower levels. The Louisiana page is the exhibit's account of the Baton Rouge Hebe, and the introduction is the exhibit's account of the fountain campaign around it.

Questions

When was the Baton Rouge Hebe erected and rededicated?

The Center for Women's History and Leadership says the Baton Rouge statue of Hebe was erected in May 1914, taken down in 1984, and rededicated on February 13, 2006.
