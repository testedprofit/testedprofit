Ronald Reagan Statue in Covington

The Ronald Reagan statue in Covington is a bronze at the Tammany Trace trailhead, titled An American Hero. He smiles and salutes walkers, joggers, and cyclists. Patrick Miller sculpted it. A crane set the figure on June 25, 2008, and a plaque dates the dedication to June 27, 2008. NOLA.com calls it reportedly the world's tallest Reagan statue. Louisiana Life calls it the world's largest of the former president. The published heights are a 9-foot figure, a 9-foot-4-inch figure in the installation caption, and a nine-foot likeness on a six-foot pedestal.

The bronze at the trailhead

The Covington Trailhead is one end of the Tammany Trace, a 31-mile path. Louisiana Life describes a nine-foot likeness towering over the park, saluting and grinning at people who walk, jog, or cycle past. NOLA.com's June 25, 2025 story, by Julia Guilbeau, describes the same bronze at 9 feet and 1,300 pounds, with a smile and a salute, and a photograph from June 22, 2025 titles it An American Hero. A caption on that story, from the day the crane worked, says the piece is 9 feet 4 inches and 1,300 pounds. Louisiana Life adds a six-foot pedestal. The figure is larger than life on every one of those accounts.

The world's-largest wording is theirs as well. NOLA.com says the statue is reportedly the world's tallest, and that it stands two feet taller than the Reagan statues in the U.S. Capitol rotunda and in Budapest. Louisiana Life says it is the world's largest statue of the former president. Both are claims in print. They are not a new measurement. If you came for the world's largest Ronald Reagan statue, this is the bronze those publications point to, at this trailhead, with the heights written above.

Patrick Miller made it. The 2008 caption calls him a Ponchatoula artist. The 2025 article calls him a Bedico artist. Both towns are on the north shore, and both names are his in those stories. He completed the statue in March 2007 for $110,000. It was commissioned by Patrick Taylor in 2005, hauled from Ponchatoula, and set by crane on Wednesday, June 25, 2008, with an official unveiling that Friday. The plaque on the six-foot pedestal, in Louisiana Life's account, says only that Patrick F. Taylor commissioned and donated the statue on June 27, 2008.

How it got to Covington

Ronald Reagan was the 40th president. He did not visit Covington during those eight years, though he did stop in New Orleans. The memorial is not a marker of a local speech. It starts with Patrick F. Taylor, a New Orleans oilman who founded Taylor Energy and, with his wife Phyllis, the Patrick F. Taylor Foundation. Phyllis Taylor dated their friendship to Reagan's years as governor of California, and to a shared liking for cowboy boots. Taylor also pushed the state scholarship law known as TOPS, the Taylor Opportunity Program for Students.

Reagan died in 2004 at his home in Bel Air, California. Taylor died five months later, at 67, of complications from bacterial endocarditis. He had wanted a Louisiana monument and had offered to pay for it. He did not live to see it. Phyllis Taylor carried the plan to the foundation, which agreed to pay. Nearly four years passed between his death and the June 27, 2008 dedication. Then-mayor Candace Watkins said the gift commemorated the Taylors' friendship with Reagan.

Covington was a plausible site even without a presidential visit. In 2003, state Rep. Michael Strain and St. Tammany Parish Councilman Marty Dean got the Legislature to name a stretch of U.S. 190, from Louisiana 25 through Covington to the parish line, the Ronald Reagan Highway. The trailhead statue stands near that road. NOLA.com also notes that St. Tammany had become a Republican stronghold, and that Reagan won Louisiana easily in 1980. The bronze is a local memorial on a highway already given his name.

The sculptor, the storm, and the park

Miller had started a small clay model, a maquette, when Hurricane Katrina hit. He packed it in an ice chest with padding and taped it shut, unsure how hard the storm would be on the north shore. He was without electricity for three to four weeks and did not resume the statue until the fall. The finished bronze took more than a year. He later called it a fun and challenging project. His work ranges from large public monuments to portrait busts and bas-reliefs. The Reagan is the one at this trailhead.

The park under the salute is a public lawn, not a museum gallery. Louisiana Life mentions art markets, farmers markets, and outdoor concerts. NOLA.com says the figure looks over a small amphitheater. You can see the bronze from the path without a ticket. Stay on the trail side of the pedestal if an event is setting up, and leave the base alone. The plaque is short. The story is longer than the metal plate.

For another north-shore bronze by a different subject, the Chesterton statue in Ponchatoula is in the sculptor's town, a short drive from this trailhead. Reagan is the Covington one: smile, salute, Tammany Trace, and a world's-largest claim you should read as Louisiana Life's words and as NOLA.com's "reportedly," next to the 9-foot and 9-foot-4-inch figures those accounts give.

Questions

Where is the Covington Reagan statue, and who sculpted it?

At the Covington Trailhead of the Tammany Trace, a 31-mile trail. Sculptor Patrick Miller, of Ponchatoula and Bedico, made the bronze. It is titled An American Hero. Reagan stands smiling, with a salute.

How tall is the Ronald Reagan statue, and is it the world's largest?

NOLA.com describes a 9-foot, 1,300-pound bronze, and a photo caption on that story says a crane set a 9-foot-4-inch figure on June 25, 2008. Louisiana Life describes a nine-foot likeness on a six-foot pedestal. NOLA.com calls it reportedly the world's tallest, two feet taller than the Reagan statues in the U.S. Capitol rotunda and in Budapest. Louisiana Life calls it the world's largest statue of the former president.

When was the statue placed?

Patrick F. Taylor commissioned it in 2005. Miller completed it in March 2007 for $110,000. A crane set it at the trailhead on June 25, 2008, and the plaque dates the dedication to June 27, 2008.

Why is there a Reagan statue in Covington?

Reagan did not visit Covington during his eight years as president. Oilman Patrick F. Taylor, a friend from Reagan's years as governor of California, wanted a Louisiana memorial after Reagan died in 2004. The statue stands near U.S. 190, named the Ronald Reagan Highway in 2003.
