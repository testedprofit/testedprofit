Ronald Reagan statue

NOLA.com, in a June 25, 2025 article by Julia Guilbeau, describes a bronze statue of Ronald Reagan at Covington's Tammany Trace trailhead. A photograph caption titles the bronze An American Hero. The article says the figure stands with a smile and a salute, gives its size as 9 feet tall and 1,300 pounds, and says it is reportedly the world's tallest, 2 feet taller than the Reagan statues in the U.S. Capitol rotunda and in Budapest. The same story's photo caption says a crane lifted a 9-foot-4-inch, 1,300-pound bronze into place on Wednesday, June 25, 2008, and that Ponchatoula artist Patrick Miller made it, commissioned by Patrick Taylor in 2005. The article's text says Bedico artist Patrick Miller completed the statue in March 2007 for $110,000 and that it was officially placed at the trailhead in June 2008.

NOLA.com says Reagan never visited Covington during his eight years as president, though he made stops in New Orleans. It traces the memorial to Patrick F. Taylor, an oilman and friend of Reagan. After Reagan's death in 2004, Taylor wanted a Louisiana monument. His wife, Phyllis Taylor, later took the trailhead plan to the Patrick F. Taylor Foundation, which agreed to pay for the statue. Then-mayor Candace Watkins said, in a Times-Picayune article quoted by NOLA.com, that the donation commemorated the Taylors' friendship with Reagan. The story also notes that in 2003 the Legislature named a stretch of U.S. 190, from Louisiana 25 through Covington to the parish line, Ronald Reagan Highway.

Louisiana Life places a nine-foot likeness at the Covington Trailhead, a terminus of the 31-mile Tammany Trace, saluting and grinning. It calls the statue the world's largest of the former president. It says the bronze stands on a six-foot pedestal whose plaque says the statue was commissioned and donated by Patrick F. Taylor on June 27, 2008, and that nearly four years passed between Taylor's death and that dedication. The magazine says Ponchatoula sculptor Patrick Miller was chosen, that he had started a clay maquette when Hurricane Katrina hit, and that the statue took more than a year. It also ties the site to Highway 190, dedicated as the Ronald Reagan Highway in 2003.

Patrick Miller's own commission list dates a President Ronald Reagan monument to 2006, near the Ronald Reagan Memorial Highway in Covington. The list calls it a bronze figure standing over 9 feet tall, life and a half, and says that makes it the tallest monument of the late president. All three describe a bronze by Patrick Miller at the Covington trailhead, placed in 2008. Published heights differ. NOLA.com's article says 9 feet and 1,300 pounds, and that story's caption says 9 feet 4 inches. Louisiana Life says a nine-foot likeness on a six-foot pedestal. Miller's list says over 9 feet. NOLA.com calls the figure reportedly the world's tallest. Louisiana Life calls it the world's largest of the former president. Miller's list calls it the tallest monument of the late president.

Questions

Where is the Covington Reagan statue, and who sculpted it?

NOLA.com places a bronze Ronald Reagan at the Tammany Trace trailhead in Covington and credits Ponchatoula and Bedico sculptor Patrick Miller. Louisiana Life calls the site the Covington Trailhead, a terminus of the 31-mile Tammany Trace.

How tall do the reports say it is?

NOLA.com's article says 9 feet tall and 1,300 pounds, and a photo caption on the same story says 9 feet 4 inches. Louisiana Life says a nine-foot likeness on a six-foot pedestal. Patrick Miller's commission list says the bronze figure stands over 9 feet tall.
