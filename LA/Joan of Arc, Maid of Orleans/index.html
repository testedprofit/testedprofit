Joan of Arc statue

NOLA.com describes the statue on Decatur Street as a gilded equestrian figure, 13.5 feet tall and 2,700 pounds. The Historic New Orleans Collection says it is one of many duplicates of a piece the French sculptor Emmanuel Frémiet created in 1875 at the request of Napoleon III. Wikipedia dates the Paris original, Jeanne d'Arc, to 1874, calls it a gilded bronze equestrian sculpture by Frémiet, and lists New Orleans among the cities with a copy. The Paris statue stands at the Place des Pyramides. NOLA.com says Frémiet made nine duplicates and that the New Orleans version stayed at the foundry until a New York company bought the facility in 1958, shipped the statue to the city, and asked about $36,500 for it.

The Historic New Orleans Collection says Charles de Gaulle partnered with the cities of Orleans, Paris, Reims, and Rouen to buy the figure and give it to New Orleans. NOLA.com spells one of those cities Rheims and says de Gaulle donated the statue with their financial help. It says a detachment of U.S. Marines accepted the statue and its base from a French naval platoon aboard the cruiser Jeanne d'Arc on April 18, 1964, during a transfer on the Mississippi riverfront. The city installed the statue in 1972 in a small park called Place de France at the foot of Canal Street. A Federal Register notice of June 14, 1999, says the Place contains a gilded bronze statue of Jeanne d'Arc and two bronze cannons manufactured during the Napoleonic Empire and donated to the city by the French government, and that the city constructed the Place de France in 1972.

That notice says Mayor Marc H. Morial asked the Secretary of the Interior to approve moving the Place de France, including the statue and the two cannons, from the ground between the International Trade Mart and the former Rivergate to the French Quarter. The Secretary approved the request on June 4, 1999. NOLA.com says the move happened in October 1999, to a city-owned triangle at the edge of the French Market, at the intersection of Decatur, North Peters, and St. Philip streets, with room for the cannons that had arrived with the statue and for the flags of the United States, France, Louisiana, and New Orleans. The Historic New Orleans Collection says the statue now resides on the 900 block of Decatur Street. Its article on lost landmarks says the gold Joan of Arc unveiled at the Rivergate in 1972 now graces the entrance to the French Market on Decatur Street.

The founder's monument, a short walk toward Canal Street, is the Bienville monument.

Questions

When did the Joan of Arc statue reach its Decatur Street site?

NOLA.com says the statue was installed in 1972 at Place de France at the foot of Canal Street and moved in October 1999 to Decatur, North Peters, and St. Philip streets, by the French Market.

What kind of sculpture is it?

The Federal Register calls it a gilded bronze statue of Jeanne d'Arc. NOLA.com describes the Decatur Street figure as a gilded equestrian statue, 13.5 feet tall and 2,700 pounds. Wikipedia lists New Orleans among the copies of Emmanuel Frémiet's Paris statue.
