Scraphouse

Wikipedia's article on the artist Sally Heller lists Scraphouse, New Orleans, as a public sculpture dated 2008 to the present and funded by the Joan Mitchell Foundation. The article says Heller was born in 1956 in New Orleans and raised there. She earned a B.S. in art at the University of Wisconsin, Madison, and an M.F.A. from the painting and printmaking department at Virginia Commonwealth University in 1980. Wikipedia says she is known for using everyday materials to make large installations that are often site-specific.

The Joan Mitchell Foundation's page on Heller says that in 2008 she received a grant from the foundation to construct an outdoor sculpture. The same page describes her as an artist who assembles mundane materials and cultural castoffs into environments that mix the architectural and the organic. That description is the foundation's account of her work. Wikipedia's separate line on Scraphouse is the entry that names the public sculpture in New Orleans and the foundation funding.

Wikipedia also lists a 2016 work called Second Story. It says Heller created that permanent metal site-specific sculpture in a private park in St. Rose, Louisiana, that it is similar to Scraphouse, and that it again references Hurricane Katrina. The Scraphouse line names New Orleans and the years 2008 to the present. The Second Story line is the sentence that places a related sculpture, and a reference to Hurricane Katrina, next to Scraphouse.

Heller's public biography on those two pages is the visitor account of Scraphouse: a New Orleans public sculpture on Wikipedia's list, tied there to Joan Mitchell Foundation funding, with the foundation recording a 2008 grant for an outdoor sculpture. The St. Rose work is a different sculpture. Wikipedia distinguishes it by name, year, and place, and then compares it with Scraphouse.
