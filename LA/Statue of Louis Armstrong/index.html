Louis Armstrong statue

Gambit columnist Blake Pontchartrain, on December 11, 2017, answered a reader about the Louis Armstrong statue in Armstrong Park. The column says the fundraising effort began in 1969 and intensified after Armstrong's death in 1971. Jazz historian Floyd Levin of Los Angeles credited Danny Barker with the idea. Gambit says the statue shows a smiling Satchmo holding a trumpet in one hand and a handkerchief in the other. It is the work of Elizabeth Catlett, a Chicago native who chaired the art department at Dillard University in the 1940s. Gambit says Catlett cast the one-ton, 11-foot-tall bronze in Mexico, where she lived for many years and where she died in 2012. The column says the statue cost $30,000.

Gambit says the statue was unveiled in Jackson Square on July 4, 1976, during the national bicentennial. The date was chosen because it was long believed to be Armstrong's birthday, though later research showed he was born on August 4, 1901. A States-Item article, the column says, reported that the statue lost its trumpet during shipping, and that local artists Lin Emery, A. W. Klein, and Gerold Dyer repaired the damage in time for the unveiling. The idea of naming a 31-acre section of Congo Square, then called Beauregard Square, for Armstrong was proposed in 1972. After delays, the park opened in 1980. Armstrong's widow, Lucille, unveiled the statue in the new park on April 15, 1980, during the New Orleans Jazz and Heritage Festival.

Wikipedia's article on Louis Armstrong Park says the park's monuments include a 12-foot statue of Louis Armstrong by Elizabeth Catlett, a bust of Sidney Bechet, and a depiction of Buddy Bolden. Gambit gives the figure as one ton and 11 feet tall. Wikipedia calls it a 12-foot statue. Both name Elizabeth Catlett as the sculptor. Gambit also describes the handkerchief and trumpet, the casting in Mexico, the $30,000 cost, the July 4, 1976 unveiling in Jackson Square, and the April 15, 1980 unveiling by Lucille Armstrong.

The square inside the same park has its own guide: Congo Square.

Questions

Who made the Armstrong statue, and what does it show?

Gambit says Elizabeth Catlett designed a smiling figure with a trumpet in one hand and a handkerchief in the other, and that she cast the bronze in Mexico.

When was it unveiled in Armstrong Park?

Gambit says Armstrong's widow, Lucille, unveiled the statue in the park on April 15, 1980, during the New Orleans Jazz and Heritage Festival. The same column says an earlier unveiling was in Jackson Square on July 4, 1976.
