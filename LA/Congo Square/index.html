Congo Square

The City of New Orleans parks page places historic Congo Square inside Louis Armstrong Park. The park is bounded by St. Philip Street, Rampart Street, Basin Street, St. Peter Street, and North Villere Street. The city lists it at 701 North Rampart Street, on the edge of the French Quarter, in the Treme neighborhood, and gives its size as 31 acres. Hours are seven days a week, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. in winter and 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. during Daylight Saving Time. The page lists Congo Square as National Register number 92001763 and the New Orleans Jazz National Historical Park as National Register number 01000277.

The city says the park is dedicated to Louis Armstrong and to the tradition of jazz, and that Treme is the birthplace of many of the city's most famous jazz musicians. Within the park, Congo Square was formerly known as Place de Negres. The city says it took its name from the tradition of enslaved people who gathered there on Sundays, their day off, to sing, beat drums, sell homemade goods, and celebrate. The park hosts Martin Luther King Day celebrations, the Red Dress Run, weddings, festivals, concerts, and filming.

The New Orleans Jazz Museum says Congo Square was a gathering place where Africans, most of them enslaved, kept music, dance, and cultural traditions, and that the rhythms sustained there became foundational to New Orleans jazz. The museum says the City Council voted in 2011 to restore the name Congo Square. It quotes the ordinance calling jazz the only truly indigenous American art form and saying that arguably its genesis was Congo Square. The museum places the square inside Louis Armstrong Park, with an entrance at North Rampart and St. Ann.

Wikipedia says that in 1817 the mayor issued an ordinance restricting gatherings of enslaved Africans to Congo Square, after people had also gathered along levees, in other squares, and on Bayou St. John. It says the square was officially named Beauregard Square in 1893, for Confederate general P. G. T. Beauregard, and that the City Council officially restored the name Congo Square in 2011. The city's Sunday-gathering account and the museum's 2011 ordinance are the civic records. Wikipedia is the account of the 1817 ordinance and the 1893 name.

The park also has a separate guide to the Louis Armstrong statue.

Questions

Where is Congo Square, and when is the park open?

The City of New Orleans places Congo Square inside Louis Armstrong Park at 701 North Rampart Street. The park is open seven days a week, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. in winter and 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. during Daylight Saving Time.

How does the city describe the square's history?

The city says the square, formerly Place de Negres, took its name from the tradition of enslaved people who gathered there on Sundays to sing, beat drums, sell homemade goods, and celebrate.
