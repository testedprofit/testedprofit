Michelle

NOLA.com describes Michelle as the title of a life-size bronze statue of a young woman on the edge of a fountain near Decatur Street, where Madison Street runs into the river wall. Her legs are stretched out. The same report says she is sometimes called the Mermaid. A later NOLA.com story says the reclining figure, dressed in summery shorts and a sheer blouse, was sometimes referred to as the mermaid, though there is no scaly tail, and that she was never nude in the public sculpture, though some people remember her that way.

The earlier report says Michelle took her seat at the fountain in 1984, the year of the Louisiana World Exposition. She was the creation of artist Paul Perret Jr., who produced two life-size statues for the riverfront promenade. Perret said that in 1983 he sculpted a seated 19th-century woman titled Martha for the Friends of the Market, with a basket of fruit meant to suggest shopping at the French Market. That figure was popular enough that he was commissioned to make another one for the edge of a fountain. He said he did not have time for an entirely new bronze while preparing other work for the exposition, so he recast the wax model of a finished reclining nude and applied 1980s-style shorts and a T-shirt. He called her Michelle after his daughter, who did not pose for the sculpture.

The later story says the bronze was carted off after vandalism and then bolted back in place, as bright as the day she made her debut in 1984. It names Gayle de la Houssaye as the model for Paul Perret Jr.'s bronze titled Michelle. De la Houssaye told the paper she modeled in the early 1980s after a friend recommended her to Perret, who had been commissioned to produce lifelike statues for a promenade on the riverside of the French Quarter. Perret told the paper he had stained the bronze a lustrous brown, with a pale blue-green on the blouse and a darker green on the shorts. The two NOLA.com accounts agree on the artist, the 1984 debut, the fountain near Decatur Street, and the name Michelle. One quotes Perret on shorts and a T-shirt added to a nude model. The later story describes the public figure in shorts and a sheer blouse.

The gilded equestrian figure a short walk along Decatur Street is the Joan of Arc statue.
