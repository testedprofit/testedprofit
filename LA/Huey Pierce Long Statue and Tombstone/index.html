Huey Long statue and grave

The Louisiana House of Representatives describes the Capitol grounds as 27 acres with oaks, magnolias, and azaleas, organized around two walkways that each measure twenty feet across. It says the focal point of the grounds is the statue of Gov. and U.S. Sen. Huey P. Long. Because the Capitol was his dream, the House page says, Long is buried under the statue. The statue was erected in 1940 to mark the spot.

A photograph record at Virginia Tech Special Collections describes the same place as the State Capitol garden, with the statue and grave at the center of the garden. The record says the statue was executed by Chas. Keck and erected in 1940. The catalog entry is part of the Charles Goodsell Collection.

Wikipedia's article on Huey Long says that on Sunday, September 8, 1935, Carl Weiss shot Long inside the Louisiana State Capitol. It says Long's remains were buried on the grounds of the Capitol and that a statue depicting him was constructed on his grave. The House date of 1940 falls five years after that shooting. Wikipedia also says the capitol Long built, at 450 feet, remains the tallest capitol in the United States. That figure in the article is the height of the building. The statue is the figure on the grave in the garden, in the House page, the Virginia Tech record, and the Wikipedia burial sentence.

The other Baton Rouge figure filed under the Louisiana directory is the Hebe statue.

Questions

Where is the Huey Long statue, and when was it erected?

The Louisiana House of Representatives says the statue of Gov. and U.S. Sen. Huey P. Long is the focal point of the Capitol grounds and that Long is buried under it. The page dates the statue to 1940.

Who does the Virginia Tech record name as the sculptor?

A Virginia Tech Special Collections record of the State Capitol garden says the statue and grave stand at the center of the garden, that the statue was executed by Chas. Keck, and that it was erected in 1940.
