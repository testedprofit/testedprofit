Bienville monument

Gambit marked 65 years since the unveiling in a column dated April 13, 2020. A commission chaired by Richard Foster was formed in 1951 after earlier efforts to honor Jean-Baptiste Le Moyne, Sieur de Bienville, had not produced a permanent monument. New Orleans sculptor Angela Gregory designed the statue. It originally stood outside the Union Passenger Terminal on Loyola Avenue. Gambit says the bronze statue of Bienville stands 26 feet tall and was cast in Paris. On Bienville's left is a figure of Father Athanase Douay, the monk who accompanied him on the expedition. A Native American figure at Bienville's right represents the original inhabitants of the region.

The dedication was April 24, 1955. Gambit says the French and Canadian ambassadors to the United States spoke, along with Louisiana Gov. Robert Kennon, New Orleans Mayor deLesseps Chep Morrison, and Archbishop Francis Rummel. The young sons of Kennon and Morrison, and the daughter of the French consul in New Orleans, helped unveil the statue. In 1997 the monument was moved from Loyola Avenue to the French Quarter. For the new site, the city created Bienville Place, a triangular strip of land bordered by Decatur, Conti, and North Peters streets.

MyNewOrleans lists the same 1955 bronze by Angela Gregory where North Peters and Decatur streets meet, between Conti and St. Louis streets. It says the statue was moved from the Union Passenger Terminal to that spot in 1997, and that the monument shows Bienville with Father Athanase, the priest who accompanied him, and an American Indian. The magazine says Bienville founded New Orleans in 1718 and served as governor of Louisiana for four years in total. Wikipedia's article on Bienville says he founded New Orleans in the spring of 1718, that May 7 has become the traditional anniversary, and that the actual day is unknown. Wikipedia lists his birth in Montreal on February 23, 1680, and his death in Paris on March 7, 1767.

The gilded equestrian figure nearby has its own guide: Joan of Arc on Decatur Street.

Questions

Who designed the Bienville monument, and when was it dedicated?

Gambit says New Orleans sculptor Angela Gregory designed the bronze, that it stands 26 feet tall and was cast in Paris, and that it was dedicated on April 24, 1955.

Where does it stand now?

Gambit says the city moved the monument in 1997 to Bienville Place, a triangle bordered by Decatur, Conti, and North Peters streets. MyNewOrleans places it where North Peters and Decatur meet, between Conti and St. Louis.
