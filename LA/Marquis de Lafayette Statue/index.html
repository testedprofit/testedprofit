Marquis de Lafayette statue

An equestrian bronze of the Marquis de Lafayette by Charles Correia stands at the Parc Lafayette complex, 705 West University Avenue. The city dedicated the memorial in 1987.

Who Lafayette was

Marie-Joseph Paul Yves Roch Gilbert du Motier, the Marquis de Lafayette, was born on 6 September 1757 at the Château de Chavaniac in Auvergne, France, and died in Paris on 20 May 1834. He was a French military officer and politician who volunteered for the Continental Army under George Washington. He commanded Continental troops at the siege of Yorktown in 1781, the last major battle of the Revolutionary War. Back in France he was a central figure in the revolution of 1789 and again in the July Revolution. The nickname that followed him was Hero of the Two Worlds.

Lafayette, Louisiana, carries his name, and the 1987 memorial is the city's tribute to him: the people of Lafayette to the Marquis de La Fayette. It remembers a high-ranking officer of the Revolutionary War, and the grounds are public.

The bronze on University Avenue

Charles Correia made the equestrian statue in 1987. It stands at the Parc Lafayette complex on West University Avenue, at 705 West University Avenue, Lafayette, Louisiana 70506. Walk the whole complex. Published descriptions place the horse and rider in front of the buildings and also at the rear, so check both lawns before you decide you have missed it. The year 1987 and the name Correia are the identifiers.

What the horse is asking you to remember

In the United States he is simply Lafayette. He volunteered for George Washington's Continental Army, and at Yorktown in 1781 he commanded Continental troops in the siege that ended the war's last major battle and secured American independence. Born in 1757, he was 24 that autumn, and he lived until 1834. Back in France he was central to the revolution of 1789 and again to the July Revolution of 1830. France and the United States both kept him as a hero. The Louisiana city that carries his name put that memory in bronze on a university-area street.

How to find the rider

University Avenue is a driving street, and the complex is the landmark. You are looking for a horse and rider. Other cities have their own figures: a 1976 bronze in LaGrange, Georgia, a 1937 concrete and plaster statue by Arnold Foerster in Lafayette Park in Westlake, Los Angeles, and a statue in front of the Golden Plough Tavern in York, Pennsylvania. Different artists, different years. On this street the bronze you want is Correia's, from 1987.

Go in daylight so the horse reads clearly against the buildings. The grounds are open to the public. Match the rider, the year, and the sculptor, and you have the city's Lafayette. The avenue around the complex is the university district. This bronze is the Revolutionary War stop on that stretch of West University.

Why the city put him here

The marquis was born Marie-Joseph Paul Yves Roch Gilbert du Motier on 6 September 1757 at the Château de Chavaniac in Auvergne. He died in Paris on 20 May 1834, at 76. Between those dates he fought under Washington, stood at Yorktown, and then stood in two French revolutions. Hero of the Two Worlds is the name that stuck. Lafayette, Louisiana, took the name and, in 1987, answered it with Correia's horse and rider on West University Avenue. The tribute line is simple: from the people of Lafayette to the Marquis de La Fayette.

Give the stop half an hour. Park near the complex, walk the front lawn, then walk the rear, and read 1987 before you leave. You are not at a copy of the Yorktown field. You are at the city's own bronze, on a busy avenue, with the university district around it. New Iberia's James Lee Burke statue and the Huey Long figure at the state capitol in Baton Rouge are other Louisiana stops on this site. They are later stories. This one is the French officer who crossed the Atlantic.

Questions

Where is the Lafayette statue?

At the Parc Lafayette complex, 705 West University Avenue, Lafayette, Louisiana 70506.

Who made it, and when?

Charles Correia, in 1987. It is an equestrian bronze.

Who was the Marquis de Lafayette?

A French officer who joined George Washington's army, commanded troops at Yorktown in 1781, and later took part in the French revolutions of 1789 and 1830.
