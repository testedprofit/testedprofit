Touchdown Jesus, the Sacred Heart Statue

Touchdown Jesus is the local name for the Sacred Heart of Jesus statue in the garden behind St. Louis Cathedral in New Orleans. The Carrara marble figure was carved by S. Lenzi of Pietrasanta, Italy, and unveiled on May 7, 1926. One arm reaches out. Lit from below at night, the statue throws a shadow on the cathedral walls, and that shadow is why people call it Touchdown Jesus.

Why it is called Touchdown Jesus

The formal name is the Sacred Heart of Jesus. New Orleanians also call it Touchdown Jesus. New Orleans Magazine, on MyNewOrleans, gives the reason: the shadow the statue casts on the cathedral walls at night, when it is lit from below. In daylight the pose is already a raised arm. NBC News, at the first Sunday Mass after Katrina, described a marble Christ outside the church with his arm outstretched. At night that arm, thrown onto the rear wall, reads to a lot of people as a referee's signal. The nickname is local, and it is about the light, not about a football shrine.

The inscription is in French. It translates to Sacred Heart of Jesus, I have confidence in Thee. That is the devotion the figure was carved to carry. Sacred Heart images show Christ pointing to his heart, or opening a hand toward the person looking. Here the open hand is also the hand the storm broke, and the hand the city waited years to restore.

The 1926 gift

In the mid-1920s a priest at St. Louis Cathedral told the congregation that the Oblate Fathers wanted a Sacred Heart statue in the cathedral garden. Banker J. E. Merilh's family answered. Merilh was born in Navarrenx, France, studied in Paris, London, and Buenos Aires, and settled in New Orleans after a few years in New York. He helped establish the Germania Bank on Rampart Street in 1881 and, with $200, was its first depositor. As president he kept the bank growing until it came under the Commercial Trust and Savings Bank in 1905. He then became a director of the Canal-Commercial bank. He died at home, at 1029 Bourbon Street, in 1925, at 79. His wife had died ten years earlier. They left five adult children.

A year later those children gave the statue to the cathedral in memory of their parents. S. Lenzi of Pietrasanta carved it in Carrara marble from the Apuan Alps. It was unveiled in the garden on May 7, 1926, by the family's only grandchild, Miss Louis Georgette Tusson. The Very Rev. Father Albert Antoine of St. Louis Cathedral gave the blessing. The garden behind the church has been the statue's place since that day. The front of the cathedral faces Jackson Square, the postcard view. Touchdown Jesus is the figure you find by walking around to the back.

Katrina, the broken hand, and 2015

Hurricane Katrina in 2005 knocked trees down through the gardens and took fingers off the statue. NBC News, on October 2, 2005, covered the cathedral's first Sunday Mass since the storm and reported that the thumb and forefinger of the outstretched hand had snapped off. Archbishop Alfred Hughes asked that those fingers not be put back until the recovery of New Orleans was further along. The church had come through the wind and water almost untouched. The broken hand was left as a visible wait.

The wait lasted until 2015. FOX8, on August 24, 2015, reported that Archbishop Gregory Aymond unveiled the restored Sacred Heart statue that Sunday morning, behind the cathedral. He had promised it would not be repaired until the city was rebuilt. With the tenth anniversary of Katrina close, he said some people were still rebuilding and some who wanted to come back had not been able to. He called the restored hand a sign of hope and a pledge to them. New Orleans Magazine records the same repair, the blessing by Archbishop Aymond, and the hope it was meant to carry while recovery was still unfinished.

Look at the hand when you go. The marble is whole again. The story in the fingers is the decade between Hughes's request and Aymond's unveiling: a public choice to leave the damage visible until the city had more of itself back. The statue is not a monument to the storm. It is a 1926 Sacred Heart that the storm marked, and that the archdiocese restored on a timetable tied to New Orleans rather than to the stone alone.

How to see it

Come to Jackson Square and face the cathedral. The triple spires are the landmark. Walk around either side of the church into the garden behind it. The marble figure is there, arm out, French inscription on the base. Daytime is the carving: Carrara stone, the Sacred Heart, the repaired fingers. After dark, if the lights are on from below, step back until the shadow sits on the cathedral wall. That is the Touchdown Jesus photograph. You do not need a ticket for the garden. The cathedral interior is its own visit, and it draws hundreds of thousands of people a year. The statue is the outdoor stop on the way around the building.

Other French Quarter figures on this site are a short walk away. Joan of Arc stands farther along Decatur Street. Bienville is in the Quarter as well. The Anne Frank statue is at the National WWII Museum, a longer trip from Jackson Square and a different kind of memorial. Touchdown Jesus stays at the cathedral garden: 1926, Carrara, a broken hand held back until 2015, and a night shadow on the wall.

Questions

Where is Touchdown Jesus in New Orleans?

In the garden behind St. Louis Cathedral, at 615 Pere Antoine Alley in the French Quarter. The church faces Jackson Square. The statue is on the garden side, behind the building. The marble figure has one arm outstretched. Locals call it Touchdown Jesus because, lit from below at night, it throws a shadow on the cathedral walls.

Who carved the Sacred Heart statue, and when was it unveiled?

S. Lenzi of Pietrasanta, Italy, carved it in Carrara marble from the Apuan Alps. The Merilh family gave it in memory of their parents. It was unveiled in the cathedral garden on May 7, 1926. The French inscription translates to Sacred Heart of Jesus, I have confidence in Thee.

What happened to the hand in Hurricane Katrina?

In 2005, falling trees knocked fingers off the outstretched hand. NBC News reported that the thumb and forefinger had snapped off. Archbishop Alfred Hughes asked that they stay off until the city's recovery was further along. The hand was repaired in 2015, and Archbishop Gregory Aymond unveiled the restored statue on August 23, 2015.

Can I see the statue without a ticket?

Yes. The statue is outdoors in the cathedral garden. Walk around the church from Jackson Square to the garden behind it. Come after dark if you want the lit figure and the shadow on the wall. The cathedral itself is a separate visit.
