Steel corn on 35th Street

The Gazette column of 31 January 2012 describes a cluster of massive metal stalks in the middle of a roundabout on 35th Street in Marion. The writer had seen them on a Christmas Day walk. Council member Joe Spinks told him the roundabout was part of a 1.5 million dollar, federal stimulus-funded extension of 35th Street, at its future intersection with Tower Terrace Road. The Vaughn family, which contributed land, wanted art that reflected the heritage of that farmland.

The winning design was by Dale Merrill of Liberty Iron Works in Mount Vernon: thick steel cornstalks with polished stainless steel tassels. The column says the sculpture cost 100,000 dollars, with the Vaughns paying 20,000 dollars and the city covering the rest. It is lighted at night. The stalks were designed to oxidize, and the tassels were meant to stay polished. Merrill said drivers looped the roundabout to take photographs while the work was going in.

The column says one online commenter referred to the sculpture as cornhenge. That nickname is why this guide keeps the Cornhenge URL. The column does not place the stalks in Lowe Park, and it does not describe concrete cobs, a Stonehenge circle, or an artist named Cliff Mann.

The column does not give a stalk count or a height. The place to look is the roundabout on 35th Street.
