Statue of Liberty replica

A copper replica of the Statue of Liberty stands on the Iowa State Capitol grounds in Des Moines. In 1950 the Tall Corn Council of the Boy Scouts of America gave it to the State of Iowa.

A scout gift on the capitol lawn

The figure is small next to the capitol, and that is the point of it. In 1950 the Tall Corn Council, the council now called Mid-Iowa, donated this replica to the State of Iowa as an annual service project. The grounds tour numbers it stop 9 and calls it a miniature Statue of Liberty, provided by Jack Whitaker. Stand near the building, look for the raised torch at a human scale, and you are in the right place. The statue is 8 feet 4 inches tall and made of 47 pieces of sheet copper.

Whitaker's idea started with a rougher copy. He was a Kansas City businessman active in the Boy Scouts. In Spirit Lake he saw a Statue of Liberty built of chicken wire and concrete, and he paid to have a mold made from that kind of figure. Scout troops then bought replicas and presented them to cities and towns. The Des Moines statue is the one those troops' wider effort placed on the statehouse lawn, in copper rather than chicken wire.

Strengthen the Arm of Liberty

The gift belongs to a national theme. Strengthen the Arm of Liberty was the Boy Scouts of America's mark for their fortieth anniversary in 1950. The campaign had opened the year before, in 1949, with a ceremony at the base of the Statue of Liberty in New York. About 200 of the scout replicas were installed across the United States, and hundreds of scale copies were made for the anniversary. The Des Moines figure is Iowa's capitol copy of that effort: same year, same donor story, a copper statue you can walk up to on the grounds.

Two other Iowa copies sit in other towns. Bloomfield's replica stands at the courthouse. Burlington's stands on the bank of the Mississippi. The Des Moines copy is the one on the capitol lawn. If your day is the capital, stay on the statehouse grounds and count the outdoor monuments until you reach stop 9.

The copper is the detail that separates this replica from the chicken-wire figure Whitaker saw in Spirit Lake. Forty-seven pieces of sheet copper make a statue you can walk around, torch above your head if you stand close, robe and tablet at eye level. It is a scout anniversary marker that the state accepted and left on the public lawn, in the same family as the roughly 200 copies set up around the country for 1950.

How to see it

Come to the capitol in Des Moines and walk the grounds. The replica is an outdoor monument on the lawn, numbered 9 on the public tour. You are looking for a small copper Liberty with the torch raised. Carry the scout story with you: 1950, Tall Corn Council, Jack Whitaker, 8 feet 4 inches, 47 pieces of sheet copper.

Other Des Moines and eastern Iowa stops on this site, including the sculpture park on McKinley Avenue and the Black Angel in Oakland Cemetery, are different trips. This one is the capitol lawn and a half hour if you are already at the building for a tour.
