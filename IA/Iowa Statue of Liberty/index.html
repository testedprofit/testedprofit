Statue of Liberty replica

The Iowa Legislature's capitol tour sheet says that in 1950 the Tall Corn Council of the Boy Scouts of America, now the Mid-Iowa Council, donated this replica of the Statue of Liberty to the State of Iowa. Wikipedia names Strengthen the Arm of Liberty as the Boy Scouts' fortieth-anniversary theme in 1950.

What the legislature says about this statue

The capitol tour sheet's Statue of Liberty entry says that in 1950 the Tall Corn Council, now the Mid-Iowa Council, donated the replica to the State of Iowa as an annual service project. The idea is credited to Jack Whitaker. After seeing a Statue of Liberty replica made of chicken wire and concrete in Spirit Lake, he paid to have a mold made. Scout troops then bought replicas and gave them to cities and towns across the country. The sheet says this statue stands 8 feet 4 inches tall and is made of 47 pieces of sheet copper.

The legislature's capitol-grounds page lists the same gift as tour stop 9. It calls the figure a miniature Statue of Liberty, donated in 1950 by the Tall Corn Council, now Mid-Iowa, and says the statue was provided by Jack Whitaker. The height, the 47 pieces of copper, and the Spirit Lake chicken-wire story stay with the tour sheet. The grounds page is the second source for the year, the council, and Whitaker.

Strengthen the Arm of Liberty

The English Wikipedia article Strengthen the Arm of Liberty says that name is the theme of the Boy Scouts of America's fortieth anniversary celebration in 1950. The campaign was inaugurated in 1949 with a ceremony at the base of the Statue of Liberty. The article says approximately 200 Boy Scout replicas were installed across the United States, and that hundreds of scale replicas were created nationwide as part of the campaign to commemorate that anniversary. The statue in the article's lead photograph is the replica at Oakdale Park in Salina, Kansas, not the Des Moines figure.

Wikipedia's Iowa list names Bloomfield, at the courthouse, and Burlington, on the banks of the Mississippi River, among the replicas. The capitol statue's year, donor, height, and copper construction are the legislature's sentences. The program name Strengthen the Arm of Liberty, the 1949 inauguration at the Statue of Liberty, and the approximate national count of 200 are Wikipedia's sentences about the anniversary campaign. The tour sheet's account of troops buying replicas and presenting them to cities and towns is the local description of that wider project.

What the pages do not say

Neither legislature page prints a street corner, a smile measurement, or a daily hours line. Neither page describes a crown on a brewery roof. The place they give is the capitol grounds in Des Moines. The map pin is the Iowa State Capitol, at 41.5910263, -93.6032079.

Questions

How tall is the capitol replica, and what is it made of?

The legislature's tour sheet says 8 feet 4 inches and 47 pieces of sheet copper.

Who gave it to the state?

Both legislature pages say the Tall Corn Council, now the Mid-Iowa Council, of the Boy Scouts of America donated it in 1950, and they credit Jack Whitaker.

What was Strengthen the Arm of Liberty?

Wikipedia says it was the theme of the Boy Scouts of America's fortieth anniversary celebration in 1950, inaugurated in 1949 at the Statue of Liberty, with approximately 200 replicas installed across the United States.
