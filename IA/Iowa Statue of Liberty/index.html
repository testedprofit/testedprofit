Statue of Liberty replica

The Iowa Legislature’s capitol tour sheet says that in 1950 the Tall Corn Council of the Boy Scouts of America, now the Mid-Iowa Council, donated this replica of the Statue of Liberty to the State of Iowa as an annual service project. The idea is credited to Jack Whitaker, a Kansas City businessman active in the Boy Scouts. After seeing a Statue of Liberty replica made of chicken wire and concrete in Spirit Lake, he paid to have a mold made. Scout troops then bought replicas and gave them to cities and towns across the country. The sheet says this statue stands 8 feet 4 inches tall and is made of 47 pieces of sheet copper.

The legislature’s capitol-grounds page lists the same gift as tour stop 9. It calls the figure a miniature Statue of Liberty, donated in 1950 by the Tall Corn Council, now Mid-Iowa, and says the statue was provided by Jack Whitaker. Those details stay with the tour sheet.

Neither page prints a street corner, a smile, or a daily hours line. Neither page describes a crown on a brewery roof. The place they do give is the capitol grounds in Des Moines. The tour sheet’s account of the wider Boy Scout project does not number the replicas. It says troops presented them across the country.

The two legislature pages agree on the year, the council, and Whitaker. The tour sheet is the source for the Spirit Lake chicken-wire story, the 8-foot-4-inch height, and the 47 pieces of sheet copper.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

How tall is the capitol replica, and what is it made of?

The legislature’s tour sheet says 8 feet 4 inches and 47 pieces of sheet copper.

Who gave it to the state?

Both legislature pages say the Tall Corn Council, now the Mid-Iowa Council, donated it in 1950, and that Jack Whitaker provided the statue. The tour sheet adds the Spirit Lake chicken-wire replica that led him to pay for a mold.
