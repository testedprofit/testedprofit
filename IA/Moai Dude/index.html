Moai Dude

KCCI’s story published 18 May 2013 says Altoona was unveiling its first public sculpture. Welder and sculptor John Brommel made an 11-foot version of an Easter Island moai out of rusted steel, with a smirk the station calls a surfer-like expression. He named it Moai Dude. The story says the 1,800-pound sculpture would be dedicated at 11 a.m. on Saturday, and that it would be the first piece in a new family park to be called Pirate’s Cove.

Visit Altoona’s art page repeats the height and the weight, 11 feet and 1,800 pounds, and says the unveiling in 2013 was the city’s first public sculpture. It describes the smirk as a playful change from the stoic faces of the Rapa Nui moai, which that page dates from 1000 C.E. to the late 1600s. The Visit Altoona page that loaded does not print a street address or a park name. KCCI’s 2013 line is the one that names Pirate’s Cove as the park then planned for the sculpture.

The two pages agree on the artist, the year, the height, the weight, and the city’s first public sculpture. They do not describe a wheelchair rating or a daily hours line. This head is not the farm moai near Alburnett.

Questions

Who made Moai Dude, and how big is it?

KCCI and Visit Altoona both say John Brommel made an 11-foot, 1,800-pound rusted-steel head, unveiled in 2013 as Altoona’s first public sculpture. KCCI describes the smirk as surfer-like.

Which park is it in?

The KCCI story of 18 May 2013 says the sculpture would be the first piece in a new family park to be called Pirate’s Cove. The Visit Altoona page that loaded does not print a street address.
