Peace sign on Dubuque Street

This guide is for a peace sign along Dubuque Street in Iowa City. The Iowa City Press-Citizen published a story on 20 May 2021 about a carved peace sign on that street.

A different sculpture on the same street should not be confused with the peace sign. Iowa PBS says the World’s Largest Wooden Nickel is on the east side of Dubuque Street in Iowa City. PBS says Jim Glasgow built the 16-foot wooden sculpture in 2006 as a political protest. Citing the Press-Citizen, PBS says that in the early 2000s Johnson County wanted to pave Newport Road from Highway 1 to Prairie du Chien Road, a project of nearly 7 million dollars that risked the natural character of the existing road. PBS says the nickel went up, the protests stopped the paving, and that before Glasgow’s project the title had belonged to a 13-foot nickel at the Historical Wooden Nickel Museum in San Antonio, Texas.

The nickel and the peace sign are two objects. This URL is the peace-sign page. The nickel facts are here so a reader on Dubuque Street can tell them apart, and because the nickel account is the public page that opened. Iowa PBS does not describe the peace sign.

Another Iowa City monument is the Black Angel in Oakland Cemetery.
