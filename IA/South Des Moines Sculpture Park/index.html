South Des Moines Sculpture Park

dsm magazine, on 31 May 2018, says Phil Barber bought the abandoned lot across from his home near Southwest Ninth and McKinley, cleared tires and brush, drained swampy ground, and put up a sculpture. The first work was a big steel clothespin by his friend, the late Chuck Mettler, in the jumbo manner of Claes Oldenburg. The magazine says a dozen pieces by local artists then filled the South Des Moines Sculpture Park. Barber calls it the poor man’s Pappajohn Park. The same article says John and Mary Pappajohn filled a 4.4-acre downtown site in 2009 with a collection then valued at about 40 million dollars. That sentence is about the downtown park, not a price tag on the McKinley lot.

CITYVIEW, on 5 September 2018, places the South Des Moines Sculpture Park at the corner of Southwest Eighth and McKinley, across from Barber’s house. It also uses the poor man’s Pappajohn line. It says that a decade earlier the strip was swampland used for dumping, and that Barber and friends bought it because there was little art on the south side. The piece says the park then had more than a dozen sculptures. dsm’s dozen and CITYVIEW’s more than a dozen stay side by side, as do Southwest Ninth and Southwest Eighth.

CITYVIEW describes an outhouse that hides a smoke machine piped to a 30-foot egg-shaped UFO, in front of a steel pyramid, near what the article calls a candidate for the world’s largest functioning paper clip, weighing 300 pounds. It says a festival on Saturday, 15 September 2018, would award a 1,000 dollar art scholarship to a Lincoln High School student, with more than two dozen vendors and an expected crowd of 800 to 1,000. That date is the 2018 event in the article. Barber told CITYVIEW the next work would be a giant typewriter built into the hill, with keys as benches. The article presents that typewriter as a plan.

Neither article prints a 24-hour schedule or an archery-target sculpture. The corner descriptions are the locations that loaded.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Who started the park, and what was first?

dsm magazine says Phil Barber cleared the lot near Southwest Ninth and McKinley and that the first sculpture was a steel clothespin by Chuck Mettler. CITYVIEW places the park at Southwest Eighth and McKinley and says Barber bought it with friends.

What objects do the 2018 stories name?

CITYVIEW describes a 30-foot egg-shaped UFO, a steel pyramid, and a 300-pound paper clip, plus a smoke machine in an outhouse. dsm says a dozen works. CITYVIEW says more than a dozen. Both counts are kept.
