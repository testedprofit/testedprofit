Little Mermaid

Audubon County’s page for Kimballton says that since 1978 a replica of the Little Mermaid statue of Copenhagen has sat in the fountain at the city park. The county page spells the city Copenhagan. The same paragraph says that in 2013 eight different bronze sculptures depicting Hans Christian Andersen’s tales were added to the garden. The county parks page repeats both dates and both counts.

Those pages do not say the figure is full size, and they do not say it was fiberglass before a later bronze recast. They do not print a street number or a phone tour. The place they name is the fountain in Kimballton’s city park. The city page also notes the Audubon County Freedom Rock in that park and names Ray Bubba Sorensen II as the artist who started the Iowa Freedom Rock tour. It also notes a ball park on the east side of town named for Lloyd Petersen, and a gazebo on Main Street.

The city page and the parks page both say future plans include the sculpture garden as the start of a Little Mermaid Trail that would eventually reach the Danish Windmill in Elk Horn. That sentence is a plan, not a statement that the trail is finished. Both pages also say the park includes colorful play equipment. They do not print a street address or a shelter description.

The year to use for the mermaid is 1978, from the city and parks pages. The year to use for the eight Andersen bronzes is 2013. The pages do not describe viewing hours.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

When did the mermaid arrive, and what else is in the garden?

The county pages say a Little Mermaid replica has been in the city-park fountain since 1978, and that eight bronze Hans Christian Andersen figures were added in the garden in 2013.

Is there a trail to the Elk Horn windmill?

The parks page says future plans include a Little Mermaid Trail from the sculpture garden to the Danish Windmill in Elk Horn. It describes that trail as a plan.
