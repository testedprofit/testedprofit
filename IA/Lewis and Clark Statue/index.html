Spirit of Discovery

A National Park Service page about the Sioux City Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center captions a photograph of a 14-foot-tall bronze sculpture named Spirit of Discovery. The same page says the center, and the adjoining Betty Strong Encounter, were built and are sustained by Missouri River Historical Development. It says the center spotlights the expedition in the present-day Sioux City area, and that Sergeant Charles Floyd died on 20 August 1804 in present-day Sioux City. The page’s story about a robot dog named Seaman is an indoor exhibit. It is not a description of the outdoor bronze.

The center’s own site gives the address as 900 Larsen Park Road, Sioux City, Iowa 51103, at exit 149 off Interstate 29, and the phone number 712-224-5242. It says admission is free. Hours on that page are closed Mondays, open Tuesday through Friday from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Saturday from 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., and Sunday from 12:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m., with last entrance half an hour before closing. It also lists closures on New Year’s Day, Easter, Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day. The site calls the center a cultural hub for more than 23 years, serving more than 39,000 guests a year.

The pages do not name a sculptor, and they do not say the bronze is life size. The height in the National Park Service caption is 14 feet. The name in that caption is Spirit of Discovery. The center page is the source for the street address and the hours.

Sergeant Floyd’s death date is part of the National Park Service account of the center.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

What is the bronze called, and how tall is it?

The National Park Service caption calls it Spirit of Discovery and says it is a 14-foot bronze at the Sioux City Lewis and Clark Interpretive Center. The pages do not name the sculptor.

When is the center open?

The center’s site says it is closed Mondays, open Tuesday through Friday 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Saturday 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., and Sunday 12:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m. Admission is free. The address is 900 Larsen Park Road, exit 149 off Interstate 29.
