The Black Angel

Wikipedia’s article on Oakland Cemetery says the Black Angel is a locally famous monument, 8.5 feet tall, by Mario Korbel, erected in 1913 as a memorial to Nicholas Feldevert. Later in the same article, the bronze is said to have been made in Chicago by the Czech-American sculptor and transported to Iowa City to be placed in the cemetery in 1915. This guide keeps 1913 and 1915 side by side. The city’s cemetery page did not load, so a separate arrival story from that page is not added.

The article says Teresa Feldevert, née Karásková, moved to Iowa City from Bohemia, now in the Czech Republic. Her son Edward Dolezal died in Iowa City in 1891. Nicholas Feldevert’s ashes were placed in a repository at the base. Teresa died in 1924, and her ashes were placed beside her husband’s. The monument displays her birth date and not her death date. Wikipedia says the bronze oxidized and acquired a greenish-black patina. That is the color account in the article that loaded. Stories that treat the color as a curse are not in that article, and they are not repeated here as history.

The cemetery itself was deeded to the residents of Iowa City on 13 February 1843 and has been the city’s main cemetery since that year. It is on the north side of Iowa City at 1000 Brown Street, covers 40 acres, and is owned by the City of Iowa City. Wikipedia calls it a public, non-perpetual-care cemetery, supported by taxpayers. It says anyone can be buried there, and that Catholics were usually buried at St. Joseph Cemetery and Jewish residents at Agudas Achim. Oakland is adjacent to Hickory Hill Park. The article does not print gate hours.

A different Iowa City roadside page is the peace sign on Dubuque Street.

Questions

Who made the Black Angel, and when was it put up?

Wikipedia says Mario Korbel made the 8.5-foot bronze. The same article says it was erected in 1913 and, in a later paragraph, placed in the cemetery in 1915. Both dates are kept.

Why is the angel dark?

Wikipedia says the bronze oxidized and acquired a greenish-black patina. The article that loaded does not treat the color as a curse.
