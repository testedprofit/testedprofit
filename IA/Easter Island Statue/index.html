Etzel moai

The Gazette story of 5 August 2009 says George Etzel had a 14-foot Easter Island head sculpted for his farm near Alburnett, in Linn County. Cedar Rapids sculptor Steve Piper, a longtime friend, made it after Etzel asked for something large that would stand out in his field. They chose a moai, the name the story uses for the Polynesian heads. The story says the originals were carved from volcanic ash some 1,500 years ago, on an island 2,200 miles west of Chile.

The same story gives the farm figure as 14 feet tall, 8 feet wide, and 3,000 pounds, and says it took Piper about a year and a half. It is steel, meant to rust. Piper sprayed it with a salt-water solution to help that along. It was installed that spring with a crane, on a concrete platform about 400 feet from Etzel’s home. A gravel road passes to the east. The story says people stop to photograph it, and that some walk closer and realize the head is metal rather than wood.

Piper built the frame from half-inch steel rods and attached 12-gauge plates. Some plates weigh more than 40 pounds. The story says the outer shell uses 500 square feet of steel at 4.3 pounds per square foot, which it calculates as 2,150 pounds of steel, on a steel plate tied to 11 yards of concrete. The 3,000-pound figure and the 2,150-pound shell figure are both in that article. This guide does not average them.

Etzel, then 78, told the paper he hoped to add other sculptures and doubted he would add another moai. Piper said he hoped not to be asked for another of that size, because he had to build scaffolding.

A different steel head, in Altoona, is the Moai Dude guide.

Questions

How big is the Alburnett moai?

The Gazette story of 5 August 2009 says the steel head is 14 feet tall, 8 feet wide, and 3,000 pounds. The same story calculates 2,150 pounds of steel for the outer shell. Both numbers are kept.

Who made it, and where does it stand?

Steve Piper made it for George Etzel’s farm near Alburnett. The story says a crane set it on a concrete platform about 400 feet from the house, with a gravel road to the east.
