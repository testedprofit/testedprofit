Squire Boone statue in Shelbyville

The Shelby County Historical Society says a statue of Squire Maugridge Boone, Jr. was created by sculptor Raymond Graf. A committee oversaw the project, and several members belonged to the historical society. The unveiling and dedication on 14 October 2017 drew over 100 descendants to Shelby County. A crowd gathered at Centenary United Methodist Church, whose heritage the society ties to Painted Stone.

East Main Street

The Boone Society held its national reunion from 20 to 24 June 2018 in Lexington, with a Shelbyville event on 21 June to celebrate the placement of the statue. The society says members placed a wreath at the statue on East Main Street. That is the street the historical society gives for the statue. A historical marker for Painted Stone Station stands on the Old Courthouse lawn at 5th and Main. That marker is not the statue.

The man the statue honors

The society dates Squire Maugridge Boone, Jr. from 5 October 1744 to 5 August 1815 and says he settled Painted Stone Station in 1779 and 1780 on Clear Creek. He carved his name and a date on a stone and painted the letters red, and the place took its name from that stone. He served two terms in the Virginia legislature, in 1789 and 1790, and the society calls him the primary sponsor of a bill to charter Louisville. It also says that on 1 May 1780 the Virginia Legislature passed an act to establish Louisville. He died of congestive heart failure at 70 and was buried in a cave on his property in Harrison County, Indiana, later known as Squire Boone Caverns.
