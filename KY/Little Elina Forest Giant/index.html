Little Elina at Bernheim Forest

Little Elina is one of three recycled-wood figures Thomas Dambo placed at Bernheim Forest for the forest's 90th anniversary. Bernheim's 17 March 2019 account, datelined Clermont, Kentucky, and written by Amy Joseph Landon, calls the installation Forest Giants in a Giant Forest. It says the three structures stand in the arboretum and were built from recycled wood from the region.

The family in the fairytale

The same account says Dambo wrote a fairytale, While the Weather Got Better, as the backstory. Mama Loumari, the mother giant, is expecting a baby and rests against a tree. Her children, Little Nis and Little Elina, take their time exploring the forest's pools and rocks. The fairytale names Isak Heartstone as the patriarch and describes him as another of Dambo's sculptures. Bernheim's 15 April 2019 artist note says Little Elina loves to play with rocks, while Little Nis is fascinated with his own reflection.

How long the 2019 account expected them to stay

That March account says Bernheim's Forest Giants were expected to remain in place for at least three years. Dambo's own portfolio lists the work as number 041, Little Elina, Bernheim Forest, Kentucky, USA, 2019, commissioned by the Isaac W. Bernheim Foundation.

Questions

Who made Little Elina, and where is she?

Bernheim Forest says Danish artist Thomas Dambo made Little Elina in 2019 as part of Forest Giants in a Giant Forest, for the forest's 90th anniversary. His portfolio places her at Bernheim Forest, Kentucky, commissioned by the Isaac W. Bernheim Foundation. The March 2019 account is datelined Clermont, Kentucky.

What is she doing in the story?

Bernheim's artist note says Little Elina loves to play with rocks, while Little Nis is fascinated with his own reflection. The March 2019 account says the children explore the forest's pools and rocks, and that Mama Loumari rests against a tree expecting a baby.
