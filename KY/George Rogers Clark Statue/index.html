George Rogers Clark statue

A bronze of George Rogers Clark by Felix W. de Weldon stands at Riverfront Plaza/Belvedere, 485 West Main Street. The Hillman-Hopkins family gave it to the people of Louisville. The figure dates to 1973, the year the plaza was dedicated.

Who Clark was

George Rogers Clark was a military officer and surveyor from Virginia, the highest-ranking Patriot officer on the northwestern frontier in the Revolutionary War. He led the Virginia militia in Kentucky through much of that war. He is credited with founding Louisville in 1778. The campaigns people still name are the captures of Kaskaskia, Illinois, in 1778 and Vincennes, Indiana, in 1779. Those wins weakened British hold on the Northwest Territory, and they earned him the name Conqueror of the Old Northwest. In the 1783 Treaty of Paris, Britain ceded that territory to the United States. The bronze on Main Street is Louisville's figure of the man tied to the city's start.

The sculptor and the gift

Felix Weihs de Weldon made the Clark bronze. His best-known work is the Marine Corps War Memorial at Arlington, the flag-raising at Iwo Jima, and he also made the Malaysian National Monument in 1966. His list of works includes a 1973 General George Rogers Clark in Louisville, and, the same year, a Benjamin Franklin at the Louisville public library. The downtown art listing names him Felix W. de Weldon, places the Clark at Riverfront Plaza/Belvedere, and records the sponsor line: a gift to the people of Louisville by the Hillman-Hopkins family. The street address on that listing is 485 West Main Street.

How the plaza is laid out

Riverfront Plaza/Belvedere is the public edge of downtown on the Ohio River. Funding of 13.5 million dollars was secured in 1969, and the project was dedicated on 27 April 1973. It runs between Third and Sixth streets. Under the grass are a parking garage and the interstate. The grassy park on the western end is the Belvedere, about seven acres. East of that grass, the Riverfront Plaza side holds fountains, shelters, and buildings that include the Galt House. The plaza is the east end of the Louisville Riverwalk.

Clark's bronze sits immediately east of the park section. York, William Clark's enslaved servant on the Lewis and Clark expedition, is a different bronze, by Ed Hamilton, erected in 2003 on the Belvedere itself, with plaques for the expedition and for York. If you came for George Rogers Clark, stay on the east side of the grass. If you are looking at a figure on the lawn with Lewis and Clark plaques, you are at York.

A short stop on the river

Come to 485 West Main and face the river. The Belvedere grass is the west end of the deck. Clark is the bronze just east of that lawn. The Franklin at the library and the Jefferson monument at Metro Hall are other Louisville bronzes, not this plaza. Give the Clark figure a few minutes, then walk the grass if you want the river view. The dedication year, 1973, is both the plaza's year and the year on de Weldon's Clark.

If you are coming along the Louisville Riverwalk, the plaza is the east end of that path. Climb onto the deck, keep the grass on your left as you face the river, and the Clark bronze is the figure just before the lawn opens up. Third Street is the east edge of the project and Sixth Street is the west edge, so a walk from one to the other passes the statue, the fountains, and the Belvedere in one stretch. The parking is under the grass. The bronze stands on the public deck.

Questions

Where is the George Rogers Clark statue?

At Riverfront Plaza/Belvedere, 485 West Main Street, immediately east of the grassy park.

Who made it, and when?

Felix W. de Weldon, in 1973. The Hillman-Hopkins family gave it to the people of Louisville.

Is the figure on the grass the same statue?

No. York, by Ed Hamilton, stands on the Belvedere and was erected in 2003. Clark is the bronze east of the park.
