Simpson County butter knife

The Franklin Favorite describes The Cutting Edge of Simpson County as a giant butter knife of stainless steel at Blackjack Sculpture Park, just past the former Blackjack Store, about 4 miles north of downtown Franklin. The paper says the knife stands 24 feet tall, three times the then-current Guinness table-knife record. It extends 6 feet into the ground, is held by 4,000 pounds of concrete, and weighs around 1,000 pounds. The comparison with the Guinness record is the newspaper's.

The school shop

Franklin-Simpson High School welding teacher Jeremy Loveall led the project, and his students helped build the knife. The Favorite quotes him: Cardwell Metal Works in Franklin cut the pieces in 2020, just before school shut down. The class returned in spring 2021 and finished about 95 percent before school ended. Loveall and several friends finished the knife and installed it on Aug. 2. The paper does not print a year on that August date. It places the return to the shop in spring 2021. The knife followed the 2018 Fork in the Road on Bunch Road. The Favorite says Junior Bush, known as the Mayor of Blackjack, once owned the land and the Blackjack Store.

A second height on the tourism page

Franklin-Simpson County Tourism lists Blackjack Sculpture Park at 4423 Blackjack Road and describes sculptures by the high school welding class, including a giant glider plane, a weather vane, a 20-foot-tall knife, a space capsule, and more. The Favorite's 24 feet and the tourism page's 20 feet both stay in this account.

Questions

How tall is the butter knife?

The Franklin Favorite says The Cutting Edge of Simpson County is a stainless-steel butter knife 24 feet tall, about 1,000 pounds, set 6 feet into the ground in 4,000 pounds of concrete. Franklin-Simpson County Tourism calls a knife at Blackjack Sculpture Park 20 feet tall. Both heights are in print.

Where does it stand, and who built it?

Tourism lists Blackjack Sculpture Park at 4423 Blackjack Road. The Favorite places the knife just past the former Blackjack Store, about 4 miles north of downtown Franklin. Welding teacher Jeremy Loveall and his students built it, with pieces cut by Cardwell Metal Works in 2020 and installation on Aug. 2 after work resumed in spring 2021.
