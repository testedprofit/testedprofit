109 West Main Street, Carbondale, Illinois

Train conductor in Carbondale

A bronze conductor stands on the town square in Carbondale, Illinois, beside the old Illinois Central depot. Roy Clark, a 44-year Illinois Central conductor, was the model. Station Carbondale placed the figure with donations. The plaque at his feet is dated 1997. This page keeps a Kentucky web address. The statue is in Jackson County, Illinois. See it

Square
109 West Main Street, Carbondale, Illinois 62901. West Main Street is Illinois Route 13, east of South Illinois Avenue, U.S. 51. West Main is one way, going west. Traveling west, the marker is on the left, at the base of the statue. Outdoors. No ticket. The pin is 37.72708, -89.21653.
Depot
The figure stands near the old passenger station on Main Street. A caboose sits on a display track beside the building, and retired Illinois Central GP11 diesel No. 8701 joined it in 2000. Locomotive and caboose tours for groups and school trips are by appointment. Displays inside the station follow the hours of Carbondale Main Street and the Chamber of Commerce.
City line
City of Carbondale, (618) 549-5302. City Hall is at 200 South Illinois Avenue.

What the plaque says

The marker at the base was put up by Station Carbondale, Inc. It says the statue was placed through donations from people dedicated to preserving Carbondale's railroad history. It dates the first train to 4 July 1854. It says as many as 53 passenger trains passed through each day at the peak of that traffic, and it salutes railroaders for their work. The City of Carbondale's own page repeats the town-square placement, the donations, and the figure of 53 trains.

The Daily Egyptian of 1 July 1998, writing ahead of that Saturday's Founder's Day, described a 10 a.m. dedication of the bronze night watchman and the bricks around it. That Saturday was the Fourth of July. Station Carbondale's history places the conductor statue near the station, and a caboose on the display track, in 1998. A 2021 caption in The Southern dates the statue's making to 1997, the same year cut on the plaque. The dedication the newspaper announced, and the year the organization gives for setting the figure by the depot, are 1998.

Roy Clark

Roy Clark was president of Station Carbondale and the model for the bronze. He had worked 44 years as a conductor for the Illinois Central. In the 1998 interview he pointed to the depot and the caboose as a way to learn how the railroad used to run, and he noted that cabooses were no longer being built. A later museum story again names him as the model for the conductor beside the old depot, and as a lender of objects to the exhibit.

The bronze is the night watchman of that 1998 dedication and the conductor of the city's later pages. Clark is the man those accounts use as the model.

Why Carbondale has a conductor

The Daily Egyptian dates the founding to 1850, when the Illinois Central announced a railroad from Chicago to Cairo. Daniel Brush, the founder, bought the land where the road from Marion to Murphysboro crossed that line. The same article says the first locomotive arrived in 1853 and that more than 3,000 people marked it with food and fireworks. Mike Kimmel, then president of the preservation commission, called that the moment Carbondale became a real town, and he tied the railroad to the prosperity that later drew Southern Illinois University.

The plaque uses a different first-train date, 4 July 1854. Both dates are in print. The statue and the bricks around it were the railroad half of the 1998 Founder's Day on the square, at the pavilion near Main Street and South Illinois Avenue.

The station beside him

Station Carbondale began in 1995 to restore the Illinois Central passenger station, which the organization dates to 1903, and to build a railroad museum. It grew out of a restoration committee inside the uptown citizens' group that started downtown work in the late 1980s. A 2008 account in The Southern dates that former depot to 1893 and says the current Amtrak station replaced it in 1978. The old building now houses Carbondale Main Street and the Chamber of Commerce.

The depot went on the National Register of Historic Places in 2003. In 2004 the display room of railroad artifacts opened. Visitors can walk a locomotive, a caboose, and the depot. The square also carries a marker for Daniel Harmon Brush a short distance from the conductor, plus the numbered station signs of the town-square trail. In February 2020 a photograph showed Amtrak's Illini passing the statue as it left town.

Questions

Where is the conductor statue?

On the town square in Carbondale, Illinois, at 109 West Main Street, 62901, beside the old Illinois Central depot. West Main Street is one way, westbound. The figure is outdoors.

Who is the figure, and when was it dedicated?

Roy Clark, a 44-year Illinois Central conductor, was the model. The plaque at the base is dated 1997. The Daily Egyptian of 1 July 1998 set the dedication of the bronze for that Saturday, the Fourth of July.

What railroad history does it mark?

The plaque dates the first train to 4 July 1854 and says as many as 53 passenger trains a day passed through Carbondale at the peak. Station Carbondale placed the statue with donations to remember that history.
