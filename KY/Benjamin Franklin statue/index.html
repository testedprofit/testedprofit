301 York Street

Benjamin Franklin statue

Felix de Weldon's sculpture of Benjamin Franklin stands on the grounds of the Louisville Free Public Library, at 301 York Street. It is not the George Rogers Clark bronze on the Belvedere. The Main Library building has been closed for renovation since 1 December 2025. This is an independent Profitnet guide.

York

Library

Main Library grounds, downtown Louisville. The Carnegie building dates to 1906. The north wing came in 1969.

See it while the building is closed

Address
Louisville Free Public Library, Main Library, 301 York Street, Louisville, Kentucky 40203. The Franklin is on the grounds, associated with the 1969 addition at the rear of the Carnegie building.
Closure
The library's site marks the Main Library closed for renovations. The doors shut on 1 December 2025 for an $8 million-plus project. A downtown development listing set a completion target of October 2026. In June 2026 the building was still closed, with work expected to run as long as a year. Stay out of the construction area. If fencing is up, the sidewalk is the place to look. Check lfpl.org before you go.
While it is shut
The Library Computer Center at 721 South Brook Street is open Monday through Friday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. The line there is (502) 574-1681. The Main Library page lists (502) 574-1781.

The sculptor and the two dates

Felix Weihs de Weldon made the sculpture. He was born in Vienna on 12 April 1907 and died in Washington, D.C., on 3 June 2003. He is buried at Arlington National Cemetery. His best-known work is the Marine Corps War Memorial, the flag-raising at Iwo Jima, dedicated on 10 November 1954. He also made Malaysia's National Monument, finished in 1966. A list of his public sculpture puts about 1,200 works in seven countries.

That same list dates a Benjamin Franklin at the Louisville Public Library to 1973. It dates his General George Rogers Clark, also in Louisville, to the same year. A published history of the Main Library tells the story a different way: in 1969 an 110,000-square-foot concrete-and-glass addition went up at the rear, and with that addition a sculpture of Benjamin Franklin by de Weldon was added to the grounds. The library's own renovation page says the 1969 wing, by Louis and Henry of Louisville, more than doubled the space. The two published dates sit side by side. The Clark bronze is a separate figure, at Riverfront Plaza.

The library around him

The Main Library is the flagship of the Louisville Free Public Library, the largest public library system in Kentucky. Andrew Carnegie supported the original building. The library dates that Carnegie block to 1906. A history of the branch says construction began in 1905 and the building opened in 1908, in the Beaux-Arts manner of the New York firm Pilcher and Tachau. The old delivery room, now the foyer, is a two-story Louis XVI space with a stained-glass barrel vault, marble columns, and murals. The main library was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980.

The grounds already held a famous bronze before Franklin arrived. George Grey Barnard's 13-foot Abraham Lincoln, given by Mr. and Mrs. Isaac W. Bernheim, was dedicated on 26 October 1922. A long local habit is to rub Lincoln's toe for luck. That Lincoln and this Franklin are two sculptures on one library site. The Hodgenville Lincoln, a seated bronze on the town square, is a third figure and a different town.

The 1937 flood put the library under water, with the high mark about thirty feet above flood stage. In 2009 seven inches of rain in seventy minutes flooded the main floor again. After that repair the building gained cisterns for excess water. The current work is different. It opens the old closed stacks of the 1906 building into more than 16,000 square feet of public space, adds an accessible entrance on York Street, and reopens the third floor, which had been shut to the public since 2018. Federal American Rescue Plan money, allocated by the mayor and Metro Council, pays for it, along with private gifts to the Library Foundation. A June 2026 note from the mayor's office described the project as adding or restoring 56,000 square feet.

Why Franklin

Benjamin Franklin was born in Boston on 17 January 1706 and died in Philadelphia on 17 April 1790. He was a printer, a scientist, and a diplomat, and a founder of the Library Company of Philadelphia, the subscription library he helped start in 1731. The Louisville sculpture puts that library founder on the grounds of a later free public library. The city's page for the work is the library itself. There is no separate downtown-art listing of the kind that records the Clark bronze's sponsor and street number.

A different de Weldon, a few blocks away

If you came for the riverfront figure, that is George Rogers Clark at the Belvedere, 485 West Main Street, also by de Weldon and also dated 1973 on his list of works. The Hillman-Hopkins family gave that bronze to the people of Louisville. Metro Hall holds the Jefferson monument. The seated Lincoln in Hodgenville is the town-square bronze, not Barnard's library figure. This page is the Franklin at 301 York Street.

Questions

Who made the Louisville Franklin, and where is it?

Felix de Weldon made the sculpture for the Louisville Free Public Library. It stands on the grounds of the Main Library at 301 York Street, Louisville, Kentucky 40203.

When was it put there?

A history of the Main Library ties the sculpture to the 1969 north building. A list of de Weldon's public works dates a Benjamin Franklin at this library to 1973, the same year as his George Rogers Clark on the Belvedere.

Can you see it now?

The Main Library building has been closed for renovation since 1 December 2025, and the library site still marks it closed. The sculpture is on the grounds. Stay out of the construction area and check the library site before you go.
