Sasquatch at Armco Park

Kentucky Today carries an Ashland Daily Independent story by Austin Johnson, dated 7 September 2023 and updated 8 September 2023. It says a local carving artist finished a 14-foot wood-carved Sasquatch on Monday in Armco Park in Ashland. Travis Williams, of Boyd County, is the creator. He told the paper it probably took about 40 hours spread throughout a week. He called it his biggest carving. The second biggest, he said, was an Ohio State Brutus, 12 feet tall. He had been wood-carving for eight years. A family road trip to Gatlinburg, Tennessee, is what he said fueled the work: he watched chainsaw carvers at the side of the road, then borrowed his father's chainsaw.

The same story says that about a year earlier people thought they had seen a bear in Armco Park, and that it was a laid-down log. Williams talked to County Administrator Justin Pruitt about carving something for the park. He first suggested a bear off the trail. Pruitt liked the idea, and the talks later turned to a Sasquatch.

WSAZ, by Andrew Colegrove, uses 26 October 2023 as the story date and 27 October 2023 in the page address. It says visitors to Armco Park were reporting a 14-foot-tall Bigfoot, and that Williams's recent work there also included a younger sasquatch and a bear. Williams said a bear sighting at the park was a laid-down log, that he contacted Justin Pruitt, who is over the parks department, and that the idea snowballed. He said the 14-foot Bigfoot took six days, and that the plan was to add more creatures. WSAZ also says Williams, who works at Marathon, decided to try chainsaw carving seven years before that story. The eight-year line and the seven-year line both stay, as do the week of about 40 hours and the six days.

An Ashland Community and Technical College libguide by Janet Thompson lists a newly added 14-foot Sasquatch among Armco Park's features, with a vintage AK Steel sign and the Boyd County War Memorial. It says the park is open year round and sits beside Crisp's Dairy Treat. The stories place the carving in Armco Park in Ashland.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

How tall is the Armco Sasquatch, and who carved it?

Kentucky Today and WSAZ both describe a 14-foot carving by Travis Williams at Armco Park in Ashland. Kentucky Today calls it wood. WSAZ also reports a younger sasquatch and a bear by the same carver.

How long did the carving take?

Kentucky Today quotes Williams that it took about 40 hours spread through a week. WSAZ quotes him that the 14-foot Bigfoot took six days. Both timings stay. Kentucky Today says he had been carving for eight years; WSAZ says he started seven years before its story.
