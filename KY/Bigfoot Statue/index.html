Ashland, Boyd County

14-foot Sasquatch at Armco Park

A 14-foot wood Sasquatch stands in Armco Park in Ashland, Kentucky. Travis Williams of Boyd County carved it from an old oak in September 2023 and gave it a squirrel in one hand so the figure would stay kid friendly. The park is open year round. A younger sasquatch and a bear, also his, share the grounds with the tall one.

Place
Armco Park, Ashland, Boyd County, Kentucky 41102. Pin 38.43964, -82.68947. The park sits beside Crisp's Dairy Treat.
Figure
14-foot chainsaw carving in oak. Kid friendly, with a squirrel in one hand, after Harry and the Hendersons. Williams called it a photo stop.
Carver
Travis Williams. In September 2023 he had been wood-carving for eight years. The October interview dates the chainsaw start to seven years earlier and names his job at Marathon.
Time on the big one
About 40 hours across a week, in the 7 September 2023 interview. Six days, in the 26 October 2023 interview.

How a log became a Sasquatch

The carving starts with a false bear. About a year before the statue went up, people thought they had seen a bear in Armco Park. It was a laid-down log with a bear's profile, and the picture moved around Facebook. Williams went to Justin Pruitt, the county administrator, who also handles the parks department. The first idea was a carved bear set off a trail in the woods. Pruitt liked it. A few months later the talk had turned into a Sasquatch.

Austin Johnson's story for the Ashland Daily Independent, published by Kentucky Today on 7 September 2023 and updated the next day, says Williams finished the 14-foot wood figure on a Monday in the park. Williams told that paper it probably took about 40 hours, spread through a week, and that the response on his personal Facebook account was heavier than for any earlier carving. He called it his biggest piece. The next one down, he said, was a 12-foot Ohio State Brutus.

On 26 October 2023, Andrew Colegrove's WSAZ story caught the same park just before Halloween, with visitors reporting a 14-foot Bigfoot. Williams told that station the tall figure took six days. He also said the plan was to keep adding creatures in the park woods. By then the grounds already held the 14-foot Bigfoot, a younger sasquatch, and a bear. If you are looking for the one in the title, use the height. The oak figure with the squirrel is the 14-foot Sasquatch. The smaller sasquatch and the bear are the other two carvings from that season.

The oak, the squirrel, and the carver

Williams described the commission in his own words on 7 September 2023. He was asked to carve a Sasquatch from an old oak in the local park in Boyd County. The request was to keep it kid friendly, so he used Harry and the Hendersons as the mood and put a squirrel in the figure's hand. He wanted a photo stop for a county that was pushing tourism. The Daily Independent story and the WSAZ story place that carving at Armco Park.

The habit is older than this tree. By the September interview he had been wood-carving for eight years. A family trip to Gatlinburg, Tennessee, is what he said fueled the work. The first try was a bear cut from a dead tree in the front yard. His wife would not let that one stay in the yard. He kept at it. The October interview puts the chainsaw start seven years before that story, with the same front-yard tree, and it notes that he works at Marathon. The tall oak Sasquatch at Armco is the piece both interviews describe.

Chainsaw carving at this size is a week of cutting, not a weekend whittle. Forty hours across seven days and a straight six-day count are the two timings he gave. Either way, the finished height he and both newsrooms use is 14 feet. That is two feet over the Brutus he named as the previous record in his own shop.

What else is in the park

Armco Park is a Boyd County park in Ashland, ZIP 41102, in the Summit area south of the river city. An Ashland Community and Technical College guide by Janet Thompson, updated 9 April 2026, still lists a 14-foot Sasquatch among the features, with a vintage AK Steel sign and the Boyd County War Memorial. The guide says the park is open year round, with walking trails, and that it sits beside Crisp's Dairy Treat. Picnic, a reunion, or a short hike are the uses it names.

Visit Boyd County's directory page adds the Iron Ore Trail, disc golf on more than one course, the war memorial, and picnic grounds. The same bureau's contact page lists (606) 585-4770 and tourism@visitboydcounty.com. Boyd County government's published office is 2800 Louisa Street in Catlettsburg. That is the county seat address, not the trailhead. For the carving, use the Ashland park.

The map pin for the park polygon is 38.43964, -82.68947. Walk until you find the tall oak figure with the squirrel. The steel sign and the war memorial are the other landmarks the college guide pairs with it, so a loop of the park covers more than one photo.

Questions

How tall is the Armco Sasquatch, and who carved it?

Travis Williams of Boyd County carved the 14-foot wood Sasquatch at Armco Park in Ashland. He finished it in September 2023. WSAZ also reports a younger sasquatch and a bear by the same carver in the park.

How long did the carving take?

Williams told the Ashland Daily Independent, in the story Kentucky Today published on 7 September 2023, that it took about 40 hours spread through a week. He told WSAZ in October 2023 that the 14-foot Bigfoot took six days.

What does the figure look like, and is it still at the park?

Williams wrote that he carved it from an old oak, kept it kid friendly, and gave it a squirrel in one hand, after Harry and the Hendersons. An Ashland Community and Technical College guide updated on 9 April 2026 still lists the 14-foot Sasquatch among the park features. The park is open year round.

How do I visit Armco Park?

Armco Park is in Boyd County at Ashland, Kentucky 41102, beside Crisp's Dairy Treat. The map pin is 38.43964, -82.68947. Visit Boyd County lists the Iron Ore Trail, disc golf, the war memorial, and picnic spots. The tourism bureau phone is (606) 585-4770 and the email is tourism@visitboydcounty.com.
