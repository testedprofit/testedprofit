Booker Noe and Dot

A Kentucky Brown Water Society note says that, while visiting the Jim Beam American Still House, the writers came across a bronze of Frederick Booker Noe II and his dog Dot. It says he was six foot four and a commanding figure even while sitting in his rocking chair. The six-foot-four figure is the society's description of Booker Noe himself. The note says he was master distiller at Jim Beam for more than 40 years and filled Jim Beam's millionth barrel. It says that during a downturn, while clear spirits rose, he released small-batch bourbons that were unfiltered, uncut, and straight from the barrel, and that this barrel-proof method gave full flavor from the cask. It says Booker's bourbon won awards, including Wine Enthusiast's gold bourbon award, and that the success led to other labels, naming Baker's, Basil Hayden's, and Knob Creek.

Jim Beam's visit page says the James B. Beam Distilling Co. is in Clermont, Kentucky, and prints the campus address as 568 Happy Hollow Road, Clermont, Kentucky 40110. It says seven generations of the Beam family built the place and that bourbon has been made the Beam way since 1795. Those lines are the visit page's account of the distillery. The page says the campus is open through the week, with regular distillery hours and seasonal updates, and that opening times, restaurant hours, and tour availability may vary. Guests can visit the gift shop, restaurant, and outdoor spaces without a tour reservation, while excursions and tastings can sell out.

The Kentucky Brown Water Society places this bronze at the Jim Beam American Still House. The street address above is the campus address printed on the visit page. The society's note is about Frederick Booker Noe II and his dog Dot.
