Booker Noe and Dot

A bronze of Frederick Booker Noe II and his dog Dot stands at the Jim Beam American Still House in Clermont. He is seated in a rocking chair, and Dot is with him.

The man in the chair

Frederick Booker Noe II was master distiller at Jim Beam for more than 40 years, and he filled the distillery's millionth barrel. He stood six foot four and was a commanding figure even when he sat. The bronze puts him in that chair, a rocking chair, with his dog Dot. Look for the pair at the American Still House, on the distillery grounds in Clermont, not on a town square.

The bourbon story is why the chair is here. During a downturn, when drinkers were moving to vodka and gin, Noe released small-batch bourbons that were unfiltered, uncut, and drawn straight from the barrel, so the whiskey kept the flavor of the cask. Booker's bourbon won awards, including a Wine Enthusiast gold for bourbon. That success opened the way for Baker's, Basil Hayden's, and Knob Creek. The statue is the distillery's portrait of the man who made those bottles possible, dog at his side.

The campus

The James B. Beam Distilling Co. sits at 568 Happy Hollow Road, Clermont, Kentucky 40110, just south of Louisville, on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail. Seven generations of the Beam family built the place, and bourbon has been made the Beam way since 1795. The bronze is one stop on a working distillery, so the hours are the distillery's hours.

Monday the campus is open 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Tuesday it is closed. Wednesday through Saturday the hours match Monday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Sunday opens a half hour later, 9:30 a.m., and closes at 5:30 p.m. Opening times, restaurant hours, and tour availability shift with the season and with holidays. You can visit the gift shop, the restaurant, and the outdoor spaces without booking a tour. Excursions and tastings sell out, so a reservation matters if the tasting is the point of the drive. Parking is on site. Give yourself time if a tour is on your ticket.

What to look for

Once you are on the grounds, ask the way to the American Still House and look for the seated figure and the dog. The six-foot-four stature is the man. The bronze is the rocking chair and Dot. Read the visit as a distillery stop that happens to hold a portrait: the still house, the yard, and a short look at the pair. Louisville monuments, including the Jefferson bronze at Metro Hall, are a separate drive north.

Clermont is a short drive south from Louisville, on the Kentucky Bourbon Trail. The campus is the destination: still house, shop, and the chair. If a tour is booked, arrive early enough to park and still make the start. If you are there for the bronze alone, the outdoor spaces are enough, inside the posted hours, on a day other than Tuesday. Sunday's later open, 9:30 a.m., is the one morning that starts half an hour behind the rest of the week.

What you take away is a person, not a barrel count. More than forty years as master distiller, the millionth barrel, and a small-batch answer to a market that was drifting toward clear spirits. Dot is in the bronze because Dot was his. The rocking chair is the pose. Happy Hollow Road is how you get there.

Questions

Where is the Booker Noe bronze?

At the Jim Beam American Still House on the distillery campus, 568 Happy Hollow Road, Clermont, Kentucky 40110.

Who is Dot?

Booker Noe's dog, shown with him. The bronze seats Noe in a rocking chair.

When is the distillery open?

Monday and Wednesday through Saturday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Tuesday closed. Sunday 9:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Seasonal changes apply to tours and the restaurant.
