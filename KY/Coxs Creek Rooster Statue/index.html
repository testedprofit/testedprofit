Nelson County, Kentucky Route 245

Ozzie Frank at Rooster Run

Ozzie Frank is the 13.5-foot fiberglass rooster in front of Rooster Run, the general store on Kentucky Route 245 in Nelson County. Joe Evans opened the place in 1967 as Evans Beverage Depot. The rooster's name and the store's name come from the same night. This page is a Profitnet guide to the statue. It is not the store, and it does not sell the caps.

The statue
13.5 feet, or 4.1 metres, fiberglass, dubbed Ozzie Frank, in front of the store. Evans toured the state with it in his 1991 campaign.
Where
On Ky. 245. Highway signs point here. Mail addressed to Rooster Run is delivered with the Cox's Creek ZIP code, 40013. The Cox's Creek community itself sits about 4.5 miles north of Bardstown on U.S. 31E/150.
2024
Missing that spring, then back with fresh paint for the reopening. Doors opened 26 June. Grand-opening weekend 23 to 25 August.
Who
Joe Evans founded the store and died in 2013. Tim Hutchins, Muni Patel, and Kash Patel reopened it after a closure in the late 2010s.

Look at that rooster run

Evans opened Evans Beverage Depot in 1967 with 200 dollars of his own and 200 dollars from his father. At the time it was the only place in Nelson County selling alcohol. One evening after closing, Evans and friends were at the store drinking beer when one man's wife arrived and he ran. A friend said, Look at that rooster run. Evans's mother then sewed baseball caps with Rooster Run on them. He trademarked the name and the logo in 1986.

A 13.5-foot fiberglass rooster dubbed Ozzie Frank was placed in front of the store. By 1991 it was famous enough to travel: Evans legally changed his name to Joe Rooster Run Evans, ran for Kentucky secretary of state, and toured with Ozzie Frank. He finished last in a three-man Democratic primary. The statue in front of the store is the one those accounts call Ozzie Frank.

The caps stayed the most popular item. Evans had a standing order for 30,000 caps a quarter, and by 1991 he estimated he had sold about 1.5 million. Truck drivers wore them across the country. A Louisville disc jockey told Rooster Run jokes on the air. The Kentucky Encyclopedia calls the store one of the best-known general stores in the country and one of Kentucky's best-known unincorporated businesses. The Lexington Herald-Leader wrote that the community of Rooster Run was named after the store and consists of the store. The Kentucky Transportation Cabinet put the locale on official highway maps, and Evans convinced the cabinet to put signs along Kentucky Route 245. The post office delivers mail addressed to Rooster Run when it carries the ZIP code of nearby Cox's Creek, 40013.

The man, then the gap, then the paint

In the mid-1980s Evans was elected a Nelson County magistrate. After the 1991 statewide race he tried again in 1998, for the Kentucky Senate 8th District, and lost to Republican Dan Kelly. He retired in 2004 and died in 2013.

The store closed in the late 2010s. Katelyn Norris, in the Kentucky Standard of 27 July 2024, wrote that Ozzie Frank was missing that spring and that local social media noticed. The rooster came back to the front of the store with a fresh coat of paint for the reopening. Tim Hutchins, Muni Patel, and Kash Patel are the new owners. Hutchins's family has long owned Handy Food Mart just down the road. They had been working toward the purchase for more than five years. Katie Robinson, Evans's daughter, was invited in the night before the opening and cried at the dining-room wall of clippings and pictures of her father. Kash Patel said Joe was the heart of the place, and that the new store would not be the same store.

General manager Brooke Hutchins said the crew prepared a 4 a.m. opening on 26 June, the day the doors opened. A grand-opening weekend ran 23 to 25 August 2024. The July story is the latest published account of the rooster back in place, painted, on Ky. 245.

How to see it, and Bardstown nearby

Drive Kentucky Route 245 and follow the Rooster Run signs. The rooster stands in front of the store. Cox's Creek, the community whose ZIP the store uses, is an unincorporated place on U.S. 31E/150, about 4.5 miles north of Bardstown, named for Colonel Isaac Cox. The store and the community are not the same pin. Use 245 for Ozzie Frank. Use 31E if you are aiming at Bardstown itself.

Two other Nelson County figures on this site sit in Clermont, on the bourbon side of the county. Booker Noe and Dot is the bronze at the Jim Beam American Still House. Little Elina is Thomas Dambo's recycled-wood giant at Bernheim Forest. Farther afield, the Lincoln statue in Hodgenville is the seated bronze on the public square. The full list is the Kentucky directory.

Questions

What is Ozzie Frank?

Ozzie Frank is a 13.5-foot, or 4.1-metre, fiberglass rooster in front of the Rooster Run store in Nelson County. Joe Evans toured Kentucky with the statue during his 1991 campaign. In the spring of 2024 the rooster was missing, and it came back with fresh paint for the store's reopening.

When did the store open, and when did it reopen?

Joe Evans opened Evans Beverage Depot in 1967. The Kentucky Standard of 27 July 2024 says new owners opened the doors on 26 June, with the staff ready for a 4 a.m. start that day, after a closure in the late 2010s. A grand-opening weekend followed on 23 to 25 August 2024.
