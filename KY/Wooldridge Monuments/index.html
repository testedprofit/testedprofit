Wooldridge Monuments in Mayfield

The Wooldridge Monuments stand in Maplewood Cemetery at 611 North 5th Street in Mayfield. Mayfield-Graves County Tourism calls the stone figures The Strange Procession That Never Moves and says they were built from 1892 until Henry Wooldridge's death on 30 May 1899. Henry, buried there in 1899, has family, friends, and favorite animals enshrined in the figures. Wikipedia says Colonel Henry G. Wooldridge is the only person buried at the site, and it uses the nickname The Strange Procession Which Never Moves. The two nicknames differ by one word. Both are in print.

Eighteen figures facing east

Wikipedia says the lot measures 17 by 33 feet and that all 18 monuments face east. The most prominent is a life-size marble statue of Wooldridge, 6 feet tall, made in Italy. Fourteen other monuments are limestone and more rigid. The largest limestone figure shows Wooldridge on his horse, Fop. Other limestone statues are 5 feet tall on 3-foot bases and represent family members. Wikipedia names the architects as Paducah Marble Works and Pryor and Radford Monument Works, and says the National Register listing 80001533 was added on 11 August 1980. The female figures include his mother Keziah, sisters Minerva, Narcissa, and Susan, and nieces Maud and Minnie. The other men are brothers Alfred, John, Josiah, and W.H. There is no statue of his father.

The ice storm and the gate hours

Wikipedia says that on 27 January 2009 a tree fell in an ice storm and broke several heads, and that only the statues of his sisters and a dog remained standing. Tourism says that after the Ice Storm of 2009 the monuments were restored on site by the Monument Conservation Collaborative. The tourism page says the Confederate Memorial Gate and the monuments are both on the National Register, and that the gates are open from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. daily. A Kentucky After Dark passport sticker is a separate stop: the tourism office at 201 East College Street, Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Questions

Where are the Wooldridge Monuments, and when were they built?

Mayfield-Graves County Tourism places them in Maplewood Cemetery at 611 North 5th Street, Mayfield. The figures were built from 1892 until Henry Wooldridge's death on 30 May 1899. Wikipedia says he is the only person buried at the site.

What are the monuments made of, and what are they called?

Tourism calls the group The Strange Procession That Never Moves. Wikipedia uses Which Never Moves, says all 18 monuments face east, and describes a 6-foot Italian marble of Wooldridge plus limestone figures, including one of him on the horse Fop. The National Register listing was added on 11 August 1980.
