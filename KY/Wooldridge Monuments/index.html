Wooldridge Monuments

The Wooldridge Monuments fill a fenced lot in Maplewood Cemetery at 611 North 5th Street in Mayfield. Henry G. Wooldridge had the figures carved for his family, friends, and animals from 1892 until his death on 30 May 1899. He is the only person buried there.

A procession that stays put

In a plot of Maplewood Cemetery, stone figures of a Civil War soldier, his family, and his animals stand inside a fence. Mayfield-Graves County Tourism calls them The Strange Procession That Never Moves. Another long-used name is The Strange Procession Which Never Moves. The two nicknames differ by a single word, and both are in print. The figures were carved from 1892 until Wooldridge's death on 30 May 1899. Henry, his family, his friends, and his favorite animals are all in the stone. He is the only person buried in the lot.

The lot measures 17 by 33 feet, and all eighteen monuments face east, the way most gravestones in the cemetery face. The centerpiece is a life-size marble of Wooldridge himself, six feet tall, made in Italy. Fourteen other monuments are limestone, stiffer in pose. The largest of those shows him on his horse, Fop. The family figures stand five feet tall on three-foot bases. Behind them are a fox, a deer, and two hounds in a chase. The dogs are his, Towhead and Bob. The makers, from Mayfield and Paducah, are recorded as Paducah Marble Works and Pryor and Radford Monument Works. The National Register listing, number 80001533, was added on 11 August 1980.

Who stands in the lot

The women are his mother Keziah, his sisters Minerva, Narcissa, and Susan, and his nieces Maud and Minnie. The men who are not Henry are his brothers Alfred, John, Josiah, and W.H. There is no figure of his father. The fence around the lot was set by the Mayfield Masonic lodge, in place of an older iron fence. Wooldridge was a Freemason. To reach the monuments you pass the Confederate Memorial Gate, which is itself on the National Register.

The ice storm, and the photograph

On 27 January 2009 a large tree fell in an ice storm and broke several of the heads. Only the statues of his sisters and a dog were left standing. Over the summer of 2010 the Monument Conservation Collaborative restored every monument on site. The photograph on this page was taken on 30 October 2008, so it shows the lot before that storm. The restoration put the procession back. The gates that let you in are open from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. every day.

The group has drawn visitors for decades. Ripley's Believe It or Not featured the monuments in September 1984, and they appear in Weird Kentucky. If you are collecting the Kentucky After Dark passport, the sticker for this stop is at the tourism office at 201 East College Street on weekday afternoons, or at the Super 8 on Links Lane when the office is closed. The monuments themselves are the cemetery lot, not the office.

Give the fence a full circuit. The marble Henry is the tall smooth figure in the center. Fop is the horse. The hounds and the deer sit lower, behind the people. The sisters and the mother are among the limestone women, and the brothers stand with him. East is the direction every figure faces, the same way the cemetery's other stones face. If you have the 2008 photograph in mind, expect the restored group, put back in the summer of 2010 after the ice storm.
