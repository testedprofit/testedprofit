Larry Bird's Childhood Home in French Lick

Larry Bird's childhood home is a private house at 983 S Washington Street in French Lick, Indiana, on the corner of South Jefferson Street. Larry Joe Bird was born on December 7, 1956, in neighboring West Baden and grew up in French Lick. The house is a roadside landmark you see from the street. It is not open for tours.

The house on South Washington

French Lick and West Baden Springs are the twin resort towns in Orange County. The French Lick West Baden Museum places the grand hotels about a mile apart, both of them grown up around mineral springs in the 1840s. Bird's life splits across that mile. CNN records his birth in West Baden on December 7, 1956, and his childhood in French Lick. The nickname that stuck is the Hick from French Lick. Larry Legend is the other one.

The childhood house is the corner place at 983 S Washington Street. A 2019 hometown tour, still carrying that address, puts it at South Washington and South Jefferson, in the southeast part of town near State Road 145, about five minutes from the French Lick Springs Hotel. The French Lick resort's own March 2022 guide, updated in October 2024, sends visitors to the same house by map. The home and garage have been updated. The original basketball hoop is gone. The later owners put up a wooden backboard so the driveway still reads as a place where a player worked. Picture the jumpers from the street. The driveway belongs to the people who live there.

That is the whole visit to the house: a curb, a garage, a backboard. There is no ticket window and no interior tour. Pull over, take the photograph from the public right of way, and leave the driveway and the porch alone. If a mapping app offers a different Washington Street, put in French Lick and 47432 before you start.

French Lick, West Baden, and a basketball education

Springs Valley High School is about a two-minute drive or a ten-minute walk from the house, on Larry Bird Boulevard. The local tour remembers him shooting 200 free throws before school. The resort guide describes the gym as a sunken bowl, the kind southern Indiana schools built for a packed Friday night. When the doors are open, or when Springs Valley has a home game, the 1974 team photograph is there: Bird's senior year, the regular season undefeated, 31 points and 21 rebounds a game. His number 33 jerseys hang in that gym. He kept 33 at Indiana State and with the Celtics. He scored 1,125 points in that gym before Indiana State and the Celtics.

Outside, the Springs Valley Teen Center sits at State Road 145 and Larry Bird Boulevard, with outdoor hoops and a bust of Bird. The boulevard sign is the easy photograph, close enough to the resort that the French Lick Springs Hotel and the casino show up behind it. These are the public pieces of a Larry Bird day. The house is the private one.

The path from that gym is short on a map and long in basketball. He attended Indiana University in 1974 and left after 24 days. He finished at Indiana State in 1979. On March 26, 1979, Indiana State played Magic Johnson's Michigan State team for the NCAA championship, the game that started their rivalry. That year he won the Naismith Award and the John Wooden Award. The Celtics had drafted him in the first round in 1978 and waited while he graduated. He debuted on October 12, 1979, was NBA Rookie of the Year in 1980, and won his first NBA title with Boston in 1981. He was a three-time NBA champion, the MVP in 1984, 1985, and 1986, and a two-time Finals MVP. In 1992 he won Olympic gold with the Dream Team, Michael Jordan and Johnson beside him, and retired on August 18, 1992. The Celtics retired number 33 on February 4, 1993, at the Boston Garden. The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame inducted him on October 2, 1998.

French Lick kept the story at street level. In the early 1980s he hosted a basketball camp at the West Baden Springs Hotel and helped set up the portable goals. The French Lick West Baden Museum, at 469 S Maple Street, Suite 103, French Lick, IN 47432, keeps a Larry Bird exhibit: jerseys and a wooden backboard from the old Springs Valley gym, and the Hick from French Lick told as a hometown career. The museum phone is 812-936-3592. Downtown, 33 Brick Street Bar and Grill displays memorabilia, including an Olympic warmup jacket the staff will bring out if you ask.

A later house, not this one

After the NBA money arrived, Bird built a different house: a 20-acre place in West Baden Springs. In 2007 the owners of a Comfort Suites bought it and turned it into a small resort, the Legend of French Lick, at 7328 W County Road 100 N, West Baden Springs, IN 47469. The basketball court there was used in the movie Blue Chips and in a Converse commercial with Magic Johnson. Bird and Johnson filmed a Converse commercial together in Indiana in 1985. That estate is a lodging stop. The childhood landmark is still the corner house on South Washington Street in French Lick. Do not trade one address for the other.

How to spend the stop

Start at the curb of 983 S Washington Street and stay there. Then use the public stops in order: the Larry Bird Boulevard sign, the bust and hoops at the Springs Valley Teen Center, the high school gym if a game or an open door lets you in, the museum exhibit on Maple Street, and 33 Brick Street if you want the jacket and the memorabilia. The West Baden Springs Hotel is the camp story, the neighboring resort, not the childhood address. The other French Lick guide on this site is Otis at French Lick Cabins, a wooden figure at the cabins, not a Bird stop. The state list is the Indiana landmark directory.

Questions

Where is Larry Bird's childhood home?

It is a private house at 983 S Washington St, French Lick, IN 47432, on the corner of South Jefferson Street. See the garage from the public street. The house is not open for tours.

Where was Larry Bird born, and where did he go to school?

Larry Joe Bird was born on December 7, 1956, in West Baden, Indiana, and grew up in French Lick. He played at Springs Valley High School. In his senior year, 1974, he averaged 31 points and 21 rebounds, and the team went undefeated in the regular season.

Can you tour Larry Bird's childhood home?

No. Later owners renovated the house and garage. The visit is a look from the curb at the wooden backboard on the garage. Public Bird stops in town are Larry Bird Boulevard, the bust at the Springs Valley Teen Center, the high school gym when it is open, and the French Lick West Baden Museum.

Why is he called the Hick from French Lick?

French Lick is the small southern Indiana town where he grew up, next to West Baden. The nickname Hick from French Lick, and Larry Legend, followed him from that town to Indiana State and the Boston Celtics, where he wore number 33.

What else in French Lick is tied to Larry Bird?

The French Lick West Baden Museum at 469 S Maple Street, Suite 103, has a Larry Bird exhibit. 33 Brick Street keeps memorabilia, including an Olympic warmup jacket. A later house he built, now the Legend of French Lick, is at 7328 W County Road 100 N in West Baden Springs, and it is a different place from the childhood home.
