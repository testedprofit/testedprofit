William Hayden English

Wikipedia’s biography of William Hayden English says identical statues of him stand in front of the Scott County Courthouse in Scottsburg and at the Crawford County Fairgrounds in English. The same encyclopedia’s list of public art in Indiana records a different pair of entries. Under English it lists William English, by John Mahoney, 1891, in English Park, bronze, the sculpture about 100 by 33 by 33 inches, owned by the Town of English. Under Scottsburg it lists William H. English, by John Mahoney, about 1894, at the Scott County Courthouse, bronze, the sculpture about 8 feet by 38 inches by 30 inches, owned by the Scott County Commissioners. This guide does not treat those measurements as the same figure, and it does not choose between English Park and the fairgrounds.

The Encyclopedia of Indianapolis entry for sculptor John H. Mahoney says his work includes William E. English, 1891, with castings for the cities of English and Scottsburg. That name, William E. English, is the form in the sculptor’s entry. The biography and the public-art list use William Hayden English, William English, and William H. English. All three wordings stay, because the loaded pages do not agree.

The biography says English was born on 27 August 1822 in Lexington, Indiana, the son of Elisha Gale English and Mahala Eastin English, and that he died at his Indianapolis home on 7 February 1896. He was Speaker of the Indiana House from 8 March 1852 to 21 June 1852, then served in the U.S. House from 4 March 1853 to 3 March 1861. In 1880 he was the Democratic nominee for vice president, with Winfield Scott Hancock, and they lost to James A. Garfield and Chester A. Arthur. He wrote Conquest of the Country Northwest of the River Ohio, 1778–1783, and a life of George Rogers Clark. English, Indiana, the Crawford County seat, is named for him.

The town’s own Wikipedia article says the settlement was laid out in 1839 as Hartford and was renamed English in 1884, when it incorporated, for William Hayden English. The county seat moved from Leavenworth to English on 28 December 1893. From 1959 to 1990 the town had six floods, and a major part of it was relocated to higher ground. The pages that loaded do not put the statue on a courthouse lawn at a Church Street address, and they do not call the base granite. A Smithsonian inventory page for the sculpture returned HTTP 403, so its pose and base notes are not added.

Questions

Where do the loaded pages place the statue?

The biography says the Crawford County Fairgrounds in English and the Scott County Courthouse in Scottsburg. The public-art list says English Park in 1891 and the Scottsburg courthouse about 1894. Both accounts are kept.

Who was William Hayden English?

Wikipedia says he was born in Lexington in 1822, served in Congress from 1853 to 1861, and was the 1880 Democratic nominee for vice president. The town of English was renamed for him in 1884.
