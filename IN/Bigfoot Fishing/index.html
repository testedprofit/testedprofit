French Lick, Indiana

Otis

25-foot fishing sasquatch

Otis is still at French Lick Cabins at Patoka Lake. Bear Hollow Wood Carvers finished the 25-foot wooden sasquatch in April 2023, and a visitor on August 9, 2025, found him in great shape. He sits on a dock with a fishing pole and a red-and-white bobber, on the cabin grounds at 7900 West County Road 1025 South, French Lick, Indiana 47432. School buses, motorcycles, and Jeeps started showing up as soon as the photographs spread. In 2024 he was on the cover of the official Indiana Travel Guide.

How Bear Hollow built him

Jason Emmons, carver and co-owner of Bear Hollow with Cindy Emmons, wanted a figure that told a lake story. The cabins sit by Patoka Lake, so he pictured a relaxed, smiling sasquatch dipping a line. The name Otis points to the Otis Redding song about sitting on the dock of a bay. The carved dock bows under the weight. A red-and-white bobber hangs from the pole. From the back, the wooden seat is cut to look as if it has cracked under him.

The planning took about six months. The carving on site was fast. Nine people from the Bear Hollow team, Jason and his son Ethan among them, worked the wood in place. Limestone Post, on May 24, 2024, put that work at about four days and the finish in April 2023. Travel Indiana, on April 18, 2023, while the news was new, counted five days for the same 25-foot build by the same team of nine, at French Lick Cabins at Patoka Lake Village. Either count, the chainsaw work was the short part. The design was the long part.

The frame is treated lumber from Paoli Bender Lumber. Twelve bundles of poplar sawmill slabs became the fur, layered so the body reads as shag instead of a smooth log. Large solid white-pine logs became the face and the feet, the parts that need a clean plane for eyes, a smile, and toes. That mix is why he looks assembled and carved at once: a lumber skeleton, a pelt of mill offcuts, and a pine head you can read from the road.

Cindy and Jason kept the build quiet until the last piece of wood went on. A car pulled up with the windows down and a child in the back said whoa. After that, the visits were not quiet. People came in school buses and in lines of motorcycles and Jeeps. Photographs moved on social media faster than the Emmonses expected. Jason said they thought it was a big deal and still did not know how people would react, and that once it hit Facebook it was overwhelming.

Visit Indiana put him on the cover of the official 2024 Indiana Travel Guide, seated on the dock with a fishing pole and a sunset behind him. Amy Howell, of the Indiana Destination Development Corporation, said the office looked through a large stack of photographs and chose Otis because he was a surprise and because the area already promotes with the state. Kristal Painter, of Discover Southern Indiana, said people were coming to the region just to see him. Cindy Emmons said drivers from Cincinnati who were not staying the night still stopped for gas or a meal. Jason said Patoka Lake Winery keeps sending people over.

A Bigfoot you can photograph

Bigfoot, also called sasquatch, is a figure in American and Canadian folklore. Since the middle of the twentieth century the name has been a popular-culture icon of its own, with clubs, festivals, and a shelf of claimed sightings, footprints, and films. Scientists treat that file as folklore, misidentification, and hoax, not as evidence of an undiscovered ape in the woods. The roadside version does not need the argument. A town or a carver builds a giant, gives it a pose, and lets you stand next to it. The mystery becomes a photograph.

Indiana already keeps a habit of oversized things. Alexandria has promoted a giant ball of paint. Otis joins that roadside list from Orange County, with a fishing story instead of a contest of size alone. He is not a report of a creature in the Hoosier National Forest. He is chainsaw art on a cabin lot, built to be seen from a car and then from a few steps away. The smile and the pole are the joke and the welcome. You are supposed to recognize the dock song and the lake, not file a sighting.

Other states on this site have their own giants, and they are not the same carving. The Bigfoot statue in Evergreen, Alabama, the 14-foot sasquatch at Armco Park in Kentucky, and the Bonanza Bigfoot on the Lost River in Oregon are separate roadside figures. Otis is the Indiana one: wood, a pole, a bobber, and a dock at Patoka Lake.

How to see him

The grounds are a cabin rental, Patoka Lake Village, south of French Lick and beside the lake. From State Road 145, just north of the bridge over Patoka Lake, turn east onto County Road 1025 South. Otis is on the left about a quarter-mile in. The address is 7900 West County Road 1025 South, French Lick, Indiana 47432.

Park so you are not in a guest's way. The figure is the stop, and the cabins are someone's lodging. Walk up, take the photograph, and leave the wood alone. Do not climb the fur, the pole, or the dock. A visitor note from August 9, 2025, also mentions a giant squirrel carving nearby, and a note from September 21, 2024, says the property holds many chainsaw pieces besides Otis. If you only have ten minutes, Otis is the one. If you have twenty, look at the rest without wandering into a rented cabin.

Bear Hollow is more than this one figure. Jason and Cindy Emmons also run a retail shop in downtown French Lick and French Licks, a coffee and ice cream shop. The carving team works about 300 show dates a year around the country. In April 2024 they built a second sasquatch, at the Mercer County Fairgrounds in Ohio. That one is not Otis. Otis stays at the cabins.

Orange County's other draws sit close enough for the same day. The French Lick Resort has the historic hotels, golf, and a casino. The French Lick Scenic Railway runs trains from town. Big Splash Adventure and Wilstem Wildlife Park are the water park and the drive-through animal stop the same article lists. In 2022, visitor spending in the county totaled $125 million, and tourism supported nearly 1,300 jobs. Otis is one more reason for a day trip, which is how Cindy described the gas-and-a-meal visitors.

The other French Lick guide here is Larry Bird's childhood home, a private house in town, not a cabin carving. The state list is the Indiana landmark directory. For a different river town, the Vincennes riverwalk is on the Wabash, and the Rosie the Riveter statue is in Clarksville.

Questions

Who made Otis, and how tall is he?

Bear Hollow Wood Carvers made him. Jason and Cindy Emmons own the shop. Nine carvers, including Jason and his son Ethan, worked on site. Otis is 25 feet tall. They finished him in April 2023 after about four days of carving. A note published April 18, 2023, counted five days.

What is Otis made of?

A treated-lumber frame from Paoli Bender Lumber, poplar sawmill slabs for the fur, and solid white-pine logs for the face and feet. He sits on a dock that bows under his weight, with a fishing pole and a red-and-white bobber.

Where is Otis, and is he still there?

At French Lick Cabins at Patoka Lake, 7900 West County Road 1025 South, French Lick, Indiana 47432. A visitor on August 9, 2025, found him in great shape. The cabins are a lodging property. Look and photograph, and leave the carving and the guests alone. Do not climb him.

Why is he called Otis?

Jason Emmons wanted a relaxed, smiling sasquatch fishing, because the cabins sit by Patoka Lake. The name points to the Otis Redding song about sitting on the dock of a bay. The carved dock bows, and a bobber hangs from the pole.
