Otis

Limestone Post, in a 24 May 2024 article that first appeared in the Southern Indiana Business Report, describes Otis as a 25-foot-tall sasquatch at French Lick Cabins at Patoka Lake. The Bear Hollow carving team finished him in April 2023. The article says nine carvers, including Jason Emmons and his son Ethan, built him on site in about four days, after about six months of planning. Travel Indiana’s note of 18 April 2023 also says a team of nine built a 25-foot sasquatch, and it says the work took five days. This guide keeps about four days and five days side by side.

The Limestone Post account says construction started with a frame of treated lumber from Paoli Bender Lumber. Twelve bundles of sawmill slabs of poplar became the fur. Large solid white-pine logs became the face and the feet. Jason Emmons, carver and co-owner of Bear Hollow with Cindy Emmons, pictured a relaxed, smiling sasquatch dipping a fishing pole in the lake because the cabins sit near Patoka Lake. The article names him for the Otis Redding line about sitting on the dock of a bay. It says the dock bows under his weight and a red-and-white bobber hangs from the pole.

Those pages place the carving at French Lick Cabins at Patoka Lake, also called Patoka Lake Village in the Travel Indiana note. They do not print a street number, and they do not name an artist called Brad Hadlock. That name is not repeated. The Business Report page that Limestone Post credits did not open for this check, so it is named and not linked. The Bear Hollow site that loaded did not describe Otis, so it is not used as a source for the size or the date.

The articles do not print sunrise-to-sunset hours or a 24-hour line. The place they do name is the cabin property at Patoka Lake, a short drive from the French Lick resorts.

Another French Lick page is Larry Bird in French Lick.

Questions

Who made Otis, and how tall is it?

Limestone Post and Travel Indiana both say the Bear Hollow team made a 25-foot sasquatch at French Lick Cabins. Limestone Post dates the finish to April 2023 and says about four days. Travel Indiana says five days.

What is it made of?

Limestone Post says a treated-lumber frame, poplar slabs for the fur, and white-pine logs for the face and feet. It describes a fishing pole, a bowing dock, and a red-and-white bobber.
