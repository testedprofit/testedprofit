Paul Bunyan and Gladwin

Wikipedia says Paul Bunyan is a giant lumberjack and folk hero in American and Canadian folklore, and that the character first appeared in print in 1893 after an earlier life in the oral tradition of North American loggers. The first known printed reference, the article says, appeared in the March 17, 1893 issue of the Gladwin County Record. Under the local news for Beaverton, it reads, "Paul Bunion [sic] is getting ready while the water is high to take his drive out." Wikipedia calls the line an inside joke that appeared more than fifteen years before any commercial use of the name, at a time when few of the general public would have known who Paul Bunyan was.

The same article says freelance writer William B. Laughead popularized the character in a 1916 promotional pamphlet for the Red River Lumber Company. Its list of places claimed as a birthplace includes Manistique and Oscoda in Michigan, along with towns in Minnesota, Wisconsin, Maine, and Halifax, Nova Scotia. The Gladwin County line is the print reference the article dates to 1893.

WCMU's 2023 account of northern Michigan describes statues of Paul Bunyan made of materials from concrete and chicken wire to massive wood carvings. It says that for 64 years a 16-foot statue of concrete and chicken wire, Paul Bunyan with his blue ox Babe, had welcomed people to Castle Rock in St. Ignace, and that Mark Eby's uncle built that figure in the late 1950s. Farther south, the story describes a 13-foot statue in Oscoda, blocks from downtown, with an axe in one hand and a tree in the other. It also describes a statue made of auto parts at Alpena Community College. Fred Glass, president of the AuSable-Oscoda Historical Society, tells WCMU that James MacGillivray wrote Round River for the Oscoda Press in 1906, and the story says Encyclopedia Britannica and the American Society for Environmental History treat that as the first time the name appeared in print. Wikipedia identifies the 1893 Gladwin County Record line as the first known print reference. The two accounts stand side by side.

The Oscoda figure has its own guide: Furtaw Field in Downtown Oscoda. WCMU says the Oscoda Au-Sable Chamber of Commerce has organized Paul Bunyan Days each September since the early 1970s, with chainsaw carving and a beard contest.
