Paul Bunyan carving

The Paul Bunyan carving is a roadside landmark in Gladwin, Michigan. It is the figure travelers look up when they come to Gladwin for the carving.

Gladwin also has the earliest print line Wikipedia records for the name. The article says the first known printed reference appeared in the March 17, 1893 issue of the Gladwin County Record. Under the local news for Beaverton, it reads, "Paul Bunion [sic] is getting ready while the water is high to take his drive out." Wikipedia calls the line an inside joke that appeared more than fifteen years before any commercial use of the name. Freelance writer William B. Laughead later popularized the character in a 1916 promotional pamphlet for the Red River Lumber Company. Wikipedia's list of places claimed as a birthplace includes Manistique and Oscoda in Michigan.

Other Michigan figures are separate roadside landmarks. WCMU's 2023 account describes a 13-foot statue in Oscoda, blocks from downtown, with an axe in one hand and a tree in the other, and a 16-foot concrete and chicken-wire figure of Paul Bunyan and Babe at Castle Rock in St. Ignace. Fred Glass, president of the AuSable-Oscoda Historical Society, tells WCMU that James MacGillivray wrote Round River for the Oscoda Press in 1906. Wikipedia identifies the 1893 Gladwin County Record line as the first known print reference. The Gladwin carving is the landmark in Gladwin. The Oscoda statue is a different stop.

The Oscoda figure has its own guide: Furtaw Field in Downtown Oscoda.
