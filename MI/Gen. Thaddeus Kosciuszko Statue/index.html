Kosciuszko monument

Wayne State University's Ethnic Layers of Detroit project describes the monument of Thaddeus Kosciuszko as one of the largest in the city. It says he was a Polish patriot born in 1746 in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, trained as a military engineer in Poland and then in France. In 1776 he volunteered for the American Revolutionary War. During seven years of service he built fortifications, the most famous at Saratoga and West Point, and in 1783 Congress promoted him to brigadier general. He later led the unsuccessful 1794 uprising in Poland. He died in Switzerland in 1817. His body was buried in Wawel Cathedral in Krakow. The project says his will ordered that his American estate be sold to help enslaved people start new lives, and that those wishes were not carried out.

The same project says the Detroit monument is a replica of the monument standing by Wawel Castle. It says the Krakow monument was erected in 1900, destroyed by the Nazis in 1940, and after the war recreated and given to the city by Dresden in 1960. The Detroit monument, the project says, was a gift the people of Krakow made to Detroit in 1978 to commemorate the United States Bicentennial of 1976. A commemorative plaque is in Polish and English.

A Detroit history page places the monument at Third Street and Michigan Avenue in downtown Detroit. It names the sculptor as the Warsaw-born Leonard Marconi, dates the original casting to 1889 for the grounds of Wawel Castle, and dates this copy to 1978. It lists the materials as granite and bronze. Both describe a copy of the Krakow monument given to Detroit in 1978. The history page adds the corner of Third and Michigan, the name Leonard Marconi, and the granite and bronze.

Another Detroit monument in this directory is the Schiller monument on Belle Isle.

Questions

Where is the Detroit monument, and who made the original?

A Detroit history page places the monument at Third Street and Michigan Avenue and credits the Warsaw-born sculptor Leonard Marconi. It dates the original casting to 1889 and this copy to 1978, in granite and bronze.

Who gave the monument to Detroit?

Wayne State's Ethnic Layers of Detroit project says the Detroit monument is a replica of the one by Wawel Castle in Krakow, and that the people of Krakow gave it to Detroit in 1978 for the United States Bicentennial of 1976.
