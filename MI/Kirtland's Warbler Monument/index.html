Kirtland's warbler monument

A Michigan Department of Natural Resources bulletin, written for the department's centennial, describes a drive down M-33 through Mio in northern Michigan. It says most visitors pass the monuments and memorials on the lawn of the county courthouse. Among the military memorials, the bulletin says, is an edifice of stone and mortar that contains the effigy of a brilliant yellow and slaty gray songbird. The department calls it a monument to the scientists, conservationists, and citizens who worked to save the Kirtland's warbler.

The bulletin says the scientific record reports that the bird was first discovered in the United States in 1851, that its wintering grounds were discovered in 1879, and that its nesting range was discovered in 1903. It describes a small songbird with lemon-yellow underparts, a slaty gray head and back, and black speckles. The birds nest on the ground in thick, patchy jack pine and overwinter in the Caribbean. Logging in the 1800s let brown-headed cowbirds move in and lay eggs in warbler nests, and fire suppression kept the young jack pine stands from regenerating. A 1951 count reached 1,000 birds. By 1958, core conservation areas had been identified on state-managed lands in Ogemaw, Crawford, and Oscoda counties.

The bulletin says the Kirtland's warbler is now off the endangered species list, and that its population has grown to over 4,500 birds across Michigan, Wisconsin, and Ontario. The courthouse lawn in Mio is the place the department points to for the stone monument. The habitat work it describes is the larger recovery across those jack pine counties.

Questions

Where is the monument?

The Michigan Department of Natural Resources describes a stone-and-mortar monument with the figure of a Kirtland's warbler on the lawn of the county courthouse in Mio, along M-33.

What does the department say about the bird?

The department says the Kirtland's warbler is off the endangered species list, with a population of over 4,500 birds across Michigan, Wisconsin, and Ontario. It nests on the ground in young jack pine and winters in the Caribbean.
