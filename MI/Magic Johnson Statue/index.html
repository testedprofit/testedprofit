Magic Johnson statue

Michigan State Athletics reported that on November 1, 2003, hundreds of fans watched Earvin Magic Johnson pull the cover off a 12-foot bronze statue of himself as a Michigan State basketball player. Johnson, teammates from the Spartans' 1979 championship team, and university officials dedicated the statue in front of the Breslin Center. The story says the figure holds a basketball in one hand and uses the other hand to direct other players. It says Johnson helped Michigan State win the NCAA championship in 1979 as a sophomore, then played in the NBA, where his team won five titles and he was named most valuable player three times. The $250,000 statue was funded by private donations. Michigan State says it was designed by Highland Park, Illinois designer Omri Amrany and is titled Always a Champion. The dedication came before that day's football game at Spartan Stadium and opened a season-long celebration of the 25th anniversary of the 1979 championship. Jud Heathcote, who coached that team, spoke.

The Rotblatt Amrany Studio page describes the same East Lansing figure, dated 2003, for client Michigan State University, and lists the artists as Gary Tillery and Omri Amrany. The studio says the figure is captured in a charging position, fingers guiding teammates, in the no-look pass stance. It says the patina of the jersey suggests the color of the Spartans' uniforms while keeping a bronze finish. Four abstract objects in the base commemorate the Michigan State championship, his years in the NBA, his role in All-Star games, and the 1992 United States Olympic basketball team.

The athletics story gives the ceremony, the 12-foot bronze, the Breslin Center, the $250,000 cost, and the title Always a Champion. The studio page adds Gary Tillery, the no-look pose, and the four objects in the base.

When was the statue dedicated, and how tall is it?

Michigan State Athletics says Earvin Magic Johnson unveiled a 12-foot bronze statue of himself in front of the Breslin Center on November 1, 2003. The statue cost $250,000 and was funded by private donations.

Who made it, and what is it called?

Michigan State says Highland Park, Illinois designer Omri Amrany designed the statue and that it is titled Always a Champion. The Rotblatt Amrany Studio page lists Gary Tillery and Omri Amrany as the artists and describes a charging, no-look pass.
