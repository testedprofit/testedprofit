Sculpture in the Park

Wikipedia describes Sculpture in the Park as a non-profit sculpture garden on Arrowhead Point in eastern Ottawa Hills, Ohio, at the five-way intersection of Secor Road, Bancroft Street, and Indian Road. It was created in 2004. The operator is the Ottawa Hills Foundation. The article says the sculptures are large enough to be easily visible from the street, and that the selection has a great deal of variety. It says thousands of cars pass the spot daily.

On the founding, Wikipedia says Peggy Grant got the idea while driving past the empty grass. The plan for the summer of 2004 called for ten sculptures. The Ottawa Hills Foundation, which sponsors the exhibit, started with six. Many sculptures come and go. The foundation purchased three to stay permanently, including a butterfly sculpture as a tribute to former Ottawa Hills mayor Jean Youngen, who died in 2004.

A January 2009 newsletter of the University of Toledo libraries, in a biography of Peggy Grant, says she organized Sculpture in the Park, an outdoor sculpture exhibit in the triangular park at the Indian, Bancroft, and Secor Road intersection at the entrance of Ottawa Hills. The same biography says she had served as art director of 20 North Gallery in downtown Toledo for the last fifteen years, that the gallery had recently celebrated its 100th exhibit, and that her honors included the Toledo Botanical Garden Crosby Award and the Milestones Award for the Arts from the Toledo YWCA. Wikipedia calls the ground Arrowhead Point. The university biography calls it the triangular park at the intersection.

Questions

Where is Sculpture in the Park?

Wikipedia places the garden on Arrowhead Point in eastern Ottawa Hills, Ohio, at the five-way intersection of Secor Road, Bancroft Street, and Indian Road. The University of Toledo biography calls it the triangular park at that intersection, at the entrance of Ottawa Hills.

When did it start, and what stayed?

Wikipedia says the garden was created in 2004 by the Ottawa Hills Foundation, which started with six sculptures after planning for ten. The foundation purchased three to stay, including a butterfly sculpture in tribute to former mayor Jean Youngen, who died in 2004.
