Woodbridge Ferris statue

The Ferris State University art walk lists the Woodbridge N. Ferris statue as a statue of the founder of the university. It names the artist Avard Fairbanks and places the work on the Big Rapids campus, with the campus address 1201 South State Street, Big Rapids, Michigan 49307.

The university's alumni history of the statue says the project gained speed after the May 1982 graduation, when a conversation with Stanford Stoddard led to a centennial sculpture. Stoddard and Robert Gerholz raised nearly $13,000 to start the fundraising, and Stoddard suggested Avard Fairbanks, a sculptor from Salt Lake City. President Robert L. Ewigleben approved negotiations. Fairbanks received photographs and other material on W. N. Ferris and prepared a proposal. In August 1982 a representative traveled to Utah to meet him. A formal agreement followed, and Fairbanks began work. During the 1982-83 academic year a campus campaign raised nearly $38,000 by July 12, 1983, from students, alumni, faculty, staff, and friends. The history says the statue site was finalized and approved in July, and work began to prepare the grounds for installation and the dedication ceremony.

A Ferris State Founders Day calendar lists the dedication of the W. N. Ferris statue in 1983, beside the 1884 founding of the Big Rapids Industrial School. The art walk names Avard Fairbanks and the Big Rapids campus. The alumni history follows the commission, the fundraising, and the July 1983 site work. The calendar records the dedication in 1983.

Who made the Ferris statue, and where is it?

The Ferris State art walk calls it a statue of the founder of Ferris State University and names the artist Avard Fairbanks, on the Big Rapids campus at 1201 South State Street.

When was it dedicated?

A Ferris State Founders Day calendar lists the dedication of the W. N. Ferris statue in 1983. The alumni history says the site was approved in July 1983, after nearly $38,000 had been raised by July 12.
