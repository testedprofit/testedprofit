Lion statue on Center Avenue

The lion statue is a roadside landmark on Center Avenue in Bay City. Travelers look for it along that street, in the residential neighborhood the lumber families built.

Wikipedia describes the Center Avenue Neighborhood Residential District as a residential historic district in Bay City. It runs primarily along Center, Fifth, and Sixth Avenues between Monroe and Green Avenues, with further portions along Fourth between Madison and Johnson, down to Tenth Avenue between Madison and Lincoln, along Green to Ridge, and around Carroll Park. The original section, along Center and portions of Fifth and Sixth, was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1982. A boundary increase covering the other sections was listed in 2012. The district contains 936 buildings, of which 879 contribute to its historic character.

The same article follows the street back to the lumber years. The first sawmill in Bay City was built in 1837. By 1854 a dozen mills were operating, and by 1860 more than eight hundred people lived in the city. By 1870 the population was 7,000. The area had been platted in 1837, and development along Center Avenue came in the 1870s and 1880s, when wealthy families, mostly in the lumber and salt trade, built there. After the turn of the century lumbering declined, and agriculture, iron, and textiles took its place. Newer houses continued the street until the Great Depression. In 2005 neighbors began meeting about preservation, and from those meetings the Center Avenue Neighborhood Association was formed.

Wikipedia lists the styles along the street, from Victorian Italianate, Stick, Queen Anne, Richardsonian Romanesque, and Châteauesque houses of the late nineteenth century to Georgian Revival, Renaissance Revival, Prairie Style, Arts and Crafts, Bungalow, Moderne, and mid-century houses. Among the residences it names are the James Shearer house at 701 Center Avenue, an Italianate residence of 1876; the Frederick Bradley house at 1400 Center Avenue, a Queen Anne house of 1887-89; and the Charles Curtiss house at 924 Center Avenue, a sandstone and wood Queen Anne house of 1891. Public buildings include the Bay City Public Library at 708 Center Avenue, a Carnegie library of 1922, and Trinity Episcopal Church at 911 Center Avenue, built in 1887 from a Gothic Revival design by Philip C. Floeter. A Bay City guide says a state historical marker records about one billion board feet of lumber from the Saginaw region in 1882, and 36 mills by 1872 between Bay City and the mouth of the river. The lion statue is the roadside landmark on that avenue.
