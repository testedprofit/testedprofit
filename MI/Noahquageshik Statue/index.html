Noahquageshik statue

Grand Valley State University reported on August 10, 2010, that a new sculpture in philanthropist Peter Secchia's Community Legends Project had been unveiled on the Pew Grand Rapids Campus. The Noahquageshik statue, also known as Chief Noonday, was installed at the Eberhard Center, just west of the Blue Pedestrian Bridge, and belongs to Grand Valley. The university said it was the second of 25 sculptures being donated to the city or a nonprofit, with a goal of one sculpture every two years for 50 years. President Thomas J. Haas thanked the Secchia family.

GVSU says artist Antonio Tobias Mendez, of Knoxville, Maryland, created the seven-foot bronze, which weighs 800 pounds and sits on a three-foot square base. A plaque says Noahquageshik, about 1770 to 1840, was an influential leader of the Grand River Ottawa Anishinabe, the Original People, who led bands throughout the Grand River basin, including the Bowtink village very near the sculpture. Mendez has made other public monuments, including figures of Thurgood Marshall, Mohandas Gandhi, and Arthur Vandenberg. For this figure he reviewed historical documents and consulted the Native American community, the Grand Rapids Public Museum, the Ziibiwing Center of Anishinabe Culture and Lifeways in Mount Pleasant, and the National Museum of American History. He said he wanted accuracy in every detail, from the ceremonial pipe to the split-feather headdress of this region.

Downtown Grand Rapids lists the same work as a 7-foot bronze from 2010 by Antonio Tobias Mendez, in the Grand Rapids Community Legends series, honoring Chief Noonday of the Grand River Band of Ottawa. It gives the directions as 50 Front Avenue Southwest. GVSU places the bronze at the Eberhard Center and gives the weight, the base, and the plaque. Downtown Grand Rapids adds the street address, 50 Front Avenue Southwest.

A second Grand River figure in this directory is the Verlen Kruger statue in Portland.

Questions

Who made the statue, and how large is it?

Grand Valley State University says artist Antonio Tobias Mendez, of Knoxville, Maryland, created a seven-foot bronze weighing 800 pounds, on a three-foot square base. Downtown Grand Rapids also calls it a 7-foot bronze from 2010.

Where does it stand, and who was Noahquageshik?

GVSU says the statue was installed at the Eberhard Center, just west of the Blue Pedestrian Bridge, in 2010. A plaque on the sculpture says Noahquageshik, about 1770 to 1840, led Grand River Ottawa bands, including the Bowtink village near the statue.
