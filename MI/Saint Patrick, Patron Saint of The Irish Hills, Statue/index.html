Saint Patrick statue

The Saint Patrick statue is a roadside landmark in Michigan's Irish Hills. It stands in the rolling country around Onsted, in Lenawee County.

The Village of Onsted's history places the village in the heart of the Irish Hills and says the village itself was mapped in February 1883, when William Onsted, J. Edwards Gibbs, and Charles Greenleaf laid it out beside the railroad. The same history says Irish Catholic settlers organized St. Joseph Church in 1854, built largely from locally gathered fieldstone. It calls Onsted a gateway to the Irish Hills, and it follows the old Chicago Road, now U.S. Highway 12, through the region.

Wikipedia places Saint Joseph Church and Shrine at 8742 U.S. Route 12 in rural Cambridge Township. It says the district was designated a Michigan Historic Site on April 19, 1990, and was added to the National Register of Historic Places on May 4, 2007. Irish Catholics had come into the area from the 1830s. Humphrey and Ann Agan, and Martin Kelly, deeded the church site to the Bishop of Detroit. Work began in 1854 and continued until 1862. The tower, sacristy, and stained glass were added in 1911. A new roof, transept, and sanctuary were added in 1928. In 1932 the shrine was completed under Leo Ouelette, Dionicio Rodriguez, and Ralph Corona. The church is the historic parish of these hills. The Saint Patrick statue is the roadside landmark in that same countryside.
