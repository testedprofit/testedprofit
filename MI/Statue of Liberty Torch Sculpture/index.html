Liberty torch in Rogers City

Lost in Michigan describes the Torch of Freedom as a sculpture of an arm holding a torch, near the marina in Rogers City. It says Tom Moran of Moran Iron Works in Onaway created it. The sculpture originally shot flames from the torch, and those flames have since been replaced with a shiny, gold-like metal. Lost in Michigan says it debuted in Onaway's Fourth of July parade in 2004 and was then donated to Rogers City.

MIGenWeb reprints a caption from the Presque Isle County Advance and the Onaway Outlook of November 12, 2015, page 7B, under the headline that Moran's Liberty's Torch moved to Rogers City. The caption says Liberty's Torch made it to the shores of Lake Huron ten years ago that week. It arrived from Moran Iron Works in Onaway as a donation from Tom Moran, was delivered to Rogers City's Department of Public Works, and was prepared for display at Lakeside Park. The caption says anyone looking east on Erie Street can see the metal sculpture.

A second MIGenWeb page reprints a caption from the same newspapers dated July 3, 2024, page 9. That caption calls the work The Hand of Liberty, says Moran Iron Works fabricated it, and says it made its debut in the Fourth of July parade in Onaway in 2004. It says the sculpture now resides at Lakeside Park in Rogers City. Lost in Michigan uses the name Torch of Freedom and places it near the marina. The 2015 caption uses Liberty's Torch and names Lakeside Park and Erie Street. The 2024 caption uses The Hand of Liberty and repeats the 2004 parade and Lakeside Park.

Questions

Who made the torch, and where is it?

Lost in Michigan says Tom Moran of Moran Iron Works in Onaway created the Torch of Freedom near the marina in Rogers City. A 2015 newspaper caption republished by MIGenWeb says Liberty's Torch was prepared for display at Lakeside Park and can be seen looking east on Erie Street.

When was it first shown?

Lost in Michigan says it debuted in Onaway's Fourth of July parade in 2004 and was then donated to Rogers City. A MIGenWeb caption dated July 3, 2024 says The Hand of Liberty debuted in that 2004 parade and now resides at Lakeside Park.
