Boat rotor statue

The boat rotor statue is a roadside landmark near South Point, on the walk to Papakōlea Beach. A hike account of that green-sand beach describes a little sculpture that looks like a boat propeller, set on a hill to the left of the trail, and calls it a landmark on the way out and back.

The same account starts the walk at the end of South Point Road, off Highway 11. With your back to South Point Road, the trail is on the right. It begins as a paved road, with abandoned structures on the left. Around two-tenths of a mile the road curves right, and a dirt trail turns left toward the ocean. At the water, the trail climbs a hill. Two abandoned cars sit on the right, and the propeller sculpture is on top of the hill to the left. On the return, the account says to keep the ocean on your left until you reach the boat propeller statue, then turn right up the dirt hill to the parking lot. It also says driving the coastal road to the beach is illegal, and that the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands does not permit commercial vehicle access on that road.

The National Park Service places the South Point Complex at the southern tip of the Island of Hawaiʻi, on Ka Lae, 16 miles south of the town of Naalehu. It calls the point the southernmost point in the Hawaiian Islands and in the United States, and it lists the complex as a National Historic Landmark. The same page says Papakolea Beach is at South Point, colored by olivine crystals from the eroded Puʻu Mahana cinder cone, and that the beach is 2.5 miles east of Kalalea Heiau. Wikipedia spells the beach Papakōlea and places it about 3 miles east of Ka Lae, legally accessible on foot. The boat rotor statue is the sculpture on that approach.

Questions

Where is the boat rotor statue?

A hike account of Papakōlea places a sculpture that looks like a boat propeller on a hill beside the trail, about two-tenths of a mile from the start of the walk off South Point Road, and calls it a landmark on the way out and back.

What do the park pages say about South Point?

The National Park Service places the South Point Complex at Ka Lae, 16 miles south of Naalehu, and describes Papakolea Beach there as a green-sand beach colored by olivine.
