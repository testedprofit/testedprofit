Papakōlea Beach and South Point

What this URL names, and what the records describe

The address of this guide still says Boat Rotor Statue, because the URL is kept. The National Park Service page for the South Point Complex, the Wikipedia article on Papakōlea Beach, and the University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo recreation note on Green Sands Beach all describe the point and the beach. None of them names a boat rotor, a propeller, or a fishermen's memorial. The older page on this URL gave a plus code, called the object a critical visual anchor, and described an inscription. Those lines are not in the three pages that loaded, so they are left out. This guide does not invent a sculptor, a date, or a material for an object those pages do not document.

South Point

The National Park Service places the South Point Complex at the southern tip of the Island of Hawaiʻi, on Ka Lae, 16 miles south of the town of Naalehu. It calls the point the southernmost point in both the Hawaiian Islands and the United States, and it lists the complex as a National Historic Landmark. The same page says the waters off the point are very deep and dangerous, with strong currents, and that swimming is not advised in the South Point area. It also describes ancient mooring holes drilled through rock ledges so fishermen could hold canoes in those currents. That fishing history is the park service's account of the point. It is not a description of a modern propeller sculpture.

The green-sand beach, and the two distances

The park service says Papakolea Beach is also at South Point, one of only four green sand beaches in the world, colored by olivine crystals from the eroded Puʻu Mahana cinder cone, which it dates at 49,000 years. It says the beach is 2.5 miles east of Kalalea Heiau and can be reached by vehicle or by hiking along the shoreline. Wikipedia spells the beach Papakōlea, also Green Sand Beach or Mahana Beach, and says it is one of only two green sand beaches in the world. The same article places it about 3 miles east of Ka Lae and says it is only legally accessible by foot. It says the landowner, the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands, has attempted to restrict vehicular traffic since 2016, and that illegal driving has cut deep ruts. The University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo note places the beach in Mahana Bay near South Point and calls the walk a moderate hike that ends with a descent of the cinder cone. This guide keeps both the four-beach and two-beach counts, and both the 2.5-mile and about-3-mile distances. It also keeps the conflict between vehicle access and foot-only access. It does not tell a reader to drive the shoreline.

Questions

Does a public record describe a boat-rotor statue here?

The National Park Service South Point page, the Papakōlea Beach article, and the University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo beach note do not name a boat rotor or a propeller sculpture. This guide does not repeat the older inscription or plus code.

How do the pages say to reach Papakōlea?

The park service says the beach is 2.5 miles east of Kalalea Heiau and can be reached by vehicle or by hiking. Wikipedia says it is about 3 miles east of Ka Lae and only legally accessible by foot, and that the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands has tried to restrict vehicles since 2016.
