Spalding Monument

The Garden Island, in a 29 April 2018 column by Hank Soboleski, says dedication services for Kauai’s Spalding Monument were held on Sunday afternoon, 20 April 1930. K. Miyasaki and S. Takata chaired them. Japanese residents of Kealia had erected the monument for former Makee Sugar Co. owner Colonel Zephaniah S. Spalding, whose years the column gives as 1837 to 1927. The column says the monument was built of concrete and lava rock two miles above Kealia, at the junction of the old Government Road, now Kealia Road, and Valley House Road, now Hauaala Road.

Soboleski writes that two plaques remain. One carried a likeness and the other a birth and death inscription. He says both plaques are still there, but the likeness and the inscription have been defaced, perhaps since the 1990s. The column does not say the family removed them. Philip L. Rice was the principal speaker. The column says Rice became, in 1956, the last chief justice of the Supreme Court of the Territory of Hawaii. I. Araki gave a Japanese translation of Takata’s speech, and Spalding’s son Rufus thanked the Kealia Japanese.

In the account of Rice’s remarks, Captain James Makee founded Makee Sugar Co. in 1877, and after Makee’s death in 1878 Spalding bought the majority and became owner and manager. Rice said Spalding sold the controlling shares to Lihue Plantation in 1916 for $1,500,000. Takata, described as president of the Society of American Citizens of Japanese Ancestry of Kauai for 1925, said Spalding was born in Ohio, commanded the 27th Ohio Infantry in the Civil War, and in 1867 arrived in Hawaii as a secret agent of the United States regarding a proposed reciprocity treaty.

Wikipedia’s article on Zephaniah Swift Spalding gives a birth date of 2 September 1837 in Warren, Ohio, and a death date of 19 June 1927 in Pasadena, California, at age 89. It says he married Wilhelmina Harris Makee in 1871. On the 1916 sale, that article says the Makee Company was sold for an undisclosed sum and that speculators at the time believed the price was about $2,000,000. This guide reports Rice’s $1,500,000 and Wikipedia’s undisclosed figure of about $2,000,000 side by side. The monument is above Kealia, not on Kauai’s North Shore.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where is the monument, and what is it made of?

The Garden Island places it two miles above Kealia, at Kealia Road and Hauaala Road, and says it is concrete and lava rock. The dedication in that column is 20 April 1930.

What happened to the plaques?

The Garden Island says both plaques remain and that the likeness and the birth and death inscription have been defaced, perhaps since the 1990s.
