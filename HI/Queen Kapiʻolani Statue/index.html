Queen Kapiʻolani

Queen Kapiʻolani’s statue stands in Kapiʻolani Park, the Waikiki park named for her. The Honolulu Advertiser story posted on 2 January 2002 says Hawaiian community members paid tribute at a ceremony the previous Monday to unveil a statue in that park. Sculptor Holly Young made it. The story says the figure depicts the regent who married King David Kalakaua and reigned from 1874 to 1891. The story does not print a height, a costume, or an age.

The City and County of Honolulu park page gives her name as Queen Kapiʻolani Napelakapuokakaʻe and her life as 1834 to 1899. It calls her the queen and consort of King Kalākaua. It says the park’s dedication ceremonies were held on Kamehameha Day, 11 June 1877, and that the king named the park for her. On that page, Kapiʻolani means the Arch of Heaven. The same page says she reigned for seventeen years. It prints her motto as Kūlia I Ka Nuʻu, Strive for the Highest. It says a sculpture of her can be seen along Kalākaua Avenue near the bandstand, and that she lived out her life at Pualelani in Waikīkī, where the Hyatt Regency Waikīkī now stands.

A Historic Hawaii Foundation walking-tour sheet for statues in Waikīkī lists Queen Kapiʻolani, 1834 to 1899, married to King David Kalākaua, with artist Holly Young. The sheet places her at 2686 Kalākaua Avenue, erected in 2001 at Kapiʻolani Park and restored in 2019. It says the City and County of Honolulu, through the Mayor’s Office of Culture and the Arts, commissioned the work. The sheet’s research note credits Cheryl Soon and a 2022 Mutual Publishing book. The 2001 erection date and the Advertiser’s January 2002 unveiling story are both printed in those sources. This guide keeps both.

A separate guide covers the Kalākaua statue in Hilo Park.

Questions

Who made the statue, and when was it put up?

Holly Young is the artist on both the Advertiser story and the walking-tour sheet. The sheet says the statue was erected in 2001 at Kapiʻolani Park and restored in 2019. The Advertiser story posted on 2 January 2002 describes the unveiling as the previous Monday.

Where should a visitor look?

The city park page says a sculpture of Queen Kapiʻolani can be seen along Kalākaua Avenue near the bandstand. The walking-tour sheet lists 2686 Kalākaua Avenue.
