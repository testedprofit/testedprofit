Captain Cook monument

Where the monument stands

Kealakekua Bay State Historical Park, on the Island of Hawaiʻi, is the site the Division of State Parks ties to the arrival of Captain Cook in 1779. The park page says visitors can see Hikiau Heiau and the Captain Cook monument at a distance across the bay. It places the monument itself on the land at Kaʻawaloa Flats. The same page lists daily hours of 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. and no entrance fee. Restrooms, it says, are only on the Nāpōʻopoʻo section of the park. There are no restrooms on the trail to the monument, and the page asks hikers to pack out their trash.

How the state says to get there

The state parks page gives two ways onto Kaʻawaloa Flats: hike from the uplands on the historic Kaʻawaloa Trail, or take a guided kayak tour with one of the three commercial vendors the Division of State Parks has permitted. The vendor list on that page names two companies, Adventures in Paradise and Kona Boys, Inc., not three. This guide keeps both the number three in the sentence and the two names in the list. Individuals may transit the bay only in a vessel that already has a valid permit. Permitted vessels are prohibited from landing at Kaʻawaloa Flat and from launching at Nāpōʻopoʻo wharf. Someone who rents a kayak does not apply for a separate permit, but must confirm that the rented vessel has one. The Division of Aquatic Resources says the only access by car is to Nāpōʻopoʻo Beach, on the bay's eastern shoreline: Highway 11 south from Kailua-Kona to the Nāpōʻopoʻo turnoff, about 18 miles, then 4 miles to the bay. It says Kaʻawaloa Cove, at the bay's northern boundary, can be reached only by boat.

The 27-foot monument, and the dates that differ

The aquatic-resources page says Captain James Cook arrived at Kealakekua Bay in January 1779 and was killed the following month in a skirmish on the shores of Kaʻawaloa Cove. It says that in 1878 a 27-foot monument was erected in his honor by his countrymen near the site where he was killed, and that the ruins of the ancient village of Kaʻawaloa are on the lava flats behind the monument. The same page calls the bay a Marine Life Conservation District of 315 acres, established in 1969. Wikipedia's article on the bay says about 180 acres around the bay were designated Kealakekua Bay State Historical Park in 1967, and that the bay was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1973 as site 73000651. The article's infobox gives that historic district an area of 375 acres. The state parks description puts 4.0 acres in parentheses after the sentence about viewing the heiau and the monument from across the bay. This guide keeps 4.0, about 180, 315, and 375 as the four acre figures those pages print. It does not average them. The pages say 27 feet and 1878. They do not say white, obelisk, 1874, a British deed, or those trail figures.

Questions

How do visitors reach the Captain Cook monument?

State parks place it at Kaʻawaloa Flats and allow a hike on the Kaʻawaloa Trail or a guided kayak tour with a permitted vendor. Permitted vessels may not land there. The only car access the aquatic-resources page describes is to Nāpōʻopoʻo Beach, where the monument is across the bay.

How tall is the monument, and when was it erected?

The Division of Aquatic Resources says a 27-foot monument was erected in 1878 by Cook's countrymen near the place he was killed.
