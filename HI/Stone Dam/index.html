Kīlauea Stone Dam

Anaina Hou Community Park describes the Wai Koa Loop as a walk that leads to the historic Kīlauea Stone Dam, which it calls a beautiful 1880s landmark with cascading water. The northernmost trailhead on that page is the park itself. Hours there are 9:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. The page places the route on Kauai’s North Shore, through a shaded mahogany forest and open pastures. It says the loop is approximately 4.3 miles round trip, about 1.5 to 2 hours, beginner and intermediate, and mostly flat.

That page says a waiver must be completed before entering. The waiver is the form linked on the page, or a waiver at the trailhead. It says the trail is protected by the Hawaii Land Trust. It tells walkers to plan for dirt and mud, to bring water, and to bring bug spray because the land is agricultural. The park’s published address is 5-2723 Kuhio Highway, Kilauea, HI 96754, and its phone number is (808) 828-2118. A visitor comment on the page mentions a swim hole. That comment is not the organization’s description of the dam. The organization’s own line is cascading water.

Hawaii Land Trust’s trail page lists the Wai Koa Loop Trail at Kalihiwai, Haleleʻa, protected in 2019 by a conservation easement. Its features include a walking trail, a Kilauea stone dam built in the 1880s, mahogany trees, and a pond system. The trust’s Kauai lands page gives the size as a 5 mile hiking trail, also protected in 2019, and adds an organic orchard, a food forest, and catch-and-release fish ponds to the same list. This guide reports 4.3 miles from Anaina Hou and 5 miles from the trust side by side. Neither page calls the mahogany stand the largest in the United States, and neither names a dog park as the entrance.

The walk itself is covered in the Wai Koa Loop Trail guide.

Questions

What is the stone dam?

Anaina Hou calls it the historic Kīlauea Stone Dam, an 1880s landmark with cascading water, on the Wai Koa Loop. Hawaii Land Trust also dates a Kilauea stone dam on that trail to the 1880s.

Is a waiver required?

Anaina Hou says a waiver must be completed before entering, either through the form linked on its trail page or at the trailhead.
