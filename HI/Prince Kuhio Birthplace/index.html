Prince Kūhiō Park

The birthplace the stewards name

Nā Kahu o Hōʻai says the land on Lawai Road, on the south shore of Kauaʻi, was set aside in 1924 to honor Prince Jonah Kūhiō Kalanianaʻole Piʻikoi, and that this location was his birthplace in 1871. The park page gives the date: Prince Kuhio Memorial Park was established on 27 October 1924. It says the park has been owned and maintained by the men of the Royal Order of Kamehameha I, Chapter 3, Kaumualiʻi, and that Nā Kahu o Hōʻai supports that work. The homepage adds that the birthplace is surrounded by several heiau and by acres of ancient archaeological features. It does not name one heiau in that sentence, and it does not describe a grass house.

The rules posted for visitors

The park page says this is a private park and that visitors are welcome if they follow the posted rules. There is no public parking inside the park. Parking is along Lawai Road only. Visitors are not to stand or sit on the walls or other structures, which the page calls fragile archaeological features, and they are not to alter or disturb the features. Harvesting fruit, flowers, or fish requires permission. Trash comes out with the visitor. Large groups, parties, and loud music are not allowed. Park hours are sunrise to sunset. The Royal Order reserves the right to restrict access. Those hours are the stewards' hours. They are not a guess added to an otherwise silent page.

The life in the park service biography

The National Park Service biography says Prince Jonah Kūhiō Kalanianaʻole Piʻikoi was born on 26 March 1871 at Kukuiʻula, Kōloa, Kauaʻi, in the Hawaiian Kingdom. It says he died on 7 January 1922 at Waikīkī, and that he is buried at Mauna ʻAla, the Royal Mausoleum. The birthplace park is not the grave. The biography says he served ten terms as Hawaii's delegate to Congress. It says he restored the Royal Order of Kamehameha I in 1903, introduced the first Hawaiʻi statehood bill in 1919, and introduced the Hawaiian Homes Commission Act in 1920. It also says he introduced the Hawaiʻi National Park bill in 1916, covering Kīlauea, Mauna Loa, and Haleakalā, and that President Woodrow Wilson signed it. The same page says that in 1928 he founded the Hawaiian civic club movement. That year is after the 7 January 1922 death date on the same page.

Questions

Where was Prince Kūhiō born, and who keeps the park?

The National Park Service says he was born on 26 March 1871 at Kukuiʻula, Kōloa, Kauaʻi. Nā Kahu o Hōʻai says the park on Lawai Road was established on 27 October 1924 and is maintained by the Royal Order of Kamehameha I, Chapter 3, with the organization's support.

Is he buried at the park?

The park service says he died on 7 January 1922 at Waikīkī and is buried at Mauna ʻAla. The park page describes a birthplace and sets visiting hours from sunrise to sunset. It does not describe a grave.
