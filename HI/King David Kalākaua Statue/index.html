King Kalākaua statue

The bronze in the park

Wikipedia's article on Kalākaua Park says the bronze statue of King Kalakaua, sculpted and cast by Henry Bianchini, was dedicated on 6 August 1988, with a time capsule embedded in its base. The article spells the king's name Kalakaua in that sentence and spells the park Kalākaua. It calls the park the central town square of Hilo. It does not give the statue's height, and it does not describe a staff or a pose. A separate encyclopedia mirror that listed a height in inches is not the park article, and it is not used here.

The square around it

The same park article says the ground was the Waiakea Mission Station-Hilo Station from 1825, after the missionaries moved off the flood plain of the Wailuku River at the urging of Queen Kaʻahumanu. It says the land was ceded to the territorial government by King Kamehameha in 1848. A sundial erected in 1877, still in the park, commemorates Queen Emma and King David Kalākaua reading proclamations from the lanai of the old courthouse. The article says the park that replaced that courthouse was designed after the site was cleared in the early 1930s and was completed by February 1934. The Federal Building and post office, designed by Henry D. Whitfield, stands across Waianuenue Avenue and was built from 1915 to 1917. The article does not print a street number for the statue. The older page's address, 136 Kalakaua Street, and a round-the-clock claim are not in this article, so they are left out.

The king the statue names

Wikipedia's article on Kalākaua says he was the last king and penultimate monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaiʻi, reigning from 12 February 1874 until his death in 1891. It says he was known as the Merrie Monarch, and that at his coronation and his birthday jubilee the hula, which had been banned in public, became a celebration of Hawaiian culture. Those sentences are about the king, not a measured description of the Hilo bronze. This guide does not add a birth year, because that article's summary as it loaded does not print one.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Who made the Hilo statue, and when was it dedicated?

The Kalākaua Park article says Henry Bianchini sculpted and cast the bronze, and that it was dedicated on 6 August 1988 with a time capsule in the base.

Where does it stand?

The article calls Kalākaua Park the town square of Hilo and places the Federal Building across Waianuenue Avenue. It does not give a street number for the statue.
