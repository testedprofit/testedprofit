Jose Rizal bust

The gift the council accepted

The Fil-Am Voice account of Kepaniwai's Filipino garden says A.B. Sevilla and Frances Sevilla gave a bust of Jose Rizal. Lolita Sevilla Eugenio, quoted in that article, says her father commissioned a company in Laoag to make the bust on a trip in the late 1960s, and that it sat in its crate at the back of the family store at 1592 Mill Street until the council accepted it. The article says the Maui County Council unanimously adopted Resolution 79-158, introduced by Councilmember Mariano M. Acoba, on 10 December 1979. The community unveiled the bust on 30 December 1979, which the article calls the 83rd anniversary of Rizal's death. Counting back 83 years from that unveiling places the death date the article is using in 1896. The article calls the work a bust. It does not describe a standing figure or a book in his hand.

The county park, not the state-monument gate

The Maui News, in a 21 November 2025 story, calls Kepaniwai Heritage Gardens a 7.2-acre county-owned park at 870 Iao Valley Road and says the gardens date to the 1960s and early 1970s. It says the displays include buildings, gates, and gardens for cultural groups that include Filipino people, along with Hawaiian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and early American displays. The Fil-Am article places the Rizal bust in that Filipino section, beside the Bahay Kubo, which it says was already complete by the mid-1970s. to 6:00 p.m. Those figures belong to a different gate. The Division of State Parks page for ʻĪao Valley State Monument lists its own hours, a nonresident entry fee, and parking rules, and it does not mention the Rizal bust.

Questions

Where is the Maui Rizal bust, and when was it unveiled?

The Fil-Am Voice places the bust given by A.B. and Frances Sevilla in the Filipino garden at Kepaniwai and dates the unveiling to 30 December 1979. The Maui News places Kepaniwai Heritage Gardens at 870 Iao Valley Road and calls the park 7.2 acres.

Are the state-monument fee and hours the park's hours?

The ʻĪao Valley State Monument page lists hours and a nonresident fee for that monument. It does not mention the bust.
