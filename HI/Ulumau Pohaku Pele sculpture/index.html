Ulumau Pohaku Pele

The Honolulu Advertiser story posted on Thursday, 8 March 2007, says Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park unveiled Ulumau Pohaku Pele the day before. The story translates the title as Forever Growing, the Rock of Pele. Kona artist John Kalewa Matsushita made it. The story calls it an 11-foot-tall sculpture built around a 7-ton lava rock that reveals an impression of Pele. The rock sits on a base of four carved ʻōhiʻa wood panels. The article spells the wood with apostrophes. The panels are the past, her journey to Hawaiʻi; the present, her home at Kīlauea and Mauna Loa; and the future, a new home at Kamaʻehu, a submarine volcano. The story says the artwork was installed in front of the Volcano Art Center near the Kīlauea Visitor Center.

The story says the park in 2004 issued a call for a sculpture of wahi kahu, which the article glosses as sacred place. More than 30 artists submitted designs for a $38,000 commission. The park’s Kupuna Consultation Committee recommended Matsushita. The story says he has a bachelor of fine arts degree from the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa and also studied at the Art Institute of Southern California. It says he was 32, and that his inspiration came from the late Emma Kauhi of the kupuna committee, who died before the work was finished. Park superintendent Cindy Orlando is quoted on the hope that visitors would see the volcanoes, and Pele, as sacred.

It does not state a park entrance fee, and it does not say the sculpture can be viewed at all hours. The phrase in the story is wahi kahu.

The pages do not give a separate street address beyond the Volcano Art Center and the Kīlauea Visitor Center.

Questions

What was unveiled, and when?

The Advertiser story posted on 8 March 2007 says the park unveiled Ulumau Pohaku Pele the day before. It describes an 11-foot sculpture and a 7-ton lava rock on four ʻōhiʻa panels, in front of the Volcano Art Center near the Kīlauea Visitor Center.

Who selected the artist?

The story says the park called for designs in 2004, more than 30 artists responded, the commission was $38,000, and the Kupuna Consultation Committee recommended John Kalewa Matsushita.
