Israel Kamakawiwoʻole

The bust the sculptor lists

Jan-Michelle Sawyer's commemorative-sculpture page lists a bronze of IZ at 36 by 16 by 20 inches and calls it a life-size bust on a stamped cement pedestal. It says the work is installed at the Waianae Neighborhood Community Center on Oahu for the City and County of Honolulu. A second page on her site repeats the place and the client, calls the material bronze on a cement pedestal, and describes Israel Kamakawiwoʻole as a figure in Hawaiian music in the 1990s whose recording of Somewhere Over the Rainbow became particularly famous after his death. The sculptor's pages do not give a weight, a street address, or an unveiling date. They spell the center Waianae.

The unveiling the newspapers dated

The Honolulu Star-Bulletin's Island Images page for 21 September 2003 says that yesterday a 200-pound bronze bust was unveiled at the Waianae Neighborhood Community Center, six years after the singer's death. It names his widow, Marlene Kamakawiwoʻole, and the sculptor, Jan-Michelle Sawyer, as the people who placed leis on the sculpture. The day before 21 September 2003 is 20 September 2003. The Honolulu Advertiser story filed the same day says the veil came off shortly after 3 p.m. yesterday at the Waiʻanae Neighborhood Community Center, and it uses that spelling with an okina. It says Sawyer withdrew the veil with Marlene, and it identifies the singer as a favorite son of Waiʻanae who died on 26 June 1997. It says Sawyer began the clay model in August 2001, that private donations dried up after the attacks of 11 September 2001, and that the project stalled until Councilmember Mike Gabbard revived it in March. It says the bust had been in a crate at a foundry in Berkeley, California, and that it was set on a pedestal facing the ocean. This guide keeps the sculptor's 36-inch bust and the Star-Bulletin's 200 pounds as two different figures, one a dimension and one a weight. It keeps both spellings of the town.

Questions

Who made the bust, and when was it unveiled?

Jan-Michelle Sawyer's pages place a bronze bust at the Waianae Neighborhood Community Center. The Star-Bulletin and the Advertiser, both dated 21 September 2003, describe an unveiling the day before, which is 20 September 2003.

How large is it?

The sculptor lists the bronze as 36 by 16 by 20 inches and calls it life-size. The Star-Bulletin calls it a 200-pound bronze bust. This guide reports both.
