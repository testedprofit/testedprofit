Marianne Cope at Kewalo Basin

The Honolulu Advertiser story posted on 23 January 2010 says the Sisters of St. Francis would unveil and dedicate a bronze of Mother Marianne Cope that day at Kewalo Basin Park. It calls the work a 6-foot statue by Sister Rosaire Kopczenski, a Pittsburgh artist and a fellow Franciscan. The story says the unveiling coincided with her feast day, and that the ceremony began at 10 a.m. near where the ship Mariposa had docked 126 years earlier. Mother Marianne and six other nuns were aboard, at the invitation of King Kalākaua, to care for residents of Kalaupapa, Molokaʻi.

The same story says she was beatified at St. Peter’s Basilica in 2005 and, at that date, awaited canonization. It describes a hula pose: one hand extended, for help and service, and the other on her heart, for empathy. It says a trust was set up for maintenance. The Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace says she was canonized on 21 October 2012 and that her feast day is 23 January. Hawaii News Now, in a story published 21 October 2012, says Pope Benedict XVI formally canonized her and calls her Hawaii’s second saint. That story says 225 Hawaii pilgrims traveled to Rome and that nine patients with Hansen’s disease from Kalaupapa were escorted forward. It quotes the pope, in English, on her love, courage, and enthusiasm at a time when little could be done for people with the disease.

The Leprosy Mission Australia places a statue of her in Kewalo Basin in Honolulu, with an outstretched hand pointing toward Molokai, and dates the canonization to 21 October 2012. The cathedral page says her mortal remains are in a reliquary at the Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace. During the cathedral renovation, that page says the reliquary is at the Co-Cathedral of St. Theresa of the Child Jesus, 712 North School Street, Honolulu, HI 96817. The same page lists the cathedral at 1184 Bishop Street, Honolulu, HI 96813. This guide does not give a street address for the Kewalo bronze, because the pages that loaded do not print one.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where is the bronze, and how tall is it?

The Advertiser places a 6-foot bronze by Sister Rosaire Kopczenski at Kewalo Basin Park and dates the dedication story to 23 January 2010. The Leprosy Mission Australia also places a statue of her in Kewalo Basin.

When was she canonized?

The cathedral page and the Hawaii News Now story both date the canonization to 21 October 2012. The Advertiser, written in 2010, still said she awaited canonization after her 2005 beatification. The cathedral page gives 23 January as her feast day.
