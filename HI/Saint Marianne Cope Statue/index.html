Saint Marianne Cope Statue at Kewalo Basin, Honolulu

The Saint Marianne Cope Statue, also searched as St. Marianne Cope, is a 6-foot bronze at Kewalo Basin Park in Honolulu. Sister Rosaire Kopczenski, a Pittsburgh artist and a fellow Franciscan, made it. The Sisters of St. Francis dedicated it on 23 January 2010, her feast day. She was canonized on 21 October 2012.

Her life

Saint Marianne Cope was Barbara Koob, born on 23 January 1838 in Hesse, Germany. Two years later the family emigrated and settled in Utica, New York. She entered the Sisters of the Third Order of St. Francis in Syracuse, and that is when she began to go by Marianne instead of Barbara. The Syracuse community is the American home she left for Hawaii.

King Kalākaua invited Franciscan sisters to care for people with leprosy. Mother Marianne and six other nuns sailed on the ship Mariposa. The 2010 dedication at Kewalo stood near that old docking place, 126 years on. Father Damien asked the sisters to continue his work. In November 1888 Mother Marianne led the first group of sister-nurses to Kalaupapa on Molokaʻi, where more than a thousand people with the disease had been exiled. She stayed in Hawaii for the last 30 years of her life.

She was beatified at St. Peter's Basilica in 2005. Pope Benedict XVI canonized her on 21 October 2012. She is Hawaii's second saint. Two hundred twenty-five Hawaii pilgrims traveled to Rome, and nine patients with Hansen's disease from Kalaupapa were escorted forward. The pope spoke, in English, of her love, courage, and enthusiasm at a time when little could be done for people with the disease. Her feast day is 23 January, the date of both her birth and the Kewalo dedication.

The statue

Sister Rosaire Kopczenski shaped a 6-foot bronze. The pose is a hula gesture: one hand extended, for help and service, and the other on her heart, for empathy. The Leprosy Mission Australia describes the Kewalo Basin figure with an outstretched hand pointing toward Molokai. A trust was set up to maintain the bronze. The Sisters of St. Francis unveiled it at 10 a.m. on 23 January 2010 at Kewalo Basin Park.

See the statue at the park, then the reliquary if you want the shrine indoors. Her mortal remains are at the Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace, 1184 Bishop Street, Honolulu, HI 96813. During the cathedral renovation, the reliquary is at the Co-Cathedral of St. Theresa of the Child Jesus, 712 North School Street, Honolulu, HI 96817.

Questions

Where is the Saint Marianne Cope Statue?

At Kewalo Basin Park in Honolulu. Sister Rosaire Kopczenski made the 6-foot bronze. The Sisters of St. Francis dedicated it on 23 January 2010, her feast day, at 10 a.m.

Who was Saint Marianne Cope?

Barbara Koob was born on 23 January 1838 in Hesse, Germany, and grew up in Utica, New York. She joined the Sisters of St. Francis in Syracuse and took the name Marianne. In November 1888 she led sister-nurses to Kalaupapa, Molokai. She was canonized on 21 October 2012.

Who made the St. Marianne Cope statue, and what does the pose show?

Sister Rosaire Kopczenski, a Pittsburgh Franciscan. One hand reaches out, toward Molokai, for help and service. The other rests on her heart, for empathy.

When was she beatified and canonized?

She was beatified at St. Peter's Basilica in 2005. Pope Benedict XVI canonized her on 21 October 2012. She is Hawaii's second saint. Her feast day is 23 January.

Where is the reliquary?

Her remains are in a reliquary at the Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Peace, 1184 Bishop Street, Honolulu, HI 96813. During the cathedral renovation, the reliquary is at the Co-Cathedral of St. Theresa, 712 North School Street, Honolulu, HI 96817.
