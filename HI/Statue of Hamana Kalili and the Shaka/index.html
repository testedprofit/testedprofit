Hamana Kalili

The Polynesian Cultural Center’s shaka page says that in 2015 a heroically proportioned bronze of Hamana Kalili, measuring 7 feet 6 inches, was unveiled during the opening ceremonies for the Hukilau Marketplace. Former center student employee Leroy Transfield, a Māori sculptor from New Zealand, made it. That page says Hamana stands day and night as the first to greet visitors and employees. A March 2015 center update dates the Hukilau Marketplace opening to 20 February 2015 and then describes a 7-foot 4-inch bronze of Hamana Kalili. This guide keeps both heights. The pages do not print a street address.

The March update says he lost most of the three middle fingers on his right hand in an industrial accident, so the thumb and little finger looked extended. It says children in the community copied the gesture, and that in 1948 he shared the wave with tourists at the old hukilau in Laie. Descendants draped the statue with maile leis, and the update also describes a chant by Kalili’s descendants. The shaka page gives three accounts of the missing fingers. One says a shark bit them while he held fish underwater. Another says they were lost while fishing with dynamite. Grand-nephew Von Logan says the true story is an accidental amputation in a sugar-mill accident.

After that accident, the shaka page says the plantation gave him a security job watching the trains, because children jumped the train as it slowed entering or leaving Kahuku. He yelled or waved to get them off. The page says the children later used that wave, the shaka, as an all-clear sign meaning Hamana was not around and it was okay to jump the train. It also says he was a chorister who led hymns with the sign. It says politician Frank Fasi later adopted the gesture. The March update’s account, that children copied a wave he shared, and the shaka page’s account, that children used the sign because he was absent, are both on the center’s own pages. This guide does not collapse them into one story.

The March update says the new marketplace was open to the public free of charge, with free parking and wi-fi. It does not say whether a center ticket is required to stand at the bronze.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

How tall is the bronze?

The center’s shaka page says 7 feet 6 inches. Its March 2015 update says 7 feet 4 inches. Both figures are left as printed.

Did Hamana Kalili invent the all-clear wave?

The shaka page says he waved to get children off the train, and that the children later used the same gesture to mean he was not around. The March update says children copied the wave and that he shared it with tourists in 1948.
