Haʻi Moʻolelo

The Honolulu Advertiser story posted on 13 October 2002 calls the work Haʻi Moʻolelo, The Storyteller. The article itself prints the name with straight apostrophes, Ha'i Mo'olelo. It says the statue was unveiled on 5 October at 2100 Kalakaua Avenue in Waikiki. Artist Shige Yamada, who was born on Maui, made it. The story says he also created Gift of Water at the Hawaiʻi Convention Center. Honu Group Inc., developers of the shopping complex, commissioned the bronze. Mona Abadir, principal of Honu Group, is quoted. The story says the seven-foot bronze depicts a Hawaiian woman in a seated position.

A second Advertiser story, posted on 2 December 2002 under the headline about a black-tie arts benefit, repeats the description. It calls Storyteller a seven-foot bronze of a seated woman storyteller outside the 2100 Kalakaua building, created by Maui artist Shige Yamada. Abadir is quoted again on the statue as a reminder of linked traditions. The year of the 5 October unveiling is 2002, taken from the date of the first story.

Both stories describe one seated woman. They do not describe an elder with two children, and they give the address as 2100 Kalakaua Avenue. They do not print viewing hours. The first story also mentions a separate mural by Shigeru Miyamoto at a school on Wheeler Army Airfield. That mural is not this statue.

The pages do not name a foundry, a weight, or a pedestal height.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

What does the statue show, and where is it?

Both Advertiser stories describe a seven-foot bronze of one seated woman at 2100 Kalakaua Avenue in Waikiki. The 13 October 2002 story dates the unveiling to 5 October and names Shige Yamada.

Who commissioned it?

The 13 October 2002 story says Honu Group Inc. commissioned Haʻi Moʻolelo. Mona Abadir is quoted as principal of Honu Group in that story and as chief operating officer in the 2 December story.
