Charles Barkley statue

Auburn’s athletics site published “Sculpting Charles Barkley: ‘I’m here to make sure everything goes well’” with the date November 22, 2017. The story says sculptor Brian Hanlon’s statue would be unveiled that Saturday outside Auburn Arena, shortly after 11 a.m., when Barkley arrived from Samford Lawn after appearing as ESPN’s guest picker for College GameDay.

The sculptor’s account

The story calls Hanlon the official sculptor for the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame and says he had created 300 public and private art pieces in 30 years. He describes looking for a pose after setting aside a dunk with a defender hanging on Barkley, because a second figure would complicate the work. He used two images, one from Barkley’s time with the 76ers and one from his time with the Suns, and substituted an Auburn jersey.

When Barkley learned Auburn would honor him with a statue, the story quotes him from the previous October: “I just hope it’s skinny. Don’t make it life-size.” Hanlon said he honored that request. The pose, with the arms in the air and the legs extended, stretches the figure so it does not gather too much mass in one place.

What this guide dropped

An earlier version called the statue life-size and placed it at the scholarship entrance of Neville Arena, formerly Auburn Arena, at 250 Beard-Eaves Court. The Auburn story says Barkley asked that it not be life-size, and it places the planned unveiling outside Auburn Arena. It does not use the later arena name or that street address, so both were removed.
