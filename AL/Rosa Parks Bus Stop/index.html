Rosa Parks arrest site

A Montgomery police report dated December 1, 1955, published in transcription by Historical Thinking Matters, says the offense was committed at 6:06 p.m. The place of occurrence is “In Front of Empire Theatre (On Montgomery Street).” The complainant is J. F. Blake. The report says the bus operator reported a Black woman sitting in the white section who would not move back, and that Rosa Parks was charged under chapter 6, section 11 of the Montgomery city code.

The corner now

Troy University’s Rosa Parks Museum page says the museum is in downtown Montgomery at the site where Parks was arrested, and it gives the museum address as 252 Montgomery Street. The page says that on December 1, 1955, she boarded a city bus and sat in the middle section, where Black riders were allowed to sit. When the white section filled, the driver demanded that she and three others give up their seats. The others moved. She refused, the driver had her arrested, and she was later found guilty of disorderly conduct. The page says the Montgomery Bus Boycott began with that case.

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says the museum was built on the site of the former Empire Theatre, where Parks made her stand in 1955 at the bus stop in front of the theater. It says Troy had planned a parking deck, then dropped that plan after people kept stopping to read the historical marker about the arrest.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where was Rosa Parks arrested?

The December 1, 1955 police report says the place was in front of the Empire Theatre on Montgomery Street. Troy University gives the museum on that site as 252 Montgomery Street.

Does this guide put the arrest at Court Square Fountain?

The police report and the Encyclopedia of Alabama place it at the bus stop in front of the Empire Theatre.
