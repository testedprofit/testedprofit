Rosa Parks arrest site

Rosa Parks was arrested at 6:06 p.m. on 1 December 1955 at the bus stop in front of the Empire Theatre on Montgomery Street. Troy University's Rosa Parks Museum now stands on that corner, at 252 Montgomery Street.

The bus stop

The Montgomery police report for 1 December 1955 records the offense at 6:06 p.m. The place of occurrence is in front of the Empire Theatre on Montgomery Street. The complainant is J. F. Blake, the bus operator. The report says that on arrival the officers were told a Black woman was sitting in the white section and would not move back, that the officers saw her, and that the operator signed a warrant. Rosa Parks, of 634 Cleveland Boulevard, was charged under chapter 6, section 11 of the Montgomery city code, warrant 14254. The officers were F. B. Day and D. W. Mixon. The report's patrol time is 7:00 p.m.

What she did

Troy University's account of the museum says that on that day Parks boarded a city bus and sat in the middle section, where Black riders were allowed to sit. When the white section filled and a white man was left standing, the driver told Parks and three other riders in the middle to give up their seats. The other three moved. She refused. The driver called the police and filed charges. Four days later she was found guilty of disorderly conduct, and the Montgomery Bus Boycott began. More than a year after that, the city was served with papers declaring segregated bus service unconstitutional. The next day she boarded a bus and sat in any open seat.

The cost was immediate. She lost her job as a seamstress and could not find work. Her family was harassed and threatened. In 1957 she moved with her mother and husband to Detroit, where her brother Sylvester lived. From 1965 until she retired in 1988 she worked on the staff of U.S. Representative John Conyers of Michigan.

The museum on the corner

The museum was built on the site of the former Empire Theatre, the bus stop where she made that stand. Troy had bought the property for a parking deck. Two buildings, including the theatre, were torn down after they were judged too far gone to restore. People kept stopping on the corner to read the historical marker about the arrest, and the university set the deck aside. A museum and library went up instead, paid for with private gifts, a federal grant, and foundation money. It opened on 1 December 2000.

The collection includes the original fingerprint arrest record, a 1950s Montgomery city bus, court papers and police reports, and a restored 1955 station wagon, a rolling church, used to carry boycott riders. The photograph on this page is the museum itself, the brick and glass building with the Rosa Parks name on the facade. It is the 2000 building on the old theatre's corner, not a picture of the 1955 arrest. The address is 252 Montgomery Street. The children's wing is next door at 220 Montgomery Street. Groups of ten or more need a reserved slot. Call 334-241-8661. The Hank Williams statue and the Rainbow Soldier at Union Station are other downtown stops. Inside, the fingerprint record, the city bus, and the rolling-church station wagon are the objects that put you back on 1 December 1955.
