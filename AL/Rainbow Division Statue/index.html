Rainbow Soldier

The Croix Rouge Farm Memorial Foundation says a bronze of the Rainbow Soldier by British sculptor James Butler stands in front of Montgomery’s Union Station. On August 28, 1917, the 167th Infantry Regiment left that station to join the Rainbow Division and sail for France. The Montgomery memorial was inaugurated on August 28, 2017, one hundred years later. The foundation says an earlier bronze casting was inaugurated in 2011 at Fère-en-Tardenois, near Château-Thierry, on the site of the battle of Croix Rouge Farm.

The dedication report

WSFA’s story on the dedication says 3,677 Alabamians left Union Station to join the Rainbow Division, and that a bronze by James Butler was dedicated in front of the station at 5 p.m. The story says the Montgomery sculpture is a second casting of the one in France, commissioned through Nimrod T. Frazer and the Croix Rouge Farm Memorial Foundation. It also says the statue was to be dedicated on August 28.

The Encyclopedia of Alabama’s media record says the statue was created by James Butler and is identical to his sculpture at Croix Rouge Farm. It dates the French unveiling to 2011 and the Alabama unveiling to 2017, and it places the Alabama statue in front of Union Station.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where is the Rainbow Soldier, and when was it inaugurated?

The Croix Rouge Farm Memorial Foundation says the bronze stands in front of Montgomery’s Union Station and was inaugurated on August 28, 2017.

What unit does it honor?

The foundation and WSFA say the 167th Infantry Regiment left that station on August 28, 1917. WSFA says 3,677 Alabamians left to join the Rainbow Division.
