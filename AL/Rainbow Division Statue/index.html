Rainbow Soldier

James Butler's bronze of the Rainbow Soldier stands in front of Montgomery's Union Station. On 28 August 1917 the 167th Infantry Regiment left that station for France. The memorial was inaugurated on 28 August 2017, one hundred years later.

The soldier in front of the station

The bronze shows a World War I infantryman carrying a fallen comrade. It stands on a stone pedestal in front of the red-brick arches of Union Station, with poppies at the base in the inauguration photograph. James Butler, R.A., a British sculptor, designed it. The Montgomery casting is the second. The first was inaugurated in 2011 at Fère-en-Tardenois, near Château-Thierry, on the ground of the battle of Croix Rouge Farm. The Encyclopedia of Alabama describes the Alabama statue as identical to that French memorial. The French unveiling was 2011. The Alabama unveiling was 2017.

The foundation calls the work a gift to the city, made through Nimrod T. Frazer, a longtime Montgomery business leader and a Silver Star recipient from the Korean War. Frazer commissioned the French memorial and wrote Send the Alabamians, an account of these soldiers. The Montgomery sculpture is that French figure, cast again, and set where the regiment boarded the train.

28 August, twice

On 28 August 1917 the 167th Infantry Regiment left Union Station to join the Rainbow Division and sail for France. WSFA's dedication story counts 3,677 Alabamians on that departure. They became the 167th U.S. Infantry Regiment of the 42nd Rainbow Division, known as the Immortals. At Croix Rouge Farm on 26 July 1918 they fought beside the French and helped push the German line at the Ourcq River. The battle remains the second deadliest in Alabama history. Survivors were welcomed home in May 1919. The Montgomery memorial was inaugurated on 28 August 2017, a hundred years after the train left, with the dedication reported at 5 p.m. in front of the station.

How to see it

Stand in front of Union Station and look for the soldier and the carried man against the brick arches. The photograph on this page was made on inauguration day, 28 August 2017, so the flowers at the base are that day's planting. The statue is the permanent part. Butler's other Montgomery bronze, Daedalus, was inaugurated at Maxwell Air Force Base on 6 April 2017 and belongs to a different memorial, the World War I pilots. The Rainbow Soldier is the station figure. The Rosa Parks museum and the Hank Williams statue are other downtown Montgomery stops.

The pose is the battle. On 26 July 1918, at Croix Rouge Farm near Château-Thierry, the 167th fought beside the French and helped push the German line at the Ourcq River. The French casting of this same bronze stands on that farm, inaugurated in 2011 at Fère-en-Tardenois. Montgomery's figure is the second casting, brought back to the station the regiment left. The foundation's name, Croix Rouge Farm, is the battlefield, not a second Montgomery site.

Frazer's Silver Star is from Korea, and his book Send the Alabamians is the regiment's story in print. The gift line on the memorial is the foundation, through him, to the city. WSFA counted 3,677 Alabamians on the 1917 departure and reported the dedication for 5 p.m. on the centennial. Survivors of the war came home to Alabama in May 1919. A century later the bronze is the marker at the platform end of that trip, the soldier and the man he carries, Rainbow Division on the base. Poppies at the foot of the pedestal in the inauguration photograph are that day's flowers. The soldier, the comrade, and the station arches are what you came to see.

Questions

Where is the Rainbow Soldier?

In front of Union Station in Montgomery. It was inaugurated on 28 August 2017.

What does the bronze show?

Who left from this station?

On 28 August 1917, 3,677 Alabamians of the 167th Infantry left Union Station to join the Rainbow Division.
