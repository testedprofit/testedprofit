Four Spirits

The Birmingham Times, in September 2013, said the memorial for the four girls killed in the 1963 bombing of the Sixteenth Street Baptist Church would be unveiled Saturday, September 14, at 4 p.m. Sculptor Elizabeth MacQueen, a Birmingham native, made it. The story calls it a life-size bronze and steel sculpture of the four girls at a bench, surrounded by doves, nearly 10 feet tall and five feet long, at the corner of Sixteenth Street North and Sixth Avenue North on the edge of Kelly Ingram Park.

The sculptor’s account

MacQueen’s project page names Addie Mae Collins, Denise McNair, Cynthia Wesley, and Carole Robertson. It says the steel and bronze sculpture was unveiled on September 14, 2013, and that hundreds of people gathered at Kelly Ingram Park. Her studio site lists this work as completed in 2013, cast bronze, 10 feet by 12 feet by 3 feet, at 1601 6th Avenue North, Birmingham, and open 24 hours. The Birmingham Times size, nearly 10 feet by five feet, does not match the studio’s 10 by 12 by 3. This guide reports both.

WBRC’s unveiling story says one girl is tying a bow on another girl’s dress while that girl releases doves. It says the six doves stand for the four girls and for Virgil Ware and Johnny Robinson, two boys also killed that day. The story spells one girl’s name Carolyn Robertson. MacQueen’s page spells it Carole Robertson. This guide uses the sculptor’s spelling.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where is Four Spirits, and whom does it honor?

The Birmingham Times places it at Sixteenth Street North and Sixth Avenue North, on the edge of Kelly Ingram Park. It honors the four girls killed in the September 15, 1963 bombing of the Sixteenth Street Baptist Church. MacQueen’s page names Addie Mae Collins, Denise McNair, Cynthia Wesley, and Carole Robertson.

What do the doves stand for?

WBRC says the six doves stand for the four girls and for Virgil Ware and Johnny Robinson, who were also killed that day.
