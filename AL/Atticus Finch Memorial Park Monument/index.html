Atticus Finch monument

RuralSWAlabama says a monument honoring Atticus Finch stands on the northeast side of the old courthouse in downtown Monroeville. The Alabama Bar Association erected it in 1997. The page says the monument presents Atticus Finch, the lawyer in Harper Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird, as a model for nondiscriminatory justice.

The monument and the courthouse

RuralSWAlabama's page is titled Atticus Finch Monument at the Old Courthouse in Monroeville. It says Harper Lee, author of the Pulitzer Prize-winning novel To Kill a Mockingbird, was born and raised in Monroeville, and that the fictional town of Maycomb is modeled on Monroeville. Atticus Finch is the lawyer and the father of Jem and Scout. The monument, the page says, was erected in 1997 by the Alabama Bar Association on the northeast side of the old courthouse and presents him as the ideal model for nondiscriminatory justice.

The English Wikipedia article on Atticus Finch says that in 1997 the Alabama State Bar erected a monument to Atticus in Monroeville and calls it the first commemorative milestone in the state's judicial history. RuralSWAlabama says Alabama Bar Association. The Wikipedia article says Alabama State Bar. Both pages use 1997 and Monroeville.

The inscription the page prints

RuralSWAlabama prints the inscription in full. It begins ATTICUS FINCH: LAWYER – HERO and quotes the novel's epigraph, Lawyers, I suppose, were children once, identifying those words as Charles Lamb's and as the epigraph to To Kill a Mockingbird. The rest of the text, as that page prints it, says the legal profession has in Atticus a lawyer-hero who knows how to see and tell the truth, who knows the price the community will pay for that truth, who uses power for moral purposes, and who will stand alone as the conscience of the community. It says he possesses a man's knowledge strengthened by a child's insight. It says children become bigots, cynics, the greedy, and the intolerant only when they are educated into hatreds, and it closes by putting the legal profession in the jury box as Atticus concludes, In the name of God, do your duty.

That inscription is the monument's own argument, as RuralSWAlabama transcribes it. It is not a court opinion and not a biography of a historical lawyer. Atticus Finch is a character in Lee's novel.

A 1997 proclamation that is about the city

Visit Monroeville says a 1997 proclamation of the state legislature named Monroeville the Literary Capital of Alabama because of writers who lived there, including Harper Lee and Truman Capote. That sentence is about the city. It does not describe this monument. The shared year is 1997. The documents are different.

Questions

Where is the Atticus Finch monument?

RuralSWAlabama places it on the northeast side of the old courthouse in downtown Monroeville and says the Alabama Bar Association erected it in 1997.

Is the 1997 literary-capital proclamation about the monument?

Visit Monroeville says the 1997 legislative proclamation named the city the Literary Capital of Alabama because of writers who lived there. It does not describe the monument.

Does the photograph show the monument?

No. The Commons file shows the courtroom inside the old courthouse. The monument page places the stone outside, on the northeast side.
