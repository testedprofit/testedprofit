Atticus Finch monument

RuralSWAlabama says a monument honoring Atticus Finch stands on the northeast side of the old courthouse in downtown Monroeville. The Alabama Bar Association erected it in 1997. The page says the monument presents Atticus Finch, the lawyer in Harper Lee’s To Kill a Mockingbird, as a model for nondiscriminatory justice.

The inscription

RuralSWAlabama prints the inscription. It begins “ATTICUS FINCH: LAWYER – HERO” and quotes the novel’s epigraph, “Lawyers, I suppose, were children once.” The rest of the text, as printed there, addresses lawyers about telling the truth and about children.

A separate 1997 proclamation

Wikipedia’s article on Atticus Finch says that in 1997 the Alabama State Bar erected a monument to Atticus in Monroeville, calling it the first commemorative milestone in the state’s judicial history.

Visit Monroeville says a 1997 proclamation of the state legislature named Monroeville the Literary Capital of Alabama because of writers who lived there, including Harper Lee and Truman Capote. That sentence is about the city. It does not describe this monument.
