Atticus Finch monument

The Atticus Finch monument stands on the northeast side of the old courthouse in downtown Monroeville. The Alabama bar put it up in 1997 to hold up Lee's lawyer as a model of fair judgment.

Plan your visit

Address
Northeast side of the old Monroe County Courthouse, downtown Monroeville. The courthouse is at 65 North Alabama Avenue.

The courtroom in the photograph is the building's interior. Step outside to the northeast side for the monument.

A stone for a fictional lawyer

Atticus Finch is the lawyer and father in Harper Lee's novel To Kill a Mockingbird, the Pulitzer Prize novel of 1960. He raises Jeremy, called Jem, and Jean Louise, called Scout, in the fictional town of Maycomb. Lee was born and raised in Monroeville, and Maycomb takes the real town as its model. She based Atticus on her father, Amasa Coleman Lee, a Monroeville lawyer. In the novel Atticus represents Tom Robinson at trial. In 1997 the Alabama bar set this monument on the northeast side of the old courthouse and held Atticus up as a model of fair judgment.

The inscription starts Atticus Finch: Lawyer – Hero, then quotes the novel's epigraph, Lawyers, I suppose, were children once, words of Charles Lamb. The rest of the text says the profession has, in Atticus, a lawyer who can see and tell the truth and who knows what that truth will cost a town he loves. It says he knows how to use power and advantage for moral purposes and will stand alone as the community's conscience. It says he joins a man's experience to a child's clear sight. Children, the inscription argues, become bigots, cynics, the greedy, and the intolerant only when they are taught hatred. It ends by putting lawyers in the jury box as Atticus closes: In the name of God, do your duty.

Why it is at the courthouse

The old courthouse is the building visitors associate with the novel's trial. The monument puts the novel's lawyer on the outside of that building, on the northeast side, where you can read the charge before you go in. The photograph here is the courtroom itself, from the balcony: benches, bar, and bench. The stone is not in that room. Walk out to the northeast wall to find it.

In 2003 the American Film Institute voted Gregory Peck's portrayal in the 1962 film the greatest hero in American film. The monument is older than that vote. It is the bar's 1997 marker, not a movie prop. The same year, 1997, the legislature named Monroeville the Literary Capital of Alabama because of writers who lived there, Harper Lee and Truman Capote among them. That title belongs to the town. The stone belongs to Atticus.

How to spend the stop

Read the inscription all the way through. It is an argument to lawyers, not a plot summary. Then go inside if the courtroom is open and compare the room in the photograph with the room in front of you. Downtown Monroeville is small enough that the courthouse square is the landmark. Other Alabama guides on this site, including the Hank Williams statue and the Rosa Parks site in Montgomery, are a longer drive.

Circle the old courthouse once before you leave. The monument sits on the northeast side, and it is easy to miss if you only face the front steps. After the stone, the balcony view in the photograph is the room to ask for inside: benches, the bar, and the judge's bench. The town's literary title and this stone share the year 1997. One belongs to the writers who lived here. The other belongs to Atticus, on the wall.

Questions

Where is the Atticus Finch monument?

On the northeast side of the old courthouse in downtown Monroeville. The Alabama bar erected it in 1997.

Who is Atticus Finch?

The lawyer in Harper Lee's To Kill a Mockingbird. Maycomb in the novel is modeled on Monroeville, where Lee grew up.

What does the inscription ask?

It calls him a lawyer and a hero, and it ends with his line to the jury: In the name of God, do your duty.
