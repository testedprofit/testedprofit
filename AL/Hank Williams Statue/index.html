Hank Williams statue in Montgomery

The Hank Williams statue in Montgomery, Alabama, stands at the intersection of Commerce and Tallapoosa streets. WSFA reported on 17 October 2016 that the city was moving it there from a spot that had overlooked City Hall for 25 years.

History

WSFA published its story on 17 October 2016, with an update the same evening. The lead says the Hank Williams statue that had overlooked Montgomery City Hall for the last 25 years was moving. Hank Williams Jr. donated the statue of his father to the city in 1991. WSFA says it was positioned to overlook City Hall, where more than 20,000 people attended Williams’s funeral.

Steve Jones, director of general services, told the station that leaving a famous Montgomerian’s statue at the old spot underused the chance for people to see it. He said the city had been improving the medians between Court Square Fountain and the tunnel that leads to Riverfront Park. About a month before the interview, crews finished the last piece of stamped-concrete median between Bibb Street and Montgomery Street. Jones said that if you stand at the fountain you can look all the way to the tunnel.

The statue’s new place is the intersection of Commerce and Tallapoosa streets. Jones called that move a first step toward a larger plan for the sidewalk. He said he would like a walk of fame for famous Montgomerians. He also said more than 2 million people visit Montgomery each year and that hundreds of thousands come downtown, and that he would dare say that 1 in 100 had never seen the statue at City Hall. Those visitor figures are his statement to WSFA.

Jones said the move had to go through the architectural review board because the board reviews work in the riverfront area. He said the approval was unanimous. The station’s account is about the relocation, the median, and the donation. It does not describe a dedication ceremony, a foundry, or a height.

Why it matters

The statue is a downtown marker for Hank Williams in the city that held his funeral at City Hall. WSFA ties the 1991 gift to that funeral setting: the figure overlooked the building where more than 20,000 people had gathered. Moving it in 2016 was a visibility decision. City staff wanted people walking between Court Square Fountain and the riverfront tunnel to meet the figure on the median instead of leaving it at a spot Jones called underused.

The Highsmith photograph in the Library of Congress collection shows the same kind of standing figure with a hat, suit, and guitar, and the Commons file title places that picture at Lister Hill Plaza. Use the photograph to recognize the statue. Use the WSFA report and the OpenStreetMap pin for where the city said it would stand after October 2016.

What to look for

Start at Court Square Fountain if you want the view Jones described. He said the finished median lets a person at the fountain look toward the tunnel to Riverfront Park. The statue is at the Commerce and Tallapoosa intersection on that line of median, not back at City Hall.

The figure in the Highsmith photograph wears a hat and a suit and holds a guitar across the body, with one hand on the neck and the other over the strings. Boots and a low base are visible. If the statue you find matches that pose, you are looking at the figure this guide is about. The photograph’s file title is Lister Hill Plaza, so the background of trees and a dark wall in the picture is the older setting, not a claim that the statue is still there.

Jones also talked about a future walk of fame along the sidewalk. WSFA does not say that walk was built as part of the October 2016 move. The finished work he described that month was the stamped-concrete median and the relocation of this one statue.

Questions

Where is the Hank Williams statue in Montgomery?

WSFA reported on 17 October 2016 that the city was moving the statue to the intersection of Commerce and Tallapoosa streets, on median work between Court Square Fountain and the tunnel to Riverfront Park. OpenStreetMap lists a monument named Hank Williams Statue on Commerce Street.

Who gave the statue to Montgomery, and when?

WSFA says Hank Williams Jr. donated the statue of his father to the city in 1991. It then overlooked City Hall for 25 years.

Why did the city move it?

Steve Jones, the city director of general services, told WSFA the statue was underused at City Hall and that more people would see it on Commerce Street. He said the architectural review board approved the move unanimously.
