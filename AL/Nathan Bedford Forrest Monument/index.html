Nathan Bedford Forrest monument

Wikipedia’s page on Old Live Oak Cemetery says the cemetery is at 110 Dallas Avenue in Selma, about 0.7 miles west of downtown. It was founded in 1829 and expanded in 1877. The page lists a Forrest Memorial from 2000 and quotes part of the inscription: “Defender of Selma, Wizard of the Saddle, Untutored Genius, The First with the Most,” and “one of the South’s finest heroes.”

How the bust moved

The Selma Times-Journal, on May 23, 2015, reported that a bust of Nathan Bedford Forrest was back on a monument at Old Live Oak Cemetery three years after it disappeared. The story says debate since the original unveiling in 2000 centers on Forrest’s distinction as Grand Wizard of the Ku Klux Klan. The monument was first unveiled at the Vaughan Smitherman Museum, where it was defaced, and was then moved to the cemetery’s Confederate Circle. In 2012 cemetery workers found the bust missing and reported it to Selma police. The original was not recovered. The Friends of Forrest offered a $20,000 reward. A Saturday ceremony by the United Daughters of the Confederacy and the Friends of Forrest unveiled the replacement.

What the biography page records

Wikipedia’s article on Nathan Bedford Forrest describes him as a Confederate general, planter, and slave trader, 1821–1877. It says he was the Ku Klux Klan’s first Grand Wizard, that he ordered the Klan dissolved in January 1869, and that he later denied being a member. Those are the article’s statements.

Questions

Where is the Nathan Bedford Forrest monument?

Wikipedia places Old Live Oak Cemetery at 110 Dallas Avenue in Selma. The Selma Times-Journal says the monument stands in the cemetery’s Confederate Circle.

What happened to the bust?

The May 23, 2015 Selma Times-Journal story says the original bust, unveiled in 2000, was stolen in 2012 and not recovered, and that a replacement was unveiled at a Saturday ceremony.
