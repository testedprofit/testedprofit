Court Square Fountain

Wikipedia says Court Square Fountain, in Montgomery’s Court Square-Dexter Avenue Historic District, was established in 1885 on an artesian well that native Alabamians used long before the area was settled. The article lists the year 1885, the medium cast iron, and the height 25 feet. It gives the coordinates 32.37722° N, 86.30917° W.

The well and the basin

The article says the well was a watering place before European settlers arrived. By June 1853 it was 475 feet deep and flowed at two gallons per minute. The spot is where Alabama Town and New Philadelphia grew together into Montgomery. Later the area was the central location of the Montgomery slave trade. A ten-foot-deep basin, with a pipe leading down, was in place by 1885, when the city council voted to appropriate $4,900 to improve it.

The figures

The fountain was long believed to be the work of Frederick MacMonnies. Wikipedia says the director of the Alabama Archives and History asked him in 1935 if it was his design, and he denied it. On top is a statue of Hebe, the Greek goddess of eternal youth, likely modeled on a sculpture by Antonio Canova. The statues came from a catalog of zinc-iron figures: “Canova’s Hebe,” four “Seated Boys” holding towels, four “Narcissus” figures, and at the bottom “Stem Bitterns,” three birds around the stem. All of those statues were replaced in 1984 by aluminum versions. The article says chlorine in the water corroded the aluminum about twenty years later. The opening summary says the statues were replaced with aluminum in the 1980s.

Rosa Parks in the same article

The same article both places Rosa Parks’s refusal at a bus stop across from the fountain and then says she refused her seat farther up the street, at the Empire Theater.

Questions

When was Court Square Fountain established, and what is it made of?

Wikipedia says it was established in 1885 on an artesian well. The fountain is cast iron, and the page lists the height as 25 feet.

Were the statues replaced?

Yes. Wikipedia says the catalog statues were replaced in 1984 with aluminum versions, which chlorine in the water corroded about twenty years later.
