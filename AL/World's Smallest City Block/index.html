World's Smallest City Block

This page keeps the address /AL/World's Smallest City Block/. The readable account is a World Record Academy page that mixes its own wording with quoted passages, and those passages do not agree.

What the page asserts

Its own wording says the block is behind the Dothan City Civic Center, between North Appletree Street, North College Street, and East Troy Street. It says the block once held a single two-story building 38 feet long and 27 feet wide, and that a granite monument by the Camellia Garden Club is there now. Driving directions on the same page end at North College, North Appletree, and East Troy.

What it quotes, which differs

Quoted passages on that page place the triangle at Troy, Appletree, and Museum streets, or at North Appletree, Museum, and Troy. One says the Camellia Garden Club honored the site in 1964 after Ripley’s Believe It or Not proclaimed it the world’s smallest, and that a granite marker dated May 1, 1964 says so. One says a snack shop was there in the 1920s and a service station in 1941. One says the Dothan Convention and Visitors Bureau says Guinness certified the record, and then says no online record was found to support that claim. It also says Dothan is not the only town that claims the smallest block.

What this guide dropped

An earlier version stated a Guinness record and a 1931 gas station as facts. The page above reports the Guinness claim as unsupported online, and the business date it quotes is 1941, not 1931. This guide does not call the block a Guinness record. The Dothan Eagle answer-man article, AL.com, and an Atlas Obscura page did not return readable copies, so they are not linked.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where do the accounts put the World’s Smallest City Block?

The World Record Academy page’s own wording uses North Appletree, North College, and East Troy. Quoted passages on the same page use Troy, Appletree, and Museum. This guide does not pick one set of street names.

Is it a Guinness record?

Not according to anything checked here. A passage quoted on that page says the Dothan visitors bureau claims a Guinness record and that no online record was found to support the claim.
