Karl Richter memorial

The National War Memorial Registry lists a statue of 1st Lt. Karl W. Richter at Maxwell Air Park on Maxwell Air Force Base in Montgomery, Alabama, zip code 36112. It classifies the memorial as a war or military statue, commemorating the Vietnam War, and lists access as military base.

The inscription on the listing

The registry prints this text with the listing: “1LT. KARL W. RICHTER / KILLED IN ACTION JULY 28, 1967 / NORTH VIETNAM / WHOM SHALL I SEND, AND WHO WILL GO FOR US? HERE AM I. SEND ME. ISAIAH 6:8.” It credits the photograph to William Fischer Jr. and the submission to HMDB.org. This guide did not get a readable copy of that marker page, so the marker page is not linked.

What this guide dropped

An earlier version called Richter the youngest American pilot to shoot down a MiG, gave a mission count, an age, an F-105, a sculptor, a dedication date, and a spot on Chennault Circle. None of that is in the registry listing. A Smithsonian art-inventory page returned an access error, and a Maxwell air-park tour PDF did not return a readable copy, so those details were removed rather than repeated.

Questions

Where is the Karl Richter memorial, and who can visit?

The National War Memorial Registry places the statue at Maxwell Air Park on Maxwell Air Force Base in Montgomery and lists access as military base.

What does the registry say was inscribed?

It prints that 1st Lt. Karl W. Richter was killed in action on July 28, 1967, in North Vietnam, and quotes Isaiah 6:8: “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Here am I. Send me.”
