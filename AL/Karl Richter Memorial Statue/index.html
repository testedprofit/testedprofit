Karl Richter memorial

The statue of 1st Lt. Karl W. Richter stands in Maxwell Air Park on Maxwell Air Force Base in Montgomery. Visitors without a Department of Defense ID need a visitor pass before they reach the park.

Plan your visit

Address
Maxwell Air Park, Maxwell Air Force Base, Montgomery, Alabama 36112.
Pass
Get a visitor pass at the Visitor Control Center beside the Maxwell Boulevard Gate. Hours are Monday through Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The center is closed weekends and federal holidays. Phone 334-953-4283.
What to bring
Since 7 May 2025 a pass requires a REAL ID-compliant credential, such as a starred driver's license or a U.S. passport. You also need a sponsor who is affiliated with the Department of Defense, and you must pass a background check. Drivers show vehicle registration and proof of insurance.
Gates
The Maxwell Boulevard Gate is open 24 hours. Expect slower traffic from about 7:45 to 8:15 a.m. When the visitor center is closed, passes are issued at the Maxwell Boulevard Main Gate or at the Cong. Dickenson Gate on Gunter Annex.
Tip
Ask your sponsor to meet you if you are a foreign national. That request goes to 42sfs.vcc.max@us.af.mil at least three weeks ahead.

Who Karl Richter was

Karl Wendell Richter was born in Holly, Michigan, on 4 October 1942, the youngest of three children. He entered the Air Force in 1964 and flew as a first lieutenant in the Vietnam War with the 421st Tactical Fighter Squadron of the 388th Tactical Fighter Wing. At 23 he became the youngest American pilot in that war to shoot down a MiG in air-to-air combat. He was killed in Vietnam on 28 July 1967, at 24. His grave is at the United States Air Force Academy Cemetery.

The Montgomery statue is a Vietnam War memorial at Maxwell Air Park. The inscription gives his rank and name, the date and place of his death, and the verse from Isaiah: Whom shall I send, and who will go for us? Here am I. Send me. His decorations included the Air Force Cross, the Silver Star, four Distinguished Flying Crosses, the Bronze Star, the Purple Heart, and 22 Air Medals, plus the Jabara Award.

The bronze shows him standing in a flight suit, helmet in one hand, on a stone base in the park. Read the name and the Isaiah verse, then leave under the same gate rules you used to come in.

Why the memorial is on a base

Maxwell is an Air Force installation, and the park sits inside the fence. The statue is for people who can enter the base, not for a downtown sidewalk. That is why the visit starts at the gate, not at the bronze. Montgomery's public statues, including the Hank Williams figure on Commerce Street and the Confederate Monument on Capitol Hill, are separate trips on the other side of town.

How to get on base

Anyone without a Defense Department credential stops at the Visitor Control Center next to the Maxwell Boulevard Gate. The center is open weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and closed on weekends and federal holidays. A pass needs three things: a REAL ID-compliant card or passport, a sponsor who already belongs to the Defense Department, and a background check. From 7 May 2025 the base will not issue a pass on a state license that lacks the REAL ID star. A passport or passport card works. Enhanced licenses from Washington, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, and Vermont are also accepted.

If you will drive onto the installation, bring the vehicle registration and proof of insurance when you ask for the pass. Every driver shows a credential at the gate. The morning rush at the gates runs about 7:45 to 8:15 a.m. The Maxwell Boulevard Gate itself stays open all night. The Day Street Gate is closed. The Kelly Street gate handles commercial vehicles on weekdays, inbound early and outbound late.

On a weekend or a federal holiday, when the visitor center is shut, the same pass can be issued at the Maxwell Boulevard Main Gate or at the Cong. Dickenson Gate on Gunter Annex. Foreign visitors use a different form. Email the Foreign National Installation Access Request to 42sfs.vcc.max@us.af.mil at least three weeks before the trip, clear the background check, and have the sponsor meet you at the visitor center.

What to do once you are in

Follow directions to Maxwell Air Park and look for the Richter statue and the Isaiah inscription. The figure is a military memorial, so the visit is short: read the name, the date, and the verse, then leave by the same gate rules you used to enter. Ride-share is limited. Uber can run on Maxwell and Gunter only with drivers who already hold a DBIDS credential, and every passenger still needs a valid Defense credential. Four Montgomery taxi companies also have base access: Checker Deluxe Cab, King's Airport Shuttle, On Time Taxi, and Triple A Taxi.

Questions

Where is the Karl Richter statue?

In Maxwell Air Park on Maxwell Air Force Base, Montgomery, Alabama 36112.

Who was Karl Richter?

A U.S. Air Force fighter pilot from Holly, Michigan. He was killed in Vietnam on 28 July 1967 at age 24, after becoming the youngest American pilot in that war to shoot down a MiG.

Can I visit without a military ID?

Yes, with a visitor pass from the Visitor Control Center beside the Maxwell Boulevard Gate, a REAL ID or passport, a Defense Department sponsor, and a background check.
