Albert Patterson statue

Troy University’s January 6, 2021 campus story describes a full-sized statue of Albert Patterson in Phenix City, completed by Phenix City native sculptor Jon Lumpkin. A photo caption in the same story spells his name John Lumpkin.

The commission

The statue was a project of the Alabama Bicentennial committee in Phenix City and Russell County. Troy University lists funding from the Russell County Commission, Russell County Tourism, the Alabama Department of Tourism, CTV Beam, WestRock, the Roy Green family, Troy Bank and Trust, and Troy University. The story says the sculptor worked from a clay model. It does not give the statue a street address, and it does not call the material bronze.

Albert Patterson

The Encyclopedia of Alabama describes Albert Patterson (1894–1954) as an attorney and the 1954 Democratic nominee for state attorney general. He promised to clean up Phenix City, in Russell County, which the encyclopedia says was largely run by organized crime and corrupt politicians. He was murdered on June 18, 1954, soon after his primary victory. His driver’s license listed his birth as January 27, 1894. Other sources, the encyclopedia notes, place the birth somewhere between 1891 and 1897.

Troy University’s story says he was assassinated outside his law office on June 18, 1954, steps away from the present-day Phenix City campus, shortly after he won the Democratic nomination. That sentence locates the killing relative to the campus. It does not say the statue stands a measured distance from the spot.
