Heaviest Corner on Earth

Four early steel-frame skyscrapers stand on the four corners of 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North in Birmingham. Together they are the Heaviest Corner on Earth, listed on the National Register on 11 July 1985.

Plan your visit

Place
The intersection of 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North, in downtown Birmingham.
The four corners
Woodward on the southwest, Empire on the northwest, Brown-Marx on the northeast, and the John A. Hand Building on the southeast.

Stand in the intersection's open space and look up. The photograph on this page is that view.

National Register, 11 July 1985. The city also lists the block as a historic district.

Four towers, one crossing

The Heaviest Corner on Earth is not a single building. It is the intersection itself, 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North, with an early steel-frame skyscraper on every corner. The National Register nomination calls the group a rare concentration of steel-frame towers on the city's first major intersection, built between 1902 and 1913. The National Park Service published the listing on 11 July 1985. Birmingham's own historic-district list carries the same name, the Heaviest Corner on Earth Historic District, at those four corners.

Stand in the crossing and look up. That is the view in the photograph from 5 February 2012: four facades against the sky, street signs and a signal in the foreground. The buildings are offices, not a museum. You are here for the outsides and for the fact that all four went up in one decade on one corner.

Walk the corners

Start on the southwest with the Woodward Building, 1927 1st Avenue North. It dates to 1902, with alterations in 1930. The style is Chicago Commercial: tan brick, terra cotta molding, ten stories arranged as a two-story base, a seven-story middle, and a one-story top under a classical cornice. William C. Weston of Birmingham designed it with the Stone Brothers of New Orleans. Purdy and Henderson of Chicago were the engineers, and John Griffiths and Sons of Chicago were the contractors.

The Brown-Marx Building, about 2000 1st Avenue North, is the northeast corner and the other Commercial-style block. It went up in 1906 and 1908, with more work in 1930, and it rises 16 stories in tan brick. Weston designed this one too. The lower floors were later refaced, and the copper-leaf cornice was removed in the 1970s. The steel frame, the brick piers, and the bands of windows are still the mass of the building. Inside, a marble lobby floor, a winding iron stair, and marble elevator walls remain.

The two later towers are dressed in classical ornament. The Empire Building, 1928 1st Avenue North, is the northwest corner, 16 stories sheathed in white and polychrome terra cotta. The room-by-room description of the district dates it to 1909. The statement of why the district matters dates the Empire to 1913 and names William Leslie Welton, with New York associates, as the architect. Both years are in the nomination. The John A. Hand Building, also called the First National Bank, is the southeast corner at 17 20th Street North. It is 1912, 21 stories, white glazed terra cotta, designed by Welton with Uffinger and Mowbray. A two-story wing came in 1940, and a five-story addition on the west in 1955.

Why the corner was listed

Three of the four were already on the National Register by themselves before the district was: the Empire on 19 March 1982, the Woodward on 30 June 1983, and the John A. Hand Building on 29 September 1983. The 1985 listing is the group. The nomination's specific dates read 1903 to 1913, and its significance paragraph says the four skyscrapers were built between 1902 and 1913. Weston and Welton are the two local architects, working with out-of-state firms on three of the buildings. Come for an hour, circle the intersection once, and you have seen the Heaviest Corner. The Forrest monument in Selma and the Rainbow Soldier in Montgomery are other Alabama stops, not this crossing.

Questions

Where is the Heaviest Corner on Earth?

At 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North in Birmingham, one early skyscraper on each corner.

When were the buildings put up?

Between 1902 and 1913. Woodward 1902, Brown-Marx 1906 and 1908, the Hand Building 1912. The Empire is dated 1909 in the building description and 1913 in the significance statement.

When was the district listed?

11 July 1985. Three of the buildings already had their own National Register listings.
