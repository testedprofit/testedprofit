Heaviest Corner on Earth

The National Register nomination for the Heaviest Corner on Earth places the district at the intersection of 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North in Birmingham. It is four early steel-frame skyscrapers, one on each corner. The National Park Service asset record gives the listing date as July 11, 1985. The City of Birmingham’s historic-district list calls it the Heaviest Corner on Earth Historic District, at the four corners of 20th Street and 1st Avenue North.

The four buildings

The nomination’s description dates them this way. The Woodward Building, 1927 1st Avenue North, on the southwest corner, is 1902, Chicago Commercial style, 10 stories. The Brown-Marx Building, about 2000 1st Avenue North, on the northeast corner, is 1906, 1908, and 1930, Chicago Commercial style, 16 stories. The Empire Building, 1928 1st Avenue North, on the northwest corner, is dated 1909 in that description, Neo-classical revival, 16 stories. The John A. Hand Building, also called the First National Bank, 17 20th Street North, on the southeast corner, is 1912 with a 1940 addition, Neo-classical revival, 21 stories.

The same form’s statement of significance says the four skyscrapers were built between 1902 and 1913, and its specific-dates line reads 1903–1913. In that statement the Empire Building is dated 1913, not 1909. This guide does not pick one Empire year. The statement calls the group a rare concentration of steel-frame skyscrapers on the city’s first major intersection.

Last updated 21 October 2026.

Questions

Where is the Heaviest Corner on Earth?

The National Register nomination places it at 1st Avenue North and 20th Street North in Birmingham. It is four early steel-frame skyscrapers, one on each corner, listed July 11, 1985.

When were the four buildings dated?

The nomination’s description dates the Woodward to 1902, the Brown-Marx to 1906 and 1908, the Empire to 1909, and the John A. Hand Building to 1912. Its significance statement dates the Empire to 1913 and the group to 1902–1913.
