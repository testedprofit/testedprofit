Civitas

Village Living says the Civitas statue stands at the corner of Cahaba Road and Fairway Drive in English Village, Mountain Brook. It was made to evoke Carolyn Cortner Smith, a self-taught architect, not to depict her literally. Project manager Philip Morris, a Mountain Brook resident and longtime editor of Southern Living, chose sculptor James Barnhill. Morris had seen Barnhill’s Echo sculpture at the Birmingham Botanical Gardens.

What the figure holds

Village Living says the name Civitas is the Latin word for citizens or a community. Morris wanted a symbol of “making a place.” In one hand she holds a compass, an architect’s tool, and in the other a miniature of the building behind her. At the base are miniatures of Smith’s home and the building where Billy’s was then located. Her dress is described as flapper style, for the era when Smith was designing houses. Morris said she is one and three-quarters life size, so she would be 9 feet tall if she stood up. The city budgeted $50,000. A group including Morris and Bob and Rebecca Moody raised about $20,000 more.

The year is not settled

That same Village Living article dates the work two ways. A caption says Morris and the Moodys took part in the commissioning and installation in 1998. A later sentence says Civitas was installed and dedicated in 1988. Bham Now says the statue was dedicated at the entrance of English Village in 1998, designed and cast by North Carolina sculptor James Barnhill, and that it recognizes Carolyn Smith. The Wikipedia page for James Barnhill lists Civitas (1997) in Mountain Brook. This guide does not pick one year.

Nimrod Long and Associates, the firm hired in 1995 to design streetscapes for Mountain Brook’s villages, says merchants in English Village commissioned the bronze figure Civitas to honor one of Alabama’s first female architects.

Questions

Where is Civitas, and who sculpted it?

Village Living places it at Cahaba Road and Fairway Drive in English Village and names sculptor James Barnhill. Nimrod Long and Associates describes it as a bronze figure commissioned by village merchants.

Which year was it installed?

Published dates disagree. Village Living says both 1998 and 1988. Bham Now says 1998. The artist’s Wikipedia page lists 1997. This guide does not choose among them.
