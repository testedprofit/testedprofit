Viola Liuzzo memorial

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says the marker honoring Viola Liuzzo is on U.S. Highway 80, about 20 miles east of Selma. It was erected in 1991 at the site where members of the Ku Klux Klan shot and killed her on March 25, 1965, after the Selma to Montgomery March. The encyclopedia says the memorial is one of several on the National Park Service’s Selma to Montgomery National Historic Trail.

What the Park Service page says

The National Park Service lists the memorial at 2093 U.S. Highway 80 East, Lowndesboro, Alabama 36040, and calls it a large stone memorial. It says Liuzzo was a 39-year-old mother of five from Detroit who came to Alabama after seeing the televised attack on marchers on Bloody Sunday. It says that on the night of March 25, 1965, she was driving Leroy Moton, and that Klansmen shot her. It also says one of the men in the car was Gary Rowe, a paid FBI informant, and that the FBI then spread false claims about Liuzzo.

Lowndes County’s visiting page says the memorial is in Lowndesboro, off U.S. Highway 80 East, next to Wright Chapel AME Zion Church. It calls Liuzzo a Detroit native who was transporting people who had marched to Montgomery back to Selma. That direction of travel is not the same sentence as the Park Service page, which says she was driving toward Montgomery to pick up more marchers. This guide does not choose between them. The county page calls the memorial a statue. The encyclopedia calls it a marker. The Park Service calls it a large stone memorial.

Questions

Where is the Viola Liuzzo memorial?

The Encyclopedia of Alabama places it on U.S. Highway 80 about 20 miles east of Selma. The National Park Service lists 2093 U.S. Highway 80 East, Lowndesboro, Alabama 36040. Lowndes County says it is next to Wright Chapel AME Zion Church.

When was Viola Liuzzo killed, and when was the marker erected?

The encyclopedia says she was shot and killed by members of the Ku Klux Klan on March 25, 1965, and that the marker was erected in 1991.
