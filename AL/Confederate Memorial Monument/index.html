Confederate Monument on Capitol Hill

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says the 88-foot Alabama Confederate Monument on Montgomery’s Capitol Hill commemorates the 122,000 Alabamians who fought for the Confederacy. The Ladies Memorial Association of Montgomery raised the $47,000 to build it. It was dedicated on December 7, 1898.

Who made which part

Jefferson Davis laid the cornerstone on April 29, 1886, before a crowd of 5,000. New York sculptor Alexander Doyle designed the monument. His bronze finial is the figure Patriotism, and his bronze battle relief encircles the column. The four heroic-scale granite figures, Infantry, Artillery, Cavalry, and Navy, are by Frederick Barnicoat of Quincy, Massachusetts. The encyclopedia says the Huntsville Weekly Democrat reported on July 20, 1898, that Barnicoat’s four statues were completed and accepted.

The stepped base used Alabama limestone from a quarry near Russellville. The encyclopedia says conservation analysis found that the limestone in the shaft came from Indiana, not Alabama, even though the Ladies Memorial Association had promoted the monument as native stone.

Flags removed in 2015

On June 24, 2015, Governor Robert Bentley ordered four Confederate flags removed from the monument.

Questions

How tall is the Confederate Monument, and when was it dedicated?

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says it is 88 feet tall, on Montgomery’s Capitol Hill, and was dedicated on December 7, 1898.

Who made the sculpture?

Alexander Doyle designed the monument and made the bronze finial Patriotism and the bronze battle relief. Frederick Barnicoat made the four granite figures of Infantry, Artillery, Cavalry, and Navy.
