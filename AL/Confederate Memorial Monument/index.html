Confederate Monument on Capitol Hill

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says the 88-foot Confederate Monument on Montgomery's Capitol Hill commemorates the 122,000 Alabamians who fought for the Confederacy. The Ladies Memorial Association of Montgomery raised the money. The monument was dedicated on 7 December 1898. On 24 June 2015, Governor Robert Bentley ordered four Confederate flags removed from it.

What the monument was built to say

The encyclopedia says the monument commemorates the 122,000 Alabamians who fought for the Confederacy during the Civil War. Fundraising began in 1865 and was largely the work of the Ladies Memorial Association of Montgomery. The association raised $47,000 through events and appeals to private donors and the state government. The dedication on 7 December 1898 included orations before each of the four granite figures was unveiled, and a tableau of 13 girls representing the states of the Confederacy.

The same article traces an earlier organization. In November 1865 the Historical and Monumental Association of Alabama resolved to ask the legislature for $5,000 for a marble monument inscribed Alabama honors her sons who died in her service. In the spring of 1866, appeals from Winchester, Virginia, about Alabama dead in shallow graves delayed the monument. On 16 April 1866 the Ladies Society for the Burial of Deceased Alabama Soldiers formed, soon renamed the Ladies Memorial Association, chaired by Sophie Lucy Ann Gilmer Bibb. The encyclopedia says that under her leadership the association raised more than $5,000 at a May Day festival and sent money for Confederate graves at Richmond, Fredericksburg, and Spotsylvania, Virginia; Jonesboro and Resaca, Georgia; Franklin, Tennessee; and Corinth, Mississippi. In 1868 it committed $700 for a marble obelisk, pavilion, and grave chart at Montgomery's Oakwood Cemetery, and from 1876 to 1882 it placed granite stones on soldiers' graves there.

Who made which part

In 1882 the association began raising money for a monument on Capitol Hill. In 1885 Montgomery men formed a Historical and Monumental Society chaired by Mayor Warren Reese, a Confederate veteran. The encyclopedia says that group apparently contracted with New York sculptor Alexander Doyle for a design with Jefferson Davis seated at the base of a shaft, surrounded by figures of the four military branches. Jefferson Davis laid the cornerstone on 29 April 1886 before about 5,000 people. The men's group then gave the association the lead and the $6,755 it had raised.

The stepped base and the pedestals used Alabama limestone from the Rockwood quarry near Russellville. The shaft stone was a separate problem. The encyclopedia says documentation is incomplete, that stone delivered in 1888 seems to have been unsatisfactory, and that scientific conservation analysis found the shaft limestone came from Indiana, not Alabama, including Bedford, Indiana, where Doyle's father owned a quarry. The association had promoted the monument as native stone. The article says it is unclear whether the association knew the shaft stone's origin, and that the relationship with Doyle soured. Doyle defended his contract and threatened suit as late as 1898.

In 1893 the association contracted through the local dealer Curbow and Clapp for heroic granite figures from Frederick Barnicoat of Quincy, Massachusetts. On 20 July 1898 the Huntsville Weekly Democrat reported that Barnicoat's four statues were completed and accepted. The finished monument has Doyle's bronze finial, Patriotism, his bronze battle relief around the column, and Barnicoat's Infantry, Artillery, Cavalry, and Navy.

Flags removed in 2015

The encyclopedia says that on 24 June 2015 Governor Robert Bentley ordered four Confederate flags removed from the monument amid a growing national controversy over displaying the flag in public spaces. That sentence is the article's account of the flags. It does not say the shaft or the figures were removed.

The article was written by Michael W. Panhorst and updated 4 February 2025. The map pin is the capitol building. The monument is the 88-foot work the article places on Capitol Hill, not a different capitol statue.

Questions

How tall is the Confederate Monument, and when was it dedicated?

The Encyclopedia of Alabama says it is 88 feet tall on Montgomery's Capitol Hill and was dedicated on 7 December 1898.

Who made the figures?

Alexander Doyle designed the monument and made the bronze finial Patriotism and the bronze battle relief. Frederick Barnicoat made the four granite figures of Infantry, Artillery, Cavalry, and Navy.

What happened to the flags in 2015?

The encyclopedia says Governor Robert Bentley ordered four Confederate flags removed from the monument on 24 June 2015.
