Woods Quad Sculpture Garden

The University of Alabama Department of Art and Art History says the Woods Quad Sculpture Garden was officially dedicated in August 2014 by then-Dean Robert Olin. The college’s August 11, 2014 announcement set the dedication for August 15, from 11:30 a.m. to 1 p.m., and said the garden was designed for 17 sculpture projects, with walks, up-lighting, benches, and plantings.

Works the department still lists

The department’s public-sculpture page lists Ringo Lisko’s Flourish on the east side of the quad’s center, unveiled in November 2021, the first sculpture in the McMahon-Pleiad Public Art Trail. A department article calls Flourish an eight-foot cast-bronze crescent of camellias, Alabama’s state flower, on a stainless-steel frame, with 93 blossoms. Billy Lee’s Homage to Brancusi, the oldest sculpture still in the quad, won the 1993 Alabama Biennial purchase award and stands in the southeast corner, in the crook of Garland Hall. It is painted steel, about 12 feet by 28.5 inches by 15 inches, not counting the base. Joe McCreary’s Goldie 1971, from 2009, is iron and steel, about 23 feet long, 4 feet thick, and 5 feet wide, between 3 and 4 tons. It commemorates Sloss Furnaces, was bought in 2010, and lies in front of Woods Hall at the northeast corner.

The same page lists Craig Wedderspoon’s Riverstone, 2012, cast bronze, 54 inches on a side, at the southwest corner by Presidents Hall, and Lindsay Jones Lindsey’s Fibonacci Spiral in stainless steel. The center holds Wedderspoon’s aluminum Quilted Vessel, over six feet high.
