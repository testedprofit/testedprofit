Man and WomanFernando Botero's bronzes in the Denver galleria

Two bronze figures by Fernando Botero stand in the galleria of the Denver Performing Arts Complex, at 14th and Curtis streets, outside the Temple Hoyne Buell Theatre. Denver Arts and Venues lists them as a 1998 public sculpture. You do not need a ticket to walk up to them.

What you'll see

Denver Arts and Venues lists Man and Woman as bronze sculpture by Fernando Botero in the Performing Arts Complex galleria, dated 1998. A Denver Post account says the 1998 bronzes stand in alcoves along the promenade in front of the Buell Theatre and entered Denver's public art collection in 2000. Visit Denver places them with theatergoers outside the Temple Hoyne Buell Theatre.

The figures use Botero's exaggerated, rounded proportions. Published heights do not agree. The Denver Gazette and Go Play Denver describe them as about 13 feet tall. A 2023 303 Magazine article said about 8 feet. The Arts and Venues guide does not list a height, so this page does not treat either number as official.

The date is also reported more than one way. The city guide dates the bronzes 1998. The Denver Post calls them 1998 bronzes that entered the public collection in 2000. A September 15, 2023 Denver Gazette article says the sculptures were installed in 1988 and, in the same story, that they have greeted visitors since 1983. 303 Magazine also says 1983.

Visiting tips

  • Use them as the meeting point: Denver Arts and Venues public-art walks start at Man and Woman, in the galleria near 14th and Curtis.
  • No ticket for the sculptures: A performance ticket is not required to see the bronzes. The city guide does not publish separate viewing hours.
  • Street address: 303 Magazine and this page use 998 14th St. That address geocodes to the 14th and Curtis block.
  • Another Botero on this site: The Madre e Hijo page covers a different Botero sculpture, not this pair.

Man and Woman FAQ

Where are Botero's Man and Woman sculptures in Denver?

In the Denver Performing Arts Complex galleria at 14th and Curtis streets. A street address used for the spot is 998 14th St, Denver, CO 80202. Denver Arts and Venues tours meet at the sculptures, and Visit Denver places them outside the Temple Hoyne Buell Theatre.

Who made Man and Woman, and when?

Fernando Botero. The Denver Arts and Venues downtown guide lists the bronzes in the galleria in 1998. A Denver Post account says the 1998 bronzes stand in alcoves in front of the Buell Theatre and entered the city's public art collection in 2000. A 2023 Denver Gazette story says they were installed in 1988 and also says they have stood there since 1983. 303 Magazine says 1983.

What are Man and Woman made of?

Bronze. That is the material listed by Denver Arts and Venues.

Do I need a ticket to see Man and Woman?

No ticket is required to see the sculptures. They stand in the public galleria. The city guide does not list separate viewing hours.

How tall are the Man and Woman sculptures?

Published heights do not match. The Denver Gazette and Go Play Denver describe them as about 13 feet tall. A 2023 303 Magazine article said about 8 feet. The Arts and Venues guide does not list a height.
