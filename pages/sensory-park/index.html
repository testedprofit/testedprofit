Westminster Sensory Park

Westminster Sensory Park is the accessible playground at Wadsworth Boulevard and West 103rd Avenue in Westminster, Colorado. A search for sensory park westminster, or for a playground near me on the west side, leads to this corner. It is a free outdoor play area built with Children's Hospital Colorado, and it is the city's first completely accessible playground.

Where to find the playground

Westminster Sensory Park sits in the west part of the city, near North Wadsworth Boulevard and West 103rd Avenue. The City of Westminster playground inventory gives the address as 10376 Wadsworth Blvd., year built 2000, west district. In July 2020, parks spokeswoman Angela Simental placed it at 7577 W 103rd Ave. A visit written up on June 11, 2018 used that same 7577 W 103rd Ave. address, ZIP 80021. Barbara O'Neil's April 16, 2024 guide in 5280 uses 7355 W 103rd Ave. and places the playground a few miles from Children's Hospital Colorado's North Campus. Use the equipment at that corner. The map pin, 39.88485, -105.07867, sits on the play surface in the city inventory.

The lot is not on the front of the shops. The 2018 visit notes that you drive behind a few storefront buildings to reach the park, and that a trail also comes in. If the street number on your map is 10376 Wadsworth, 7577 W 103rd, or 7355 W 103rd, you are looking for the same playground.

Sensory and inclusive play

Children's Hospital Colorado approached the city with funds in 1998 to create an accessible play area. A grant paid for the rest of the project. The city playground record gives the year built as 2000. A January 19, 2012 Colorado Community Media report says Westminster and Children's Hospital built the playground that year so children with mobility needs or other special needs would have equipment. The hospital therapy center then sat at 7577 W 103rd Ave., across from the park.

The 5280 guide describes supportive swings and an undulating walkway designed for wheelchairs. Steps and paths are painted for people with visual impairments. Sand pits and brightly colored decorative fish add to the sensory play. Colorado Parent's February 5, 2021 guide, by Anna Sutterer, describes ramps through the equipment, adaptive swings, a sand pit, instruments, a water area, and a wavy sidewalk for motor skills and perception. That guide uses the address 10376 Wadsworth Blvd.

The city inventory records two play surfaces, engineered wood fiber and unitary, and a Replace Sand / EWF project finished in August 2016. The 2024 guide still describes sand pits. If sand is something your child seeks, or something they need to avoid, look at the ground when you arrive. The wood-fiber and unitary areas are the surfaces on the city record.

Accessibility

In the July 22, 2020 Colorado Community Media story, Angela Simental called Sensory Park an ADA-compliant, therapy-focused playground that is still fun for every child. She called it the city's first completely accessible play area, built in cooperation with Children's Hospital Colorado, and easy to reach from the local trail system. A June 2018 visit found that same trail coming into the park.

For a child who uses a wheelchair, the published pieces to look for are the undulating walkway, the ramps through the equipment, and the supportive or adaptive swings. For a child with low vision, the painted steps and paths are the detail the 2024 guide names. The trail is a second way in, and a way to step away from the equipment without getting back in the car.

Amenities, hours, and parking

There is no admission fee. Westminster Sensory Park is an outdoor city playground, so you park and walk in. The June 2018 visit found a parking lot, a playground, a sand play area, picnic tables, and a covered shelter. It also found a drinking fountain and no bathrooms. Colorado Parent in 2021 likewise notes that there are no bathrooms, so plan a restroom stop before you arrive. The shelter and picnic tables are the place for a snack if a child needs a break off the equipment.

Parking is the lot behind the storefronts at Wadsworth and West 103rd. Drive past the shops and you are still going the right way. Trail users can come in on foot and skip the lot.

Tips for families with sensory needs

Start with the feature that matches the child. Supportive swings and adaptive swings hold a child more securely than a belt swing. The wheelchair walkway and the ramps are the route onto the equipment. Painted paths help a child who uses contrast more than small print. Sand, decorative fish, instruments, and the water area described in 2021 are the louder sensory pieces. A child who is overwhelmed by sand or water can stay on the wood-fiber and unitary surfaces, or walk the trail.

Build in an exit before you need one. There is no restroom on the visits from 2018 and 2021, and the drinking fountain is the water that is there for drinking. Use the covered shelter when the equipment is too much. The playground is free, so a short visit is a complete visit. If this corner is busy, the other Westminster park guides below are the next places to try.

Other Westminster parks

Sunset Park, Central Plaza Park, Torii Square, and Oakhurst II are the other Westminster guides on this site. Sunset Park is another city playground. Central Plaza Park is the downtown plaza. Torii Square is the park at 76th Avenue and Lowell Boulevard. Oakhurst II is a multi-use grass field. None of them replaces the accessible equipment at Westminster Sensory Park, and each is a different kind of stop if you want a change of scene.

Questions

Where is Westminster Sensory Park?

Westminster Sensory Park is the playground at Wadsworth Boulevard and West 103rd Avenue in Westminster, CO 80021. The address on the city playground record is 10376 Wadsworth Blvd., in the west district. What inclusive and sensory features does the playground have?

Colorado Parent in February 2021 describes ramps through the equipment, adaptive swings, a sand pit, instruments, a water area, and a wavy sidewalk. Is Westminster Sensory Park accessible?

Yes. Parks spokeswoman Angela Simental, in July 2020, called it an ADA-compliant, therapy-focused playground and the city's first completely accessible play area, built with Children's Hospital Colorado. It is easy to reach from the local trail. Supportive swings and a wheelchair walkway are part of the 5280 description.

Is there a fee, and where is parking?

There is no admission fee. It is an outdoor city playground. A June 2018 visit found a parking lot behind the storefronts at the corner, plus trail access. Park in that lot and walk to the equipment.

Are there restrooms and other amenities?

The June 2018 visit found a drinking fountain, picnic tables, and a covered shelter, and no bathrooms. Colorado Parent in February 2021 also notes there are no bathrooms. Plan a restroom stop before you arrive.
