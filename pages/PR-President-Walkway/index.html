Walkway of the Presidents

The English Wikipedia article on the Capitol of Puerto Rico lists a Walkway of the Presidents, meaning presidents who have visited Puerto Rico, among the monuments in the landscaped park on the south side of the capitol.

Plan your visit

Address
Wikipedia places the park between Juan Ponce de León Avenue on the north and Paseo Covadonga on the south, and between Calle Esteves on the east and Calle Gral. Contreras on the west. The walkway is one monument in that park.
Parking
The capitol article does not name a garage for the walkway. The pin on this page is the capitol building, so plan the stop as a visit to the capitol grounds.
Hours
The article does not print gate hours for the landscaped park.
Accessibility
The article describes an elongated landscaped park facing the south façade. It does not describe ramps or restrooms at the walkway.
Best time
Daylight makes the south façade and the line of monuments easier to tell apart. The article does not name a season.

Where the walkway sits

The capitol article describes an elongated landscaped park in the center of the capitol building complex. It faces the rear, south, façade. The park runs between Juan Ponce de León Avenue on the north and Paseo Covadonga, the Covadonga Promenade, on the south. East to west, it runs from Calle Esteves to Calle Gral. Contreras.

The article says the park contains memorials, sculptures, statues, and plaques to people and events in Puerto Rican history, and that it serves as a commemorative center of patriotism and civic virtues. From east to west, it names the most prominent monuments in this order: the Rafael Hernández Marín Monument, the Santiago Iglesias Pantín Monument, the Altar to the Nation, the Carlos Luis Cáceres Collazo Monument, the Walkway of the Presidents (who have visited Puerto Rico), the Recipients of Medal of Honor Monument, the World War I Monument, the Memorial Monument, the Holocaust Memorial, the Teachers Monument, Lions' Plaza, the Police Monument, the Puerto Rican Woman Monument, and the Ramón Mellado Parsons Monument.

That parenthetical is the article's definition of the walkway: presidents who have visited Puerto Rico. The article does not print a dedication year, a sculptor's name, a count of figures, or the metal.

How to use the list

If you are on the south side of the capitol, the article's east-to-west order is the finding aid. The walkway is the name that comes after the Carlos Luis Cáceres Collazo Monument and before the Recipients of Medal of Honor Monument. The same paragraph says the park connects toward the San Juan Cruise Port on San Juan Bay from the Memorial Monument through Calle Gral. Contreras. That connection is about the park, not a claim that the walkway itself is the port.

The map pin is the OpenStreetMap place for the Capitol of Puerto Rico, at 18.4667626, -66.1059339. It is the building the park faces. It is not a separate surveyed node named for the walkway.

What this page does not add

The subject of this URL stays the Walkway of the Presidents. The locator in the capitol article is the monument list and the four streets around the landscaped park. The same article says the park connects toward the San Juan Cruise Port on San Juan Bay from the Memorial Monument through Calle Gral. Contreras. That path is a way across the grounds. It is a separate monument from the walkway.

Just before the landscaped-park section, the article describes a square whose center has a large abstract bronze of John the Baptist on a low semicircular pedestal with the coats of arms of Puerto Rico's 78 municipalities. That square is not the walkway. The walkway is the later name in the east-to-west list on the south side of the capitol. Keeping the two descriptions apart is how the article itself is organized: one square with John the Baptist, then the elongated park that includes the Walkway of the Presidents.

Questions

Where is the Walkway of the Presidents?

The English Wikipedia article on the Capitol of Puerto Rico lists it in the landscaped park facing the south façade, between Juan Ponce de León Avenue and Paseo Covadonga.

What does the article say the walkway commemorates?

The article's own parenthesis says presidents who have visited Puerto Rico.

Which monuments does the article place beside it?

From east to west, the Carlos Luis Cáceres Collazo Monument comes before the walkway and the Recipients of Medal of Honor Monument comes after it.
