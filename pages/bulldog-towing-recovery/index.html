After a breakdown or a slide-off

Craig sits in Moffat County. Wikipedia lists U.S. Route 40 and Colorado Highway 13 as the city's major highways. US 40 is the east-west road through town. CO 13 is the north-south road. A winter slide on either one is a highway problem first and a tow problem second.

If anyone is hurt, call 911 before you call a tow company. If the vehicle can leave the travel lane, move it there. Put the hazard lights on. Stay out of the lane. Do not stand between your car and traffic while you wait. Note the highway, the direction you were heading, and the nearest mile marker or crossroad so the driver is not guessing.

Then call the number the listings show if you want this shop, and ask plain questions before the truck rolls:

  • Where you are, including US 40 or CO 13 and a mile marker if you have one.
  • What the vehicle is, and whether it is in a lane, on a shoulder, or off the road in snow or a ditch.
  • Where you want it taken, a Craig shop or a lot in town.
  • What the hookup will cost, and whether a second stop or a wait changes the price.
  • How long the drive is from Ranney Street in the weather you are in.

Listings describe towing and road assistance. They do not print a menu of winching, fuel delivery, or tire changes. Ask on the phone. If the answer is no, you still have the location and the time.

Winter roads: COtrip and 511

The Colorado Department of Transportation tells drivers to prepare the vehicle and the tires for Colorado winter law, and it publishes passenger-vehicle traction and chain laws. It also says to stay with current conditions. Call 511 for road and weather information, or open COtrip.org. That is the check before you restart a trip on US 40 or CO 13, and it is the check if you are already stopped and deciding whether to wait for a plow.

CDOT's winter page covers snow removal and, on the mountain corridors it names, avalanche work. Craig is not the I-70 canyon. The same habit still applies: look at COtrip for the highway you are on, not for a forecast from yesterday. A tow truck cannot fix a closed road. If COtrip shows a closure, stay put or turn around where the signs say, and tell the tow driver the closure when you call.

Craig around the shop

Ranney Street is in Craig, the Moffat County seat, in the same town as the Yampa River and Fortification Creek. The listed pin is on that street, not a promise that a truck is in the bay when you knock. Listings are directories. The phone is how you learn if someone is rolling.

The City of Craig publishes its offices at 300 W. 4th Street, Craig, CO 81625, phone (970) 826-2000. The city site lists Police, Public Works, and Road and Bridge among its departments. Those are the municipal desks for a city street, a water problem, or a local road question. A highway closure still belongs on COtrip or 511, and an injury still belongs with 911. A private tow is the listed shop number, not the city switchboard.

If you are waiting in town, two other Craig guides on this site are not tow shops. Woodbury Park is the city sports park, entrance on W. 2nd Street and Mack Lane. Cedar Mountain is the ridge near Craig. The county list is the Colorado directory.

Treat those as directory text. They are not a warranty from this guide, and they are not the company writing about itself.

Questions

Is this the Bulldog Towing website?

No. This is an independent local guide on Profitnet. It is not the company site. Call the number listings show if you need a truck.

What phone and address do listings show?

Trucker Guide and Loc8NearMe both list 21 S Ranney St, Craig, CO 81625, and the phone (970) 824-3733. Trucker Guide also shows a map pin at 40.50536, -107.55258.

Do listings show 24 hour towing in Craig?

Yes. Trucker Guide marks the shop open 24 hours, and its hours run 12:00 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. every day. Loc8NearMe lists Monday through Sunday as open 24 hours. Call before you count on a truck.

What should I do if I slide off US 40 or CO 13?

If anyone is hurt, call 911. If you can, get the vehicle out of the travel lane, put the hazards on, and stay out of the lane. Check COtrip.org or call 511 for road and weather conditions. Then call a tow number the listings show. Tell them where you are, what the vehicle is, and where it should go.
