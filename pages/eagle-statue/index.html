Eagle at Denver West PlazaLakewood, and the Denver eagle it is not

Searches for an American eagle statue mix two places. The eagle that published guides actually document stands on Denver's City and County Building. This page does not turn that record into a sculptor, date, or street address for Denver West Plaza.

What you'll see

If you follow the documented eagle, you are looking at the tower of the City and County Building, finished in 1932. The Colorado Encyclopedia says the tower is crowned by an eagle with outspread wings. SAH Archipedia calls it a gold eagle and says that eagle and the carillon clock tower were donated in Mayor Robert Speer's memory by his widow, Kate.

9NEWS, published August 2, 2017, names Joseph Nicolosi as the sculptor and says he was chosen sometime between the March 26, 1929 groundbreaking and completion in 1932. The same report says the wings-up eagle was already in the Allied Architects drawing signed June 30, 1925, and in the model the architects showed the city. Mayor Benjamin Stapleton, not Speer, shepherded the building. Speer died in 1918.

Denver West Plaza is a different place. Public Art Archive's record titled Eagle is filed under Denver Public Art and, in the text available on October 21, 2026, does not give a Lakewood address. A Denver Public Art URL still indexed for an eagle returned "page not found" that day, and an older collection page said the item is not available for view. This guide does not copy artist, material, or gilding lines from those closed pages onto either eagle.

The building

Encyclopedia address: 300 W. Colfax Avenue. SAH and Wikipedia: 1437 Bannock Street. The encyclopedia says the block is bounded by West Colfax and West 14th Avenues between Bannock and Cherokee.

The pose

9NEWS asked Denver Zoo bird curator John Azua. He said the sculpture looks like an eagle taking off, and that eagles can defecate from many positions.

The plaza

No sculptor, year, or material for a Denver West Plaza eagle appears in the pages opened for this guide. The old street address on this page is a Best Buy listing, not a catalog location.

Visiting tips

  • Use the Civic Center pin for the documented eagle. Wikipedia's building coordinates are 39.73944, -104.98889. The encyclopedia and SAH disagree on which street number to print, and both numbers are listed above.
  • Do not navigate to 1400 Denver West-Marriott Blvd for a cataloged statue. Bluepages lists a Best Buy at that Lakewood address. None of the art pages opened here use it.
  • Read the legend next to the check. The encyclopedia still repeats a story that the pose insults the building. 9NEWS calls that legend false.
  • No ticket is listed for the tower view. These sources describe the eagle on the building. They do not give hours or an admission price for looking at it from Civic Center.

Eagle statue FAQ

Is the Denver West Plaza eagle the one on Denver's City and County Building?

No. The Colorado Encyclopedia and SAH Archipedia place an eagle on the tower of the City and County Building in Civic Center. Public Art Archive has a record titled Eagle in Denver under Denver Public Art. None of those pages, as opened on October 21, 2026, describe a separate eagle at Denver West Plaza in Lakewood.

Who made the City and County Building eagle?

9NEWS, in an August 2, 2017 Verify report, names the sculptor as Joseph Nicolosi and says he was picked sometime between the 1929 groundbreaking and the building's 1932 completion. The same report says an eagle with its wings up was already in the Allied Architects drawing signed and dated June 30, 1925, and in the model shown to the city.

Where do you go to see that documented eagle?

The Colorado Encyclopedia gives the building as 300 W. Colfax Avenue. SAH Archipedia and Wikipedia give 1437 Bannock Street. Wikipedia's coordinates are 39.73944, -104.98889. The map on this page uses that building pin. It is not a surveyed pin for Denver West Plaza.

Was the tower eagle posed as an insult to Mayor Speer?

The Colorado Encyclopedia repeats that folklore. 9NEWS, published August 2, 2017, calls the legend false. Mayor Robert Speer died in 1918, before the 1925 design. The wings-up pose was already in that 1925 drawing. Denver Zoo bird curator John Azua told 9NEWS the pose looks like an eagle taking off, and that eagles can defecate from many positions.

Who gave the gold eagle and the clock tower?

SAH Archipedia says the gold eagle and the carillon clock tower were donated in Mayor Speer's memory by his widow, Kate. The Colorado Encyclopedia says Kate Speer donated the clock and chimes, and it does not say she donated the eagle.
