The bald eagle as an American symbol

The bald eagle statue you came to see sits in a long national story. On June 20, 1782, the bald eagle was adopted as the coat of arms for the Great Seal of the United States. Public Law 118-206, also S. 4610, was approved on December 23, 2024. It added section 306 to title 36 of the United States Code: The bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) is the national bird.

Congress wrote down why. The bird is unique to North America. Its image is the leading insignia of all branches of the United States military, and it appears on hundreds of United States stamps and on many coins. Bald eagles are integral to the spiritual lives of most Indigenous peoples and Tribal communities. The law also records that the bird's recovery came from joint work by the federal government, state and local governments, nonprofit organizations, and individuals.

Other eagle statues near Denver

Joseph Nicolosi's Eagle is a 1932 bronze on the Denver City and County Building in the Civic Center neighborhood. Denver Public Art lists the material as bronze and says the eagle on top of that building was painted in 23 karat gold leaf in 1949. That is a large bronze eagle statue in Denver, not this Lakewood figure. The two places are miles apart. The Bronco Buster in Civic Center Park is the other bronze on that Denver lawn, and it is a cowboy on a horse, not an eagle.
