Albert Einstein Statue (the Einstein Bench) in Vail Village

Gary Lee Price's bronze Albert Einstein statue sits on a bench in front of the Sitzmark in Vail Village. It is free to visit, and it has been in the Town of Vail Art in Public Places collection since a 2014 donation. Here is how to find it, whether you came for an Einstein statue, an Einstein sculpture, or a statue Einstein in the mountains.

What you will see

The Albert Einstein statue in Vail is a bronze of Albert Einstein seated on a bench. Guides also call it an Albert Einstein sculpture, an Albert Einstein monument, Albert Einstein Colorado, and the Albert Einstein bench sculpture Vail. Vail Daily, on October 9, 2014, calls the work Einstein by Gary Lee Price and places it in his Great Contributors series. Discover Vail names other figures in that series around the country, including Abraham Lincoln, Amelia Earhart, Harriet Tubman, George Washington, Joan of Arc, Claude Monet, and Mark Twain.

Price's studio page dates the bronze editions to 2005. The larger bronze is listed at 52 inches high, 70 inches wide, and 60 inches deep, and the page also lists an 18-inch bronze. On that page he writes that the figure sits relaxed in the park and that he wanted an approachable, come-have-a-chat-with-a-genius welcome. What inspired the piece, in his telling, includes the line Imagination is more important than knowledge. Vail Daily quotes him on Einstein's hair, facial features, and casual attire, and on Einstein's efforts toward peace and understanding.

Vail Daily says Price graduated from the University of Utah in 1981 in painting and drawing, joined the National Sculpture Society ten years later, and received Utah's Governor's Mansion Artist Award in 2001. Both Vail Daily and Discover Vail call this one of the most photographed sculptures in Vail. The town collection, in the 2014 article, held more than 40 works, from sculptures and murals to playground pieces. Discover Vail's Art in Public Places page, a separate tourism page, says the program includes more than 70 pieces and names a bronze Albert Einstein relaxing on a bench.

Why is there an Einstein statue in Vail?

Why is there an Albert Einstein statue in Vail? Bob Fritch of Sitzmark Vail told Vail Daily the sculpture was put in front of the hotel and became popular. Helen and Bob Fritch then donated it to the Town of Vail Art in Public Places collection. Who owns the bench now is the Town of Vail Art in Public Places. Coordinator Molly Eppard said that, unless something unforeseen happened, it would stay where it had been, in front of the Sitzmark. That is the reason this Einstein statue is here: a hotel gift that the town agreed to keep.

Vail Winterfest ice sculptures

The same Art in Public Places program presents the annual Vail Winterfest, and the ice sculptures Vail, CO are a short walk from this bench. The town news of January 15, 2026 describes the 19th edition, theme Gnome Home, on the Gore Creek Promenade by the International Bridge in Vail Village. Paul Wertin and Alpine Ice installed 25,000 pounds of sculpted ice. Sculpting began January 14, 2026. The opening and lighting were January 25, 2026. Guests can walk the ice by day or from dusk until 10 p.m., and Gnome Home stays up until the ice melts. The opening celebration was free.

Those details belong to the 2026 winter. The town release is the source for the pounds of ice, the artists, the January dates, and the dusk-to-10 lighting. Questions

Where is the Albert Einstein statue in Vail?

In front of the Sitzmark at 183 Gore Creek Drive, Vail, Colorado 81657, in Vail Village. The map pin is 39.640891, -106.375169. Vail Daily said on October 9, 2014 that the sculpture had stood there for many years and that the town planned to leave it there.

Why is there an Einstein statue in Vail, Colorado?

Sitzmark Vail put Gary Lee Price's bronze in front of the hotel and it became popular. Bob and Helen Fritch donated it to the Town of Vail Art in Public Places collection in 2014. The story is that gift, not a visit by Einstein to Vail.

Can you sit on the Einstein bench?

Yes. Discover Vail invites visitors to take a seat with him. Price wrote that the figure sits relaxed and that he wanted a come-have-a-chat-with-a-genius welcome.

Who made the Einstein statue?

Gary Lee Price. Vail Daily and Discover Vail call it a bronze titled Einstein, from his Great Contributors series. His studio dates the bronze editions to 2005 and lists a 52-inch bronze at 52 by 70 by 60 inches, plus an 18-inch bronze.

When are the Vail ice sculptures up?

The Town of Vail Art in Public Places presents Vail Winterfest each winter on the Gore Creek Promenade. For 2026, sculpting began January 14, the opening and lighting were January 25, and the ice is illuminated daily from dusk to 10 p.m. until it melts.

Is it free?

Yes. The Einstein bench is outdoor public art. The 2026 Winterfest opening celebration was also free.
