Bronco Buster Statue (Broncho Buster) in Civic Center Park, Denver

The Bronco Buster statue in Denver Civic Center is A. Phimister Proctor's 1920 bronze of a cowboy on a bucking horse in Civic Center Park, a gift of J.K. Mullen. Here is how to see it. The visit is free. This is not the Denver Broncos horse. Alexander Phimister Proctor is who made the bronze.

Directions

The 1920 bronze

What you see is a cowboy on a bucking horse, cast in bronze. Denver Public Art says the original plaster was made for the Chicago exhibition of 1893, titled The Buckaroo, later given to Denver, where it deteriorated. Proctor was then commissioned to make a bronze. That plaster was slightly changed for the Denver monument, titled The Broncho Buster. A van der Krogt directory visit on 17 October 2010 records the inscription BRONCHO BUSTER, presented to Denver by J.K. Mullen, A.D. 1920, and the signature a. phimister proctor. Denver Public Art also notes the marks signed A.P. Proctor and a trademark coin.

In the mid-1910s, at the request of Mayor Robert Speer, two large bronzes in Civic Center were given by two residents. The second Proctor bronze is On the War Trail, dated 1922 by the conservancy and presented by Stephan Knight. It shows an American Indian riding bareback with a spear. Find the pair when you walk the park. Denver Public Art says other casts from Proctor's lifetime can be seen at the Denver Public Library, the Mead Art Museum at Amherst College, and the R.W. Norton Gallery in Shreveport.

Proctor was not credited on site for nearly a century. In June 2014, Denver Arts and Venues and the Alexander Phimister Proctor Foundation unveiled bronze plaques naming him for Broncho Buster and On the War Trail.

The rider and the horses

The model is Bill Slim Ridings. Denver Public Art says he was arrested for cattle rustling while modeling, then wrote to Proctor, who provided bail. The Civic Center Conservancy also names Slim Ridings and says the charge was horse rustling. Both accounts agree that the bail let the modeling finish. Denver Public Art says Proctor used several horses from a Pendleton, Oregon round-up as models.

What is bronco busting?

Bronco busting, in the Civic Center Conservancy's words for this statue, is breaking a wild horse for saddle riding. The conservancy calls the bronze a depiction of a cowboy bronco busting. The horse is not yet a saddle horse, and the rider is staying with him. The same name is written bronchobuster, broncobuster, and broncs, and the catalog form bronco buster (proctor) names this same bronco buster statue.

Looking for the Denver Broncos horse statue?

A Broncos statue, the Denver Broncos statue, a Bronco statue, Broncos statues, a Broncos horse statue, the Denver Broncos horse statue, a Bucky Bronco statue, or Bronco statue Denver means Bucky, not this park bronze. Bucky is a 27-foot, 1,600-pound white fiberglass horse. He first stood atop Mile High Stadium in 1975. Jim and Mildred Rorie of Fiberglass Menagerie made him from the mold of Roy Rogers's Trigger, then destroyed that mold. In 2023 he was taken down for a scoreboard renovation and put back at Empower Field at Mile High, where he stands 161 feet above the field.

A second horse people mix in is Blue Mustang, also called Blucifer. Luis Jimenez's 32-foot cast-fiberglass mustang stands at Denver International Airport. Denver Public Art says the 9,000-pound sculpture was commissioned for the airport in 1993 and that Jimenez died in 2006 while working on it, when a section came loose from a hoist. Wikipedia dates the completed sculpture to February 11, 2008. Bucky and Blue Mustang are different horses from this Bronco Buster.

Not the Oval Office Bronco Buster

The bronco buster oval office is a different sculpture. Frederic Remington's The Bronco Buster was modeled in 1895. The White House Historical Association calls it his first sculpture and the most popular small American bronze of the 19th century. It grew from an 1888 Century Magazine drawing for an article by Theodore Roosevelt. That small bronze is not the bronco buster (proctor) in Civic Center. When you want the park statue, look for the broncho spelling, the broncho buster, on the pedestal.

The Nicolosi eagle on the City and County Building is the other famous bronze in this same Civic Center, covered on the American Eagle Statue in Lakewood, CO (Denver West) as a contrast with the Lakewood eagle. Questions

Where is the Bronco Buster statue in Denver?

In Civic Center Park. The Civic Center Conservancy lists the park at 101 14th Ave, Denver, CO 80204. Denver Public Art files the sculpture with the McNichols Civic Center Building. The pin used here is 39.738912, -104.989069.

Is the Bronco Buster the Denver Broncos statue?

No. This is A. Phimister Proctor's 1920 bronze cowboy on a bucking horse in Civic Center Park. The Denver Broncos horse statue is Bucky, a white fiberglass horse at Empower Field at Mile High.

Where is the Broncos horse statue (Bucky)?

Bucky is the 27-foot, 1,600-pound white fiberglass horse on the Empower Field at Mile High scoreboard. He first went up at Mile High Stadium in 1975. After a 2023 renovation he stands 161 feet above the field.

Why is it spelled Broncho Buster?

Denver Public Art titles the work Broncho Buster. The 1893 Chicago plaster was The Buckaroo. The Denver bronze is The Broncho Buster, and a 2010 transcription records the pedestal word as BRONCHO BUSTER.

Who was the model?

Bill Slim Ridings. Denver Public Art says the charge was cattle rustling. The Civic Center Conservancy says horse rustling. Both say Proctor put up bail so the modeling could finish. The horses were several from a Pendleton, Oregon round-up.

Is this the Bronco Buster in the Oval Office?

No. Frederic Remington's The Bronco Buster was modeled in 1895 and is a small bronze, published by the White House Historical Association. Proctor's Denver Bronco Buster is the monumental 1920 bronze in Civic Center Park.
