World's Largest Kachina Doll in Cave Creek

The kachina monument at the edge of Cave Creek is the Kachina Woman monument: a giant Hopi corn maiden at the entrance to Tonto Hills, on the Carefree side of town. People call it the world's largest kachina doll and the world's tallest kachina doll. It is free to view from the roadside. The address used for the stop is 42027 N Old Mine Rd, Cave Creek, AZ 85331.

The figure

A 1993 Smithsonian survey describes a full-length painted-concrete figure. The mask is three-tiered, with a small beak and an evergreen necklace. One hand holds a gourd rattle and the other a feather wand. A belt carries geometric designs. The survey places the figure on a square concrete base inside a low-walled square enclosure. Roadside America describes a circular rock garden facing south. Both descriptions are of this roadside kachina monument.

Roadside America and Atlas Obscura list the height as 39 feet. ABC15 says nearly 40 feet and about 14.5 tons, built of cement blocks and steel. The Smithsonian names sculptor Phillips Sanderson, 1908 to 1987, and engineer Carl Ludlow, and dates the work to about 1962. Atlas Obscura gives the fuller name Raymond Phillips Sanderson and says Ludlow assembled it around 1970. ABC15 places the building work in the 1970s.

It is widely called the world's largest kachina doll. Roadside America calls the figure the world's largest and adds the words we think. ABC15 says it is believed by some to be the largest. The address on ABC15 is 42027 N Old Mine Rd, Cave Creek. Roadside America prints 42027 N. Old Mine Rd, Carefree, AZ. A U.S. Census Bureau address match puts 42027 N Old Mine Rd in Cave Creek, AZ 85331, at 33.8673, -111.8296.

Kachina and katsina

The roadside figure is a sculpture of a Hopi corn maiden. Hopi religious carvings are a separate tradition. The Heard Museum describes Hopi katsina dolls as carvings of religious figures, traditionally presented as gifts to babies and girls during ceremonies held in February and July, given with a prayer-wish for future growth and well-being. The same museum notes that katsina dolls are also carved for sale, and that they are made from cottonwood root. Accounts of this Cave Creek figure spell the word kachina. The Heard Museum uses katsina for the Hopi carvings.

The Cave Creek monument is painted concrete and steel, about 39 feet tall, standing in the open at a neighborhood entrance. It is not a cottonwood katsina carving given in a ceremony, and it is not a substitute for one. Look from the roadside. Stay off the figure. The Tonto Hills Volunteer Fire Department maintains it.

The drive out

From Cave Creek, go about 6 miles east on Cave Creek Road. Stay left at the Y marked for Bartlett Lake. The figure is on the left, over the hill, at the Tonto Hills entrance. Park so you do not block the neighborhood gate. There is no visitor center at the figure itself. A roadside stop is the whole visit.

The painted colors, turquoise, red, white, grey, and black, are the ones to look for. The mask and the two objects in the hands, rattle and feather wand, are the details the Smithsonian survey recorded. Those parts confirm you are at the Kachina Woman monument.

Questions

Where is the Kachina Woman monument?

At 42027 N Old Mine Rd, Cave Creek, AZ 85331, at the entrance to Tonto Hills near Carefree. From town, go about 6 miles east on Cave Creek Road, stay left at the Bartlett Lake sign, and look on the left over the hill.

Who made the kachina monument?

The Smithsonian inventory names Phillips Sanderson as sculptor and Carl Ludlow as engineer, and dates the work to about 1962. Atlas Obscura gives the sculptor as Raymond Phillips Sanderson and says Ludlow assembled the figure around 1970.

How tall is the world's largest kachina doll, and what does it show?

Roadside America and Atlas Obscura list it at 39 feet. ABC15 describes it as nearly 40 feet and about 14.5 tons, and says it represents a Hopi corn maiden dancer. The Smithsonian survey describes painted concrete and steel in turquoise, red, white, grey, and black.

What is a kachina, or katsina?

The Heard Museum describes Hopi katsina dolls as carvings of religious figures, traditionally given to babies and girls, and also carved for sale. The Cave Creek figure is a large painted-concrete sculpture of a corn maiden, not one of those cottonwood carvings.

Can I visit the kachina monument for free?

It is free to view from the roadside. Roadside America lists the hours as always. It stands at a residential entrance, so park clear of the road and stay off the sculpture.
