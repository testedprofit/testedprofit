What Content Delivery actually shares

Default Network Sharing is Honeygain's main way of earning. Your device stays in a network that trusted partners use for public web data: web statistics, content delivery, market research, and web performance testing. You earn credits for the traffic that passes, and the help center says there is no fixed amount per gigabyte or per hour of that ordinary sharing, because demand moves.

Content Delivery is the heavy slice of that work. It shares bandwidth-heavy content such as videos, images, and larger websites. Because that traffic needs more bandwidth, Honeygain pays it differently: 6 credits for every hour the status is Active, however much traffic goes through in that hour. Data use can rise noticeably while the mode is on. You do not pick one mode and drop the other. The hourly credits sit on top of whatever the ordinary share earns in the same stretch.

The credit unit is the same one used for payouts. The features page sets the cash-out line at 20,000 credits, which is $20. At that rate, 6 credits are $0.006, and 144 credits, a full day of Active time, are $0.144. That figure is the arithmetic of an uninterrupted Active day. It is not a promise that your computer will be Active for 24 hours. The queue decides that.

Who can use it

Honeygain requires three things at once. The device must be Windows or macOS, running the latest app. Phones are out, because Honeygain says mobile connections are less stable. The IP address must be an ISP or MOB address, the kinds tied to a home or mobile carrier rather than a data center. And the country must be on the supported list. The help article updated October 22, 2026, counts 45 countries.

  • Western Europe: Austria, Belgium, Denmark, France, Germany, Ireland, Netherlands, Portugal, Switzerland, United Kingdom.
  • Northern Europe: Finland, Norway, Sweden.
  • Southern Europe: Greece, Italy, Spain, Türkiye.
  • Eastern Europe: Czechia, Hungary, Moldova, Poland, Romania, Serbia.
  • North America: Canada, Mexico, United States.
  • South America: Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Peru.
  • Asia: China, Georgia, Hong Kong, India, Israel, Japan, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand.
  • Africa: South Africa. Oceania: Australia, New Zealand.

A country on that list is not a guarantee of pay. Only a limited number of devices can run Content Delivery at the same time. When the spots are full, the app shows In Queue. It switches on by itself when a spot opens. Honeygain says the wait can be short or long, that a wait is normal, and that there is nothing to click to skip the line. Keep the app running.

The rest of the app is wider than this mode. Windows, macOS, Android, and Linux can all do ordinary sharing, and more than one device can be linked. Content Delivery stays on the two desktop systems. If you only have a phone, this page does not apply. Use the Honeygain review for the per-megabyte mode.

Privacy, power, and streaming

Honeygain's features page says partners receive encrypted web requests, not your files, messages, or browsing history, and that private data is not accessed or stored. It also describes encrypted traffic, monitoring, and GDPR-compliant practices, and it says you can pause or stop the app. Those lines cover the network as a whole, including the work Content Delivery adds.

The heavier mode has a separate warning. Honeygain says that in rare cases a Content Delivery user may lose access to a streaming platform. You are responsible for making sure the sharing follows local law and the agreements of the services you use. That is the fair version of the risk: the company says it does not read your mail, and it also says a streaming login can break. If a show stops playing after you turn the mode on, turn the mode off and see if the service returns, then read that service's rules before you leave it on.

Power and data are the practical costs. Our test: Content Delivery used more power and more bandwidth. In our test region, time marked Active did not consistently cover that use, because the device spent long stretches in the queue. We use standard bandwidth sharing. A laptop that sits In Queue is still drawing power for a mode that is not paying the hourly rate yet. A desktop that flips to Active can move a lot of video and image traffic. Watch the data cap on a metered connection before you leave it on overnight.

Content Delivery is an extra mode on Windows and macOS. It shares heavier content, such as videos, images, and larger websites, and it pays 6 credits for every hour the status is Active. It runs on top of ordinary sharing, so both can earn at once. Spots are limited.

How is it different from regular bandwidth sharing?

Ordinary sharing pays credits for the traffic you share, on Windows, macOS, Android, and Linux, and Honeygain sets no fixed rate per gigabyte. Content Delivery pays by the hour, only on Windows and macOS, and data use can rise a lot while it is Active. Phones cannot run it.
