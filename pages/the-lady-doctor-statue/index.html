Justina Ford, the Lady Doctor Statue in Denver

Justina Ford, the Denver physician patients called the Lady Doctor, is the subject of a bronze on the plaza of the L Line station at 30th and Downing. RTD dedicated the sculpture in 1998. The artist is Jess DuBois, and the title of the work is Lady Doctor. The Black American West Museum, in the house where she saw patients, stands across the street. The stop is free to view from the station plaza. Justina Ford Colorado searches land on this station and this name.

The physician

Justina Laurena Warren was born on 22 January 1871 in Knoxville, Illinois. Her parents, Pryor and Melissa Warren, had been enslaved. Melissa was a nurse, and Justina went with her to treat neighbors. She graduated from Hering Medical College in Chicago in 1899, practiced briefly in Alabama, and moved to Denver in 1902. On 2 October 1902 she received Colorado license number 3800. The Colorado Encyclopedia describes her as Denver's first licensed African American female doctor. RTD's sculpture paragraph uses the same idea, the first female African American doctor licensed in the state, while the opening line of that article calls her the first African American doctor licensed in Colorado. In plain words she is remembered as Colorado's first licensed Black woman physician, the 1902 license that opened her Denver practice.

Two published wordings of the licensing encounter sit side by side. The National Library of Medicine records a clerk's line as: I feel dishonest taking a fee from you. You've got two strikes against you to begin with. First of all, you're a lady, and second, you're colored. The Colorado Encyclopedia records: I'd feel dishonest taking a fee from you. You've got two strikes against you. First of all, you're a lady, and second, you're colored.

The National Library of Medicine page title includes the surname Carter. That page says Carter was her sister's married name and was not the name Justina Ford used. After a 1912 divorce from the Reverend John L. Ford she married Alfred Carter. Patients knew her by more than one nickname. The sculpture title is Lady Doctor, and the National Library of Medicine says patients knew her as The Lady Doctor. RTD and the encyclopedia say The Baby Doctor.

The practice and the house

RTD describes a 50-year practice from her Five Points home, in obstetrics, gynecology, and pediatrics, and more than 7,000 babies, sometimes with payment in produce or chickens. The encyclopedia treats that baby total as an estimate and says patients called her the Baby Doctor. By 1912 she had bought the house at 2335 Arapahoe Street in Curtis Park and saw patients in a first-floor room for forty years. RTD places the practice in Five Points.

In February 1984 the house was moved about a mile to 3091 California Street. The Black American West Museum opened there in 1989. The museum is the relocated house. The 1998 bronze visitors come for is on the station plaza, and the museum house is across the street at 3091 California Street. RTD and the encyclopedia's station account place it there. One account of the house also mentions a statue outside the building.

Medical societies turned her down, and hospital privileges were refused for years. After 33 years she joined the faculty at Denver General, now Denver Health. RTD's account says she was still not allowed to practice there. The National Library of Medicine records that she was eventually allowed to practice at Denver General. She was admitted to the Denver and Colorado Medical Societies in 1950. She died on 14 October 1952 and worked until an illness two weeks earlier. In 1950 Denver's Cosmopolitan Club gave her its Human Relations Award.

The bronze

Artist Jess DuBois created the bronze titled Lady Doctor. RTD dedicated it in 1998 on the station plaza along Downing Street. The encyclopedia names Jess E. DuBois and says a statue of Ford was erected across the street at the station that year. Stand on the plaza and look for the bronze of Justina Ford, then walk to the museum if it is open. The rail station is the landmark you can see from the sidewalk.

A second bronze of Dr. Ford is in Aurora, and it is a different work. The Aurora Highlands announced on 17 May 2024 an unveiling of a Gillie and Marc bronze, through Statues for Equality, near that community's clocktower entrance. She is shown in a lab coat, looking down at a baby. That sculpture is not the 1998 Jess DuBois Lady Doctor Statue at 30th and Downing.

Give the station a few minutes, then cross to the museum. Other Denver bronzes on this site include the Bronco Buster Statue in Denver's Civic Center Park, the Cowboy in Pajamas Sculpture in Denver, and Botero's Man and Woman in Denver. Questions

Where is the Justina Ford Lady Doctor Statue?

On the plaza at RTD's 30th and Downing station, along Downing Street in Denver. The 1998 bronze stands across the street from the Black American West Museum at 3091 California Street.

Who made the Lady Doctor Statue, and when?

Jess DuBois created the bronze titled Lady Doctor, and RTD dedicated it in 1998. The Colorado Encyclopedia records the artist as Jess E. DuBois.

Who was Dr. Justina Ford?

Justina Laurena Warren was born on 22 January 1871 in Knoxville, Illinois, and she died on 14 October 1952. She graduated from Hering Medical College in Chicago in 1899. On 2 October 1902 she received Colorado license number 3800.

Where did Justina Ford see patients?

By 1912 she practiced from the house at 2335 Arapahoe Street in Curtis Park. In February 1984 the house was moved to 3091 California Street, where the Black American West Museum opened in 1989.

Is the Aurora bronze the same Lady Doctor Statue?

No. On 17 May 2024 the Aurora Highlands unveiled a different bronze of Justina Ford by Gillie and Marc, near that community's clocktower. The Denver work is the 1998 Lady Doctor Statue at 30th and Downing.
