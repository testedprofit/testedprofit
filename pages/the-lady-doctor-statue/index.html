Five Points · Bronze · Dedicated 1998

Lady DoctorThe Justina Ford sculpture at 30th and Downing

RTD dedicated this bronze in 1998 on the plaza of its L Line station at 30th and Downing streets. The agency names the artist as Jess DuBois and the title as Lady Doctor. The Black American West Museum, in Dr. Ford's relocated house, is across the street.

What you'll see

A bronze figure of Dr. Justina Ford at the rail station. RTD's February 8, 2022, article says the work pays tribute to her as the first female African American doctor licensed in Colorado. The same article's opening line calls her the first African American doctor licensed in the state, without the word female. The Colorado Encyclopedia's biography says she was Denver's first licensed African American female doctor.

The encyclopedia says Justina Laurena Warren was born January 22, 1871, in Knoxville, Illinois. Her parents, Pryor and Melissa Warren, had been enslaved. Melissa was a nurse, and Justina went with her to treat neighbors. Justina graduated from Hering Medical College in Chicago in 1899, practiced briefly in Alabama, and moved to Denver in 1902. The National Library of Medicine also gives 1871, Knoxville, Hering in 1899, a brief Alabama practice, and Denver in 1902. Its page title includes the surname Carter, and the biography says Carter was her sister's married name and was not used by Justina Ford. The encyclopedia says that after a 1912 divorce from the Reverend John L. Ford she married Alfred Carter.

Two published wordings of the licensing encounter do not match exactly. The National Library of Medicine says a clerk told her: "I feel dishonest taking a fee from you. You've got two strikes against you to begin with. First of all, you're a lady, and second, you're colored." The encyclopedia says the examiner said: "I'd feel dishonest taking a fee from you. You've got two strikes against you. First of all, you're a lady, and second, you're colored."

The practice

RTD says she practiced 50 years from her Five Points home, specialized in obstetrics, gynecology, and pediatrics, and delivered over 7,000 babies, sometimes paid in produce or chickens. The encyclopedia says the total is an estimate and that patients called her the Baby Doctor.

Hospitals

The encyclopedia says medical societies turned her down and she could not get hospital privileges. RTD says that after 33 years she joined the faculty at Denver General, now Denver Health, but was still not allowed to practice there. The National Library of Medicine says she was eventually allowed to practice at Denver General.

1950 and 1952

RTD says she was admitted to the Denver and Colorado Medical Societies in 1950, two years before her death. The encyclopedia dates her death to October 14, 1952, and says she worked until an illness two weeks earlier. It also records a 1950 Human Relations Award from Denver's Cosmopolitan Club.

Visiting tips

  • Use the station, not the old house number. 2335 Arapahoe Street is the historic address. The house was towed to 3091 California Street in February 1984.
  • The museum and the statue are across the street from each other. RTD and the encyclopedia's station sentence agree on that. Do not follow the encyclopedia's shorter line that puts the statue outside the house.
  • Nicknames differ by source. The sculpture title is Lady Doctor. The National Library of Medicine says patients knew her as The Lady Doctor. RTD and the encyclopedia say The Baby Doctor.
  • A second Ford bronze is in Aurora. The Aurora Highlands described a May 17, 2024, unveiling of a Gillie and Marc bronze near its clocktower. That is a different sculpture.

Lady Doctor FAQ

Where is the Lady Doctor statue?

On the plaza at RTD's 30th and Downing light-rail station, along Downing Street. RTD says the 1998 bronze stands across the street from the Black American West Museum. The museum's address, from the Colorado Encyclopedia, is 3091 California Street. The map pin is the OpenStreetMap point for the 30th and Downing station, 39.758970, -104.973525, not a separate pedestal survey.

Who made the Lady Doctor statue, and when?

RTD says artist Jess DuBois created the bronze sculpture called Lady Doctor, and that RTD dedicated it in 1998. The Colorado Encyclopedia names Jess E. DuBois and says a statue of Ford was erected across the street at the station that year. A simplified paragraph on the encyclopedia's house article also says a statue was placed outside the house in 1998. This guide follows RTD and the encyclopedia's station sentence.

Who was Dr. Justina Ford?

The Colorado Encyclopedia says Justina Laurena Warren was born January 22, 1871, in Knoxville, Illinois, to Pryor and Melissa Warren, former slaves, and that she died October 14, 1952. She graduated from Hering Medical College in Chicago in 1899. On October 2, 1902, the encyclopedia says, she received Colorado license number 3800 as Denver's first licensed African American female doctor. RTD's article lead calls her the first African American doctor licensed in Colorado, and its sculpture paragraph says the first female African American doctor licensed in the state.

Where did she live and see patients?

The Colorado Encyclopedia says that by 1912 she had bought the house at 2335 Arapahoe Street in Curtis Park and saw patients in a first-floor room for forty years. RTD places the practice in Five Points. In February 1984 the house was moved about a mile to 3091 California Street. The Black American West Museum opened there in 1989. It is not still standing at the Arapahoe address.

Is the Aurora Highlands statue the same sculpture?

No. The Aurora Highlands announced on May 17, 2024, a different bronze of Dr. Ford by Gillie and Marc, through Statues for Equality, near that community's clocktower entrance. She is shown in a lab coat, looking down at a baby. That is not the 1998 Jess DuBois sculpture at 30th and Downing.
