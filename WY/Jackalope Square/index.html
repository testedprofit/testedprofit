Jackalope Square

Downtown Douglas

Jackalope Square is the downtown square in Douglas. The City of Douglas facility page lists it at 100 S. 3rd, Douglas, WY 82633, with free admission, and lists hours of 5:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. seven days a week. The page names a gazebo, picnic shelter, fire pit, restrooms, and parking among the features. Converse County Tourism lists Jackalope Square and the statue at 700 Center St., Douglas, WY 82633. That page says Douglas, on the banks of the North Platte River, is known for the jackalope, a cross between a jackrabbit and an antelope, and that the statue stands at the heart of downtown in Jackalope Square. The city address is 100 S. 3rd. The tourism address is 700 Center St.

A proclamation by Governor Ed Herschler, dated May 15, 1985, says the first jackalope prepared for exhibit was made by taxidermist Doug Herrick of Douglas in 1939. It says the State of Wyoming issued Jackalope as a registered trademark to the Douglas Chamber of Commerce on April 5, 1960, and Home of the Jackalope on April 7, 1970. It says an 8-foot statue created by Leonard Lore of Douglas, at the chamber's request, stood in the center of Douglas from 1965 until it was damaged in an accident on Jackalope Day, May 19, 1984. A new, slightly taller statue, also by Lore for the Douglas Area Chamber of Commerce, was to be dedicated on Jackalope Day, May 18, 1985. The proclamation names Douglas the official home of the jackalope.

The city's Meet Jack page says jackalope figures can be seen in downtown Jackalope Square and at the visitors center, and that a thirteen-foot cutout stands on a hillside near the north entrance from Interstate 25. The thirteen-foot figure is that hillside cutout. The square's statue is the one the proclamation describes as 8 feet and then slightly taller. The city marks Jackalope Days each June.
