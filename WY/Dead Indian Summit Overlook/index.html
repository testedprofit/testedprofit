Dead Indian Summit Overlook

The high point of the Chief Joseph Scenic Byway

Dead Indian Summit Overlook is the named turnout on Wyoming Highway 296, the Chief Joseph Scenic Byway. The Shoshone National Forest page says the overlook is on the pass at 8,071 feet, with vistas of the Absaroka Mountains, the Beartooth Mountain Range to the north, and the Clarks Fork River below. Interpretive signs there cover the Nez Perce route and the surrounding ranges. The Forest Service says the site is open 24 hours a day, charges no fee, has ample parking and a vault toilet, and has no garbage collection.

WYDOT's Buffalo Bill and Yellowstone Country scenic-byway guide says the Chief Joseph Scenic Byway's 47 paved miles run from the junction with U.S. 120, 17 miles north of Cody, northwest to U.S. 212, the Beartooth Highway. It says the road crests at 8,060-foot Dead Indian Pass. It lists Dead Indian Summit Overlook at an elevation of 8,060 feet, a paved turnout at the high point of the byway, with a panorama of the Absaroka and Beartooth mountains and a 220-degree view. Cody Yellowstone's Beartooth Loop page says that sixteen miles north of Cody, the driver turns onto Wyoming Highway 296 and winds up to the top of Dead Indian Hill, where the overlook looks over Sunlight Basin, the North Absaroka Wilderness, the Clarks Fork Canyon, and the Beartooth Plateau. The Forest Service elevation is 8,071 feet. The WYDOT elevation is 8,060 feet. The turnoff distances are seventeen miles in the WYDOT guide and sixteen miles on the Cody Yellowstone page.

The WYDOT guide says various legends claim the origin of the placename. It says most versions agree that a skirmish between early prospectors or the U.S. Army and a party of Bannock Indians occurred here in 1878, and that one of the Bannocks died and was left at the summit. The byway itself is named for Chief Joseph. The Forest Service signs at the overlook address the Nez Perce route.

Questions

Where is Dead Indian Summit Overlook?

The Shoshone National Forest places it on the pass, with views of the Absaroka Mountains, the Beartooth range to the north, and the Clarks Fork River below. WYDOT's byway guide calls it a paved turnout at the high point of the Chief Joseph Scenic Byway, Wyoming 296. That guide says the byway leaves U.S. 120 seventeen miles north of Cody. Cody Yellowstone says the turn onto Wyoming 296 is sixteen miles north of Cody.

How high is the overlook?

The Forest Service page says the overlook is on the pass at 8,071 feet. WYDOT's byway guide says the road crests at 8,060-foot Dead Indian Pass and lists the overlook at an elevation of 8,060 feet.
