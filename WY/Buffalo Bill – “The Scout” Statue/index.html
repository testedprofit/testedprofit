The Scout

Buffalo Bill, The Scout

The Scout is the equestrian bronze of William F. Buffalo Bill Cody at the Buffalo Bill Center of the West in Cody. The Center's August 18, 2015 sculpture guide titles it Buffalo Bill, The Scout, by Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, 1924. It says that after Cody's death in 1917, a committee from Cody commissioned Whitney, a sculptor and arts patron from New York, to create a monumental tribute. Dedicated in 1924, the figure shows him as an army scout, peering down a trail and signaling troops to follow. The guide says the bronze was cast by Roman Bronze Works, New York, and lists it as 149 inches high by 84 inches wide, a gift of the artist. It stands north of the building and can be seen from inside the Whitney Western Art Museum.

The Center's highlights page says that after William F. Cody died in 1917, the Buffalo Bill Memorial Association commissioned Whitney to sculpt the statue. After it was finished, she chose the site and designed the base. The page says she also purchased the forty adjacent acres and donated them to the memorial association along with the sculpture, and that the land later became the home of the Buffalo Bill Center of the West. The 2015 guide says a committee from Cody commissioned the sculpture. The highlights page says the Buffalo Bill Memorial Association commissioned it.

The Center's history timeline says Buffalo Bill, the Scout, a heroic equestrian bronze by Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, was unveiled and presented to the Memorial Association in 1924. The same timeline says that in 1935 Whitney donated the Center's current 40-acre site. The highlights page places the gift of the forty acres with the sculpture. Questions

Who made The Scout, and how large is it?

The Buffalo Bill Center of the West says Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney made Buffalo Bill, The Scout, in 1924. It is bronze, cast by Roman Bronze Works in New York, and the museum lists the size as 149 inches high by 84 inches wide. It is a gift of the artist.

Where does it stand?

The Center's sculpture guide places it north of the building, visible from inside the Whitney Western Art Museum. The highlights page says Whitney chose the site, designed the base, and donated forty adjacent acres with the sculpture. The Center's history timeline says she donated the current 40-acre site in 1935.
