Rock Paper Scissors Sculpture in Gillette, WY

The Rock Paper Scissors sculpture in Gillette, Wyoming, is a street-corner stack by Kevin Box and Warren Cullar. Rock is the base, paper is the middle, and scissors sit on top, so scissors wins. It was installed in 2011 in the Avenues of Art program, at 1201 W 2nd St, on the southwest corner of S Burma Avenue and W 2nd Street, US-16, in front of the Gillette News-Record. It is free, outdoors, and one of the quick things to do in Gillette, Wyoming.

The stack

Three pieces of the game become one tower. The rock is the base, the paper is the middle, and a pair of scissors balances on top. Kevin Box is known for origami-inspired sculpture cast in metal. His studio describes rock paper scissors as a way of making decisions or resolving conflicts for not so serious things, the kind of call that picks a front seat or the last piece of cake. Serious matters, he writes, take a real conversation. On this corner, scissors wins.

Roadside America records Warren Cullar and Kevin Box, installed in 2011, part of Avenues of Art, at 1201 W. 2nd St., southwest corner of S. Burma Ave. and W. 2nd St. (US Hwy 16). Admission is free. A 2012 visitor tip places it in front of the Gillette News-Record offices, just west of the Rockpile Museum, and says scissors wins. Another note on that page says the piece is smaller than people expect and sits on the street corner, in front of a brick wall.

Avenues of Art is the Mayor's Art Council program. The Gillette News Record of 23 July 2009 describes sculptures on city streets and the 25 percent of sales used to buy work for the city's collection. Keep that story with the program. The Rock Paper Scissors piece itself is the 2011 Roadside America record. The materials named for the Gillette work are powder-coated cast bronze and stainless steel.

The corner, and where to leave the car

The rock paper scissors parking garage search leads to this sidewalk sculpture. It stands at 1201 W 2nd St, on the southwest corner of S Burma Avenue and W 2nd Street, in front of the Gillette News-Record offices. US-16 runs through this stretch of 2nd Street. The piece is on the corner itself. Park at the curb along 2nd Street, or at the corner building, and walk to the stack. A photo from the sidewalk is the whole visit, and you can throw your own rock, paper, or scissors beside it.

Give it five to ten minutes. It is outdoors and free at any time, which makes it an easy pause on a Wyoming drive through Gillette.

A short history of the game

Rock paper scissors is a hand game for two people who throw at the same moment. A fist is rock, a flat hand is paper, and two extended fingers are scissors. Rock breaks scissors, scissors cut paper, and paper covers rock. Each sign beats one and loses to one, so a tie means you throw again.

A written ancestor is Chinese. The Ming writer Xie Zhaozhe, in the book Wuzazu around 1600, described a hand game called shoushiling and said players had used it since the Han dynasty. The game reached Japan, where fist games of this kind are called sansukumi-ken, games of the three who are afraid of one another. An early import used a frog, a slug, and a snake. The version with rock, paper, and scissors is jan-ken, and that is the game the English name translates. It spread beyond East Asia in the early 20th century. Kevin Box's line fits the history: it is a cheerful way to settle a small argument, and the Gillette stack lets scissors have the last word.
