Soil to Riches statue

Broadway at 6th Street

The Soil to Riches statue is the bronze cowboy and horse in downtown Thermopolis. A July 1, 2008 account of the downtown calls the work the Pioneer Cowboy and Horse and places it on Broadway Street, Highway WY-120, at 6th Street, Highway US-20. It says sculptor Carl Jensen of Wheatland, Wyoming, designed it. Quoting the Thermopolis Independent Record of June 29, 2000, the account says the cowboy is depicted sifting dirt through his hands, standing beside his quarter horse. The theme is, From this soil come the riches of the world. The soil theme was chosen because of its relationship to farming, ranching, mining and minerals, and fossils in the development of Thermopolis.

The same account quotes Jensen on the weight. The man weighs 250 to 300 pounds and was modeled after a picture of Allen Schweighart, who spearheaded the statue effort. Ten pieces were cast, then welded together. The horse weighs about 1,500 to 2,000 pounds, bears Jensen's brand, and is modeled after one of his favorite horses. The account says the wide area on both sides of the base features livestock brands from area ranches, etched in Corian, and that each of 138 brand owners paid 100 dollars to have a brand listed. Buttons and functional spurs were enhanced in the casting with a silicon bronze compound, and acid washes made the shades for skin, clothing, and leather.

Those two weight lines, 250 to 300 pounds for the cowboy and about 1,500 to 2,000 pounds for the horse, are the figures the 2000 newspaper report gives. The bronze on Broadway carries the line, From this soil come the riches of the world.
