Robert Burns statue in Cheyenne

Henry Snell Gamley's bronze of the Scottish poet Robert Burns stands in Gilchrist Park, a small triangle of grass at Pioneer Avenue, Randall Avenue, and 26th Street. Mary Gemmell Gilchrist gave it to the city. The dedication was 11 November 1929. This is an independent Profitnet guide to that statue.

See it

The figure and the stone

Burns stands full length in eighteenth-century dress: hatless, coat open, waistcoat, knee breeches, stockings, and shoes. His left hand holds a book against his hip. The left leg is bent and set a little forward. The Cheyenne public art listing gives the bronze as 6 and a half feet tall, on a pedestal of Georgia gray granite 12 and a half feet tall. A 1993 outdoor-sculpture survey records the figure as bronze and the base as granite, and it places the work in this same triangle.

The sculptor's name is cut on the lower left edge of the bronze: Henry Snell Gamley, R.S.A., sculptor, Edinburgh, Scotland, born 2 April 1865, died 25 October 1928. The back right corner names the founder, Alexis Rudier of Paris. The front of the base names Robert Burns and then these lines from The Cotter's Saturday Night:

From scenes like these old Scotia's grandeur springs
That makes her lov'd at home, rever'd abroad
Princes and lords are but the breath of kings.
An honest man's the noblest work of God

Under the verse the base reads that Mary Gilchrist donated the statue to the City of Cheyenne in 1928. A bronze plaque names William DuBois as architect, W. S. Higman as builder, and the trustees for the Burns memorial: Marshall S. Reynolds, George E. Brimmer, and Daniel W. Gill.

Mary Gilchrist

Mary Gemmell Gilchrist was born in Scotland in 1840. In 1869 she emigrated to join her husband, Andrew Gilchrist. They spent several years in northern Colorado, then settled in Cheyenne in 1875. They began in the mercantile trade and built a fortune in cattle, real estate, and bank stock. Andrew died in 1894. Mary stayed in Cheyenne and gave a large share of her time to philanthropy. She gave this bronze to the city in honor of her native Scotland.

The stone dates the gift to 1928. The dedication, with a prayer and a benediction, was 11 November 1929, Armistice Day. Mary died in January 1931 and is buried with Andrew in Lakeview Cemetery in Cheyenne. The Wyoming State Archives holds the statue records, dated 1927 to 1950, given in 1972 by Mrs. Marshall Reynolds. Photographs of the sculptor and the work went into the archives photo collection as P72-19.

Gamley, and a statue he never saw unveiled

Gamley was a Scottish sculptor, born at Logie-Pert, Craigo, near Montrose. The family moved to Edinburgh. He trained under Charles P. Hodder, then under Edouard Lanteri in London, and under William Grant Stevenson in Edinburgh. He became an Associate of the Royal Scottish Academy in 1908. His work includes war memorials, tomb figures, sculpture on the Usher Hall, and the Edward VII memorial at Holyrood.

In 1928 he hired Auguste Rodin's studio in Paris to work on a Burns statue for Wyoming. Rodin had died in 1917, so this was the studio, not a collaboration with Rodin. Gamley died that year on a country estate near Paris, on 25 October 1928. His body was brought back to Dean Cemetery in Edinburgh. The Cheyenne bronze was dedicated on 11 November 1929, so he did not see the unveiling. A smaller bronze of this figure is at Ellisland, Burns's farm in Dumfries. Another was shown at Lady Stair's House in Edinburgh in 1953 and in 1959.

Burns himself was the Ayrshire poet who lived from 1759 to 1796. The Cheyenne figure is one of many Burns memorials. This one is the Gamley bronze in Gilchrist Park, not the statues in Edinburgh, Glasgow, or other American cities.

Other Cheyenne stops

Gilchrist Park is a pocket of grass, not a large grounds with a visitor center. You can walk up to the pedestal from the sidewalk. Two other Cheyenne figures on this site are separate works: the Boy Scout Statue of Liberty replica and Just LeDoux It. Lakeview Cemetery, where Mary and Andrew Gilchrist are buried, is a different visit from the park.

Questions

Where is the Robert Burns statue?

It stands outdoors in Gilchrist Park, the small triangular park at Pioneer Avenue, Randall Avenue, and 26th Street in Cheyenne. The public art listing gives the address as 2610 Pioneer Avenue.

Who paid for it, and when was it dedicated?

Mary Gemmell Gilchrist gave the bronze to the City of Cheyenne in honor of her native Scotland. The base dates the gift to 1928. The dedication was 11 November 1929.

Who made the bronze?

Scottish sculptor Henry Snell Gamley modeled it. He was born on 2 April 1865 and died on 25 October 1928, before the unveiling. Alexis Rudier of Paris cast the bronze. William DuBois was the architect.
