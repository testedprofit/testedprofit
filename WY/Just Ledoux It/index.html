Frontier Park, Cheyenne

Just LeDoux It

Just LeDoux It is the bronze of Chris LeDoux on the west side of Frontier Park. Wyoming sculptor D. Michael Thomas made it. Cheyenne Frontier Days unveiled it on 23 July 2021, opening day of the 125th Daddy of 'em All, the year the rodeo was dedicated to LeDoux. Garth Brooks and the LeDoux family were there. This is an independent Profitnet guide to that statue, not the earlier bronze in Kaycee.

See it

Statue
West side of Frontier Park, southwest corner, facing Hynds Boulevard. Outdoors. Visible from Interstate 25. The park-and-ride drop-off is near Hynds Boulevard, just south of the large Cheyenne Frontier Days sign.
Grounds
Cheyenne Frontier Days Old West Museum, 4610 Carey Avenue, Cheyenne, Wyoming 82001, is on the same grounds. The statue is outside, on the Hynds side of the park.
Foundation
Cheyenne Frontier Days Foundation, 1230 West 8th Avenue, Cheyenne, Wyoming 82001. Phone (307) 778-1491.

The ride and the guitar

LeDoux is on a bucking horse, contestant number 125 on his back, with the Cheyenne Frontier Days mark. The horse is Stormy Weather, the bronc he rode at the 1976 National Finals Rodeo in Oklahoma City, the ride that made him world champion bareback rider. A bronze guitar leans against the base. Thomas's own page lists the scale as life and a half, in bronze, with a 2020 copyright.

The Wyoming Tribune Eagle watched a crane set the piece on 22 June 2021 and reported a bronze 12 and a half feet tall and 17 feet long. Thomas told the Buffalo Bulletin the figure is roughly 13 feet tall, and about 17 to 18 feet once it is on the pedestal. The Bulletin also reported a casting of 2,000 pounds of bronze at the Caleco Foundry in Cody. He rented space in a wool mill so the ceiling would clear the clay. The sculpture then rode a flatbed through Buffalo on 21 June 2021 and was lifted into place the next day, under a tarp, until the unveiling.

The CFD Foundation's 365 to 125 campaign helped pay for the statue and for the Marietta Dinneen Carriage Barn. The public art listing puts that campaign above $300,000.

Opening day, 23 July 2021

Cheyenne Frontier Days that year ran from 23 July through 1 August. The statue came out of the tarp on opening day. Ned LeDoux, Chris's son, said his father loved the place, rode bareback horses there for years, and later showed them how it was done with live music on the main stage. Garth Brooks, who opened the concert series that night, helped dedicate the bronze and said he would make sure his own children knew his friend.

Whoa Cheyenne, well that is like the Grand Ole Opry to me.

That is what LeDoux said the first time he headlined Frontier Days, in 1993. His first ride at the Daddy of 'em All was in 1968. He had gone to Cheyenne Central High School, played football there, and won the Wyoming state high school bareback title. Thomas, who knew him, said he was glad the 125th was the year they honored him.

The man on the horse

Christopher Lee LeDoux was born on 2 October 1948 in Biloxi, Mississippi, and grew up moving with his father's Air Force postings. He learned horses on his grandparents' Wyoming farm, rode his first rodeo at 13, and landed at Cheyenne Central when the family moved there. A rodeo scholarship took him to Casper College. In his junior year at Eastern New Mexico University he won the intercollegiate national bareback title. He turned professional in 1970, wrote songs to pay the road, and sold tapes from the back of his pickup. He and his father set up American Cowboy Songs.

He married Peggy Rhoads on 4 January 1972. They had five children: Clay, Ned, Will, Beau, and Cindy. In 1976 he won the world bareback championship at the National Finals, the only rider to cover all ten head. He retired from competition in 1980 and settled on a ranch in Kaycee. By the end of that decade he had self-released 22 albums. In 1989 Garth Brooks named him in the hit Much Too Young (To Feel This Damn Old). LeDoux signed with Liberty Records and, in 1992, the duet Whatcha Gonna Do with a Cowboy reached number seven. It was nominated for a Grammy for best country vocal collaboration.

He died of cancer on 9 March 2005 in Casper, at 56. He had been diagnosed with primary sclerosing cholangitis in August 2000 and received a liver transplant on 7 October 2000. Brooks had offered part of his own liver, which was not a match. In 2005 LeDoux entered the ProRodeo Hall of Fame in two categories, bareback riding and notables, for what his music did for the sport. On 24 July 2007 his son Beau spread his ashes over the Frontier Park arena during Cheyenne Frontier Days.

Kaycee is a different bronze

Thomas's first LeDoux monument is Good Ride Cowboy, at Chris LeDoux Park in Kaycee, unveiled in 2010 after about five years of work. The family had loaned the chaps, spurs, rigging, and the Guild guitar so the tack would be his. In Kaycee the guitar is the base, and the contestant number is the 1976 championship number. In Cheyenne the guitar leans on the base, and the number is 125. Same rider, same horse, same sculptor, two towns about 250 miles apart. This page is the Frontier Park bronze.

Thomas had known LeDoux from the feed store in Buffalo, where LeDoux bought ranch supplies. After the death in 2005, Thomas waited about a year, then talked with Peggy in 2006. When Frontier Days later proposed a sculpture competition, the family said they wanted Mike. Ned LeDoux has said his father called Thomas the Charlie Russell of our day.

Other Cheyenne figures

The Robert Burns statue is across town in Gilchrist Park, at Pioneer and Randall. The Boy Scout Statue of Liberty replica is another Cheyenne bronze. Frontier Park itself is the rodeo grounds. Outside the July run, the LeDoux rider is the piece you can pick out from Hynds Boulevard and from the interstate.

Questions

What is Just LeDoux It?

It is D. Michael Thomas's bronze of Chris LeDoux on a bucking horse, with a guitar at the base. Cheyenne Frontier Days unveiled it on 23 July 2021, opening day of the 125th rodeo, the year the event was dedicated to LeDoux.

Where does the statue stand?

On the west side of Frontier Park in Cheyenne, at the southwest corner, facing Hynds Boulevard. It is outdoors and visible from Interstate 25, near the park-and-ride drop-off just south of the large Cheyenne Frontier Days sign.

How is the Kaycee statue different?

Kaycee has a separate bronze, Good Ride Cowboy, also by Thomas, at Chris LeDoux Park. There the guitar is the base and the contestant number is the one from his 1976 world title. In Cheyenne the guitar leans on the base, and the number is 125.
