Dawson Creek nature statue

The Dawson Creek nature statue is a roadside landmark in Hillsboro, Oregon. Wikipedia places Hillsboro in the Tualatin Valley on the west side of the Portland metropolitan area and names it the county seat of Washington County. The city hosts high-technology companies, including Intel, in the district known locally as the Silicon Forest. The population was 106,447 at the 2020 census, which made Hillsboro the fifth-most populous city in Oregon.

The same article says settlers founded a community here in 1842, later named for David Hill, an Oregon politician. Riverboats on the Tualatin River were part of the settler economy. A railroad reached the area in the early 1870s, and an interurban electric railway arrived about four decades later. Those lines, with the highways, accompanied growth to about 2,000 people by 1910 and about 5,000 by 1950, before high-tech companies arrived in the 1980s. Hillsboro uses a council-manager government, with a city manager and a city council headed by a mayor. Besides the high-tech industry, the article lists health care, retail sales, and agriculture, including grapes and wineries.

Wikipedia places Hillsboro about 17 miles west of Portland and immediately west of Beaverton, at an elevation of 194 feet. Streams there include the Tualatin River, Dairy Creek, McKay Creek, Rock Creek, Dawson Creek, and Turner Creek. Neighboring communities include Aloha, Cornelius, and North Plains. The Dawson Creek nature statue is the roadside landmark in that city, along the creek that carries the same name.
