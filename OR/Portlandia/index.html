Portlandia

Portlandia is the roadside landmark above the entrance of the Portland Building in downtown Portland. Wikipedia says Raymond Kaskey made the copper repoussé statue, the second largest of its kind in the United States after the Statue of Liberty. The city commissioned it in 1985. Kaskey and his assistant Michael Lasell built sections in a Maryland suburb of Washington, D.C., and sent the parts to Portland by ship. The statue was assembled at a barge-building facility owned by Gunderson, Inc., floated up the Willamette River, and installed on the Portland Building on October 6, 1985.

Wikipedia says the figure is based on the Portland city seal. Lady Commerce wears classical clothes, holds a trident in her left hand, and reaches down with her right. She is above street level and faces a narrow, tree-lined street. The statue is 34 feet 10 inches high and weighs 6.5 short tons. A plaque carries Ronald Talney's poem Portlandia, which begins with the line about kneeling down and reaching out from the quietness of copper. Wikipedia says Kaskey was paid $228,000 in public funds and reportedly another $100,000 in private donations.

The Portland Tribune's notes for the 30th birthday in 2015 match the height of 34 feet 10 inches, the weight of 6.5 tons, and the makeup, a steel frame covered in hammered copper. The Tribune says construction ran about three years and finished in 1985, and it lists a $198,000 city arts commission plus a reported $100,000 in private donations. It also calls the work the second largest sculpture of its kind in the United States after the Statue of Liberty.

Questions

Where is Portlandia?

Wikipedia places Raymond Kaskey's statue above the entrance of the Portland Building in downtown Portland.

How tall is it, and when was it installed?

Wikipedia says the statue is 34 feet 10 inches high, weighs 6.5 short tons, and was installed on October 6, 1985. The Portland Tribune matches that height and weight.

What do the payment figures say?

Wikipedia says Kaskey was paid $228,000 in public funds and reportedly $100,000 more in private donations. The Tribune lists a $198,000 city arts commission and a reported $100,000 in private donations.
