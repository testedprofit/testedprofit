Bonanza Bigfoot statue

The Bonanza Bigfoot statue is a roadside landmark at Big Springs Park in Bonanza, Oregon. Discover Klamath places the park at 3001 Main Street, Bonanza, OR 97623, and lists the phone number (541) 281-9805. The Lost River runs alongside the park, and a natural spring rises inside it. Facilities include picnic tables, barbecue grills, a small walking bridge over the springs, and restrooms open in the summer. A small island sits across the river. The bureau lists seasonal events there, including Concerts in the Park, the Oregon State Chili Cook-Off, a barbecue rib cook-off and homemade hobby festival, and a holiday lights display.

Wikipedia places Bonanza in Klamath County, near Klamath Falls, with 415 people at the 2010 census. The town sits at 4,127 feet, at the east end of Oregon Route 70, a spur off Oregon Route 140. By highway it is about 21 miles from Klamath Falls. The Lost River flows through town. The article says the name comes from the Spanish word for prosperity, tied to the good springs nearby. The Bonanza post office was established in 1875. J. P. Roberts founded the community in 1876 and opened a store. The town was formally platted in 1878.

By around 1900, Wikipedia says, Bonanza was considered the third most important town in Klamath County, after Klamath Falls and Merrill, with daily mail by stagecoach. The region produced cattle, horses, grain, hay, fruits, and vegetables. The article still describes an agricultural community of alfalfa, cattle, and dairy farms, and it places the Bonanza Big Springs near downtown where the Lost River runs. The Bigfoot statue is the roadside figure at that park.
