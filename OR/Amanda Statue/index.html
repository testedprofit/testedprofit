Amanda statue

The Amanda statue is a roadside landmark on Amanda's Trail near Yachats. The U.S. Forest Service describes a descent from the Cape Perpetua day-use area through coastal woodland to a grotto with a concrete statue. The trail and statue remember Amanda, a woman of the Coos Tribe who was forcibly marched to the Alsea Sub-agency camp in the 1860s. The City of Yachats calls her a blind Coos woman who suffered injustices during the reservation years.

Oregon Hikers places the concrete figure near a footbridge over Amanda Creek, in a forest amphitheater, and says it is usually draped with necklaces. That account names her Amanda De Cuys, a blind Coos woman taken north in 1864 when a detachment of the 4th California Infantry rounded up people for the Alsea Sub-agency at Yachats. Signs near the statue describe the forced removal.

The Forest Service lists Amanda's Trail as trail 1386, 2.9 miles, moderate to difficult, starting at Cape Perpetua Day Use. Travel south on Highway 101 for 2 miles from Yachats and turn east onto Forest Road 55. The site charges a day-use fee of $5 a vehicle or a valid recreation pass. The City of Yachats starts the walk at the south end of Yachats Ocean Road and calls the hike to the statue a moderate mile. From the statue the city route crosses Amanda Creek on a suspension bridge and climbs 2.2 miles to the Cape Perpetua lookout, 3.2 miles one way in all, with bikes excluded. The Forest Service says the trail is maintained with the City of Yachats, Oregon State Parks, the Oregon Department of Transportation, and a private landowner.

Questions

Where is the Amanda statue?

The Forest Service places a concrete statue in a grotto on Amanda's Trail, on the descent from Cape Perpetua toward Highway 101. The City of Yachats places the statue about a mile from the south end of Yachats Ocean Road.

Who is the statue for?

The Forest Service and the City of Yachats dedicate the trail and statue to Amanda, a Coos woman forced to the Alsea Sub-agency in the 1860s. Oregon Hikers names her Amanda De Cuys.

How long is the trail?

The Forest Service lists 2.9 miles from Cape Perpetua Day Use. The City of Yachats lists 3.2 miles one way from Yachats Ocean Road to the Cape Perpetua lookout, with about a mile of that walk reaching the statue.
