Barclay Park

Harold and Dorothy Barclay Park is a small downtown green on a closed block of Ash Street in Sisters, Oregon. The town dedicated it on 1 August 2003.

A park on a closed street

The park occupies a block of Ash Street that the city took out of traffic, tucked between The Gallery Restaurant and The Jewel. On dedication day, 1 August 2003, about 200 people filled it. A jazz combo from Sisters Middle School played, and the Sisters-Camp Sherman fire district served barbecue. Mayor Dave Elliott told the crowd the project would have run another month or two, and over budget, without Peter Storton, Gary Frazee, and Glenn Miller.

Storton chaired the committee that pushed the park through. Frazee ran public works. Miller, known as GJ, built the restroom quickly and at a price the mayor was glad to mention. Those restrooms opened about a month early so Rodeo weekend and the Quilt Show could use them. For years Village Green had been the only public restroom in the city limits. Forest Service restrooms near the Y on the west side came next. Barclay Park made three.

What you sit with

The design is simple and meant for a pause on a walking street: wooden benches, a grassy swale, and a water feature from SisterScapes. People stop, sit, and move on. Oregon's highway department added curb extensions, the local paper called them bubbles, along Cascade Avenue so cars on Highway 20 can see pedestrians where no cross street breaks the block. Storton's point was safety: without those extensions, drivers have a harder time seeing anyone stepping off the sidewalk.

The names on the park

Harold and Dorothy Barclay owned a mill on north Pine Street until the logging work thinned out. When the Sisters Library moved from the Depot Deli site to Spruce Street and grew, Barclay's old office went with it as an addition. Harold also pressed Sisters to shift from a timber town toward visitors, and he was behind the Western architectural theme that still marks the storefronts. The couple developed Barclay Square on Cascade Avenue, and Dorothy had interests in several downtown businesses. The park is the town's thanks for that pair, set on the street where people already walk.

Sisters around the block

Sisters is a small city in Deschutes County, in the Bend metropolitan area, named for the Three Sisters mountains to the west. In 2020 the census counted 3,064 residents. The park is the downtown pause in that town, not a trailhead. For a longer walk, other central Oregon stops on this site include the Two Bits sculpture in Bend and the wildland firefighter monument nearer Prineville. Barclay Park itself is the bench, the water, and the closed street.

How to use the stop

Treat the park as a break in a downtown walk, not as a trailhead. Come in off Cascade Avenue, which is Highway 20 through town, and use the curb extensions where the closed street meets the highway. Those extensions, which people in Sisters called bubbles, push the sidewalk toward the travel lane so a driver can see you where no cross street interrupts the block. Once you are on the old Ash Street pavement, the park is the block itself: benches, a grassy swale, the SisterScapes water feature, and the restroom.

The restroom is the practical half of the gift. Before this park, Village Green was the only public restroom inside the city limits, with Forest Service restrooms near the Y on the west end as the second stop. Barclay Park made a third, and it opened about a month before the dedication so Rodeo weekend and the Quilt Show could use it. If you are downtown for those crowds, or for an ordinary afternoon, this is the green with a door.

The name on the park is the rest of the story you can carry while you sit. Harold and Dorothy Barclay's mill stood on north Pine Street. When the library moved from the Depot Deli site to Spruce Street, Harold's old office went along as an addition. He also pressed the Western storefront theme that still marks downtown, and the couple developed Barclay Square on Cascade Avenue. Dorothy had interests in several downtown businesses. The closed street is the town's thanks, a block you walk through rather than drive.
