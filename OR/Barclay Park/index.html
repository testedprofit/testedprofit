Barclay Park

The Nugget Newspaper reported that Harold and Dorothy Barclay Park in Sisters, Oregon, was dedicated on Friday, 1 August 2003, on a decommissioned block of Ash Street. Travel Oregon, using a Sisters Chamber listing, places the park in downtown Sisters and says it is named for Harold and Dorothy Barclay.

Plan your visit

Address
The Nugget places the park on a closed block of Ash Street between The Gallery Restaurant and The Jewel, in downtown Sisters. OpenStreetMap names Barclay Park in Sisters.
Parking
Neither the Nugget dedication story nor the Travel Oregon listing names a lot. The story describes a downtown pedestrian stop, with Oregon Department of Transportation curb extensions on Cascade Avenue for people crossing Highway 20.
Hours
The 2003 stories and the Travel Oregon listing do not print daily gate hours.
Accessibility
The Nugget says the park opened with public restrooms built by contractor Glenn Miller, wooden benches, a grassy swale, and a water feature by SisterScapes. It does not describe a ramp grade.
Best time
The dedication was a Friday evening in August. The paper does not name a best season for a later visit.

The dedication the paper described

Jim Cornelius and Tom Chace wrote in The Nugget on 5 August 2003 that the park was already one of the popular spots in Sisters when about 200 people gathered on Friday, 1 August, for the dedication. A jazz combo from Sisters Middle School played. The Sisters-Camp Sherman Rural Fire Protection District served barbecue. Mayor Dave Elliott gave a history of the project and thanked Peter Storton, Gary Frazee, and Glenn Miller. Elliott said that without those three the park would have taken another month or two and probably gone over budget.

The restrooms opened about a month early, in time for Rodeo weekend, and were heavily used during the Quilt Show. The paper said they brought the number of public restrooms inside the city limits to three. Village Green had been the first for many years, and Forest Service facilities near the Y on the west end of town were the second. The park itself, the paper said, has wooden benches, a grassy swale, and a water feature designed and built by SisterScapes.

A side benefit the paper attributes to Peter Storton is work the Oregon Department of Transportation did on Cascade Avenue: curb extensions, which the paper calls bubbles, jutting into Highway 20 so drivers can see people crossing where there is no cross street.

Who the park is named for

The same article says the park was named for Harold and Dorothy Barclay. The Barclays owned a mill on north Pine Street until the local logging industry faded. Barclay's old office became an addition to the Sisters Library when that building was moved from the Depot Deli site to Spruce Street and expanded. A companion Nugget story on 29 July 2003, announcing the 5:30 p.m. dedication, says Harold Barclay pushed Sisters from a resource economy toward visitors and gave the impetus for the town's Western architectural theme, and that the Barclays developed Barclay Square on Cascade Avenue.

Travel Oregon's listing, credited to the Sisters Chamber of Commerce, is shorter. It says the park is in downtown Sisters and is named for Harold and Dorothy Barclay. It also labels a photograph as sculpture at Barclay Park. The listing does not name the sculptor or the subject of that sculpture.

The town around the park

The English Wikipedia article on Sisters, Oregon, places the city in Deschutes County, in the Bend metropolitan area, and says the 2020 census counted 3,064 people. It says the community takes its name from the Three Sisters mountains. Those census and name lines are about the city, not about the park's acreage.
