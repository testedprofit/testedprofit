Black Bird statue

The Black Bird statue is a roadside landmark in front of the Black Bird store at 1810 W. Main Street, Medford, Oregon 97501. The store says Lee Hobbs founded it in 1965 as an army surplus store and designed and built the 29-foot bird himself from rebar, hog wire, and fiberglass. The store says the figure was fashioned after a 10-foot magpie at Yard Birds in Centralia, Washington, a shop owned by a friend of Hobbs. Staff dress the bird in a tank top and shorts for the Pear Blossom Run and in a Santa hat and candy cane for Christmas.

The Oregonian, in a May 2022 feature, says Hobbs had worked as a fabricator at Boeing and as a mechanic. It describes bulging biceps, toes that curl upward, and claws that bend at opposing angles, with arms about twice the length of the legs. The open beak looks down from two concrete pillars painted B.B. Jonathan Quitt, then vice president of the shopping center, told the paper the statue is a Medford icon. The paper prints the same street address, 1810 W. Main Street.

Southern Oregon Historical Society describes a skeleton of concrete bars, hog wire clipped over the frame, and fiberglass sprayed over the wire, with the underpinning 30 feet below the surface. It describes a yellow and black head wired with a speaker, three talons on each hand and two on each foot, and a small plaque under the left foot memorializing Hobbs, who died in 1973. The account says the bird still gets a paint job and seasonal wardrobe changes.

Questions

Where is the Black Bird statue?

The store and The Oregonian place the statue at 1810 W. Main Street in Medford, in front of the Black Bird store.

Who built it, and when?

The store says Lee Hobbs designed and built the 29-foot bird in 1965 from rebar, hog wire, and fiberglass. Southern Oregon Historical Society describes a concrete-bar skeleton, hog wire, and fiberglass.

What does the store put on the bird?

The store says the bird wears a tank top and shorts for the Pear Blossom Run and a Santa hat and candy cane at Christmas.
