Medford, West Main

Black Bird statue

The Black Bird statue is the 29-foot roadside bird in the parking lot of Black Bird Shopping Center at 1810 W. Main Street, Medford, Oregon 97501. Lee Hobbs designed and built it in 1965 for the army surplus store he had just opened. This page is a Profitnet guide to the statue. It is not the store, and it does not sell the gear inside.

Place
In front of the store, on two concrete pillars marked B.B. Pin 42.32450, -122.89311.
Built
1965, by Lee Hobbs. 29 feet. Rebar, hog wire, and fiberglass in the store's account. A concrete-bar skeleton in the historical society's account.
Look
Yellow and black head, beak open, long arms, bulging biceps, claws that do not match a field guide. A plaque under the left foot remembers Hobbs, who died in 1973.
Store hours
The store lists Monday through Saturday, 9 a.m. to 7 p.m., and Sunday, 9:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. Phone (541) 779-5431.

A surplus store that needed a bird

Hobbs opened the Medford shop in 1965 as an army surplus store and put a giant bird in the lot to pull drivers in. He had worked as a fabricator at Boeing in Seattle and as a mechanic. He had no art training. He built the figure anyway, with his nephew Tom on the wire frame of a bicep before the pieces went up outside his service station. The finished bird went onto two concrete pillars painted with the store initials, B.B., and it looks down at the cars turning in.

The store describes the construction as rebar, hog wire, and fiberglass. The Southern Oregon Historical Society describes a skeleton of concrete bars, hog wire clipped over that frame, and fiberglass sprayed over the wire, with the underpinning 30 feet below the surface. Both accounts are the same bird: 29 feet, built by Hobbs, still the mascot out front. The head is yellow and black and was wired with a speaker. Three talons sit on each hand and two on each foot. The Oregonian's 12 May 2022 portrait adds the proportions people notice from the sidewalk: arms about twice the length of the legs, biceps, toes that curl upward, and claws that bend at opposing angles. Jonathan Quitt, then vice president of the shopping center, called it a Medford icon and also a little grotesque. The beak stays open.

The idea was older than Medford. Yard Birds, a surplus chain, started in 1947 in Centralia, Washington, when Bill Jones and Rich Gillingham used a name for a trainee soldier. Hobbs built the original Yard Bird statue for Jones, a friend. The Medford store says its own 29-foot bird was fashioned after a 10-foot magpie at that Centralia shop. The Medford figure is the one on West Main. The Washington birds are a different chain.

Paint, clothes, and a plaque

Hobbs died of lung cancer in 1973 at age 51. His sons later sold their shares to longtime employee Bill Quitt, who stayed on as president. A small plaque under the bird's left foot memorializes Hobbs. The historical society, writing about fifty years after the build, said the bird still stood, still got a paint job when it needed one, and still changed clothes with the season.

The store still dresses it. For Medford's Pear Blossom Run the bird wears a tank top and shorts. At Christmas it wears a Santa hat and holds a candy cane. Other occasions get other props. In 2021 the company hired Augie's Fiberglass to repair and repaint the figure after decades of weather and the occasional car that rear-ended it. Quitt bought a hydraulic lift so staff could get back to holiday dressing, and he hoped to restore the speaker in the beak, which had gone quiet. The Rogue Valley Times, covering the later Phoenix store in June 2024, described the Medford bird as standing 29 feet above the parking lot, on a 4-foot stand.

Inside, the theme was once live myna birds that talked to customers. The historical society said several birds had been there and that one remained. By the early 2020s the store had gone years without them. In 2023 the owners brought in two new mynas, Ace and Betty. The June 2024 account still placed that pair in Medford, with a chance one would move south. The outdoor statue does not depend on the live birds. It is the thing you see from West Main before you park.

How to see it, and the indoor twin

The statue is outdoors, in the customer lot, on the pillars at the front of Black Bird Shopping Center. The store lists Medford hours as Monday through Saturday, 9 a.m. to 7 p.m., and Sunday, 9:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. The phone at that address is (541) 779-5431, and the public email is hello@theblackbird.com. Those lines belong to the shop.

A second bird now stands in Phoenix, Oregon, and it is easy to mix the two up in a search. In May 2022 Quitt told The Oregonian that a new store would get its own statue, then hoped for at about 20 feet. The store that opened is Black Bird ACE Hardware at 4403 S. Pacific Highway, Phoenix, OR 97535, near the Bear Creek Greenway. The Rogue Valley Times reported a ribbon cutting in June 2024. Phoenix sign and art rules kept the new figure indoors. Augie's Fiberglass and Boat Repair of Medford built it in three parts. It is fiberglass, 25 feet tall, in the center of the store, on extra concrete that runs six feet below the floor, with rebar and metal inside. The Medford original is the outdoor 1965 bird on West Main. The Phoenix bird is the indoor 2024 one.

If you want the paper trail, the Southern Oregon Historical Society library is open to the public at 106 N. Central Avenue in Medford, Tuesday through Friday, noon to 4 p.m., with free access to the archives. Their file on the statue is the one that records the footing, the talons, and the plaque.

Other Oregon figures

The Black Bird is the Medford roadside one. Elsewhere in Oregon, the Dancing Mary in Portland is a cemetery statue with a different story, the Wildland Firefighter Monument in Prineville is a memorial, and the Bonanza Bigfoot is another oversized roadside figure. The full list is the Oregon directory.

Questions

Where is the Black Bird statue?

In the lot in front of Black Bird Shopping Center at 1810 W. Main Street, Medford, Oregon 97501. The pin is 42.32450, -122.89311. This page is an independent guide, not the store.

Who built it, and when?

Lee Hobbs designed and built the 29-foot bird in 1965 for the army surplus store he founded that year. The store describes rebar, hog wire, and fiberglass. The historical society describes a concrete-bar skeleton, hog wire, and sprayed fiberglass, with the footing 30 feet down.

What does the store put on the bird?

The store dresses the bird in a tank top and shorts for the Pear Blossom Run and in a Santa hat and a candy cane at Christmas. In 2021 Augie's Fiberglass repaired and repainted it after decades of weather and the occasional car bump.

Is there another Black Bird statue?

Yes. A second store opened in June 2024 at 4403 S. Pacific Highway in Phoenix, Oregon, with a 25-foot fiberglass bird inside the building. The Medford statue is the outdoor 1965 original on West Main.
