Wildland Firefighter Monument

The Wildland Firefighter Monument is a roadside landmark in Prineville, Oregon. The Spokesman-Review, on June 9, 1996, reported that a bronze of three firefighters gathered around a charred tree had been loaded on a truck in Denver for a 1,200-mile trip to Prineville. The paper said the statue would be dedicated there on June 15 as the centerpiece of a monument to all wildland firefighters.

Sculptor David Nelson, then 44, of Marble, Colorado, told the paper he was chosen from 70 artists. A committee of parents of Prineville Hotshots commissioned the work. Nine of the 14 firefighters who died at Storm King were Prineville Hotshots. Two Idaho-based smokejumpers, including Jim Thrash of New Meadows, also were killed. Ochoco National Forest spokesman Norm Hesseldahl told the paper that part of the monument's $120,000 cost would be paid with $72,000 in donations collected after the Storm King deaths, and that the parents' committee voted to put that money toward the monument.

The same report says the committee planned to raise more money by selling gallery-sized replicas and figurines. The bronze of the three firefighters is the monument the paper described on its way to Prineville for that June 15 dedication.
