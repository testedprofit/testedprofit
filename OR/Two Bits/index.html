Two Bits

Two Bits is a roadside landmark at Farewell Bend Park in Bend. The Bulletin's April 8, 2022 park-art tour says local metal sculptor Greg Congleton crafted Two Bits in 2011 as a tribute to Bend's logging history. It is a sculpture of two draft horses pulling a log, commissioned by Phil and Penny Knight, on the east bank of the Deschutes River at 1000 SW Reed Market Road. The writer tells visitors to look at the objects Congleton built into the sculpture.

Cascade Arts and Entertainment, in March 2013, calls it a heroic sculpture 28 feet long, made of steel found objects. One horse is male and one is female. The male side uses a gas engine and a truck transmission. The female side uses ropes, pulleys, an antique hay trolley, and a Singer sewing machine. The log holds two vintage chain saws and a misery whip. Penny and Phil Knight commissioned the piece. Working with Don Horton, then executive director of Bend Park and Recreation, they chose a horse-logging theme, two life-size draft horses pulling a 42-inch ponderosa log, and chose Farewell Bend Park as the place to mark Bend's timber heritage.

Together the two accounts place Two Bits at Farewell Bend Park in 2011, name Greg Congleton as the maker, name Phil and Penny Knight as the commissioners, and describe a scrap-steel team of draft horses pulling a log on the east bank of the Deschutes.

Questions

Where is Two Bits?

The Bulletin places Greg Congleton's Two Bits at Farewell Bend Park, on the east bank of the Deschutes at 1000 SW Reed Market Road.

What does it show?

The Bulletin describes two draft horses pulling a log. Cascade Arts and Entertainment calls the work 28 feet long and made of steel found objects, with a gas engine, a truck transmission, a hay trolley, a sewing machine, chain saws, and a misery whip.

Who commissioned it?

The Bulletin and Cascade Arts and Entertainment say Phil and Penny Knight commissioned the 2011 sculpture.
