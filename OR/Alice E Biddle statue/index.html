Alice E. Biddle statue

The Alice E. Biddle statue is a roadside landmark on the Oregon State University campus in Corvallis. Beaver's Digest and Wikipedia both name the bronze The Quest. Kirk St. Maur made it in 1983. It stands near the path between Memorial Union and Strand Agriculture Hall.

Beaver's Digest says the figure is Alice E. Biddle, the first woman to graduate from the university, at 16 in 1870, and that she organized the first alumni reunion. The university chronology places her in the class of 1870 with Robert M. Veatch and James K. P. Currin. At the June 30 commencement she read a paper, Progress of Mind. In December 1870 she married one of her professors, William W. Moreland. The plaque text quoted by Wikipedia says The Quest symbolizes Alice E. Biddle and reflects the energy, dedication, and strength of all students in the pursuit of learning. It credits Oregon's Art in Public Places program.

Wikipedia, citing the Smithsonian Save Outdoor Sculpture survey, says the bronze measures about 5 feet 11 inches by 4 feet by 4 feet 6 inches. The figure stands with her head turned to her right and holds a rolled paper in that hand. An inscription on the back of the skirt carries the artist's name, a copyright mark, and the year. The Oregon Arts Commission commissioned the work through its 1 percent for Art in State Buildings program. The Smithsonian survey of March 1993 recorded the condition as treatment needed. Oregon State University administers the sculpture.

Questions

Who does the Alice E. Biddle statue depict?

Beaver's Digest and Wikipedia say The Quest is a 1983 bronze of Alice E. Biddle, the first woman to graduate from Oregon State, made by Kirk St. Maur.

Where does the statue stand?

It stands on the Oregon State campus near the path between Memorial Union and Strand Agriculture Hall.

What does the university chronology say about Biddle?

She was 16 when she received her degree in the class of 1870, read a paper called Progress of Mind at the June 30 commencement, and married professor William W. Moreland in December 1870.
