Memorial to the Start Westward

Muskingum Park, Marietta

The Memorial to the Start Westward is in Marietta, Ohio. Wikipedia gives its official name as the Memorial to the Start Westward of the United States, and also calls it the Start Westward Memorial, the National Start Westward Memorial of The United States, and the Start Westward Monument. It is a 1938 sculpture designed by Gutzon Borglum and located in Muskingum Park. The City of Marietta places the Start Westward Monument on the 300 block of Front Street in Muskingum Park.

Wikipedia says Borglum agreed to the commission at the request of George White, his friend and a former governor of Ohio. The memorial commemorates the westward expansion of the United States and the sesquicentennial of the establishment of Marietta in 1788. It stands on an esplanade designed by Borglum and the architect John Schooley. Borglum visited Marietta in October 1936. His appointment by the Federal Commission for the Celebration of the 150th Anniversary of the Ordinance of 1787 was announced in the Marietta Daily Times on November 18, 1936, according to Wikipedia. He designed the memorial at his studio in San Antonio, Texas, in late 1936 and 1937, working in plaster of Paris. A small clay maquette went to stonemasons hired with Works Progress Administration funding.

Eighty tons and twenty-five tons

Wikipedia says the carvers sculpted the design from an 80-short-ton block of sandstone from a quarry near old lock number 18 on the Ohio River, at a workshop at Briggs Station. With its weight reduced to 25 short tons, the partially completed carving was moved ten miles on May 7, 1938 to Muskingum Park, then known as East Muskingum Park, on the east bank of the Muskingum River. Wikipedia says that position is where Arthur St. Clair was inaugurated as first governor of the Northwest Territory. The Start Westward Memorial Society caption for December 13, 1937 says Borglum viewed the 80-ton block of sandstone from which the six heroic figures were carved. Its caption for May 7, 1938 says the partially carved block, then weighing 25 tons, was loaded for the move to Muskingum Park. The society's 80 tons and 25 tons, and Wikipedia's 80 short tons and 25 short tons, are the two weight lines those pages print.

The society says the memorial was sculpted from local sandstone with New Deal WPA funding, and that carving began at the quarry near old Ohio River Lock 18. A caption says that on June 1, 1938, John Schooley supervised rotation of the standing group of pioneers so they faced eastward toward Front Street. Wikipedia says Borglum also sculpted four eagles, a gift to the city, to top pylons at entrances to the park on Front Street. The City of Marietta says that in 1961 the central figure lost his head and Fred Mitchum of Beverly carved a replacement, and that photographs from about the mid-1950s and from 2007 show the erosion and the difference in that head.

July 8, 1938, and a 1937 completion line

Wikipedia says President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated the memorial on July 8, 1938, after traveling to Marietta by train, and that Borglum refused to attend because he believed the stone was of inferior quality. The society says Roosevelt arrived at 9:00 a.m. and dedicated the Memorial to the Start Westward of the United States at 10:00 a.m. Wikipedia says the sculpture appeared on a 3-cent United States postage stamp issued on July 15, 1938. In 2020 the memorial was covered by a temporary canopy. In August 2021, Marietta City Council approved a 7 million dollar proposal by the Start Westward Memorial Society to replicate the memorial in bronze and move the original stone sculpture indoors.

The Marietta Times of July 11, 2026 reports Fred Smith, hired by the city as an adviser, telling a council committee that work was comparing a 3D scan of the existing monument with historic images of the sculpture newly completed in 1937. Smith said the stone is heavily eroded, particularly in the faces, that McKay Lodge Conservation Laboratory helped scan it, and that the next step was a wax model for a bronze casting. He estimated bronze maintenance at about 6,000 to 9,000 dollars a year. He said identified funding included 500,000 dollars through the Ohio History Connection, 150,000 dollars in American Rescue Plan Act funds, and 100,000 dollars in state capital funding. Wikipedia and the society date the dedication to July 8, 1938, and the move into the park to May 7, 1938. Smith's 1937 completion line is the date he gave the committee.

Questions

Where is the Memorial to the Start Westward?

It stands in Muskingum Park in Marietta, Ohio. The City of Marietta places it on the 300 block of Front Street. Wikipedia says the park is on the east bank of the Muskingum River, at the place where Arthur St. Clair was inaugurated as first governor of the Northwest Territory.

Who designed it, and when was it dedicated?

Gutzon Borglum designed it at the request of former Ohio governor George White. President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated it on July 8, 1938. The Start Westward Memorial Society says that dedication was at 10:00 a.m. Wikipedia says carvers worked an 80-short-ton sandstone block down to 25 short tons. Society captions say an 80-ton block and a 25-ton block on May 7, 1938.
