Jackson Memorial Fountain

City Park entrance

The Jackson Memorial Fountain stands at the entrance to City Park in Parkersburg, Wood County. Wikipedia places it at Park Avenue and 17th Street. The National Park Service record for National Register number 84003686 lists the same corner, Park Ave. and 17th St., and gives 1905 as the significant year. The State of West Virginia nomination, prepared in 1984, places the fountain at the corner of Park Avenue and 17th Street in Parkersburg City Park.

Wikipedia says the fountain was built in 1905 as a cast-iron structure that originally had three tiers, with Parkersburg's Lady of the Lake statue topping the second tier, and elaborately sculpted basins and pedestals. It is dedicated to the Jackson family: General John Jay Jackson and his sons, federal judge John Jay Jackson, Jr., Governor Jacob B. Jackson, and circuit judge and congressman James M. Jackson. The nomination says the same family. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1984.

Thirty feet, then about twenty

The 1984 nomination describes the original fountain as three tiers. It gives the upper basin a diameter of about 8 feet, the second basin about 13 feet, and the lowest bowl about 30 feet. The Lady of the Lake was mounted on the upper tier. The nomination says the fountain was originally about 30 feet high. With the top basin off, it was about 20 feet high, and at the time of the nomination the statue stood on the second tier. The nomination calls the fountain cast iron, with the exception of the lion ornaments, and describes it as the largest and most significant known Victorian cast-iron object of ornamental design in West Virginia.

The 2020 replica

The News and Sentinel of June 16, 2020 says the original fountain was built in 1905 and that portions were replaced with fiberglass after a lightning strike in the early 1990s. Heavy winds toppled it in October 2018. A replacement, fabricated to be as close to the original as possible, was delivered in pieces to City Park from Robinson Iron in Alexander City, Alabama. City Engineer Adam Stout said installation would take about three weeks. The story says approximately 15,000 pounds of cast iron, pipes, and hardware were unloaded, and that one bowl weighed 6,600 pounds. Stout said the replacement structure would weigh close to 30,000 pounds with water flowing through it. The city used a charitable trust, an insurance reimbursement, and the general fund, for a total cost of approximately $508,100. Alan Stone Co. was the project contractor.

Wikipedia says the original cast-iron bowls were destroyed by lightning in the early 1990s, that their glass-fibre replacements and the rest of the original structure were destroyed in an October 2018 storm, and that a replica was erected in 2020. The News and Sentinel gallery of July 16, 2020 says the 21st century fountain was joined together on Wednesday, with the Lady of the Lake placed at the top. It says Robinson Iron reconstructed the fountain, Alan Stone Co. erected it, and about 15,000 pounds of cast iron was used. The June figure of about 15,000 pounds of cast iron and the close-to-30,000-pound weight with water are both in that June story. The July gallery repeats the 15,000-pound cast-iron figure.
