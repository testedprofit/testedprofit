Muffler Man Big Red

Big Red is the fiberglass muffler man in south Parkersburg, reworked as an Indian brave with his right arm raised in a wave. He stands at Cherokee Village, the landmark for Cherokee Village Auto Center and the title and license office there. A 2018 guide gives the address as 1704 Dupont Rd., Parkersburg, West Virginia 26101. Roadside America sends you to the corner of Highway 95 and Jeffrey Street. This page is a Profitnet guide to the statue. It is not the auto center.

Name
Big Red on the muffler-man list. The 2018 Parkersburg guide and Roadside America call him an Indian muffler man, or a brave.
Where
Near the South Parkersburg Shopping Plaza. 1704 Dupont Rd., 26101. Also: I-77 exit 173, Highway 95 west just past Highway 14, corner of Highway 95 and Jeffrey Street, Cherokee Village Title and License Center.
When
The list dates his erection in Parkersburg to the early 1980s. International Fiberglass, which built most muffler men, had closed in 1976.
Still there
A July 2019 note says he was still at the corner, with patchwork on the hands. A visitor in October 2024, writing on 1 February 2026, found the waving brave still in Parkersburg.

The figure on Dupont Road

The Wikipedia list of muffler men files one West Virginia entry. Big Red stands in Parkersburg near the South Parkersburg Shopping Plaza. He was erected in the early 1980s and has served as a landmark for Cherokee Village Auto Center and other businesses at that spot. In the mid-2010s, Parkersburg Big Reds head football coach Don Reeves tried to move Big Red to PHS Stadium Field. Later accounts still find him at Cherokee Village, so the stadium move did not take him off this corner.

Travel Inspired Living, on 28 August 2018, calls him an Indian muffler man standing watch over the Cherokee Village Auto Center Title and License Service Center, at 1704 Dupont Rd. That guide lists the stop as free and notes that fiberglass repairs are costly, which is why many muffler men have gone to the junkyard instead of the shop. Roadside America describes a fiberglass muffler man reworked as an Indian brave, waving south of Parkersburg at the corner of Highway 95 and Jeffrey Street. The raised right arm is the mark of the Indian muffler man. The two direction sets name the same kind of business: Cherokee Village, title and license. Use Dupont Road if you have the 2018 address. Use exit 173 and Highway 95 if you are coming off the interstate. Look for the raised arm over the lot.

A July 2019 field note on Roadside America says he is right off Highway 95, has seen better days, and shows patchwork on the lower hand and possible molding on the upper hand, and that he was still there. Rachael, writing on Girlie Road Trip on 1 February 2026, stopped in October 2024. She describes the waving brave, pants over the boots in the standard muffler-man cut, pockets on the pants, defined nostrils, and defined irises. That October visit is the latest published look: he was still waving in Parkersburg.

What a muffler man is

Muffler men are large molded fiberglass sculptures used as advertising icons, roadside attractions, or decoration, mostly in the United States. As a group they stand about 18 to 25 feet. The first figure was a Paul Bunyan made to hold an axe. Later ones held mufflers, tires, or whatever the roadside business sold. International Fiberglass of Venice, California built most of them. Boatbuilder Steve Dashew started the company in 1963 by buying Bob Prewitt's workshop, Prewitt Fiberglass. Prewitt had made the first Bunyan in 1962 for the Lumberjack Cafe on Route 66 in Flagstaff. Bill Swan helped design that face. The molds kept the original pose, right palm up and left palm down, and let makers swap heads, arms, and torsos. Food figures, an Amish man, Uniroyal gals, tigers, and Phillips cowboys all came off those molds. Thousands went out in a little over a decade, often for about 1,000 to 2,800 dollars. Delivery of the light but huge figures got expensive, and the 1973 oil crisis hurt the business. International Fiberglass was sold and closed for good in 1976.

Big Red's Parkersburg date is the early 1980s, after that closing. The company that made most of these figures was already gone when this one went up at Cherokee Village. The height of 18 to 25 feet is the range for muffler men as a group. The Parkersburg list does not print a separate measurement for Big Red. What you use in the lot is the raised right arm, the Indian rework, and the auto-center sign.

How to see him, and what else is close

He is outdoors, in the commercial lot, so you see him from the road and the parking area. The 2018 guide lists the Dupont Road stop as free. From I-77, take exit 173 and go west on Highway 95, just past Highway 14, to the corner of Highway 95 and Jeffrey Street, at Cherokee Village Title and License Center. The street address in that 2018 guide is 1704 Dupont Rd., Parkersburg, West Virginia 26101. Across the street, the same guide points to McHappy's Donuts and Bake Shoppe, then open 5 a.m. to 9 p.m., seven days a week.

Two other guides on this site sit in the same river towns. The Jackson Memorial Fountain is in Parkersburg. The Memorial to the Start Westward is in Marietta, Ohio, across the Ohio River. Roadside America also lists, within a couple of miles of this muffler man, the Oil and Gas Museum and the Grave of the Weeping Woman. The state list is the West Virginia directory.
